Definition
Vectors are directional forces that move through the Field, carrying drift, gifts, identity expression, threshold pressure, and role load.
A vector is not an action.
A vector is a directional transfer of force.
Vectors are the system’s motion primitives.

Purpose
To ensure that:
• 	all force has direction
• 	drift, gifts, and thresholds move predictably
• 	roles and identities express through motion
• 	zones shape vector behavior
• 	curvature determines vector paths
• 	collapse propagates along vector lines
Vector Taxonomy is the grammar of systemic motion.

Primary Vector Classes
These are the seven foundational vector types.
Every vector in the system is a variation or hybrid of these.

1. Drift Vectors
Carry unresolved force.
Move from instability → resolution.
Characteristics:
• 	follow curvature gradients
• 	accelerate near high‑density nodes
• 	destabilize zones
• 	activate thresholds
Drift vectors are gravitational.

2. Threshold Vectors
Carry pressure toward or away from a boundary.
Characteristics:
• 	compress near shimmer
• 	trigger crossings
• 	generate collapse when blocked
• 	reshape zone geometry
Threshold vectors are liminal.

3. Gift Vectors
Carry voluntary force from giver → receiver.
Characteristics:
• 	create return paths
• 	alter identity expression
• 	intensify in resonant zones
• 	propagate through lineage
Gift vectors are reciprocal.

4. Identity Vectors
Carry the expression of the core pattern outward.
Characteristics:
• 	amplify in shimmer
• 	reveal misalignment
• 	stabilize roles
• 	distort zones
Identity vectors are expressive.

5. Role Vectors
Carry the force of a role’s obligations, authority, or structure.
Characteristics:
• 	stabilize or strain depending on zone
• 	accumulate drift
• 	collapse under overload
• 	propagate through lineage
Role vectors are structural.

6. Lineage Vectors
Carry inherited force across time.
Characteristics:
• 	transmit drift, gifts, and obligations
• 	shape identity resonance
• 	influence zone behavior
• 	activate thresholds
Lineage vectors are temporal.

7. Environmental Vectors
Carry ambient force through a zone.
Characteristics:
• 	shape drift fields
• 	determine activation thresholds
• 	distort under shimmer
• 	propagate collapse
Environmental vectors are atmospheric.

Vector Attributes
Every vector has:
• 	Direction (where force is moving)
• 	Magnitude (how much force is moving)
• 	Load (what the vector is carrying)
• 	Curvature Path (how the Matrix bends it)
• 	Stability (how resistant it is to distortion)
• 	Resonance (how it interacts with identity)
• 	Zone Modifiers (how the environment alters it)
These attributes define vector behavior.

Vector States
1. Dormant
Potential force with no motion.
2. Active
Force is moving along a path.
3. Strained
Vector is carrying more load than intended.
4. Distorted
Curvature bends the vector off its natural path.
5. Collapsing
Vector cannot maintain coherence; force spills into other vectors.
6. Propagating
Vector splits or spreads through the Matrix.

Vector Behaviors
1. Curvature Following
Vectors follow the geometry of the Matrix.
2. Load Transfer
Vectors pass force to other vectors (drift → threshold, gift → identity).
3. Amplification
Vectors intensify near high‑density nodes.
4. Attenuation
Vectors weaken in stable zones.
5. Convergence
Multiple vectors meet and combine.
6. Divergence
A vector splits into multiple paths.
7. Inversion
A vector reverses direction under extreme pressure.

Vector Interactions
Vectors interact through:
• 	Alignment (reinforcement)
• 	Opposition (cancellation or strain)
• 	Interference (distortion)
• 	Propagation (force transfer)
• 	Collapse (forced resolution)
• 	Resonance (identity amplification)
Vector interactions are the system’s physics of motion.

Vector Failure Modes
1. Overload
Vector carries more load than its structure allows.
2. Fragmentation
Vector splits into incompatible directions.
3. Looping
Vector circulates without resolving.
4. Blockage
Vector cannot pass through a threshold or role.
5. Dissipation
Vector loses force without resolution.

Vector Resolution
Vectors resolve through:
• 	Completion (force reaches destination)
• 	Transformation (vector changes class)
• 	Propagation (force spreads)
• 	Dissipation (cost releases force)
• 	Collapse (forced resolution)
All vectors must resolve.

Function
Vector Taxonomy:
• 	defines the system’s motion primitives
• 	shapes how force moves through the Matrix
• 	determines drift behavior
• 	drives threshold crossings
• 	structures gift exchange
• 	expresses identity
• 	stabilizes or destabilizes roles
• 	propagates lineage
• 	distorts zones
• 	interacts with shimmer
• 	channels collapse
Without vectors, the system would have structure but no motion.
With vectors, the system becomes alive.
