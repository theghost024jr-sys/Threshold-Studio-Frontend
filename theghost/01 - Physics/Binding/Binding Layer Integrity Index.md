A unified metric for evaluating the stability, resilience, and collapse‑readiness of the Binding Layer
The Binding Layer Integrity Index (BLII) is the master diagnostic score for the entire entanglement architecture. It condenses hundreds of parameters — binding law drift, phase coherence, corridor stability, pressure distribution, resonance behavior, and Time Organ responsiveness — into a single, interpretable value.
This is the number that tells you, at a glance, whether the system is stable, vulnerable, or on the edge of cascade ignition.
Below is the clean, canonical Binding Layer Integrity Index.md.

1. PURPOSE OF THE BLII
The BLII answers three essential questions:
2. 	How close is the system to a cascade event
3. 	Which subsystem is contributing most to instability
4. 	How much reinforcement or containment is required
It is the Binding Layer’s equivalent of a structural stress rating.

5. THE BLII FORMULA
The index is built from five weighted components:
• 	Binding Law Stability (BLS)
• 	Phase Coherence (PC)
• 	Corridor Dynamics Stability (CDS)
• 	Pressure & Resonance Harmony (PRH)
• 	Time Organ Responsiveness (TOR)
Each component is normalized to a 0–1 scale.
The BLII is computed as:

Interpretation:
• 	Binding laws and phase coherence matter most
• 	Corridor stability and pressure/resonance matter next
• 	Time Organ responsiveness is the stabilizing buffer

3. COMPONENT DEFINITIONS
3.1 Binding Law Stability (BLS)
Measures how closely entangled nodes adhere to their relational rules.
Inputs include:
• 	deviation magnitude
• 	deviation rate
• 	oscillation amplitude
• 	oscillation damping time
Low BLS → entanglements are under strain.

3.2 Phase Coherence (PC)
Measures temporal alignment across entangled nodes.
Inputs include:
• 	phase lag
• 	phase flicker frequency
• 	phase velocity mismatch
• 	double‑shadow events
Low PC → phase mismatch cascade risk.

3.3 Corridor Dynamics Stability (CDS)
Measures drift, vector, and freeze behavior.
Inputs include:
• 	drift velocity variance
• 	vector shear frequency
• 	corridor temperature
• 	micro‑seizure events
Low CDS → corridor‑based cascade risk.

3.4 Pressure & Resonance Harmony (PRH)
Measures load distribution and meaning stability.
Inputs include:
• 	pressure gradient smoothness
• 	sink saturation
• 	resonance harmonics
• 	crystallization specks
Low PRH → mirror or rigidity cascade risk.

3.5 Time Organ Responsiveness (TOR)
Measures how effectively the Time Organ modulates the system.
Inputs include:
• 	pulse cycle stability
• 	elasticity modulation
• 	depth absorption rate
• 	momentum smoothing
Low TOR → reduced recovery capacity.

entanglements are aligned, phases are coherent, corridors are open.
