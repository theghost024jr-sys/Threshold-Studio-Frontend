The movement topology of Threshold, mapping drift corridors, oscillation zones, recursive loops, spiral vectors, and collapse‑aligned drift across all environments.

Purpose
The Environmental Drift Map defines how drift — the universal movement pattern of Threshold — behaves within each environment.
It tracks:
• 	drift corridors
• 	drift loops
• 	drift spirals
• 	drift storms
• 	drift‑pressure coupling
• 	drift‑resonance distortion
• 	drift‑collapse alignment
Drift is not wind, water, or motion.
It is systemic movement — the way forces, identities, signals, and environments shift under pressure.

The Five Drift Forms
Drift expresses itself in five canonical forms:

1. Stable Drift
Predictable, linear movement.
Effects:
• 	stable identity
• 	clear signals
• 	low collapse risk

2. Oscillating Drift
Movement that flips direction under pressure or resonance.
Effects:
• 	unstable clarity
• 	boundary wobble
• 	collapse risk spikes

3. Recursive Drift
Movement that loops back on itself.
Effects:
• 	identity recursion
• 	signal looping
• 	collapse vector activation

4. Spiral Drift
Movement that curves inward or outward.
Effects:
• 	drift storms
• 	resonance distortion
• 	collapse alignment

5. Drift Storm
Chaotic, nonlinear movement.
Effects:
• 	boundary rupture
• 	collapse identity
• 	worldphase fracture

Environmental Drift Profiles
Each environment shapes drift in a distinct way.

Branch — Boundary‑Guided Drift
Signature: Stable, linear drift guided by strong boundaries
Effects:
• 	predictable movement
• 	low collapse risk
• 	stable identity
Failure Mode: Boundary thinning → oscillating drift

Mist — Drift‑Dominant Environment
Signature: Drift corridors form naturally and shift constantly
Effects:
• 	oscillating drift
• 	recursive drift loops
• 	signal bending
• 	identity instability
Failure Mode: Drift recursion → drift storm

Deep — Pressure‑Driven Drift
Signature: Drift shaped by compression and nonlinear pressure
Effects:
• 	spiral drift
• 	drift curvature
• 	collapse vector alignment
• 	resonance distortion
Failure Mode: Pressure spike → drift storm

Shore — Oscillation Drift
Signature: Drift flips direction under pressure‑resonance interaction
Effects:
• 	oscillation corridors
• 	unstable drift patterns
• 	identity wobble
• 	collapse risk spikes
Failure Mode: Oscillation break → recursive drift

Ruin — Collapse‑Aligned Drift
Signature: Drift pulled toward collapse wells and absence lines
Effects:
• 	inward spiral drift
• 	collapse identity formation
• 	lineage rupture
• 	echo storms
Failure Mode: Collapse well expansion → drift storm

Grove — Lineage‑Stabilized Drift
Signature: Drift stabilized by active lineage threads
Effects:
• 	stable drift
• 	identity reinforcement
• 	collapse suppression
• 	signal clarity
Failure Mode: Lineage rupture → oscillating drift

Field — Low‑Pressure Drift
Signature: Shallow, stable drift with minimal curvature
Effects:
• 	predictable movement
• 	low collapse risk
• 	weak lineage activation
Failure Mode: Pressure rise → oscillating drift

Forge — Ignition Drift
Signature: Drift sharpened by ignition pressure
Effects:
• 	high‑velocity drift
• 	catalytic movement
• 	collapse ignition potential
• 	signal amplification
Failure Mode: Overpressure → spiral drift

Drift Corridors Between Environments
Drift flows along predictable environmental pathways:
Branch → Mist
Stable drift becomes oscillating.
Mist → Shore
Oscillating drift becomes unstable oscillation.
Shore → Deep
Oscillation collapses into spiral drift.
Deep → Ruin
Spiral drift aligns with collapse vectors.
Ruin → (Lost Myth aligned)
Collapse‑aligned drift falls into absence wells.
Grove → Branch
Lineage‑stabilized drift reinforces boundaries.
Field → Mist
Low‑pressure drift becomes drift‑sensitive.
Forge → Deep / Branch
Ignition drift either stabilizes or destabilizes depending on pressure.
This is the drift circuit of the world.

Drift–Pressure Coupling
Pressure shapes drift:
• 	high pressure → spiral drift
• 	nonlinear pressure → drift storm
• 	low pressure → stable drift
Drift redistributes pressure:
• 	drift corridors pull pressure
• 	drift storms create pressure spikes

Drift–Resonance Coupling
Resonance shapes drift:
• 	clear resonance → stable drift
• 	distorted resonance → spiral drift
• 	inversion → recursive drift
• 	void → drift storm
Drift shapes resonance:
• 	oscillating drift → resonance flipping
• 	spiral drift → resonance curvature
• 	drift storm → resonance collapse

Drift–Collapse Coupling
Collapse follows drift:
• 	recursive drift → collapse vectors
• 	spiral drift → collapse wells
• 	drift storm → systemic collapse
Collapse reshapes drift:
• 	collapse wells pull drift inward
• 	collapse identity creates drift recursion
• 	collapse storms create drift storms
