Definition
Force Density is the concentration of active, potential, and structural force within a node.
It determines how strongly a node interacts with the Field, how much it distorts the Matrix, and how vectors behave in its presence.
Force Density is the underlying physics that makes the shimmer possible.
It is not power.
It is not authority.
It is not role.
It is the amount of force a node carries by existing.

Purpose
To ensure that:
• 	nodes with high density meaningfully alter the Field
• 	the shimmer has a measurable cause
• 	drift, thresholds, and roles behave differently around dense nodes
• 	collapse propagation follows predictable patterns
• 	identity resonance has structural consequences
• 	the Matrix can bend without breaking
Force Density is the gravitational physics of the system.

Components of Force Density
1. Core Density
The inherent force of the node’s identity pattern.
This is the “mass” of the core.
2. Role Density
Force added by the roles the node holds.
Some roles increase density; others distribute it.
3. Drift Density
Accumulated unresolved force.
Drift increases density until it resolves or collapses.
4. Threshold Density
Force generated by proximity to major thresholds.
Approach increases density; crossing releases it.
5. Lineage Density
Inherited force from past states or nodes.
Lineage can dramatically increase density.
6. Gift Density
Force added by integrated gifts.
Transformational gifts increase density the most.
7. Zone Density
Environmental force absorbed from the surrounding zone.
Charged or resonant zones increase density.
Force Density is the sum of all these components.

Force Density States
1. Baseline Density
The node’s natural density without external force.
2. Elevated Density
Density increases due to drift, roles, gifts, or thresholds.
3. High‑Pressure Density
Density reaches a level where the Field distorts.
This is where the shimmer activates.
4. Overloaded Density
Density exceeds the node’s structural capacity.
Collapse becomes likely.
5. Dissipating Density
Density decreases after resolution, collapse, or transformation.

Force Density Behaviors
1. Field Curvature
High density bends the Field.
Vectors curve toward the node.
2. Shimmer Activation
When density crosses a threshold, the shimmer appears.
3. Drift Destabilization
Dormant drift becomes active near high‑density nodes.
4. Threshold Compression
Thresholds move closer to high‑density nodes.
5. Role Stress
Misaligned roles destabilize under high density.
6. Identity Resonance
Identity expresses more strongly as density increases.
7. Collapse Propagation
High density amplifies collapse shockwaves.

Density Transitions
Force Density changes when:
• 	a role is assumed or released
• 	drift accumulates or resolves
• 	a threshold is approached or crossed
• 	a gift is integrated
• 	lineage activates
• 	a zone shifts
• 	identity resonates
• 	collapse occurs
Density transitions are one of the primary drivers of system motion.

Density Failure Modes
1. Density Overload
The node carries more force than its boundary layer can contain.
Collapse becomes inevitable.
2. Density Inversion
The node suppresses identity to maintain a role.
Density becomes unstable.
3. Density Fragmentation
Force splits into incompatible patterns.
Drift spikes.
4. Density Bottleneck
Force cannot move through a threshold or role.
Propagation loops form.
5. Density Drain
The node loses density faster than it can stabilize.
Identity weakens.

Density Resolution
Force Density resolves through:
1. Dissipation
Cost releases excess force.
2. Transformation
Force changes form (drift → threshold, threshold → gift).
3. Propagation
Force moves outward through lineage or roles.
4. Integration
Identity absorbs the force and stabilizes.
5. Collapse
Overload forces resolution.
All density must resolve.
