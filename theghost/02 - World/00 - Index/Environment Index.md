Purpose
The Environment Index is the canonical directory of all environments in Threshold.
It organizes environments by:
• 	pressure profile
• 	drift behavior
• 	collapse style
• 	signal expression
• 	species alignment
• 	structural type
This file is the entry point for navigating the entire environmental subsystem.

1. Environment Categories
Environments fall into six canonical categories, each defined by its dominant pressure and drift behavior.

1.1 Stable Environments
High memory · low distortion · linear drift
Examples:
• 	Groves
• 	Old Forests
• 	Deep Snowfields
These environments stabilize identity and straighten drift arcs.

1.2 Distortion Environments
High distortion · curved drift · perception drift
Examples:
• 	Mist Corridors
• 	Spiral Groves
• 	Mirage Plains
These environments bend drift and warp signals.

1.3 Density Environments
High density · compressive drift · slow motion
Examples:
• 	Ruins
• 	Stone Basins
• 	Deep Ice
These environments compress identity and slow collapse.

1.4 Gradient Environments
Directional pressure · long drift arcs · stable flow
Examples:
• 	Plains
• 	Slopes
• 	Wind Channels
These environments create predictable drift corridors.

1.5 Collapse‑Aligned Environments
Collapse pressure · spiral or brittle drift · unstable identity
Examples:
• 	Spiral Fields
• 	Shatter Zones
• 	Recursive Hollows
These environments amplify collapse tendencies.

1.6 Recovery Environments
Stabilizing pressure · re‑coherence · drift straightening
Examples:
• 	Warm Pockets
• 	Quiet Groves
• 	High Branches
These environments support recovery flow.
