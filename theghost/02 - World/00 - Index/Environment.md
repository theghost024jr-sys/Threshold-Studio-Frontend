[[00 - System Architecture/11 - Threshold Map/Threshold Map|Threshold Map]]


1. The environment’s physics come first
Every environment has:
• 	A core behavior
• 	A pressure logic
• 	A collapse mode
• 	A recovery pattern
• 	A signal grammar
These are the primary determinants.
The soil is simply the substrate that can hold those physics.
Soil is not chosen.
Soil is revealed by the environment’s nature.

2. Soil is the environment’s memory of itself
The environment expresses:
• 	How it moves
• 	How it reacts
• 	How it stores contradiction
• 	How it metabolizes truth
• 	How it handles time
The soil forms underneath as the accumulated residue of those behaviors.
Soil is the sediment of physics.

3. Each environment has a canonical soil type
Examples:
Brinicle Spiral → Compressed + Structural Soil
Because:
• 	Time has a tail
• 	Echoes layer
• 	Pressure builds
• 	Geometry matters
Ember Lattice → Luminous + Residual Soil
Because:
• 	Heat is information
• 	Emotion imprints
• 	Truth brightens
• 	Contradiction dims
Mist Corridor → Dissolved + Absorptive Soil
Because:
• 	Boundaries blur
• 	Memory diffuses
• 	Identity softens
• 	Collapse is gentle
Stone Basin → Structural + Compressed Soil
Because:
• 	Weight is law
• 	Pressure is lineage
• 	Collapse is fracture
• 	Recovery is slow
The environment’s physics determine the soil’s composition.

4. Soil cannot contradict the environment
A Spiral cannot have:
• 	Luminous soil
• 	Dissolved soil
• 	Null soil
Because those substrates cannot hold:
• 	Echo geometry
• 	Pressure layering
• 	Temporal drag
Likewise, the Ember Lattice cannot have:
• 	Compressed soil
• 	Structural soil
Because those substrates would suffocate its heat‑logic.

5. Soil is the environment’s “body”
Environment = the organism
Soil = the tissue that grows from its behavior
NPCs = the cells shaped by that tissue
Everything downstream inherits the environment’s physics through the soil.

6. The rule you stated is the canonical rule
Environment dictates soil.
Soil expresses environment.
NPCs inherit soil.
That’s the chain of causality.
