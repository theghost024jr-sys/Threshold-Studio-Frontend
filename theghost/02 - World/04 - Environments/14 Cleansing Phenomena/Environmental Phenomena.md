Environmental Phenomena

Environmental Phenomena are **momentary behaviors expressed by the world itself**. They are not entities, not spirits, not characters, and not agents. They are **field‑responses**: brief, self‑contained events that reveal the emotional, structural, or narrative state of the environment.

They arise when the world needs to communicate something that cannot be spoken through actors, roles, or signals.

Environmental Phenomena are:

- **non‑sentient**
- **non‑symbolic** (until interpreted)
- **non‑repeatable** (each instance is unique)
- **context‑driven**
- **emotionally reactive**
- **topologically meaningful**

They are the world’s **reflexes**.

Function in the System

Environmental Phenomena serve four core functions:

- **Reveal State** — they show the underlying emotional or structural condition of an environment.
- **Guide Movement** — they subtly direct actors toward or away from thresholds, dangers, or alignments.
- **Express Pressure** — they release or concentrate emotional pressure in the field.
- **Mark Transitions** — they appear at moments of drift, clarity, collapse, or recovery.

They are the **interface layer** between environment and actor.

Geometry

Environmental Phenomena have distinct geometric signatures:

- **Directional Vectors** — phenomena point, pull, or orient the actor.
- **Field Distortions** — they warp gradients, pressure, or flow.
- **Boundary Ripples** — they reveal hidden edges or thresholds.
- **Phase Shifts** — they alter timing or rhythm in the local field.

Geometry determines how the phenomenon _moves_.

Topology

Topologically, Environmental Phenomena:

- **Expose hidden surfaces** (paths, layers, or chambers).
- **Create temporary homeomorphisms** (bridges between states).
- **Open or close navigable spaces**.
- **Alter continuity** (smooth → jagged, stable → unstable).
- **Mark critical points** (Forks, Drift Clarity, Collapse edges).

Topology determines what the phenomenon _connects_.

Emotional Physics

Environmental Phenomena express the **emotional stance of the world**, not the actor.

They respond to:

- Drift
- Distortion
- Collapse pressure
- Recovery signals
- Threshold proximity
- Role presence (especially Gate‑Openers and Guides)

Each phenomenon is a **field‑emotion made visible**.

Behavioral Rules

Environmental Phenomena obey strict behavioral constraints:

- **No agency** — they do not choose; they arise.
- **No repetition** — the same phenomenon never appears twice in the same form.
- **No communication** — they do not speak; they only behave.
- **No permanence** — they vanish once their function is complete.
- **No ownership** — no actor “causes” them; they are emergent.

They are **signals of the environment**, not tools of the actor.
