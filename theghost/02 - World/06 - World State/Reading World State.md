# Reading World State
*How to interpret the live environmental condition of Threshold*

World State files are snapshots of the world’s current condition. They are not rules, not mechanics, and not predictions. They are the **diagnostic readout** of the symbolic, emotional, memory, and scene substrates at the moment they were captured.

Reading World State means interpreting these snapshots as signals of the world’s health, turbulence, and seasonal alignment.

---

## What World State Represents
World State reflects:
- drift pressure and direction
- density levels and gradients
- emotional substrate tone
- memory substrate activity
- seasonal resonance
- symbolic weather patterns
- astral alignment and high‑layer vectors

These signals tell you how the world is behaving right now.

---

### Stars.md

### soup.png

---

## How to Interpret World State Changes
Changes in world state usually indicate:
- a seasonal shift  
- a prototype activating or dissolving  
- a collapse or recovery cycle completing  
- a ritual of significant scale  
- a drift/density threshold being crossed  
- emotional substrate turbulence  
- symbolic weather forming or dissipating  

World State is always ecological, never arbitrary.

---

## How Other Systems Use World State
- **Rituals** read world state to determine safety and volatility.  
- **Artifacts** adjust resonance, dormancy, or charge behavior.  
- **Prototypes** express seasonal or mythic cycles through it.  
- **Scene Substrate** uses it to shape environmental resonance.  
- **Symbolic Substrate** uses it to orient drift and density.  

World State is the shared reference point for all systems.

---

## How to Read World State as a Player or Architect
When reading world state:
- look for **patterns**, not details  
- compare current state to previous snapshots  
- treat each file as a **signal**, not a command  
- read across substrates (astral, seasonal, regional, symbolic)  
- interpret changes as **ecological shifts**  

World State is the world speaking in its own language.
