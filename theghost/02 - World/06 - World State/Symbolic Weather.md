# Symbolic Weather
*Patterns of drift, density, turbulence, and substrate interaction*

Symbolic weather describes the dynamic patterns formed when drift, density, emotional substrate, and scene substrate interact. These patterns behave like atmospheric weather but operate on symbolic physics: vectors, gradients, resonance, turbulence, and substrate overlap.

Symbolic weather is one of the primary indicators of world state. It reveals how stable, volatile, or transitional the environment is at any moment.

---

## Core Weather Types

### Eddies
Small, circular drift patterns.
- indicate low‑level turbulence  
- form around density pockets  
- harmless unless they accumulate  
- often appear before larger events  

Eddies are the “micro‑weather” of the symbolic substrate.

---

### Fronts
Linear boundaries between two symbolic conditions.
- density shift on one side  
- drift orientation shift on the other  
- emotional substrate often changes tone  
- can trigger traversal difficulty  

Fronts are the symbolic equivalent of pressure boundaries.

---

### Vortices
Rotational drift structures with strong inward pull.
- form when drift vectors collapse toward a center  
- can destabilize artifacts  
- distort resonance signatures  
- often precede collapse or inversion  

Vortices are high‑risk symbolic weather.

---

### Storms
Large‑scale turbulence across multiple substrates.
- drift vectors multiply and collide  
- density becomes uneven or chaotic  
- emotional substrate becomes volatile  
- memory substrate may fragment  

Storms are the most dangerous symbolic weather state.

---

### Fog
Low‑visibility symbolic conditions caused by substrate overlap.
- drift becomes diffuse  
- density loses orientation  
- emotional substrate becomes muted  
- scene substrate becomes soft or ambiguous  

Fog indicates transitional or unstable world state.

---

### Soup
Mixed, unresolved substrate.
- drift, density, emotional, and scene substrates overlap  
- no clear orientation  
- turbulence without direction  
- often appears pre‑collapse or post‑collapse  

Soup is the “undifferentiated” symbolic state.

---

## Transitional Weather States

### Spiral-In
Density and drift collapse inward.
- often follows storms  
- can lead to vortices  
- memory substrate becomes active  
- emotional substrate stabilizes  

A sign of Autumn‑like behavior.

---

### Spiral-Out
Density expands outward.
- often follows stillness or Winter  
- drift re‑ignites in outward spirals  
- emotional substrate becomes porous  
- memory substrate begins sorting  

A sign of Spring‑like behavior.

---

### Stillness Pockets
Localized zones of near‑zero drift.
- density compresses  
- emotional substrate quiets  
- memory substrate intertwines  
- often caused by Winter Crown influence  

Stillness pockets are safe but fragile.

---

## Reading Symbolic Weather
To interpret symbolic weather:
- look for drift direction and intensity  
- observe density gradients  
- note emotional substrate tone  
- check for substrate overlap  
- compare to seasonal resonance  
- identify whether patterns are stable or transitional  

Symbolic weather is the world’s emotional and structural language.

---

## Interaction With Other Systems
Symbolic weather affects:
- **Rituals** — stability, volatility, activation thresholds  
- **Artifacts** — resonance, dormancy, charge behavior  
- **Prototypes** — seasonal and mythic expression  
- **Traversal** — difficulty and orientation  
- **Scene Substrate** — environmental resonance  

It is both a symptom and a driver of world state.
