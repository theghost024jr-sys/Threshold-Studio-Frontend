# Worldstate Pressure Map
*How pressure forms, releases, and propagates across astral, symbolic, emotional, memory, and scene layers*

Pressure is the force created when drift and density interact. It determines how intense, volatile, or stable the world feels. Pressure is not a single value—it is a multi‑layer phenomenon that expresses differently in the astral layer, symbolic substrate, emotional substrate, memory substrate, and scene substrate.

Pressure is the world’s internal tension.

---

## Pressure Across Layers

### Astral Layer
Pressure appears as:
- brightness concentration  
- constellation compression  
- high‑altitude resonance thickness  
- astral fronts and pressure zones  

Astral pressure is the earliest indicator of world tension.

---

### Symbolic Substrate
Pressure expresses as:
- symbolic mass buildup  
- sharp gradients  
- storm formation  
- drift collisions  

Symbolic pressure is the engine of symbolic weather.

---

### Emotional Substrate
Pressure expresses as:
- emotional amplitude  
- volatility thresholds  
- resonance sharpness  
- sudden spikes or drops  

Emotional pressure determines how reactive the world feels.

---

### Memory Substrate
Pressure expresses as:
- recursion depth  
- lineage weight  
- thread tension  
- activation strain  

Memory pressure determines how much the past is “pulling” on the present.

---

### Scene Substrate
Pressure expresses as:
- spatial rigidity  
- threshold tension  
- environmental compression  
- physical resonance  

Scene pressure determines how heavy or tight the world feels physically.

---

## Pressure States

### Low Pressure
- soft resonance  
- gentle drift  
- porous emotional substrate  
- shallow memory recursion  
- flexible scene thresholds  

Low pressure corresponds to early Spring or late Autumn.

---

### Rising Pressure
- density increasing  
- drift accelerating  
- emotional amplitude rising  
- memory threads tightening  
- scene thresholds stiffening  

Rising pressure is the pre‑Summer buildup.

---

### High Pressure
- peak density  
- volatile emotional substrate  
- sharp symbolic gradients  
- deep memory recursion  
- rigid scene structure  

High pressure is the Summer Expansion state.

---

### Falling Pressure
- density dispersing  
- drift collapsing inward  
- emotional substrate stabilizing  
- memory substrate activating  
- scene thresholds cooling  

Falling pressure is the Autumn Shedding state.

---

## Pressure Failure Modes

### Overpressure
Pressure exceeds tolerance.
- emotional spikes  
- symbolic storms  
- memory overload  
- spatial distortion  

This is the Summer failure mode.

---

### Pressure Collapse
Pressure drops too quickly.
- drift becomes chaotic  
- density voids form  
- emotional substrate destabilizes  
- memory threads tear  

Collapse often follows inversion.

---

### Pressure Shear
Pressure differs sharply across layers.
- astral vs symbolic mismatch  
- emotional vs memory mismatch  
- scene substrate lagging  

Shear produces fronts, fog, and turbulence.

---

### Pressure Inversion
Pressure flips direction abruptly.
- symbolic weather reverses  
- emotional amplitude snaps  
- memory recursion breaks  
- scene thresholds warp  

Inversion is rare but catastrophic.

---

## Pressure Recovery Modes

### Equalization
Pressure redistributes evenly.
- gradients soften  
- storms dissipate  
- emotional tone settles  
- memory recursion unwinds  

Equalization is the most common recovery.

---

### Compression Recovery
Pressure collapses gently inward.
- emotional substrate quiets  
- memory intertwines  
- scene stabilizes  

This is the Winter‑like recovery.

---

### Expansion Recovery
Pressure expands gently outward.
- emotional substrate warms  
- memory sorts  
- scene softens  

This is the Spring‑like recovery.

---

### Clarification
Pressure separates cleanly from drift.
- fog clears  
- soup resolves  
- symbolic weather becomes readable  

Clarification is the world regaining coherence.

---

## Cross-Layer Pressure Mapping

### Astral → Symbolic
Astral pressure sets symbolic tension.

### Symbolic → Emotional
Symbolic pressure determines emotional amplitude.

### Emotional → Memory
Emotional pressure influences recursion depth.

### Memory → Scene
Memory pressure shapes spatial rigidity.

### Scene → Astral (feedback)
Scene pressure anchors astral fronts.

Pressure is a loop, not a one-way cascade.

---

## Seasonal Pressure Patterns

### Winter
- low pressure  
- compressed but stable  
- quiet emotional substrate  

### Spring
- rising pressure  
- expanding density  
- porous emotional tone  

### Summer
- high pressure  
- saturated density  
- volatile emotional substrate  

### Autumn
- falling pressure  
- dispersing density  
- stabilizing emotional tone  

Seasonal pressure defines the world’s tension cycle.

---

## Reading the Pressure Map
To interpret pressure across layers:
- observe astral brightness and clustering  
- check symbolic gradients  
- read emotional amplitude  
- examine memory recursion depth  
- look for scene rigidity or softness  
- compare to seasonal resonance  

Pressure is the world’s internal tension made visible.
