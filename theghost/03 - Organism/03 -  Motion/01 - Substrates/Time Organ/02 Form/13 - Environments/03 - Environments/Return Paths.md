How an environment restores itself after distortion, collapse, or pressure imbalance.

Purpose
Return Paths describe the recovery mechanics of an environment.
They define how a location in Threshold:
• 	re-stabilizes its pressure field
• 	clears accumulated distortion
• 	restores drift patterns
• 	re-aligns its internal geometry
• 	re-establishes its native signals
• 	returns to its canonical environmental state
These paths ensure that environments do not remain permanently warped by collapse events, role interference, or external pressure.

The Three Environmental Return Paths
1. Pressure Return
The environment restores its baseline pressure profile.
• 	Dissipates excess pressure
• 	Rebalances gradients
• 	Re-establishes stable zones
• 	Cancels runaway compression or expansion
Used when:
Environmental pressure has spiked, collapsed, or inverted.
Mechanics:
• 	Pressure bleed-off
• 	Gradient smoothing
• 	Boundary reformation
• 	Stabilization pulses

2. Drift Return
The environment restores its native drift behavior.
• 	Cancels chaotic drift
• 	Re-aligns flow vectors
• 	Re-stabilizes movement patterns
• 	Reconnects drift to environmental geometry
Used when:
Distortion fields, collapse drift, or role interference have disrupted flow.
Mechanics:
• 	Drift harmonization
• 	Vector re-alignment
• 	Flow re-binding
• 	Distortion cancellation

3. Signal Return
The environment restores its signal field.
• 	Clears noise
• 	Re-establishes native signals
• 	Restores environmental communication
• 	Reconnects species, roles, and artifacts to the environment
Used when:
Signal inversion, overload, or collapse has occurred.
Mechanics:
• 	Harmonic reset
• 	Signal purification
• 	Pattern re-stabilization
• 	Lineage resonance reactivation

Environmental Return Path Progression
Environments recover in a predictable order:
1. 	Pressure Return
2. 	Drift Return
3. 	Signal Return
This mirrors environmental physics:
• 	Drift cannot stabilize on unstable pressure.
• 	Signals cannot stabilize on chaotic drift.
