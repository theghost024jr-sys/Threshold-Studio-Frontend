Air — The Hesitation Veil
Geometric Form
The Air phenomenon manifests as a thin, translucent veil of motion — a shimmer, a pause, a suspended ripple in the atmosphere. It is not wind, not breeze, not weather. It is attention made visible.
Its geometry is defined by:
• 	Micro‑currents that move around the actor rather than past them
• 	Localized stillness that forms a pocket of suspended air
• 	Directional hesitation — the field leans toward a direction but does not commit
• 	Phase flicker — a subtle rhythmic pulse that syncs with the actor’s internal timing
The shape is a hovering pause, a moment of held breath.

Topological Behavior
Topologically, the Hesitation Veil creates a temporary soft boundary between:
• 	the actor’s current trajectory
and
• 	the trajectory the environment prefers
This boundary is:
• 	permeable — the actor can pass through
• 	biased — the field subtly encourages one direction
• 	non‑binding — the actor retains full agency
• 	informational — the topology encodes a suggestion, not a command
It is the world’s way of saying: “Wait. Look again.”

Emotional Physics
The Air phenomenon expresses:
• 	attention
• 	curiosity
• 	uncertainty
• 	invitation
• 	subtle warning
It appears when the environment wants the actor to notice something — a threshold, a misalignment, a hidden path, or a danger that is not yet visible.

Behavioral Signature
The Hesitation Veil behaves with extreme restraint:
• 	It never pushes.
• 	It never pulls.
• 	It never forces.
• 	It only pauses the field long enough for clarity to arise.
It is the gentlest of all phenomena — the world’s softest intervention.

Collapse Interaction
Under collapse pressure, the Veil becomes:
• 	jagged
• 	turbulent
• 	flickering
• 	unstable
It loses its subtlety and becomes a warning stutter in the field.

Recovery Interaction
During recovery, the Veil becomes:
• 	smooth
• 	rhythmic
• 	directional
It often appears immediately before Drift Clarity.
