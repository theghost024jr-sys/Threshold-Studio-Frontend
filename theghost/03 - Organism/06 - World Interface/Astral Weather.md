# Astral Weather
*High‑layer drift, constellation behavior, and resonance patterns in the astral field*

Astral weather describes the dynamic patterns that form in the astral layer: constellation drift, resonance tone shifts, vector fields, distortions, and high‑altitude turbulence. These patterns operate above the four substrates and often appear before substrate‑level changes.

Astral weather is the world’s long‑range forecast.

---

## Core Astral Weather Types

### Constellation Drift
Constellations shift position or orientation.
- indicates long‑range drift changes  
- reveals prototype influence  
- shows early signs of seasonal transition  
- can predict symbolic weather days in advance  

Drift is the astral layer’s “wind.”

---

### Astral Fronts
Linear boundaries between astral resonance zones.
- brightness changes across the boundary  
- drift vectors shift direction  
- constellation density changes  
- often precede substrate‑level fronts  

Astral fronts are high‑altitude pressure lines.

---

### High‑Layer Vortices
Rotational astral fields with strong inward pull.
- distort constellation shapes  
- pull drift vectors into rotation  
- dim or brighten stars unevenly  
- often precede vortex chains below  

Astral vortices are dangerous and destabilizing.

---

### Astral Storms
Large‑scale turbulence across the astral field.
- constellations flicker or distort  
- drift vectors collide  
- brightness becomes uneven  
- resonance tone becomes sharp or chaotic  

Astral storms often trigger symbolic storms below.

---

### Astral Fog
Low‑clarity astral conditions.
- constellations blur  
- drift vectors lose definition  
- brightness becomes muted  
- resonance tone becomes soft or unreadable  

Astral fog is a sign of transition or instability.

---

### Astral Soup
Mixed, unresolved astral conditions.
- constellations dissolve into noise  
- drift vectors scatter  
- brightness becomes chaotic  
- resonance tone becomes incoherent  

Astral soup is the high‑layer equivalent of substrate soup.

---

## Transitional Astral States

### Spiral-Out Field
Constellations drift outward in gentle spirals.
- indicates Spring‑like emergence  
- drift re‑ignites  
- brightness rises  
- resonance becomes porous  

This is the astral signature of early Spring.

---

### Spiral-In Field
Constellations collapse inward.
- indicates Autumn‑like dispersal  
- drift slows and folds  
- brightness warms  
- resonance softens  

This is the astral signature of early Autumn.

---

### Stillness Field
Constellations tighten and dim.
- drift approaches zero  
- brightness lowers  
- resonance becomes inward and curved  
- astral motion slows  

This is the astral signature of Winter Crown influence.

---

## Prototype Influence on Astral Weather

### Winter Crown
- tightens constellations  
- lowers brightness  
- collapses drift  
- stabilizes astral fronts  

### First Seed
- opens constellations  
- increases brightness  
- re‑ignites drift  
- creates spiral-out fields  

### Expansion Prototype (Summer)
- flares constellations  
- peaks brightness  
- accelerates drift  
- increases astral storms  

### Shedding Prototype (Autumn)
- softens constellations  
- warms brightness  
- collapses drift inward  
- unwinds astral vortices  

Prototypes shape the astral layer before they shape the substrates.

---

## Astral Failure Modes

### Constellation Break
Patterns fracture or dissolve.

### Vector Scatter
Drift vectors lose orientation.

### Astral Flicker
Brightness becomes unstable.

### Rotational Field
Astral layer begins spinning.

These failures appear in the astral layer before substrate‑level collapse.

---

## Astral Recovery Modes

### Reorientation
Drift vectors realign.

### Reformation
Constellations reassemble.

### Brightness Stabilization
Astral tone returns to baseline.

### Motion Smoothing
Astral movement becomes coherent.

Recovery begins at altitude.

---

## Reading Astral Weather
To interpret astral weather:
- observe constellation shape and motion  
- check brightness tone  
- read drift vector direction  
- watch for distortions or gaps  
- compare to seasonal resonance  
- identify prototype influence  

Astral weather is the earliest indicator of worldstate change.
