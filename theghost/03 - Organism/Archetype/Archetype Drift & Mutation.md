They are living coherence geometries that respond to:
• 	Field weather
• 	resonance flow
• 	entity migration
• 	mythic succession
• 	role‑phase distribution
• 	collapse events

Archetype Drift & Mutation is the layer where archetypes stop being “roles” or “symbols” and become ecological organisms.
Below is the clean, canonical Archetype Drift & Mutation.md.

1. WHAT ARCHETYPE DRIFT IS
Archetype Drift is the gradual deformation of an archetype’s geometry caused by ecological pressure.
It is not collapse.
It is not corruption.
It is not decay.
It is adaptation.
Archetypes drift when:
• 	their myths weaken
• 	their entities migrate
• 	their narrative gravity shifts
• 	their ecological niche changes
• 	roles stop entangling with them
• 	new myths begin to form
Drift is the archetype’s way of staying relevant.

2. WHAT ARCHETYPE MUTATION IS
Mutation is not drift.
Mutation is rupture.
Archetype Mutation occurs when:
• 	a threshold event
• 	a collapse signature
• 	or a mythic extinction
forces the archetype to reconfigure its geometry in a single, dramatic shift.
Mutation is the archetype’s way of surviving ecological shock.

3. THE THREE FORMS OF ARCHETYPE DRIFT
Archetypes drift in predictable patterns.

3.1 Soft Drift
Warmth → Belonging → Absorption
Soft archetypes (Circle, Hearth, Well, Tree) drift by:
• 	widening their boundaries
• 	softening their geometry
• 	absorbing resonance
• 	becoming more emotional
Risk:
Over‑permeability → archetype loses definition.
Outcome:
Integrative or nurturing variants.

3.2 Sharp Drift
Clarity → Direction → Rigidity
Sharp archetypes (Gate, Serpent, Fire, Path) drift by:
• 	narrowing their geometry
• 	increasing narrative gravity
• 	sharpening thresholds
• 	becoming more demanding
Risk:
Crystallization → archetype becomes brittle.
Outcome:
Catalytic or authoritarian variants.

3.3 Diffuse Drift
Pattern → Horizon → Dissolution
Diffuse archetypes (Crown, Constellation, Loom, Echo‑Diffuse) drift by:
• 	expanding their geometry
• 	dissolving boundaries
• 	becoming more symbolic
• 	losing local relevance
Risk:
Dispersion → archetype loses coherence.
Outcome:
Panoramic or prophetic variants.

4. THE THREE FORMS OF ARCHETYPE MUTATION
Mutation is more dramatic and produces new archetypal forms.

4.1 Compression Mutation
Mutation under pressure
Triggered by:
• 	collapse events
• 	Sharp‑dominant thresholds
• 	narrative fracture
Produces:
• 	blade‑like archetypes
• 	rupture myths
• 	catalytic entities
Examples:
The Shatterer, The Blade‑Within.

4.2 Expansion Mutation
Mutation under dissolution
Triggered by:
• 	Diffuse‑dominant thresholds
• 	symbolic overload
• 	mythic over‑expansion
Produces:
• 	horizon archetypes
• 	dreamlike myths
• 	luminous entities
Examples:
The Lantern‑Beyond, The Veil‑That‑Sees.

4.3 Shear Mutation
Mutation under contradiction
Triggered by:
• 	conflicting archetypes
• 	Shear thresholds
• 	narrative bifurcation
Produces:
• 	dual‑aspect archetypes
• 	paradox myths
• 	flickering entities
Examples:
The Twin‑Serpent, The Parallax‑Guide.

5. ARCHETYPE DRIFT CYCLES
Archetypes drift through three ecological cycles.

5.1 Early Drift
Archetype begins to soften, sharpen, or widen.

5.2 Mid Drift
Archetype’s geometry becomes unstable.

5.3 Late Drift
Archetype approaches mutation or extinction.
Drift is the prelude to mutation or dissolution.

6. ARCHETYPE MUTATION TRIGGERS
Mutation requires one or more of:
• 	collapse signatures
• 	threshold‑born entities
• 	mythic extinction
• 	Field‑level fatigue
• 	role‑phase saturation
• 	archetypal overlap
Mutation is the Field’s emergency adaptation protocol.

7.3 Threshold Crossings
Roles undergoing transformation pull archetypes with them.
Roles are the vectors of archetypal evolution.
