3. HOW THE CYCLE SHAPES ENVIRONMENTS
A. Coherence → Gathering Environments
Environments curve inward and stabilize.
Spatial behavior:
• 	radial paths
• 	clustering structures
• 	stable microclimates
Material behavior:
• 	low strain
• 	high cohesion
• 	predictable load distribution
Ecological behavior:
• 	flora densify
• 	fauna cluster
• 	drift gathers
Environment is aligned.

B. Tension → Compressed Environments
Environments narrow and brace.
Spatial behavior:
• 	corridors tighten
• 	verticality increases
• 	pressure zones form
Material behavior:
• 	rigidity rises
• 	micro‑fractures appear
• 	load concentrates
Ecological behavior:
• 	flora stiffen
• 	fauna brace
• 	drift compresses
Environment is over‑defined.

C. Collapse → Fracturing Environments
Environments dissolve, scatter, and break open.
Spatial behavior:
• 	paths fracture
• 	debris sheds
• 	boundaries dissolve
Material behavior:
• 	cohesion drops
• 	structures soften
• 	load redistributes violently
Ecological behavior:
• 	flora shed
• 	fauna disperse
• 	drift scatters
Environment is unmade.

D. Void → Dormant Environments
Environments rest, settle, and quiet.
Spatial behavior:
• 	stillness
• 	downward settling
• 	minimal movement
Material behavior:
• 	low strain
• 	low activity
• 	residue absorption
Ecological behavior:
• 	flora dormant
• 	fauna resting
• 	drift still
Environment is empty but intact.

E. Renewal → Forming Environments
Environments re‑form, sprout, and re‑organize.
Spatial behavior:
• 	upward spirals
• 	early pathways
• 	warm pockets
Material behavior:
• 	soft formation
• 	early crystallization
• 	low load
Ecological behavior:
• 	flora sprout
• 	fauna explore
• 	drift flickers
Environment is becoming.

4. HOW ENVIRONMENTS SHAPE THE CYCLE
Environments do not merely respond —
they actively modulate the Cycle.

A. Environmental Density → Phase Duration
• 	High density prolongs Coherence and Void.
• 	Low density accelerates Tension and Renewal.
• 	Fragmented density intensifies Collapse.
Density is the mass of the environment.

B. Environmental Strain → Transition Timing
Strain predicts transitions.
• 	rising strain → Coherence → Tension
• 	rigid strain → Tension → Collapse
• 	structural exhaustion → Collapse → Void
• 	warming pockets → Void → Renewal
• 	clustering forms → Renewal → Coherence
Strain is the pressure gauge of the land.

C. Environmental Flexibility → Transition Violence
Flexibility determines how violently transitions occur.
• 	high flexibility → soft transitions
• 	low flexibility → sharp transitions
Flexibility is the shock absorber of the world.

D. Environmental Memory → Phase Expression
Environments retain memory.
Coherence memory:
• 	stable renewal
• 	long coherence cycles
Collapse memory:
• 	fragile renewal
• 	short coherence cycles
Void memory:
• 	slow renewal
• 	deep rest cycles
Environmental memory is the inertia of the land.

5. CYCLE–ENVIRONMENT FEEDBACK LOOPS
Cycle and environment reinforce each other.
Positive feedback (amplifying):
• 	stable environments → deeper Coherence
• 	rigid environments → deeper Tension
• 	fractured environments → deeper Collapse
Negative feedback (balancing):
• 	Collapse reduces environmental strain
• 	Void reduces collapse residue
• 	Renewal reduces void emptiness
Feedback loops determine the shape of environmental cycles.

6. ENVIRONMENTAL RESIDUE ACROSS THE CYCLE

Residue shapes the next cycle.
