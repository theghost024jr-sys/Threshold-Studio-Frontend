Fauna are the **mobile sensors** of the Cycle.

**2. THE FIVE FAUNAL STATES OF THE CYCLE**

Each phase produces a distinct behavioral ecology.

**A. Coherence Fauna (Circle)**

_Clustering, stabilizing, harmonizing_

**Behavioral patterns:**

- group formation
- synchronized movement
- cooperative foraging
- territorial stabilization

**Movement:**

- circular or radial patterns
- low‑variance gait
- predictable routes

**Drift interaction:**

- gather drift around bodies
- stabilize drift corridors

**Resonance interaction:**

- respond to harmonic resonance with calm or alignment

**Environmental role:**

- reinforce coherence pockets
- anchor ecosystems

**Transition signal:**

- clustering becomes rigid → Tension approaching.

**B. Tension Fauna (Gate)**

_Bracing, guarding, heightening_

**Behavioral patterns:**

- alertness
- defensive posturing
- reduced social tolerance
- resource guarding

**Movement:**

- vertical or linear routes
- stiff gait
- sudden freezes

**Drift interaction:**

- compress drift along movement paths
- create tension corridors

**Resonance interaction:**

- react strongly to sharp resonance
- heightened sensitivity

**Environmental role:**

- reveal strain points
- amplify pressure zones

**Transition signal:**

- repeated freezing or bracing → Collapse imminent.

**C. Collapse Fauna (Crown)**

_Scattering, shedding, fleeing, dissolving_

**Behavioral patterns:**

- dispersal
- frantic movement
- shedding fur, feathers, or forms
- abandoning territories

**Movement:**

- erratic
- outward bursts
- unpredictable trajectories

**Drift interaction:**

- scatter drift with each movement
- carry collapse residue

**Resonance interaction:**

- destabilized by diffuse resonance
- disorientation common

**Environmental role:**

- redistribute ecological load
- break stagnation

**Transition signal:**

- movement slows → Void forming.

**D. Void Fauna (Tree)**

_Resting, withdrawing, conserving_

**Behavioral patterns:**

- hibernation
- burrowing
- low‑energy states
- minimal social interaction

**Movement:**

- slow
- downward or inward
- minimal range

**Drift interaction:**

- drift settles around dens and burrows
- fauna become drift anchors

**Resonance interaction:**

- respond to muted resonance with stillness

**Environmental role:**

- stabilize collapse residue
- prepare ecosystems for renewal

**Transition signal:**

- subtle exploratory movements → Renewal awakening.

**E. Renewal Fauna (Lantern)**

_Exploring, initiating, re‑forming_

**Behavioral patterns:**

- early mating rituals
- exploratory foraging
- re‑establishing territories
- playful or curious behavior

**Movement:**

- upward or outward spirals
- soft, rising gait
- rapid micro‑movements

**Drift interaction:**

- attract spark drift
- create upward drift spirals

**Resonance interaction:**

- respond to flickering resonance with curiosity

**Environmental role:**

- re‑seed ecosystems
- initiate new patterns

**Transition signal:**

- exploratory behavior becomes structured → Coherence returning.

**3. FAUNAL TRANSITION BEHAVIORS**

Animals are the earliest and most reliable indicators of phase transitions.

**Coherence → Tension**

- group cohesion becomes rigid
- grooming decreases
- alertness rises

**Tension → Collapse**

- sudden dispersal
- frantic movement
- abandonment of nests or dens

**Collapse → Void**

- collapse behaviors cease
- animals hide or burrow
- metabolic slowdown

**Void → Renewal**

- early foraging
- tentative exploration
- first mating calls

**Renewal → Coherence**

- stable territories
- synchronized group behavior
- predictable movement routes

Fauna transitions are precise and diagnostic.
