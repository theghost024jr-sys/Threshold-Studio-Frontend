2. THE FIVE FLORAL STATES OF THE CYCLE
Each phase produces a distinct botanical behavior.

A. Coherence Flora (Circle)
Radial growth, clustering, stabilization
Growth patterns:
• 	radial symmetry
• 	inward‑curving stems
• 	dense clustering
• 	stable canopy formation
Drift interaction:
• 	gathers drift into root systems
• 	low turbulence around leaves
Resonance interaction:
• 	harmonic hum detectable in dense groves
Environmental role:
• 	stabilizes soil
• 	anchors drift
• 	creates coherence pockets
Transition signal:
• 	growth becomes rigid → Tension approaching.

B. Tension Flora (Gate)
Vertical growth, rigidity, strain
Growth patterns:
• 	tall, narrow stalks
• 	rigid stems
• 	upward pressure in branches
• 	reduced lateral spread
Drift interaction:
• 	compresses drift upward
• 	creates linear drift corridors
Resonance interaction:
• 	sharp, directional resonance along stems
Environmental role:
• 	channels pressure
• 	reveals strain in the environment
Transition signal:
• 	micro‑fractures in stems → Collapse imminent.

C. Collapse Flora (Crown)
Shedding, scattering, dissolution
Growth patterns:
• 	leaf drop in waves
• 	petal shedding
• 	stem softening
• 	root loosening
Drift interaction:
• 	releases stored drift
• 	scatters spores, seeds, and debris
Resonance interaction:
• 	diffuse, unstable resonance around branches
Environmental role:
• 	redistributes nutrients
• 	breaks stagnation
• 	opens canopy
Transition signal:
• 	shedding slows → Void forming.

D. Void Flora (Tree)
Dormancy, stillness, minimal metabolic activity
Growth patterns:
• 	halted growth
• 	closed buds
• 	low sap flow
• 	deep root settling
Drift interaction:
• 	drift sinks into soil around roots
• 	minimal movement
Resonance interaction:
• 	muted, low‑frequency hum
Environmental role:
• 	stabilizes collapse residue
• 	prepares ground for renewal
Transition signal:
• 	subtle warming at root tips → Renewal awakening.

E. Renewal Flora (Lantern)
Sprouting, ignition, early formation
Growth patterns:
• 	first shoots
• 	soft, upward spirals
• 	rapid micro‑growth
• 	shimmering leaf edges
Drift interaction:
• 	spark drift gathers around sprouts
• 	upward flicker visible in early growth
Resonance interaction:
• 	rising, flickering resonance in new leaves
Environmental role:
• 	re‑establishes structure
• 	initiates new ecological patterns
Transition signal:
• 	sprouts cluster → Coherence returning.

3. FLORAL TRANSITION BEHAVIORS
Plants are the most reliable indicators of phase transitions.
Coherence → Tension
• 	stems stiffen
• 	radial growth becomes vertical
• 	leaves narrow
Tension → Collapse
• 	stems crack
• 	leaves curl
• 	sudden shedding
Collapse → Void
• 	shedding stops
• 	branches still
• 	roots deepen
Void → Renewal
• 	buds warm
• 	micro‑sprouts appear
• 	leaf edges shimmer
Renewal → Coherence
• 	sprouts cluster
• 	radial patterns re‑form
• 	canopy stabilizes
Flora transitions are precise and diagnostic.
