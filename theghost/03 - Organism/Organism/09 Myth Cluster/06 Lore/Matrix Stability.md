Definition
Matrix Stability is the system’s ability to maintain structural coherence under force, distortion, drift, collapse, and transformation.
It is not stillness.
It is not rigidity.
It is the capacity to remain continuous while changing shape.
Stability is the Field’s resilience.

Purpose
To ensure that:
• 	the Matrix remains intact under extreme curvature
• 	identity threads remain traceable
• 	drift fields do not tear the world open
• 	collapse reorganizes rather than destroys
• 	zones shift without fragmenting
• 	force density can spike without breaking topology
• 	shimmer intensifies truth without annihilating structure
Matrix Stability is the system’s survival mechanism.

Stability Components
1. Topological Integrity
The Matrix cannot break.
Connectivity persists through all transformations.
2. Curvature Tolerance
The Matrix can bend without tearing.
Curvature has limits, but they are wide.
3. Force Distribution
Force spreads across the Matrix to prevent overload.
4. Drift Absorption
The Matrix can absorb unresolved force into cavities and wells.
5. Threshold Elasticity
Thresholds stretch, compress, and migrate without disappearing.
6. Role Flexibility
Roles can strain, collapse, and reform without breaking identity.
7. Zone Adaptability
Zones can invert, distort, or collapse while maintaining continuity.
