
[[Signal Types Index]]

The structural rules governing how species influence, respond to, and reshape each other within Threshold.

Purpose
Species Interaction defines the relational physics between species.
It explains how species:
• 	exchange signals
• 	alter each other’s drift
• 	influence role expression
• 	co‑shape environments
• 	trigger collapse or stabilization
• 	form lineage‑linked patterns
• 	generate emergent behaviors
This file is the interaction engine for the entire Species subsystem.

The Four Interaction Layers
Species interact across four structural layers:

1. Signal Interaction
Species exchange, distort, or amplify each other’s signals.
Mechanics:
• 	harmonic resonance
• 	interference
• 	inversion
• 	drift coupling
• 	boundary reflection
Outcomes:
• 	clarity
• 	distortion
• 	collapse drift
• 	lineage activation
Signal interaction is the fastest and most volatile layer.

2. Behavioral Interaction
Species influence each other’s movement, posture, and role expression.
Mechanics:
• 	role affinity
• 	pressure response
• 	drift synchronization
• 	threat or comfort signals
Outcomes:
• 	cooperation
• 	avoidance
• 	mimicry
• 	escalation
• 	stabilization
Behavioral interaction is the visible layer.

3. Environmental Interaction
Species co‑shape the environment and respond to each other’s environmental effects.
Mechanics:
• 	pressure redistribution
• 	drift alteration
• 	boundary thinning or strengthening
• 	collapse zone formation
• 	signal density shifts
Outcomes:
• 	environmental stabilization
• 	distortion fields
• 	collapse wells
• 	resonance pockets
Environmental interaction is the slowest but most structurally impactful layer.

4. Lineage Interaction
Species with shared or opposing lineage threads influence each other’s identity and memory.
Mechanics:
• 	resonance activation
• 	thread entanglement
• 	inheritance echo
• 	mythic interference
Outcomes:
• 	lineage strengthening
• 	lineage confusion
• 	identity drift
• 	ancestral activation
Lineage interaction is the deepest and most persistent layer.

Interaction Patterns
Species interactions follow predictable patterns:

1. Resonant Interaction
Species harmonize.
• 	signals align
• 	drift synchronizes
• 	pressure stabilizes
• 	lineage threads strengthen
Outcome:
Mutual stabilization.

2. Interference Interaction
Species partially conflict.
• 	signals cross
• 	drift vectors misalign
• 	pressure pockets form
Outcome:
Mild distortion or behavioral tension.

3. Dominance Interaction
One species overrides another’s signals or drift.
• 	pressure imbalance
• 	boundary compression
• 	role suppression
Outcome:
Behavioral collapse or avoidance.

4. Collapse Interaction
Species interactions trigger environmental or identity collapse.
• 	spiral drift
• 	echo fields
• 	boundary rupture
• 	signal inversion
Outcome:
Collapse wells or distortion zones.

Environmental Influence on Interaction
Environment determines:
• 	which species dominate
• 	how signals travel
• 	how drift behaves
• 	how pressure distributes
• 	which lineage threads activate
Examples:
• 	Mist amplifies distortion interactions
• 	Branch stabilizes resonance interactions
• 	Deep intensifies dominance interactions
• 	Shore oscillates between resonance and interference

Role Influence on Interaction
Species with strong role affinities interact differently:
• 	Seer‑aligned species amplify signal interactions
• 	Anchor‑aligned species stabilize environmental interactions
• 	Flame‑aligned species escalate behavioral interactions
• 	Bridge‑aligned species mediate or redirect interactions
Roles shape how species meet.

Interaction Failure Modes
Species interactions fail when:
• 	signal density exceeds capacity
• 	drift becomes unstable
• 	pressure spikes
• 	lineage threads conflict
• 	boundaries collapse
Failure produces:
• 	distortion fields
• 	collapse drift
• 	behavioral loops
• 	identity thinning
