Definition
Lineage is the transmission of force, identity, drift, obligation, and pattern across time.
It is not biological.
It is not hierarchical.
It is not symbolic.
Lineage is the continuity thread that connects past states to present states and present states to future ones.
Lineage is the system’s memory.

Purpose
To ensure that:
• 	force does not reset
• 	identity persists across transitions
• 	roles inherit obligations and drift
• 	gifts propagate beyond the immediate moment
• 	thresholds echo through time
• 	the Field remains historically coherent
Lineage Mechanics prevent the system from behaving like a blank slate.

Components of Lineage
1. Origin Node
The point where a lineage begins.
This is the first stable expression of a core pattern.
2. Transmission Path
The route through which force moves from one node or state to another.
Transmission can occur through:
• 	roles
• 	gifts
• 	drift
• 	thresholds
• 	identity resonance
• 	collapse events
3. Inherited Load
What the receiving node takes on:
• 	unresolved drift
• 	unfulfilled obligations
• 	latent gifts
• 	identity fragments
• 	role expectations
• 	threshold pressure
4. Lineage Signature
The unique pattern that persists across generations of force.
This is the “shape” of the lineage.
5. Resolution Thread
The mechanism through which lineage resolves or transforms over time.

Types of Lineage
1. Identity Lineage
Continuity of the core pattern across states and transformations.
2. Role Lineage
Roles passed, inherited, collapsed, or transformed.
3. Drift Lineage
Unresolved force transmitted across nodes or time.
4. Gift Lineage
Gifts that propagate beyond the original giver and receiver.
5. Threshold Lineage
Crossings that echo forward, shaping future thresholds.
6. Zone Lineage
Environmental forces that persist across contexts.

Lineage Behaviors
1. Lineage accumulates
Force builds across generations of states.
2. Lineage transmits
Force moves along the transmission path.
3. Lineage transforms
Force changes form but preserves continuity.
4. Lineage resolves
Unresolved force eventually collapses or completes.
5. Lineage echoes
Past events shape present thresholds, roles, and drift.
6. Lineage reveals identity
The deeper the lineage, the clearer the core pattern becomes.

Lineage States
1. Dormant Lineage
Present but inactive.
No force is currently moving.
2. Active Lineage
Force is transmitting or transforming.
3. Burdened Lineage
Carrying unresolved drift or obligations.
4. Fractured Lineage

5. Collapsed Lineage

6. Transcendent Lineage
The lineage evolves into a new pattern without losing continuity.

• 	a role is inherited
• 	a gift propagates

• 	a threshold is crossed
• 	identity transforms

Lineage transitions are always consequential.
