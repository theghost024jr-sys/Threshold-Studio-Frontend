The structural shape of an identity: its axes, curvature, boundaries, and deformation behaviors.

Purpose
Identity Geometry defines the spatial and structural form of an identity within Threshold.
It describes how identity:
• 	holds shape
• 	bends under pressure
• 	deforms during drift
• 	collapses under overload
• 	restores itself during reformation
• 	resonates when aligned
Identity Geometry is the physics of self‑shape.

The Four Axes of Identity Geometry
Identity is not a point — it is a four‑axis structure:

1. Boundary Axis
Defines the edge of the identity.
• 	permeability
• 	thickness
• 	stability
• 	responsiveness
Strong boundary: clear, stable, coherent
Weak boundary: porous, thin, collapse‑prone

2. Role Axis
Defines the functional orientation of the identity.
• 	direction of expression
• 	behavioral vector
• 	relational stance
• 	purpose alignment
The Role Axis determines how identity moves.

3. Signal Axis
Defines the harmonic signature of the identity.
• 	clarity
• 	resonance
• 	distortion
• 	lineage threads
The Signal Axis determines how identity communicates.

4. Lineage Axis
Defines the origin and inheritance of the identity.
• 	continuity
• 	memory threads
• 	mythic inheritance
• 	ancestral resonance
The Lineage Axis determines how identity remembers.

Identity Geometry Table


Curvature Types
Identity Geometry bends under pressure in predictable ways:

1. Linear Curvature
Identity bends evenly along one axis.
• 	predictable
• 	reversible
• 	low‑risk
Often seen in mild drift.

2. Radial Curvature
Identity bends outward or inward from its center.
• 	expansion
• 	contraction
• 	pressure imbalance
Often seen in environmental overwhelm.

3. Spiral Curvature
Identity twists around its axes.
• 	rotational distortion
• 	directional confusion
• 	inward collapse potential
Often seen in collapse drift or distortion fields.

4. Fractured Curvature
Identity bends along multiple conflicting axes.
• 	fragmentation
• 	contradictory signals
• 	unstable boundaries
Often seen in identity rupture.

Geometry States
Identity Geometry exists in three canonical states:

1. Coherent Geometry
All axes aligned, curvature minimal.
• 	stable boundaries
• 	clear role vector
• 	tuned signal
• 	active lineage threads
This is the geometry of Identity Resonance.

2. Distorted Geometry
One or more axes misaligned.
• 	drift
• 	inversion
• 	noise
• 	boundary thinning
This is the geometry of Identity Drift.

3. Collapsed Geometry
Axes lose structural integrity.
• 	fragmentation
• 	spiral collapse
• 	rupture
• 	dissociation
This is the geometry of Identity Collapse.

Geometry Transitions
Identity Geometry transitions through predictable phases:
1. 	Coherent → Distorted
Pressure exceeds capacity.
2. 	Distorted → Collapsed
Axes lose alignment.
3. 	Collapsed → Reformed
Identity Reformation rebuilds architecture.
4. 	Reformed → Resonant
Identity Resonance re‑establishes harmony.

Environmental Influence
Identity Geometry is shaped by:
• 	pressure fields
• 	drift vectors
• 	signal density
• 	distortion zones
• 	collapse wells
• 	lineage‑active environments
Identity does not exist in isolation — it is co‑shaped by place.
