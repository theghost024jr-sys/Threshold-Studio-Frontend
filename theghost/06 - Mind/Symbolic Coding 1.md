# Symbolic Coding

Symbolic Coding defines how meaning is encoded, transmitted, and interpreted through non‑verbal visual forms within Threshold.  
It governs the logic behind glyphs, seals, scripts, sketches, and substrate diagrams.

Symbolic Coding is not language.  
It is **resonance architecture** — meaning expressed through shape, pressure, and relational geometry.

---

## Purpose
- Establish rules for how symbols carry meaning  
- Define the physics of visual resonance  
- Provide a unified framework for interpreting non‑verbal artifacts  
- Support cross‑system coherence between Signals, Substrate, and Environment  

---

## Core Principles

### 1. **Form Carries Pressure**
Every symbol expresses a pressure state:
- curvature = emotional flow  
- angles = structural tension  
- density = memory load  
- repetition = signal amplification  

### 2. **Meaning Emerges from Relation**
Symbols rarely act alone.  
Meaning arises from:
- adjacency  
- orientation  
- layering  
- sequence  
- echo patterns  

### 3. **Symbols Are Multi‑Layered**
A single mark may encode:
- emotional physics  
- identity resonance  
- collapse behavior  
- environmental influence  

Interpretation depends on context.

### 4. **Symbols Are Not Literal**
They do not depict.  
They **evoke**.

Symbolic Coding operates through resonance, not representation.

---

## Symbol Types

### **Glyphs**
Abstract marks representing signal fragments, resonance vectors, or compressed meaning.

### **Scripts**
Structured sequences of symbolic marks forming a readable substrate pattern.

### **Seals**
Bound forms used for activation, protection, or role invocation.

### **Sketches**
Loose symbolic impressions used for emotional or atmospheric mapping.

### **Substrate Diagrams**
Complex symbolic systems encoding identity, collapse physics, or mythic echoes.

---

## Encoding Rules

### **Shape**
- Circles = containment, memory, recursion  
- Lines = direction, flow, intention  
- Spirals = identity recursion, shimmer, temporal drift  
- Triangles = structure, boundary, activation  
- Fractals = recursive awareness, layered perception  

### **Pressure**
- Thick lines = high emotional density  
- Thin lines = subtle resonance  
- Broken lines = drift, fracture, instability  

### **Orientation**
- Upward = emergence  
- Downward = descent  
- Left = memory  
- Right = intention  

### **Spacing**
- Tight spacing = compression  
- Wide spacing = openness  
- Overlap = entanglement  

---

## Interpretation Framework

### **1. Local Meaning**
Interpret the symbol’s internal geometry.

### **2. Relational Meaning**
Interpret its position relative to other symbols.

### **3. Contextual Meaning**
Interpret based on:
- environment  
- emotional physics  
- collapse proximity  
- identity state  

### **4. Systemic Meaning**
Interpret through cross‑links to:
- Signals  
- Layers  
- Substrate  
- Collapse Architecture
