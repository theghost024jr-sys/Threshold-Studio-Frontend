Purpose
The Shadow Role defines the inverted behavioral state that emerges when a role undergoes collapse.
It is not a separate role.
It is the negative geometry of the existing role — the shape revealed when:
• 	identity is exposed
• 	collapse pressure exceeds stability
• 	drift curvature overwhelms intention
• 	resonance fails
• 	signals distort
Shadow Roles are inevitable under sufficient pressure.
They are the world’s way of revealing the unacknowledged structure inside a being.

1. Core Principles
2. Shadow Roles are inversions, not opposites.
They reveal the hidden geometry of the role, not its contradiction.
3. Shadow Roles emerge only under collapse pressure.
No collapse → no Shadow Role.
4. Shadow Roles are structural, not moral.
They are not “evil.”
They are unfiltered behavior.
5. Shadow Roles are recursive.
A Shadow Role can itself collapse into a deeper shadow.
6. Shadow Roles propagate distorted signals.
Their presence alters the environment.
7. Shadow Roles complete the Shadow Narrative Arc.
They are the Reversal Phase expressed behaviorally.

8. How Shadow Roles Emerge
Shadow Roles appear when the following sequence occurs:
3. 	Identity Exposure
The being sees themselves structurally.
4. 	Role Activation
Identity shifts into a role state.
5. 	Collapse Pressure
The environment applies force beyond the role’s stability.
6. 	Behavioral Inversion
The role flips into its shadow geometry.
7. 	Signal Distortion
The new behavior propagates through the world.
This is the Role → Shadow Role phase shift.

8. Components of a Shadow Role
Each Shadow Role has:
A. Inverted Posture
The stance becomes reactive, defensive, or catalytic in unintended ways.
B. Distorted Interaction Style
Communication becomes misaligned, over‑amplified, or under‑filtered.
C. Collapse‑Driven Drift
Movement follows collapse vectors instead of drift curvature.
D. Boundary Leakage
Identity boundaries blur or spill into the environment.
E. Resonance Failure
The role cannot stabilize through normal signals.
F. Behavioral Loops
Repetitive patterns emerge as the role tries to self‑correct.
G. Shadow Expression
The purest form of the Shadow Role — the “core inversion.”
These components define the Shadow Role profile.

4. Shadow Role Geometry
Shadow Roles use:
• 	inversion arcs (behavior flipping back on itself)
• 	collapse vectors (movement toward instability)
• 	distortion halos (identity leakage)
• 	signal interference patterns (propagation of instability)
• 	loop spirals (repetitive behavior under pressure)
Shadow geometry must align with:
• 	Collapse Layer adjacency
• 	Identity Layer exposure
• 	Role Layer bias
• 	Signal Layer distortion
Shadow Roles are not free-floating — they are physically constrained.

5. Shadow Role Rules
6. 	A Shadow Role cannot emerge without collapse.
Collapse is the trigger.
7. 	A Shadow Role cannot stabilize without resonance.
It must find a compatible signal to return to a normal role state.
8. 	A Shadow Role cannot contradict its parent role.
It is an inversion, not a replacement.
9. 	A Shadow Role must propagate distorted signals.
This is how the world “feels” the inversion.
10. 	A Shadow Role must influence the Shadow Narrative Arc.
It is the behavioral expression of the Reversal Phase.
11. 	A Shadow Role can recurse.
Under extreme collapse, a Shadow Role can invert again.

12. Shadow Roles in Columns
Each Column contains:
• 	Identity node
• 	Role node
• 	Shadow Role (implicit)
• 	Signal node
The Shadow Role is not a separate node —
it is the inverted state of the Role node under collapse.
Example (Mist Corridor Column):
• 	Role: Trickster/Listener Bias Zone
• 	Shadow Role: Distortion Catalyst
(the being amplifies distortion instead of navigating it)
Shadow Roles give Columns their narrative teeth.
