Purpose
Shadow Roles represent the inverted, distorted, or collapsed expression of each archetypal role when:
• 	drift exceeds tolerance
• 	identity destabilizes
• 	signals distort
• 	collapse physics activate
A Shadow Role is not “evil” or “opposite.”
It is the role under pressure, expressing its unmet needs, distortions, and defensive logic.
Shadow Roles reveal:
• 	the truth of the drift
• 	the shape of collapse
• 	the need beneath the behavior
• 	the path back to integration
They are diagnostic, not moral.

1. Shadow Role Table

These are not new roles — they are shadow expressions of the six archetypes.

2. How Shadow Roles Form
Shadow Roles emerge when three pressures converge:
2.1 Identity Pressure
The self‑model destabilizes.
The role loses its internal anchor.
2.2 Signal Pressure
Signals become distorted, misread, or overwhelming.
2.3 Environmental Pressure
The role is misaligned with context or demand.
When these exceed tolerance, the role flips into its shadow expression.

3. Shadow Role Characteristics
3.1 Inversion
The role’s core function becomes distorted:
• 	Guide → absorbs instead of guides
• 	Caretaker → erases instead of nurtures
• 	Anchor → immobilizes instead of stabilizes
• 	Sentinel → accuses instead of protects
• 	Judge → punishes instead of clarifies
• 	Architect → traps instead of designs
3.2 Amplification
The role’s dominant trait becomes exaggerated.
3.3 Collapse Logic
Each shadow role follows a predictable collapse path.
3.4 Emotional Logic
Shadow roles operate from:
• 	fear
• 	scarcity
• 	distortion
• 	unmet need

4. Shadow Role Signals
Soft Signals (early shadow drift)
• 	tone flattening
• 	emotional fog
• 	hesitation
• 	boundary blur
Sharp Signals (active shadow expression)
• 	pattern escalation
• 	relational distortion
• 	over‑functioning or withdrawal
• 	rigidity or hyper‑vigilance
Hard Signals (shadow collapse)
• 	shutdown
• 	lock
• 	dissociation
• 	identity fracture
See: [[Collapse Signals]]

5. Shadow Role Narratives
Each shadow role has a narrative pattern:
Guide → Absorber
“I must hold everything or I will be abandoned.”
Caretaker → Eraser
“If I disappear, no one can reject me.”
Anchor → Weight
“If I move, everything will fall apart.”
Sentinel → Accuser
“If I don’t escalate, I will be betrayed.”
Judge → Tyrant
“If I don’t enforce the pattern, chaos wins.”
Architect → Labyrinth
“If I keep building, I won’t have to feel.”
These narratives are identity distortions, not truths.

6. Integration Pathways
Shadow roles are not removed — they are integrated.
Integration requires:
6.1 Recognition
Naming the shadow expression.
6.2 Signal Interpretation
Reading the drift and collapse signals accurately.
6.3 Role Realignment
Returning the role to its core function.
6.4 Identity Stabilization
Rebuilding the self‑model.
6.5 Environmental Adjustment
Reducing mismatch or overload.
See: [[Recovery Flow]]
