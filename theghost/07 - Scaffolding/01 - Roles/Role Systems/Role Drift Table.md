Purpose
This table defines the three primary drift modes for every Role in Threshold:
• 	Primary Drift — the predictable, directional deformation of a Role under sustained pressure.
• 	Shadow Drift — the inversion pattern that emerges when a Role suppresses or denies its core function.
• 	Collapse Drift — the failure mode where the Role loses structural integrity and falls into a behavioral loop.

Drift Types (Definitions)
Primary Drift
Slow, directional deformation that maintains the Role’s silhouette but warps its expression.
• 	Predictable
• 	Mapped by pressure vectors
• 	Reversible with correct signals
Shadow Drift
The Role flips into its inverse logic.
• 	Emergent under repression
• 	Often unconscious
• 	Produces contradictory signals
Collapse Drift
The Role loses coherence and falls into a loop.
• 	Triggered by overload, fragmentation, or identity rupture
• 	Non‑functional state
• 	Requires external stabilization

Role Drift Table


Drift Progression Model
1. Pressure → Primary Drift
A Role under sustained pressure begins deforming along its natural axis.
2. Suppression → Shadow Drift
If the Role denies its own function, the inversion emerges.
3. Overload → Collapse Drift
When identity, signal, or environmental load exceeds capacity, the Role collapses into a loop.
