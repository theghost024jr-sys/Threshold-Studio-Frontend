Purpose
Role Dynamics defines how roles behave in motion — how they interact, influence each other, drift under pressure, and stabilize or destabilize identity.
Where Role Physics defines what a role is,
Role Dynamics defines what a role does.
This subsystem governs:
• 	role interaction patterns
• 	role pressure
• 	role drift tendencies
• 	role compatibility and friction
• 	role switching behavior
• 	role collapse modes
• 	role resonance with signals
Every role inherits these dynamics and expresses them differently.

1. Role Interaction Modes
Roles interact through four primary modes:
1.1 Alignment
Two roles reinforce each other’s stance.
Effects:
• 	identity stabilizes
• 	drift slows
• 	collapse risk decreases
Examples:
• 	Guardian + Anchor
• 	Weaver + Listener

1.2 Tension
Roles pull in different directions.
Effects:
• 	drift accelerates
• 	identity stretches
• 	collapse risk increases
Examples:
• 	Hunter + Scholar
• 	Anchor + Trickster

1.3 Overlap
Roles share functional territory.
Effects:
• 	identity blends
• 	drift becomes ambiguous
• 	collapse becomes brittle
Examples:
• 	Weaver + Scholar
• 	Guardian + Hunter

1.4 Opposition
Roles directly contradict each other.
Effects:
• 	identity destabilizes
• 	drift becomes turbulent
• 	collapse becomes likely
Examples:
• 	Trickster + Anchor
• 	Hunter + Listener

2. Role Pressure Types
Each role exerts a specific kind of pressure on identity and behavior:
2.1 Directive Pressure
Pushes action, decision, or movement.
Roles:
• 	Hunter
• 	Guardian
• 	Trickster

2.2 Interpretive Pressure
Pushes meaning, pattern, or understanding.
Roles:
• 	Scholar
• 	Weaver
• 	Listener

2.3 Stabilizing Pressure
Pushes grounding, continuity, or containment.
Roles:
• 	Anchor
• 	Guardian
• 	Weaver

2.4 Disruptive Pressure
Pushes change, inversion, or drift.
Roles:
• 	Trickster
• 	Hunter
• 	Listener (in specific contexts)

3. Role Drift Behaviors
Role Dynamics defines how roles drift under pressure:
3.1 Primary Drift
The direction a role naturally moves when unpressured.
Examples:
• 	Hunter → Guardian
• 	Scholar → Weaver
• 	Trickster → Listener

3.2 Shadow Drift
The direction a role moves under stress or misalignment.
Examples:
• 	Guardian → Anchor
• 	Weaver → Scholar
• 	Listener → Trickster

3.3 Collapse Drift
The direction a role falls when identity destabilizes.
Examples:
• 	Anchor → Guardian (rigidity collapse)
• 	Trickster → Hunter (chaotic collapse)
• 	Scholar → Listener (overwhelm collapse)

4. Role Switching
Role switching is governed by:
4.1 Pressure Threshold
Switching occurs when role pressure exceeds identity tolerance.
4.2 Drift Momentum
Roles with high drift momentum switch faster.
4.3 Signal Influence
Signals can trigger role switching:
• 	Stillwatch → Anchor
• 	Winter Crown → Guardian
• 	Spiral Echo → Trickster
• 	Ember Pulse → Hunter
4.4 Environmental Influence
Environments bias switching:
• 	dense forests → Weaver
• 	open plains → Hunter
• 	ruins → Scholar
• 	spirals → Trickster

5. Role Collapse Styles
Each role collapses differently:
• 	Hunter collapses into aggression or overreach
• 	Guardian collapses into rigidity
• 	Anchor collapses into immobility
• 	Scholar collapses into fragmentation
• 	Weaver collapses into overconnection
• 	Listener collapses into overwhelm
• 	Trickster collapses into chaos
Collapse style determines recovery difficulty.

6. Role Resonance
Roles resonate with:
6.1 Signals
Signals amplify or suppress role tendencies.
6.2 Species
Species archetypes align with specific roles.
6.3 Environments
Pressure fields bias role expression.
6.4 Other Roles
Alignment or tension modifies identity stability.
