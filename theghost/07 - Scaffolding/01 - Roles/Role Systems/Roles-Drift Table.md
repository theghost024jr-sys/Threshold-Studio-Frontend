Overview
Role Drift describes how a spirit’s archetypal function shifts under pressure, resonance, collapse, or prolonged misalignment.
Drift is slow, rare, and follows the geometry of the Archetypal Roles Wheel:
Sentinel → Weaver → Hunter → Oracle → Echo → Lantern → Sentinel
Primary drift follows the wheel.
Shadow drift crosses it.
Collapse drift moves backward or inward.

Primary Drift (Clockwise Along the Wheel)

Primary drift is the most stable and most predictable form of role change.

Shadow Drift (Cross‑Wheel Under Pressure)

Shadow drift is rare and indicates significant layer pressure or misalignment.

Collapse Drift (Backward Along the Wheel)

Collapse drift is involuntary and usually temporary.

Drift Stability Factors
Increase Drift Likelihood
• 	prolonged layer pressure
• 	unresolved signal distortion
• 	species‑role mismatch
• 	elemental dissonance
• 	shadow expression dominance
Decrease Drift Likelihood
• 	high resonance with species
• 	strong elemental alignment
• 	stable signal patterns
• 	supportive role adjacency
• 	coherent overlay tint
