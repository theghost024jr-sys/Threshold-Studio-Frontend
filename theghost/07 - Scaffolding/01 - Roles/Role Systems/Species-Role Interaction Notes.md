Purpose
Species–Role Interaction describes how species-level traits influence role expression, drift patterns, collapse tendencies, and behavioral loops.
It is the bridge between biological/instinctive architecture and archetypal behavioral architecture.
Species do not determine roles.
They bias, shape, and modulate them.

1. Interaction Principles
1.1 Species as Baseline Pattern
Each species provides a default behavioral substrate:
• 	sensory bias
• 	threat response
• 	relational orientation
• 	movement logic
• 	environmental preference
This substrate influences how a role is expressed but does not override the role’s core identity.

1.2 Role as Behavioral Frame
Roles provide:
• 	direction
• 	purpose
• 	relational stance
• 	decision logic
The role overlays the species substrate, producing a species‑specific expression of the role.

1.3 Interaction is Bidirectional
Species influences role expression.
Role influences species behavior.
This creates a feedback loop:
Species → Role Expression → Identity → Behavior → Species Reinforcement

2. Species Influence on Role Expression
2.1 Sensory Bias
Species determine what the role notices first.
Examples:
• 	Owl‑Guide notices patterns in silence
• 	Wolf‑Sentinel notices movement and pack dynamics
• 	Deer‑Caretaker notices emotional tone and tension
Sensory bias shapes the entry point of the role.

2.2 Threat Response
Species define the default collapse direction.
Examples:
• 	Predator species drift toward Sentinel → Judge
• 	Prey species drift toward Caretaker → Anchor
• 	Solitary species drift toward Architect → Guide
This is one of the strongest species‑role interactions.

2.3 Relational Orientation
Species determine how the role engages socially:
• 	Pack species amplify Guide, Caretaker, Sentinel
• 	Solitary species amplify Architect, Judge
• 	Fluid‑boundary species amplify Anchor, Guide
This affects role stability and drift speed.

2.4 Movement Logic
Species movement patterns influence:
• 	how the role transitions
• 	how drift manifests
• 	how collapse signals appear
Examples:
• 	A feline Architect collapses silently
• 	A canine Guide collapses loudly
• 	A bird Sentinel collapses through scanning overload

3. Role Influence on Species Expression
3.1 Role Directs Instinct
Roles channel instinct into purposeful behavior.
Examples:
• 	Wolf‑Guide becomes a pathfinder
• 	Owl‑Judge becomes a pattern‑arbiter
• 	Deer‑Anchor becomes a stabilizer
Role gives instinct a vector.

3.2 Role Modulates Species Tension
Roles can:
• 	soften
• 	sharpen
• 	redirect
• 	suppress
• 	amplify
species‑level impulses.
Example:
A hawk with a Caretaker role becomes a watchful guardian, not a predator.

3.3 Role Determines Collapse Interpretation
Species determines how collapse appears.
Role determines why collapse happens.
Together they produce unique collapse signatures.

4. Drift Interactions
4.1 Species‑Biased Drift
Each species has a preferred drift path.
Examples:
• 	Wolves drift toward Sentinel → Judge
• 	Deer drift toward Caretaker → Anchor
• 	Owls drift toward Architect → Guide
This interacts with the universal Role Drift Loop.

4.2 Species‑Role Drift Amplification
When species drift direction matches role drift direction, drift accelerates.
When they oppose, drift slows or becomes chaotic.

4.3 Species‑Role Collapse Patterns
Collapse signals differ by species:
• 	Predators: sharp, externalized
• 	Prey: soft, internalized
• 	Birds: scanning, dissociative
• 	Aquatics: boundary dissolution
Role determines the interpretation of these signals.
