Artifact Architecture

_Physics, rooting behavior, and functional agency of symbolic objects_

Artifacts are **rooted symbolic constructs** that have achieved enough coherence, density, and resonance stability to act as _objects with agency_ inside Threshold. They are not items, props, or lore objects. They are **symbolic organs** that store, conduct, amplify, or transform symbolic charge.

An artifact is a _stable node_ that has crossed the rooting threshold and gained functional presence.

Artifact Physics

Artifacts obey four governing forces:

- **Charge Capacity** — how much symbolic charge the artifact can hold without destabilizing.
- **Resonance Signature** — the artifact’s unique interaction pattern with substrates and rituals.
- **Rooting Stability** — the strength of its anchoring in symbolic density.
- **Drift Susceptibility** — how easily the artifact’s meaning deforms under turbulence.

These forces determine the artifact’s behavior, risk profile, and ecological role.

Rooting Behavior

Artifacts emerge when symbolic nodes undergo successful rooting. Rooting requires:

- **Density Support** — sufficient symbolic mass to sustain structure.
- **Vector Collapse** — drift vectors converge into a stable orientation.
- **Resonance Lock** — the artifact’s signature stabilizes into a repeatable pattern.
- **Interface Formation** — the artifact binds to at least one substrate or ritual surface.

Rooting is not permanent; artifacts can unroot under extreme drift or density collapse.

Artifact Classes

Artifacts fall into six functional classes:

- **Conductive Artifacts** — channel symbolic charge between nodes or substrates.
- **Resonant Artifacts** — amplify or modulate resonance signatures.
- **Anchoring Artifacts** — stabilize density, drift, or symbolic weather.
- **Transformative Artifacts** — alter charge orientation or symbolic state.
- **Memory Artifacts** — store lineage, recursion, or symbolic history.
- **Interface Artifacts** — connect rituals, roles, or substrates.

Classes define the artifact’s ecological function and failure modes.

Artifact Surfaces

Artifacts express themselves through **surfaces**—physical or symbolic skins that hold charge and resonance:

- **Glyph Surfaces** — directional or activation patterns.
- **Crown Surfaces** — presence-linked resonance membranes.
- **Lattice Surfaces** — density-distribution structures.
- **Ledger Surfaces** — memory-anchoring inscriptions.
- **Seed Surfaces** — generative or recursive patterns.

Surfaces determine how the artifact interacts with rituals and environments.

Substrate Interfaces

Artifacts interface with four substrates:

- **Symbolic Substrate** — provides charge, drift, density, and rooting conditions.
- **Emotional Substrate** — modulates volatility and resonance amplitude.
- **Memory Substrate** — anchors lineage, recursion, and artifact history.
- **Scene Substrate** — shapes spatial constraints and environmental turbulence.

Artifacts are **cross-substrate nodes** that stabilize or redirect symbolic flow.

Charge Dynamics

Artifacts manage symbolic charge through:

- **Accumulation** — gathering charge from density or rituals.
- **Conduction** — transferring charge to other nodes or artifacts.
- **Amplification** — increasing resonance or charge intensity.
- **Transformation** — altering charge orientation or symbolic meaning.
- **Release** — dispersing charge into environment or substrate.

Charge dynamics define the artifact’s operational cycle.

Artifact Behaviors

Artifacts exhibit predictable behaviors:

- **Activation** — triggered by presence, ritual, or substrate conditions.
- **Dormancy** — low-energy state with minimal resonance.
- **Overload** — density exceeds capacity, causing instability.
- **Inversion** — charge flips orientation under stress.
- **Echoing** — resonance reflects or repeats symbolic patterns.
- **Propagation** — artifact influences nearby nodes or surfaces.

Behavior depends on class, rooting stability, and environmental conditions.

Failure Modes

Artifacts fail in ecological ways:

- **Rooting Collapse** — artifact loses anchoring and dissolves.
- **Charge Overload** — density exceeds capacity, causing rupture or bleed.
- **Resonance Inversion** — signature flips, altering meaning.
- **Parasitic Binding** — unwanted symbolic attachments form.
- **Drift Corruption** — artifact meaning mutates uncontrollably.
- **Recursive Echo** — artifact loops its resonance indefinitely.

Failure modes propagate through symbolic substrate unless stabilized.

Purpose in the System

Artifacts exist to:

- stabilize symbolic environments
- store and conduct charge
- amplify resonance
- anchor rituals
- shape density and drift
- provide players with tangible interfaces to symbolic physics

Artifacts are the **object organs** of meaning.
