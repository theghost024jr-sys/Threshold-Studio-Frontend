How the system halts entangled collapse before it becomes catastrophic
Cascade Containment Protocols (CCPs) are the counter‑operations that stop a failure cascade mid‑flow. They don’t repair the system — that’s the job of recovery models — but they freeze the spread, stabilize the entanglements, and reopen the corridors needed for healing.
These protocols are the Binding Layer’s equivalent of firebreaks, pressure vents, and temporal dampers.
Below is the clean, canonical architecture.

1. PRINCIPLE OF CONTAINMENT
A cascade can only be stopped by interrupting one of three things:
2. 	Binding Law Enforcement
Reassert the relational rule so the entanglement stops propagating collapse.
3. 	Phase Realignment
Force the collapsing element back into the correct temporal phase.
4. 	Pressure Redistribution
Move weight, drift, or resonance away from the overloaded node.
Every containment protocol is built from these three primitives.

5. MIRROR CASCADE CONTAINMENT
Stopping paired-opposite collapse
Mirror cascades propagate as paired pathologies (e.g., Fracture Bloom ↔ Pressure Cascade).
Containment Strategy:
Break the mirror by decoupling the pair’s collapse states.
Protocol Steps:
3. 	Activate Containment Sink (Stone Basin or equivalent)
4. 	Dampen Fracture Resonance in the origin node
5. 	Reduce Pressure Gradient in the partner node
6. 	Reassert Binding Law (fracture_intensity ≤ containment_capacity)
Outcome:
The mirror breaks; collapse stops propagating.
Best Containment Window:
Immediately after the first partner collapses.

7. COUNTERWEIGHT INVERSION CONTAINMENT
Stopping polarity flips
Counterweight cascades flip one side into collapse and the other into runaway.
Containment Strategy:
Rebalance the counterweight before the inversion accelerates.
Protocol Steps:
4. 	Stabilize the Overloaded Side (Quiet Grove or Plains)
5. 	Inject Counter‑Momentum or Counter‑Depth
6. 	Reopen Drift Corridors to bleed excess energy
7. 	Reassert Binding Law (depth * momentum = constant)
Outcome:
The seesaw stabilizes; runaway stops.
Best Containment Window:
During the first polarity flip.

8. PHASE MISMATCH CONTAINMENT
Stopping temporal misalignment
Phase mismatch cascades occur when entangled nodes fall into different collapse phases.
Containment Strategy:
Force both nodes into the same temporal phase.
Protocol Steps:
5. 	Identify Phase Divergence (Amplification vs Propagation, etc.)
6. 	Apply Temporal Dampening to the faster node
7. 	Apply Temporal Acceleration to the slower node
8. 	Lock Both Nodes into Interruption Phase
9. 	Reassert Phase Coupling
Outcome:
Temporal coherence is restored; collapse stops spreading.
Best Containment Window:
Before the mismatch reaches the Depth Chamber.

10. RIGIDITY CASCADE CONTAINMENT
Stopping system-wide freeze
Rigidity cascades are the most dangerous — they spread freeze through the entire grid.
Containment Strategy:
Reintroduce elasticity before rigidity reaches the Time Organ.
Protocol Steps:
6. 	Initiate Thaw‑Flow Recovery in Deep Ice
7. 	Inject Warm‑Linear Flow into Elasticity Chamber
8. 	Reopen Drift Corridors to restore motion
9. 	Soften Pressure Mesh to prevent hardening
10. 	Dissolve Resonance Crystallization
11. 	Reassert Binding Law (flex_index + rigidity_index = constant)
Outcome:
Freeze breaks; elasticity returns.
Best Containment Window:
At the very first sign of corridor seizure.

12. COMPOUND CASCADE CONTAINMENT
Stopping multi-binding collapse storms
Compound cascades ignite multiple entanglements at once.
This is the catastrophic mode.
Containment Strategy:
Protect the Elasticity Chamber at all costs.
If elasticity survives, the system can recover.
If elasticity fails, the system enters total temporal lock.
Protocol Steps:
7. 	Isolate Elasticity Chamber from incoming collapse vectors
8. 	Activate Thaw‑Flow Recovery
9. 	Force Momentum Chamber into Interruption Phase
10. 	Drain Depth Chamber via Pressure Sinks
11. 	Break Non‑Critical Entanglements (temporary decoupling)
12. 	Reassert Binding Laws in Priority Order:
• 	QE‑103 (Elasticity–Freeze)
• 	QE‑001 (Depth–Momentum)
• 	QE‑004 (Loop–Exit)
• 	QE‑003 (Fracture–Containment)
Outcome:
The cascade loses coherence and collapses inward instead of outward.
Best Containment Window:
Only one — before Elasticity Chamber enters Frozen Collapse.

14. CONTAINMENT SUCCESS CONDITIONS
A cascade is considered contained when:
• 	No entangled partner is still in collapse
• 	All binding laws are within tolerance
• 	Temporal phases are aligned
• 	Drift corridors are open
• 	Pressure gradients are stable
• 	The Time Organ has resumed pulse rhythm
Containment does not mean healing — it means the system has stopped falling.
