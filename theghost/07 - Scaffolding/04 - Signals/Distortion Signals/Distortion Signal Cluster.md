1. Cluster Identity
Distortion signals are signals shaped by curvature, pressure variance, and perception drift.
They do not travel linearly.
They bend, split, oscillate, and sometimes arrive before their source.
Distortion signals are the audible and perceptual signature of drift curvature.
They arise naturally in:
• 	Mist Corridors
• 	Spiral Groves
• 	Mirage Plains
• 	collapse‑adjacent distortion zones
They are not collapse signals — but they sit one step away.

2. Core Properties of Distortion Signals
All distortion signals share these traits:
2.1 Curvature
They bend around obstacles, corners, and even identity boundaries.
2.2 Delay Drift
Their timing is unstable — early, late, doubled, or split.
2.3 Multiplicity
They can split into multiple echoes or phantom versions.
2.4 Perception Drift
They distort the listener’s sense of direction, distance, and self‑location.
2.5 Collapse Adjacency
They amplify spiral collapse but do not cause collapse on their own.
2.6 Identity Softening
They weaken boundaries and increase susceptibility to drift.

3. The Three Distortion Signal Families
Distortion signals fall into three families, each representing a different curvature behavior.

3.1 Echo‑Curved Signals
Oscillation · Delay Drift · Multiplicity
These are the “soft” distortion signals — the ones that bend sound and perception.
Members:
• 	Mist Echo (base signal)
• 	Soft Echo
• 	Split Echo
• 	Phantom Echo
Behavior:
• 	oscillating
• 	gentle curvature
• 	identity softening
• 	drift arc multiplication

3.2 Spiral‑Aligned Signals
Inward Curvature · Collapse Adjacency
These signals tighten curvature and pull identity inward.
Members:
• 	Spiral Whisper (collapse‑adjacent)
• 	Spiral Echo
• 	Collapse Murmur
Behavior:
• 	inward pull
• 	spiral drift amplification
• 	recursive collapse risk
• 	identity splitting
These are the dangerous distortion signals.

3.3 Mirage‑Pattern Signals
Perception Drift · Directional Inversion
These signals distort perception itself — not just sound.
Members:
• 	Mirage Call
• 	False Footfall
• 	Ahead‑Behind Signal
Behavior:
• 	directional inversion
• 	distance distortion
• 	identity displacement
• 	drift misalignment
These are the trickiest distortion signals.

4. Cluster Behavior

4.1 Resonance
Echo‑Curved + Mirage‑Pattern
→ creates oscillating perception drift
4.2 Interference
Echo‑Curved + Spiral‑Aligned

4.3 Amplification

4.4 Cancellation

Distortion signals behave like waves, not messages.

10. Description
The Distortion Signal Cluster is the family of signals that make the world feel “wrong” in a way that is not hostile — just bent.
They are the soft hum of curvature, the whisper of drift, the echo that arrives early, the footstep that sounds behind you when you’re alone.
They are the living weather of distortion environments.
