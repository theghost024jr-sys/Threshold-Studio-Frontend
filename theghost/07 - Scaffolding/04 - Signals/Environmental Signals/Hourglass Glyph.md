# Hourglass Glyph
*The collapse–recursion sigil encoding temporal pressure, inversion symmetry, and reversible flow*

The Hourglass Glyph is the symbolic core of the Hourglass Signal. While the Hourglass Signal expresses temporal behavior dynamically, the glyph is the **compressed, static form** of that behavior — a sigil that encodes collapse geometry, inversion symmetry, and time‑pressure cycling in a single visual unit.

The glyph is not a representation of an hourglass.  
It is the **mathematical essence** of one.

---

## Essence
The Hourglass Glyph encodes:
- collapse toward a central throat  
- mirrored upper and lower chambers  
- reversible flow  
- temporal recursion  
- pressure cycling  
- inversion symmetry  

It is the world’s shorthand for **time under tension**.

---

## Geometry
The glyph’s geometry is defined by:
- two mirrored lobes (upper and lower)  
- a narrow central constriction  
- rotational or spiraling internal lines  
- a symmetry axis running vertically  
- a pressure node at the throat  
- particulate or mist‑like flow indicators  

The geometry is recursive: each half contains a faint echo of the whole.
