Purpose
Collapse Signals are the early, mid, and late‑stage indicators that a system (role, identity, species‑expression, or signal‑processing structure) is approaching collapse.
They are not symptoms.
They are structural warnings emitted by the system itself.
Collapse Signals allow:
• 	early detection
• 	drift interpretation
• 	collapse prevention
• 	collapse mapping
• 	recovery planning
They are the language of structural failure.

1. Collapse Signal Categories
Collapse signals fall into three universal classes:
1.1 Soft Signals (Early Stage)
These appear when drift begins to exceed tolerance but the structure is still intact.
Characteristics:
• 	subtle
• 	emotional
• 	relational
• 	easily dismissed
Examples:
• 	boundary blur
• 	hesitation
• 	emotional fog
• 	reduced initiative
• 	scanning without action
• 	tonal softening or flattening
Soft signals indicate incipient drift, not collapse.

1.2 Sharp Signals (Mid Stage)
These appear when the system is actively destabilizing.
Characteristics:
• 	sudden
• 	directional
• 	patterned
• 	interpretable
Examples:
• 	abrupt tone shifts
• 	pattern enforcement
• 	hyper‑vigilance
• 	over‑analysis
• 	rigidity
• 	relational withdrawal
Sharp signals indicate active destabilization.

1.3 Hard Signals (Late Stage)
These appear when collapse is imminent or already underway.
Characteristics:
• 	non‑negotiable
• 	structural
• 	overwhelming
• 	system‑wide
Examples:
• 	shutdown
• 	lock
• 	dissociation
• 	emotional inversion
• 	signal overload
• 	identity fracture
Hard signals indicate collapse activation.

3. Collapse Signals by Identity State
Identity collapse produces its own signal set:
3.1 Identity Soft Signals
• 	self‑doubt
• 	narrative fog
• 	emotional mismatch
• 	reduced coherence
3.2 Identity Sharp Signals
• 	contradictory narratives
• 	role misalignment
• 	emotional inversion
• 	relational distortion
3.3 Identity Hard Signals
• 	identity fracture
• 	dissociation
• 	collapse drift
• 	self‑model failure
Identity collapse often propagates into Role Collapse.

4. Collapse Signals by Species Pattern
Species bias determines how collapse appears:
Predator Species
• 	sharp externalized signals
• 	threat projection
• 	escalation
Prey Species
• 	soft internalized signals
• 	withdrawal
• 	freeze patterns
Bird Species
• 	scanning overload
• 	dissociative drift
Aquatic Species
• 	boundary dissolution
• 	emotional flooding
These interact with the Species–Role Expression Table.
