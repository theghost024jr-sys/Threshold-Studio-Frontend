The structural mechanics by which Threshold shifts from one long‑cycle condition to another, reconfiguring pressure, resonance, lineage, and collapse vectors.

Purpose
Era Transitions define how Threshold moves between:
• 	pressure epochs
• 	resonance eras
• 	lineage cycles
• 	collapse/reformation arcs
• 	worldphase states
They are not narrative beats — they are systemic reorientations.

The Four Transition Types
Era Transitions occur through four canonical mechanisms:

1. Pressure‑Driven Transition
Triggered by global pressure shifts.
Mechanics:
• 	pressure spike or collapse
• 	environment destabilization
• 	species migration
• 	drift vector reorientation
Example:
The Great Compression → Drift Era

2. Resonance‑Driven Transition
Triggered by harmonic alignment or rupture.
Mechanics:
• 	synchronized resonance across environments
• 	signal clarity or distortion dominance
• 	lineage activation or silence
Example:
Harmonic Era → Shimmering Drift

3. Collapse‑Driven Transition
Triggered by systemic failure.
Mechanics:
• 	collapse vector propagation
• 	identity rupture
• 	role inversion
• 	environmental fracture
Example:
Spiral Collapse → Rebinding Cycle

4. Lineage‑Driven Transition
Triggered by mythic substrate reconfiguration.
Mechanics:
• 	thread activation or rupture
• 	memory surge
• 	myth emergence or disappearance
• 	symbolic realignment
Example:
Lantern Cycle → Lost Thread Emergence

Era Transition Table


Transition Phases
Each Era Transition unfolds in four phases:

1. Tension Phase
Pressure builds, resonance destabilizes, lineage flickers.
2. Break Phase
Collapse vectors activate, boundaries rupture, drift intensifies.
3. Reorientation Phase
New pressure gradients form, resonance realigns, lineage threads reconnect.
4. Stabilization Phase
New era conditions settle, species adapt, worldstate updates.

Era Transition Dynamics
Transitions propagate through three channels:

1. Layer Stack
Transitions deform multiple Layers simultaneously.
• 	Mythic → Identity → Role → Signal → Species → Environment → Worldstate

2. Environmental Spread
Transitions move across environments via drift corridors and pressure zones.
• 	Mist → Deep → Shore → Ruin → Grove → Branch → Field → Forge

3. Lineage Echo
Transitions echo through mythic substrate and memory threads.
• 	Lantern → Whisper → Stone → Ember → Lost Myth

Era Transition Failure Modes
Transitions fail when:
• 	pressure overloads
• 	resonance mismatches
• 	lineage threads rupture
• 	collapse becomes recursive
• 	reformation fails to stabilize
Failure produces:
• 	drift storms
• 	distortion fields
• 	identity thinning
• 	species destabilization
• 	worldphase fracture
