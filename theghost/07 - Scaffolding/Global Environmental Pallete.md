Overview
The Global Environmental Palette defines the materials, colors, textures, densities, and sensory cues that all environments in Threshold can use.
It ensures that every space — regardless of phase — feels like it belongs to the same world.
This palette is phase‑agnostic.
Individual phases select from it, distort it, or collapse it.

1. Color Logic
Colors in Threshold are not symbolic — they are behavioral.
Primary Color Families
• 	Edge Colors — soft whites, pale golds, muted blues
• 	Pattern Colors — rhythmic greens, structured cyans, repeating teals
• 	Pressure Colors — sharp reds, fractured magentas, volatile oranges
• 	Dissolution Colors — fogged greys, washed lavenders, low‑contrast neutrals
• 	Gravity Colors — deep browns, heavy umbers, grounded charcoals
• 	Integration Colors — swirling violets, recombining blues, coherent gradients
• 	Continuity Colors — stone greys, memory blacks, weighted neutrals
Shadow Color Distortions
Shadow phases collapse color:
• 	False Gate: misleading warmth, false glows
• 	Noise Spiral: chaotic color noise
• 	Destructive Rupture: violent contrast
• 	Dissociation: fading, disappearing color
• 	Rigidity: frozen, unchanging color
• 	Failed Re‑Entry: broken gradients
• 	Immobilizing Weight: oppressive dimness

2. Material Logic
Materials express phase physics, not realism.
Global Material Families
• 	Threshold Materials — fog, mist, soft stone, shifting glass
• 	Pattern Materials — etched metal, repeating tile, woven fiber
• 	Rupture Materials — cracked stone, splintered wood, fractured crystal
• 	Dissolution Materials — vapor, dust, soft fabric, dissolving surfaces
• 	Gravity Materials — dense stone, packed earth, heavy metal
• 	Integration Materials — braided fiber, recombining metal, spiral‑grain wood
• 	Continuity Materials — aged stone, layered sediment, memory‑dense surfaces
Shadow Material Distortions
• 	False Gate: hollow materials, fake solidity
• 	Noise Spiral: glitch textures, contradictory patterns
• 	Destructive Rupture: unstable fractures
• 	Dissociation: disappearing surfaces
• 	Rigidity: locked, immovable materials
• 	Failed Re‑Entry: incomplete or broken materials
• 	Immobilizing Weight: hyper‑dense, crushing materials

3. Texture Logic
Textures communicate state.
Global Texture Families
• 	Smooth → opening
• 	Patterned → signal
• 	Jagged → rupture
• 	Soft → drift
• 	Dense → anchor
• 	Braided → return
• 	Layered → burden
Shadow Texture Distortions
• 	False Gate: misleading smoothness
• 	Noise Spiral: chaotic texture noise
• 	Destructive Rupture: unstable jaggedness
• 	Dissociation: fading texture
• 	Rigidity: locked, unchanging texture
• 	Failed Re‑Entry: broken texture
• 	Immobilizing Weight: oppressive density

4. Density Logic
Density is the felt weight of an environment.
Global Density States
• 	Low Density → Gate, Drift
• 	Medium Density → Signal, Return
• 	High Density → Fault, Anchor, Burden
Shadow Density Distortions
• 	False Gate: deceptive low density
• 	Noise Spiral: fluctuating density
• 	Destructive Rupture: density spikes
• 	Dissociation: density collapse
• 	Rigidity: locked high density
• 	Failed Re‑Entry: inconsistent density
• 	Immobilizing Weight: extreme density

5. Ambient Logic
Ambient cues are behavioral signals, not decoration.
Global Ambient Families
• 	Gate: soft wind, distant hum
• 	Signal: rhythmic pulses, patterned tones
• 	Fault: crackling, pressure pops
• 	Drift: muffled ambience, soft reverb
• 	Anchor: low hum, steady resonance
• 	Return: swirling harmonics
• 	Burden: deep resonance, weighted silence
Shadow Ambient Distortions
• 	False Gate: misleading cues
• 	Noise Spiral: chaotic audio noise
• 	Destructive Rupture: violent spikes
• 	Dissociation: disappearing ambience
• 	Rigidity: frozen ambience
• 	Failed Re‑Entry: broken loops
• 	Immobilizing Weight: oppressive silence

6. Palette Integration Rules
Every environment must:
• 	choose from the global palette
• 	distort according to its phase
• 	collapse according to its shadow phase
• 	recover by shifting palette families forward
This keeps the world coherent.

7. Palette Purpose
The palette ensures:
• 	visual consistency
• 	emotional clarity
• 	phase legibility
• 	collapse readability
• 	recovery visibility
It is the material language of Threshold.
