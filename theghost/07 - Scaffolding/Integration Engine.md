The system that synthesizes all inputs across Layers, pressures, signals, and lineage to produce coherent worldstate.

Purpose
The Integration Engine is the central processing logic of Threshold.
It determines:
• 	how systems combine
• 	how contradictions resolve
• 	how collapse propagates
• 	how reformation stabilizes
• 	how worldstate updates
• 	how meaning becomes actionable
• 	how Layers influence each other
It is not a machine.
It is a logic field that governs coherence.

The Four Core Functions of the Integration Engine
The Integration Engine performs four essential operations:

1. Aggregation
Collects all active inputs across the system.
Inputs include:
• 	pressure fields
• 	drift vectors
• 	resonance patterns
• 	boundary states
• 	lineage threads
• 	role vectors
• 	identity conditions
• 	environmental geometry
• 	species expression
• 	collapse vectors
Aggregation is total — nothing is ignored.

2. Resolution
Reconciles contradictions between systems.
Mechanics:
• 	pressure vs. resonance
• 	identity vs. environment
• 	lineage vs. collapse
• 	signal vs. drift
• 	role vs. boundary
Resolution does not eliminate conflict — it chooses the dominant force.

3. Synthesis
Combines resolved inputs into a unified state.
Mechanics:
• 	merges pressure gradients
• 	aligns or misaligns resonance
• 	stabilizes or destabilizes boundaries
• 	amplifies or suppresses lineage
• 	clarifies or distorts signals
Synthesis produces system‑level behavior.

4. Output
Updates the worldstate.
Outputs include:
• 	worldphase
• 	environmental conditions
• 	identity stability
• 	species behavior
• 	role expression
• 	signal clarity
• 	collapse or reformation states
The worldstate is the final output of the Integration Engine.

Integration Engine Dynamics
The Integration Engine follows four dynamic principles:

1. Dominance Logic
The strongest force wins.
• 	pressure > drift
• 	resonance > distortion
• 	collapse > boundary
• 	lineage > identity
• 	environment > role
Dominance determines the shape of the output.

2. Inheritance Logic
Higher Layers influence lower Layers.
• 	mythic → identity → role → signal → species → environment → worldstate
Inheritance determines direction.

3. Feedback Logic
Lower Layers influence higher Layers.
• 	worldstate → environment → species → signal → role → identity → mythic
Feedback determines correction.

4. Threshold Logic
When forces exceed capacity, collapse occurs.
• 	pressure overload
• 	resonance inversion
• 	boundary rupture
• 	lineage fracture
• 	drift recursion
Thresholds determine failure.

Integration Engine Failure Modes
The Integration Engine fails when:
• 	contradictions cannot be resolved
• 	collapse becomes recursive
• 	lineage ruptures
• 	resonance inverts
• 	pressure becomes nonlinear
• 	drift becomes self‑sustaining
Failure produces:
• 	collapse identity
• 	distortion fields
• 	drift storms
• 	environmental fracture
• 	worldphase instability

Integration Engine and Long‑Cycle Events
Long‑Cycle Events are macro‑scale outputs of the Integration Engine:
• 	Pressure Epochs
• 	Resonance Eras
• 	Lineage Cycles
• 	Collapse/Reformation Cycles
Era Transitions occur when the Integration Engine shifts dominant logic.

Integration Engine and Myth
Myths are integration patterns:
• 	Lantern → boundary + resonance
• 	Whisper → drift + signal
• 	Stone → memory + structure
• 	Ember → pressure + ignition
• 	Lost Myth → collapse + absence
Myths are not stories — they are integration templates.
