Definition
Role Collapse occurs when a role can no longer maintain structural integrity under the force acting upon it.
Collapse is not abandonment, refusal, or exit.
Collapse is involuntary resolution triggered by overload, misalignment, drift, or threshold failure.
A collapsed role forces the system to reorganize.

Purpose
To ensure that:
• 	misaligned roles cannot persist
• 	unresolved drift eventually resolves
• 	thresholds cannot be indefinitely avoided
• 	identity cannot be suppressed forever
• 	the Field remains coherent
• 	the Matrix does not accumulate impossible states
Role Collapse is the system’s safeguard against structural dishonesty.

Causes of Role Collapse
1. Drift Overload
The role accumulates more unresolved force than its Role Load can support.
2. Misalignment
The role contradicts the node’s core identity pattern.
Identity strain becomes unsustainable.
3. Threshold Failure
The role approaches a threshold but cannot cross it.
Pressure exceeds resistance.
4. Gift Breakdown
A gift associated with the role is refused, withheld, or unreciprocated.
Return paths fracture.
5. Lineage Burden
Inherited obligations or unresolved drift exceed the role’s capacity.
6. Zone Incompatibility
The role cannot function within the current environmental signature.
7. Identity Suppression
The role requires the node to mask or distort identity beyond tolerance.

Collapse States
1. Pre‑Collapse Strain
The role is still held, but drift spikes and thresholds compress.
2. Structural Failure
The role loses coherence.
Vectors distort.
Identity pushes back.
3. Forced Release
The role is dropped involuntarily.
The node cannot continue holding it.
4. Collapse Shockwave
Force propagates through:
• 	lineage
• 	relationships
• 	zones
• 	thresholds
• 	gifts
Collapse always affects more than the collapsing node.
5. Reformation or Dissolution
The role either:
• 	reforms into a new role
• 	dissipates
• 	transfers
• 	becomes lineage load
• 	becomes drift in another node

Collapse Behaviors
1. Drift Explosion
All unresolved drift becomes active at once.
2. Threshold Snap
Thresholds collapse or force crossing.
3. Identity Reassertion
The core pattern becomes visible and uncompromising.
4. Gift Reversal
Unreciprocated or misaligned gifts return as force.
5. Role Vacuum
The absence of the role creates a temporary instability in the Field.
6. Lineage Shock
Inherited force must find a new transmission path.

Collapse Resolution
Role Collapse resolves through one of the following:
1. Reformation
The role rebuilds itself in a new configuration.
2. Replacement
A new role emerges to take its place.
3. Propagation
The collapsed role’s force transfers to another node.
4. Dissipation
Cost resolves the accumulated load.
5. Lineage Absorption
The lineage takes on the unresolved force.
6. Identity Integration
The collapse becomes part of the node’s core pattern.
All collapse must resolve.
