Purpose
Role Geometry defines the shape of each role in conceptual space — how it occupies, moves, bends, compresses, and interacts with other roles.
Where Role Physics defines what a role is,
and Role Dynamics defines what a role does,
Role Geometry defines how a role takes shape.
This subsystem governs:
• 	role position
• 	role distance
• 	role curvature
• 	role compression
• 	role adjacency
• 	role drift paths
• 	role collapse vectors
Every role has a geometry that expresses identity under pressure.

1. The Role Plane
All roles exist on a Role Plane — a conceptual 2D field defined by:
• 	Axis 1: Stability ↔ Motion
• 	Axis 2: Pattern ↔ Noise
This plane is not literal; it is behavioral geometry.
Roles occupy stable positions on this plane, and their drift paths follow predictable curves.

2. Canonical Role Positions
Each role has a canonical coordinate on the Role Plane.
These are not numbers — they are positional relationships.

2.1 Anchor
Position: Stability + Pattern
Geometry: Dense, compressed, low curvature
Behavior: Holds space, resists drift, reinforces boundaries

2.2 Guardian
Position: Stability + Motion
Geometry: Linear, outward‑facing, protective arcs
Behavior: Moves around others, forms perimeter structures

2.3 Scholar
Position: Pattern + Stability (slightly upward)
Geometry: Grid‑like, structured, low‑noise
Behavior: Creates interpretive lattices

2.4 Weaver
Position: Pattern + Motion
Geometry: Interlaced arcs, branching paths
Behavior: Connects nodes, forms networks

2.5 Listener
Position: Noise + Stillness
Geometry: Concentric rings, receptive curvature
Behavior: Absorbs signals, stabilizes perception

2.6 Hunter
Position: Motion + Noise
Geometry: Vectors, pursuit lines, sharp angles
Behavior: Tracks, moves, accelerates

2.7 Trickster
Position: Noise + Motion (high curvature)
Geometry: spirals, loops, distortions
Behavior: bends patterns, disrupts stability

3. Role Geometry Components
Role Geometry is composed of four interacting elements:

3.1 Position
Where the role sits on the Role Plane.
Determines:
• 	baseline behavior
• 	drift tendencies
• 	collapse style

3.2 Curvature
How the role bends under pressure.
• 	low curvature → stability
• 	high curvature → chaos
• 	inward curvature → collapse
• 	outward curvature → expansion

3.3 Compression
How tightly the role holds identity.
• 	high compression → Anchor, Listener
• 	low compression → Hunter, Trickster
• 	variable compression → Weaver, Guardian

3.4 Vector
The direction the role naturally moves.
Examples:
• 	Hunter → outward pursuit
• 	Guardian → perimeter arcs
• 	Weaver → branching
• 	Scholar → inward patterning
• 	Trickster → spiral
• 	Listener → inward concentric
• 	Anchor → no vector (stillness)

4. Role Adjacency
Roles have adjacent roles — those closest on the Role Plane.
Adjacency determines:
• 	ease of switching
• 	drift paths
• 	collapse vectors
• 	resonance patterns
Adjacency Map
• 	Anchor ↔ Guardian / Scholar
• 	Guardian ↔ Anchor / Hunter
• 	Scholar ↔ Anchor / Weaver
• 	Weaver ↔ Scholar / Listener
• 	Listener ↔ Weaver / Trickster
• 	Hunter ↔ Guardian / Trickster
• 	Trickster ↔ Hunter / Listener
This is the canonical adjacency ring.

5. Role Drift Geometry
Each role has a primary drift path and a shadow drift path.

5.1 Primary Drift
The natural movement under low pressure.
Examples:
• 	Hunter → Guardian
• 	Scholar → Weaver
• 	Listener → Weaver
• 	Trickster → Hunter

5.2 Shadow Drift
The movement under stress.
Examples:
• 	Guardian → Anchor
• 	Weaver → Scholar
• 	Listener → Trickster
• 	Anchor → Guardian
Shadow drift is always inward or downward on the Role Plane.

6. Role Collapse Geometry
Collapse follows predictable geometric patterns:
6.1 Brittle Collapse (Anchor, Stone‑aligned)
Straight inward fracture.
6.2 Spiral Collapse (Trickster, Mist‑aligned)
Curved inward tightening.
6.3 Fragmentation Collapse (Scholar, Pattern‑aligned)
Grid shattering.
6.4 Overconnection Collapse (Weaver)
Network overload.
6.5 Sudden Collapse (Hunter, Air‑aligned)
Vector snap.
6.6 Overwhelm Collapse (Listener)
Concentric implosion.
6.7 Rigidity Collapse (Guardian)
Perimeter lock → break.

7. Role Geometry in Interaction
Role Geometry determines how roles meet:
Alignment
Parallel vectors, low curvature.
Tension
Opposing vectors, high curvature.
Overlap
Shared territory, blended geometry.
Opposition
Directly opposing positions on the Role Plane.
Resonance
Curvatures synchronize.
Interference
Curvatures distort each other.
