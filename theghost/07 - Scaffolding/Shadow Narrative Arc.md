Purpose
The Shadow Narrative Arc defines the submerged storyline that unfolds beneath the seven layers of Threshold.
It is the hidden cycle that governs:
• 	how the world shapes the being
• 	how the being shapes the world
• 	how collapse reveals identity
• 	how identity inverts into role
• 	how signals propagate change
This arc is not chosen.
It is encountered.
It is the story the world tells about the character, regardless of what the character believes they are doing.

1. Core Principles
2. The Shadow Arc is recursive.
It repeats at multiple scales:
per environment, per region, per character, per Column.
3. The Shadow Arc is structural, not emotional.
It describes what happens, not how it feels.
4. The Shadow Arc is driven by physics.
Drift → Field → Collapse → Identity → Role → Signal.
5. The Shadow Arc is inevitable.
Once initiated, it completes unless interrupted by collapse or resonance.
6. The Shadow Arc is the world’s arc and the character’s arc simultaneously.
The world is not a backdrop.
It is a participant.

7. The Seven Phases (mapped to the Seven Layers)
The Shadow Narrative Arc aligns directly with the Multi‑Layer Map System.

3. Unseen (Environment Layer)
The character moves through the world unaware of the deeper forces shaping them.
Everything appears stable, even when it is not.
This is the pre‑narrative state.

4. Distortion (Drift Layer)
Motion bends.
Paths curve.
Choices feel guided or subtly biased.
The world begins to nudge the character.
This is the first sign that the world has intentions.

3. Pressure (Field Layer)
Invisible forces accumulate.
The character feels pushed, pulled, compressed, or expanded.
Identity begins to strain under environmental pressure.
This is the tension phase.

4. Collapse (Collapse Layer)
Something breaks.
A belief, a boundary, a certainty, a pattern.
Collapse reveals the underlying structure of the world.
This is the rupture.

5. Exposure (Identity Layer)
The character sees themselves without the protective layer.
Identity becomes visible, vulnerable, and malleable.
This is not a moral revelation — it is a structural exposure.
This is the mirror moment.

6. Reversal (Role Layer)
Identity flips into a new behavioral geometry.
Protector becomes threat.
Observer becomes catalyst.
Listener becomes trickster.
This is the emergence of the Shadow Role.
This is the inversion.

7. Integration (Signal Layer)
The new identity propagates through the world.
Signals shift.
The environment responds.
The character’s presence now alters the physics around them.
This is the broadcast.

8. Re‑emergence (Return to Environment)
The cycle completes.
The character returns to the world changed —
and the world now reacts differently to them.
This is the reset, but not a return to zero.

5. Shadow Arc & Character Arcs
The Shadow Arc is the world’s arc, but it becomes the character’s arc when:
• 	drift curvature aligns with personal bias
• 	collapse intersects with internal fault lines
• 	identity exposure reveals something the character avoids
• 	role inversion contradicts self‑image
• 	signal propagation affects relationships or environments
This is how Threshold creates emergent narrative without scripting.
