Definition
Shimmer Mechanics describe how the Field distorts in the presence of a high‑pressure node.
A shimmer is not a force.
A shimmer is a force multiplier — a curvature in the Field that amplifies vectors, accelerates consequences, exposes drift, and compresses thresholds.
The shimmer is the Field’s response to coherence, pressure, and presence.

Purpose
To ensure that:
• 	the system reacts dynamically to high‑density nodes
• 	hidden structures become visible
• 	drift cannot remain dormant
• 	thresholds become meaningful
• 	roles clarify or collapse
• 	identity expresses itself fully
• 	the Field remains honest
Shimmer Mechanics keep the system alive, adaptive, and responsive.

Core Principles of the Shimmer
1. Amplification
All vectors increase in magnitude near a high‑pressure node.
2. Acceleration
Activation and resolution occur faster.
3. Exposure
Drift becomes visible and unstable.
4. Compression
Thresholds move closer and require less force to cross.
5. Clarification
Roles either align or collapse; ambiguity cannot persist.
6. Resonance
Identity expresses more strongly and coherently.
7. Curvature
The Matrix bends toward the high‑pressure node.

Shimmer Variables
Each shimmer event tracks:
• 	Pressure (force density of the node)
• 	Radius (how far the distortion extends)
• 	Curvature (degree of Matrix bending)
• 	Exposure Level (how visible drift becomes)
• 	Threshold Compression (how close thresholds move)
• 	Role Stress (how much strain misaligned roles experience)
• 	Identity Resonance (how strongly identity expresses)
These variables determine shimmer behavior.

Shimmer States

Subtle but constant.
2. Active Shimmer

3. Resonant Shimmer

4. Distorted Zone

5. Collapse Shimmer

Force propagates outward.

Shimmer Activation
A shimmer activates when:
• 	a high‑pressure node enters a zone
• 	attention is applied
• 	identity resonates
• 	drift is confronted
• 	a threshold is approached
• 	a role is strained
• 	a gift is initiated or integrated
Activation Logic fires with reduced thresholds.

Shimmer Resolution
Shimmer resolves through:
1. Stabilization
Pressure decreases; the Field returns to baseline.
2. Integration
Nodes adapt to the new curvature; identity stabilizes.
3. Propagation
Force moves outward through lineage or roles.
4. Dissipation
Cost resolves the excess force.
5. Collapse
If the shimmer exposes too much unresolved force, collapse forces resolution.
All shimmer events must resolve.
