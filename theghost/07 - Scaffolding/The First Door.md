The visual and spatial logic of the first door

The player stands in total darkness. No walls, no floor, no sky. Just the sense of being suspended in a place that hasn’t decided what it is yet. Then the Hub’s outer layer—still invisible—begins to respond to the player’s internal stance.

The door appears as:

- a **thin vertical seam of light**, like a crack in reality
- a **soft spiral**, like a wormhole forming in slow motion
- a **pressure shift**, as if the air is being pulled toward a single point
- a **color gradient**, matching the petal the player corresponds to

It doesn’t open outward.  
It **unfolds inward**, like the world is turning itself inside out to reveal the correct layer.

Why it feels like a wormhole

Threshold’s transitions aren’t physical. They’re **alignment events**. A wormhole metaphor fits because:

- space bends around the player
- the world collapses into a single point
- the player is pulled, not pushed
- the motion is smooth, inevitable, and strangely gentle
- the destination feels like it was always there, waiting

It’s not teleportation.  
It’s **recognition**.

The world isn’t moving the player.  
The world is moving _with_ the player.

Why it feels like a light

Light in Threshold is never illumination—it’s **revelation**. The first door appears as light because:

- the Hub is revealing the player’s resonance
- the darkness is giving way to identity
- the world is showing the shape of the next motion
- the player is stepping into a layer that matches their internal stance

The light is not bright.  
It’s **true**.
