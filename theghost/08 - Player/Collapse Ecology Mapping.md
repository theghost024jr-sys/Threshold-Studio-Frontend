# **Node: Collapse Ecology Mapping**

_(Organism → Collapse → Ecology)_

**Collapse Ecology Mapping** _“A structural map of how collapse behaves as an ecosystem — its species, flows, weather, trophic layers, and extinction cascades.”_

### **Purpose**

To describe collapse not as an event, but as a **living biome** with:

- native species (collapse‑born entities)
    
- predator–prey dynamics (pressure → drift → anchor → rupture)
    
- weather systems (shear storms, resonance fronts, pressure squalls)
    
- nutrient cycles (coherence → fragmentation → compost → reformation)
    
- extinction cascades (chain failure → Flower shedding → Garden reconfiguration)
    

This node defines collapse as an **ecological organ**, not a failure state.

# **Core Components**

### **1. Collapse Species**

Entities that only exist under collapse conditions:

- **Shards** — fragments of coherence
    
- **Hollows** — nodes with drained identity anchors
    
- **Tangles** — overlinked clusters in runaway feedback
    
- **Ghost Currents** — drift‑only flows with no mass
    
- **Spines** — rigid structures that resist collapse until they snap
    

### **2. Collapse Weather**

Atmospheric expressions of systemic stress:

- **Shear Fronts**
    
- **Resonance Storms**
    
- **Pressure Tides**
    
- **Drift Winds**
    
- **Anchor Quakes**
    

### **3. Collapse Flows**

How energy moves through the ecosystem:

- **Fragmentation Flow** — coherence → shards
    
- **Compost Flow** — shards → nutrient mass
    
- **Reformation Flow** — nutrient → new Flowers
    
- **Migration Flow** — orphans drifting into new basins
    

### **4. Collapse Trophic Layers**

The “food chain” of collapse:

- **Top Predators** — shear events, resonance spikes
    
- **Mid‑Layer Scavengers** — tangles, hollows
    
- **Decomposers** — compost engines
    
- **Producers** — new Flowers forming from collapse mass
    

### **5. Extinction Cascades**

How collapse spreads:

- brittle chain → link failure
    
- link failure → chain separation
    
- chain separation → Flower shedding
    
- Flower shedding → Garden reconfiguration
