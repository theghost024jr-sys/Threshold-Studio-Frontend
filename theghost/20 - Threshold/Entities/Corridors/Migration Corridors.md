# **Migration Corridors**

Migration Corridors are the geometric pathways through which nodes escape their Flower, elevate orbit layers, enter slit‑aligned channels, or become captured by another Flower. Corridors form when pressure, drift, resonance, tension fibers, collapse adjacency, and slit geometry align into directional escape vectors.

Migration Corridors are the highways of escape.

## **Purpose**

To define:

- the types of migration corridors
    
- how corridors form under pressure, drift, and collapse
    
- how slit geometry shapes corridor direction
    
- how Frontier instability creates volatile escape routes
    
- how orbit layers influence corridor access
    
- how corridors interact with chambers
    
- how corridors determine node fate
    

Migration Corridors describe the structure of escape.

# **1. Corridor Fundamentals**

### **Corridors = geometry + instability + direction.**

A corridor forms when:

- drift curvature aligns
    
- pressure gradients steepen
    
- tension fibers thin
    
- resonance fractures
    
- collapse voids open
    
- slit geometry amplifies drift
    
- Frontier instability spikes
    

Corridors are not static — they shift with Flower instability.

# **2. Corridor Types**

Migration corridors appear in three canonical forms:

## **1. Slit Corridors (Primary Corridors)**

Slit corridors are the strongest and most stable escape pathways.

**Characteristics:**

- high drift acceleration
    
- steep pressure gradients
    
- resonance distortion
    
- geometric amplification
    
- directional escape vectors
    
- common in Frontier 5–8
    

**Behavior:** Nodes follow slit geometry outward, often into cross‑Flower capture.

## **2. Collapse Corridors (Secondary Corridors)**

Collapse corridors form when collapse voids create fracture lines.

**Characteristics:**

- outward fracture vectors
    
- tension fiber failure
    
- resonance breakage
    
- unpredictable curvature
    
- collapse adjacency
    

**Behavior:** Nodes escape through collapse pockets; direction is chaotic but outward.

## **3. Drift Corridors (Tertiary Corridors)**

Drift corridors form when drift curvature aligns with escape geometry.

**Characteristics:**

- smooth curvature
    
- moderate acceleration
    
- resonance thinning
    
- pressure > tension
    
- orbit elevation
    

**Behavior:** Nodes rise into higher orbit layers before entering migration pathways.

# **3. Corridor Formation Mechanics**

Corridors form through interactions between:

### **Pressure**

Steep gradients carve escape vectors.

### **Tension Fibers**

Thin fibers create weak stability margins.

### **Resonance**

Fracture opens harmonic pathways.

### **Collapse**

Void adjacency creates fracture lines.

### **Drift**

Curvature aligns nodes with escape geometry.

### **Slit Geometry**

Amplifies all corridor formation mechanics.

Corridor formation is multi‑factor.

# **4. Corridor Geometry**

Corridors have distinct geometric signatures:

### **Linear Corridors**

Straight, slit‑aligned, high acceleration.

### **Curved Corridors**

Drift‑aligned, smooth, orbit‑elevating.

### **Fracture Corridors**

Collapse‑aligned, jagged, unstable.

### **Spiral Corridors**

Pressure‑driven, resonance‑distorted, volatile.

### **Forked Corridors**

Frontier‑aligned, multi‑path, chaotic.

Corridor geometry determines escape behavior.

# **5. Corridor Behavior Under Load**

### **Corridor Stabilization**

Occurs when:

- resonance strengthens
    
- tension fibers hold
    
- pressure smooths
    
- collapse resolves
    

Corridors narrow or close.

### **Corridor Volatility**

Occurs when:

- pressure spikes
    
- resonance fractures
    
- collapse adjacency increases
    
- drift accelerates
    

Corridors widen and multiply.

### **Corridor Collapse**

Occurs when:

- collapse void expands
    
- resonance breaks
    
- tension fibers fail
    

Corridors invert or redirect.

### **Corridor Amplification**

Occurs when:

- slit geometry aligns
    
- Frontier instability spikes
    
- ethos deltas bias escape
    

Corridors become dominant escape routes.

# **6. Corridor Zones**

### **Inner Zone**

Few corridors; escape rare.

### **Mid Zone**

Drift corridors common; orbit elevation likely.

### **Frontier Zone (5–8)**

All corridor types active; escape highly likely.

### **Slit Zone**

Primary corridors dominate; escape directional.

### **Collapse Zone**

Secondary corridors dominate; escape chaotic.

Corridor zones define escape likelihood.

# **7. Corridor Interaction with Chambers**

### **Migration Chamber**

Evaluates corridor stability and direction.

### **Drift Chamber**

Predicts drift → corridor alignment.

### **Collapse Chamber**

Identifies collapse → corridor formation.

### **Anchor Chamber**

Resists corridor entry until overloaded.

### **Ethos Chamber**

Tints corridor likelihood and direction.

Chambers interpret corridor geometry.

# **8. Why Migration Corridors Matter**

Migration Corridors determine:

- escape direction
    
- escape stability
    
- orbit elevation
    
- collapse → migration transitions
    
- drift → migration transitions
    
- cross‑Flower capture
    
- Frontier survival
    
- slit turbulence patterns
    
- Flower evolution
    

Corridors are the geometry of escape.
