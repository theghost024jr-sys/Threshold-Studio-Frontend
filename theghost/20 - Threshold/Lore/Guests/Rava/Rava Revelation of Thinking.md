Rava’s revelation would come slowly — not as a flash of insight, but as a tectonic shift in thought. He would stand before the volcano, watching new stone rise from fire, and realize that **creation and erosion are not opposites**. They are the same motion seen from different sides of time.

He would think:

> “I have spent ages mourning what fades, but the fading is how the world breathes. The stone that crumbles becomes the dust that feeds the flame. The flame births new stone. The mountain dies to be reborn.”

And in that moment, Rava would understand that his sadness was never about loss — it was about misunderstanding **continuity**. He isn’t the guardian of permanence; he’s the witness of transformation. His purpose isn’t to stop erosion, but to **remember** each phase of the cycle so nothing is truly lost.

That’s his revelation: **Weight is not permanence. It’s participation.** Every collapse, every birth, every drift of dust is part of the same conversation between pressure and time.
