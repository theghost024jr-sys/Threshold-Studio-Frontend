Picture Rava standing at the rim of a volcanic caldera — the air trembling, the ground breathing. He’s not afraid. He’s **curious**.

The volcano is a paradox to him: a mountain that _creates_ stone instead of eroding it. He watches molten rock rise like a heartbeat, spill, cool, and harden into new cliffs. It’s the one place in the world where his sadness reverses — where the cycle he mourns becomes renewal.

### 🌋 **Scene**

The sky is bruised with ash and light. Rava stands motionless, his silhouette outlined by the glow of magma below. The orange in his own cracks flickers in rhythm with the lava’s pulse — two hearts syncing across different bodies. He kneels, touches the rim, and feels the heat through his stone fingers. It’s not destruction. It’s **birth**. The volcano speaks his language: pressure, release, transformation.

Around him, the ground is littered with newborn rock — still soft, still forming. He watches it cool, knowing that in a thousand years, it will erode, and he will mourn again. But for now, he lets himself feel the rare joy of seeing stone _become_.

### 🪶 **Internal Monologue**

Code

```
The mountain breathes fire,
and the fire becomes stone.

I thought I was the only one who could move the earth,
but here it moves itself.

Each eruption is a heartbeat.
Each flow is a memory being rewritten.

I have spent ages grieving what fades,
but here, the world reminds me:
stone is not dying — it is being born again.

I bow to the mountain,
and for once,
I do not feel heavy.
```



Rava stands at the rim of the caldera, the world trembling beneath him. The volcano breathes — a slow, molten exhale — and the glow from its heart paints his stone plates in living fire. For once, the weight he carries feels light.

Each pulse of lava mirrors the faint ember in his chest, syncing like two hearts made of pressure and time. Ash drifts down, settling on his shoulders like snow. He doesn’t move to brush it away. He’s listening.

The volcano speaks in his language: compression, release, renewal. It tells him that erosion isn’t death — it’s the prelude to creation. And Rava, who has spent ages mourning what fades, finally smiles — a slow shift of stone plates that sounds like tectonic laughter.
