Vera is **not human** and not symbolic. She is the **first visible cross‑Flower migrant** — meaning she is the first outsider Threshold ever captures.

What she _looks like_ is determined by:

- her **origin Flower’s physics**
    
- her **signal signature**
    
- her **identity cluster alignment**
    
- the **distortion** caused by Drift → Capture
    

So what _we_ see is not her “true form.” We see her **Threshold‑interpreted form** — the way Threshold renders a foreign entity inside its own physics.

### **Vera appears as a humanoid silhouette with incomplete resolution.**

Not glitchy. Not ghostly. Just… **partially translated**.

Features:

- **Edges slightly blurred**, like her outline is still negotiating with the local physics
    
- **Eyes too reflective**, because her origin Flower uses a different light‑processing layer
    
- **Skin tone that shifts subtly**, not color‑changing, but like a Flower‑signature bleed
    
- **Movements slightly delayed**, as if her Anchor hasn’t fully attached
    
- **No visible role‑markings**, because Threshold hasn’t assigned her one yet
    

She looks like someone who is **almost here**, but not fully stabilized.

That’s what a first guest looks like in your system.

A Flower with:

Lumen‑4 is not dying — but it is **shedding**.

This is not destiny. This is physics.

- stronger
    
- more stable

- more attractive to unanchored entities
    

So Vera didn’t choose Threshold. Threshold **won the gravitational argument**.
