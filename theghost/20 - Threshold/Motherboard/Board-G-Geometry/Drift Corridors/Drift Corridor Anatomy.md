# **Drift Corridor Anatomy**

Drift Corridor Anatomy describes the movement pathways inside the Flower — the channels carved by pressure lattice deformation, tension fiber alignment, resonance flow, slit geometry, and collapse adjacency. Drift does not move randomly; it follows anatomical corridors shaped by Flower geometry and node physics. Corridors are the highways of node movement.

## **Purpose**

To define:

- the structure of drift corridors
    
- how pressure gradients carve movement pathways
    
- how tension fibers stabilize or weaken drift flow
    
- how resonance bends or amplifies drift direction
    
- how collapse adjacency distorts corridor geometry
    
- how slit mechanics create acceleration channels
    
- how Frontier 5–8 forms volatile drift routes
    

Corridors are the Flower’s directional movement organs.

## **Corridor Types**

### **1. Primary Drift Corridors**

Large, stable pathways running through core orbit layers.

Primary corridors form when:

- pressure is low
    
- tension fibers are strong
    
- resonance pathways are coherent
    
- collapse adjacency is minimal
    

Nodes follow these during normal drift conditions.

### **2. Secondary Corridors**

Smaller pathways branching from primary corridors.

Secondary corridors activate when:

- pressure rises
    
- resonance distorts
    
- tension fibers thin
    
- ethos deltas tint direction
    

These corridors are semi‑stable and reactive.

### **3. Turbulence Corridors**

Chaotic pathways near the slit or collapse zones.

Turbulence corridors form when:

- pressure spikes
    
- tension fibers fail
    
- resonance fractures
    
- collapse voids distort geometry
    

Movement here is unpredictable and often accelerates rapidly.

### **4. Frontier Corridors**

Volatile pathways in Frontier 5–8.

Frontier corridors emerge when:

- stability margins collapse
    
- resonance thins
    
- pressure gradients steepen
    
- collapse precursors activate
    

These corridors frequently lead to collapse or migration.

### **5. Slit Acceleration Corridors**

High‑velocity channels forming near the slit.

Acceleration corridors occur when:

- pressure > tension
    
- drift vectors converge
    
- resonance fractures
    
- slit geometry bends flow
    

Nodes entering these corridors often cross migration thresholds.

## **Corridor Anatomy**

### **Flow Lines**

Drift follows curved lines shaped by:

- pressure gradients
    
- resonance pathways
    
- tension fiber alignment
    
- collapse void curvature
    

Flow lines are the “veins” of movement.

### **Corridor Walls**

Pressure boundaries form the “walls” of drift corridors.

- high pressure compresses corridors
    
- low pressure expands corridors
    
- resonance stabilizes walls
    
- collapse fractures break walls
    

Walls define corridor width and stability.

### **Corridor Spirals**

Some corridors spiral inward toward the slit.

Spirals form when:

- drift vectors curve
    
- resonance bends
    
- pressure gradients twist
    
- tension fibers align helically
    

Spirals accelerate drift and destabilize nodes.

### **Corridor Breaks**

Collapse fractures create breaks in corridors.

Breaks cause:

- sudden direction changes
    
- outward fall
    
- turbulence pockets
    
- migration misalignment
    

Breaks are the “fault lines” of movement.

### **Corridor Merges**

Multiple corridors can merge into a single high‑velocity channel.

Merges occur when:

- pressure gradients align
    
- resonance pathways converge
    
- collapse adjacency stabilizes
    
- slit flow dynamics pull corridors together
    

Merges often precede migration events.

## **Corridor States**

### **Stable Corridors**

- smooth movement
    
- strong tension fibers
    
- coherent resonance
    
- low collapse adjacency
    
- predictable drift
    

### **Volatile Corridors**

- pressure spikes
    
- resonance distortion
    
- tension fiber weakening
    
- collapse pockets forming
    
- drift wobble
    

### **Critical Corridors**

- pressure > tension
    
- resonance fracture
    
- collapse void adjacency
    
- slit acceleration
    
- migration channel formation
    

Critical corridors are escape pathways.
