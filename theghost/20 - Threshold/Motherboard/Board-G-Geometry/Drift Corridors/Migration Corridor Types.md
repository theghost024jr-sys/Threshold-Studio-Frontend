# **Migration Corridor Types**

Migration Corridor Types define the distinct geometric classes of escape pathways that form inside the Flower. Each corridor type emerges from different combinations of pressure gradients, drift curvature, resonance fracture, tension fiber thinning, collapse adjacency, slit geometry, and Frontier instability.

Corridor types determine how nodes move, where they escape, and how Flowers exchange identity.

## **Purpose**

To define:

- the canonical corridor types
    
- their geometric signatures
    
- their formation triggers
    
- their stability characteristics
    
- their behavior under load
    
- their role in migration, collapse, and capture
    

Migration Corridor Types describe the taxonomy of escape geometry.

# **1. Slit Corridors (Primary)**

Slit corridors are the strongest, fastest, and most stable escape pathways.

### **Geometry Signature**

- straight or slightly curved
    
- high acceleration
    
- steep pressure gradients
    
- resonance distortion
    
- tension fiber thinning
    
- aligned with slit geometry
    

### **Formation Triggers**

- slit pressure amplification
    
- drift acceleration
    
- resonance fracture
    
- Frontier instability
    

### **Behavior**

- dominant escape routes
    
- high migration likelihood
    
- frequent cross‑Flower capture
    
- stable under load
    

Slit corridors define the Flower’s primary escape vector.

# **2. Collapse Corridors (Secondary)**

Collapse corridors form when collapse voids create fracture lines.

### **Geometry Signature**

- jagged
    
- outward‑bending
    
- fracture‑driven
    
- unstable curvature
    
- tension fiber failure
    

### **Formation Triggers**

- collapse adjacency
    
- void formation
    
- resonance breakage
    
- pressure spikes
    

### **Behavior**

- chaotic escape
    
- unpredictable direction
    
- collapse‑driven migration
    
- high volatility
    

Collapse corridors are dangerous and unstable.

# **3. Drift Corridors (Tertiary)**

Drift corridors form when drift curvature aligns with escape geometry.

### **Geometry Signature**

- smooth curvature
    
- orbit‑elevating
    
- moderate acceleration
    
- resonance thinning
    
- pressure > tension
    

### **Formation Triggers**

- drift acceleration
    
- drift wobble
    
- pressure gradients
    
- slit influence
    

### **Behavior**

- orbit elevation
    
- controlled escape
    
- corridor alignment
    
- moderate stability
    

Drift corridors are transitional escape routes.

# **4. Spiral Corridors**

Spiral corridors form under resonance distortion and pressure rotation.

### **Geometry Signature**

- spiral curvature
    
- rotational pressure gradients
    
- harmonic instability
    
- resonance bending
    

### **Formation Triggers**

- resonance fracture
    
- pressure rotation
    
- collapse adjacency
    
- slit turbulence
    

### **Behavior**

- rotational escape
    
- unpredictable capture
    
- high instability
    

Spiral corridors are rare and volatile.

# **5. Forked Corridors**

Forked corridors split into multiple escape paths.

### **Geometry Signature**

- branching geometry
    
- multi‑path curvature
    
- pressure bifurcation
    
- resonance thinning
    

### **Formation Triggers**

- Frontier instability
    
- collapse adjacency
    
- slit turbulence
    
- drift wobble
    

### **Behavior**

- multi‑direction escape
    
- chaotic migration
    
- unpredictable capture
    
- high volatility
    

Forked corridors dominate Frontier 5–8.

# **6. Corridor Stability Classes**

### **Stable Corridors**

- slit‑aligned
    
- strong resonance
    
- smooth drift
    
- low collapse adjacency
    

### **Volatile Corridors**

- collapse‑aligned
    
- resonance fracture
    
- pressure spikes
    
- drift wobble
    

### **Critical Corridors**

- collapse void
    
- slit turbulence
    
- pressure > tension
    
- Frontier instability
    

Stability classes determine escape reliability.

# **7. Why Corridor Types Matter**

Corridor types determine:

- escape direction
    
- escape stability
    
- orbit elevation
    
- collapse → migration transitions
    
- drift → migration transitions
    
- cross‑Flower capture
    
- Frontier survival
    
- slit turbulence patterns
    
- Flower evolution
    

Corridor types define the structure of escape.
