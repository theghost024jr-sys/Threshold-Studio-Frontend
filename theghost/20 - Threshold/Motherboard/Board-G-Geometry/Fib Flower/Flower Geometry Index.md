# Flower Geometry Index

The Flower Geometry Index is the master reference for all geometric structures inside the Fib Flower. It organizes the spatial, dynamic, and pressure‑driven components that define how nodes move, stabilize, collapse, and migrate. Every geometric subsystem — slit, frontier, corridors, layers, and migration channels — originates from this index.

## Geometry Overview
Flower geometry is composed of:
- orbit layers
- slit structures
- pressure gradients
- drift corridors
- frontier instability zones
- migration channels
- collapse fields
- resonance fields

These components form the spatial anatomy of Threshold.

## Core Geometry Components

### Orbit Layers
The concentric structure of the Flower.  
Defines stability, resonance density, and drift velocity.

### Slit Geometry
The turbulence core of the Flower.  
Includes slit mechanics, pressure gradients, and flow dynamics.

### Drift Corridors
Movement pathways carved by pressure and resonance.  
Determine how nodes travel through the Flower.

### Frontier 5–8
The instability band.  
Low stability margins, high drift volatility, and collapse amplification.

### Migration Geometry
Channels and thresholds that determine escape routes.  
Includes migration rules and migration thresholds.

### Collapse Geometry
Fracture lines, void zones, and collapse corridors.  
Defines how the Flower fails and how collapse-born nodes move.

### Resonance Geometry
Tone density, harmonic pathways, and interference zones.  
Shows how resonance shapes stability and movement.

## Geometry Interactions

### Pressure ↔ Drift
Pressure gradients carve drift corridors and bend flow lines.

### Slit ↔ Frontier
Slit turbulence amplifies frontier instability.

### Collapse ↔ Resonance
Collapse fractures distort resonance fields and create void zones.

### Drift ↔ Migration
High‑velocity corridors become migration channels when thresholds are crossed.

### Chambers ↔ Geometry
Ethos, Collapse, Drift, and Anchor chambers tint geometry through deltas.

## Why It Matters
The Flower Geometry Index reveals:
- how the Flower is shaped  
- how movement pathways form  
- where instability originates  
- how collapse spreads  
- how migration channels emerge  
- how resonance stabilizes or fractures  
- how chambers influence geometry  

This index is the map of the Flower’s body — the structure beneath all motion and behavior.
