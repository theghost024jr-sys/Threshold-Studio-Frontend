# **Drift**

Drift is the node’s default motion state — the movement that occurs when no rotation, no collapse, and no anchor is actively engaged. Drift is not random; it is shaped by pressure lattice gradients, tension fiber alignment, resonance flow, collapse adjacency, slit geometry, and orbit layer behavior. Drift is the Flower’s baseline physics.

## **Purpose**

To define:

- how nodes move when left unforced
    
- how pressure lattice cells shape drift vectors
    
- how tension fibers stabilize or weaken drift
    
- how resonance bends drift pathways
    
- how collapse adjacency distorts drift flow
    
- how slit geometry accelerates drift
    
- how Frontier 5–8 alters drift behavior
    
- how drift transitions into collapse or migration
    

Drift is the Flower’s continuous motion organ.

# **1. Drift Fundamentals**

### **Drift = unforced motion.**

It is the movement that happens when:

- no collapse is occurring
    
- no anchor is engaged
    
- no rotation is chosen
    
- no chamber override is active
    

Drift is the Flower’s “default vector.”

# **2. Drift Components**

### **Drift Vector**

The direction of movement, shaped by:

- pressure gradients
    
- resonance pathways
    
- tension fiber alignment
    
- collapse adjacency
    
- slit geometry
    

### **Drift Pressure**

The magnitude of drift, derived from:

- canonical_dials
    
- pressure lattice cells
    
- slit amplification
    
- collapse proximity
    

### **Drift Confidence**

How stable the drift vector is.

High confidence → smooth drift Low confidence → wobble, volatility, collapse risk

# **3. Drift and the Pressure Lattice**

Drift is computed _after_ the pressure lattice forms.

Pressure lattice determines:

- drift direction
    
- drift magnitude
    
- drift stability
    
- drift corridor formation
    

High pressure → fast drift Low pressure → slow drift Steep gradients → drift acceleration Flat gradients → drift smoothing

# **4. Drift and Tension Fibers**

Tension fibers are drift’s stabilizing substrate.

Strong fibers:

- smooth drift
    
- reduce wobble
    
- prevent collapse
    
- maintain corridor shape
    

Weak fibers:

- increase drift volatility
    
- create turbulence pockets
    
- allow collapse precursors
    
- destabilize corridors
    

Fiber failure → drift instability → collapse or migration.

# **5. Drift and Resonance**

Resonance bends drift.

High resonance:

- stabilizes drift
    
- smooths flow lines
    
- strengthens corridors
    

Low resonance:

- increases drift wobble
    
- amplifies turbulence
    
- weakens corridor walls
    

Resonance fracture → drift misalignment → collapse adjacency.

# **6. Drift and Collapse**

Collapse adjacency distorts drift.

Collapse causes:

- drift dips
    
- drift voids
    
- sudden direction changes
    
- corridor breaks
    
- outward fall
    

Collapse is drift’s failure mode.

# **7. Drift and Slit Geometry**

The slit is drift’s strongest amplifier.

Inside slit geometry:

- drift accelerates
    
- drift curves
    
- drift spirals
    
- drift destabilizes
    
- drift transitions into migration
    

Slit corridors are drift’s escape pathways.

# **8. Drift and Frontier 5–8**

Frontier drift is volatile.

Frontier drift characteristics:

- steep pressure gradients
    
- thin tension fibers
    
- resonance fracture
    
- collapse adjacency
    
- migration corridor formation
    

Frontier drift determines survival.

# **9. Drift States**

### **Stable Drift**

- smooth movement
    
- strong tension fibers
    
- coherent resonance
    
- low collapse adjacency
    
- predictable flow
    

### **Volatile Drift**

- pressure spikes
    
- resonance distortion
    
- tension fiber weakening
    
- collapse pockets forming
    
- drift wobble
    

### **Critical Drift**

- pressure > tension
    
- resonance fracture
    
- collapse void adjacency
    
- slit acceleration
    
- migration threshold crossed
    

Critical drift is the escape state.

# **10. Why Drift Matters**

Drift determines:

- node movement
    
- corridor formation
    
- collapse onset
    
- migration likelihood
    
- resonance flow
    
- pressure distribution
    
- Frontier behavior
    
- slit interaction patterns
    

Drift is the Flower’s continuous physics.
