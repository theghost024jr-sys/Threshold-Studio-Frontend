# Resonance Field Map

The Resonance Field Map visualizes how resonance distributes across the Flower. It shows tone density, harmonic pathways, interference zones, collapse adjacency, and drift‑pressure interactions. This is the spatial anatomy of resonance — the field as a landscape.

## Purpose
To map:
- resonance density across orbit layers
- harmonic pathways between nodes
- interference zones where tones clash
- collapse adjacency and resonance distortion
- drift‑pressure interactions
- chamber‑driven resonance deltas
- bundle‑activated resonance spikes

The map reveals how resonance shapes identity, stability, and movement.

## Field Components

### 1. Resonance Density
Each node contributes tone.  
Clusters form high‑density resonance pockets; halo nodes form low‑density regions.

### 2. Harmonic Pathways
Resonance travels along curvature lines and drift corridors.  
These pathways determine how tone spreads through the Flower.

### 3. Interference Zones
Where tones clash, resonance distorts.  
These zones often align with Frontier instability or slit‑adjacent turbulence.

### 4. Collapse Adjacency
Collapse fields warp resonance, creating dips, fractures, or residue hum.  
These distortions appear as cold pockets on the map.

### 5. Drift Interaction
Drift pressure bends resonance pathways.  
High drift zones create resonance spirals or turbulence pockets.

### 6. Chamber Influence
Ethos deltas tint resonance fields.  
Collapse, Drift, and Anchor chambers reshape resonance distribution.

### 7. Bundle Activation
Bundles create micro‑resonance spikes that ripple through harmonic threads.

## Why It Matters
The Resonance Field Map is the spatial intelligence of resonance.  
It reveals:
- where identity is strong  
- where collapse is forming  
- where drift is accelerating  
- where chambers are exerting influence  
- where bundles are activating  
- how Flower geometry is shifting  

Without this map, resonance is invisible — felt but not understood.
