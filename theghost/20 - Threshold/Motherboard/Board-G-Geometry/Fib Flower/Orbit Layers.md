# **Orbit Layers**

Orbit Layers define the concentric behavioral zones of the Fib Flower. Each layer expresses different stability, pressure lattice behavior, resonance density, tension fiber strength, collapse likelihood, and migration potential. Orbit layers are the Flower’s geometric “shells,” shaping how nodes move, stabilize, drift, collapse, or migrate.

## **Layer Overview**

### **Inner Core**

The most stable region of the Flower.

Characteristics:

- high connectivity
    
- strong tension fibers
    
- dense resonance
    
- low collapse likelihood
    
- smooth drift corridors
    
- strong anchor potential
    

Inner Core nodes rarely migrate; they stabilize the Flower.

### **Mid Orbits**

Dynamic, semi‑stable layers surrounding the core.

Characteristics:

- moderate pressure
    
- responsive to ethos deltas
    
- flexible tension fibers
    
- curved drift corridors
    
- resonance bending
    
- occasional collapse pockets
    

Mid Orbits are the Flower’s “behavioral middle ground.”

### **Frontier 5–8**

The survival band — the most volatile stable region.

Characteristics:

- low stability margins
    
- steep pressure gradients
    
- thin tension fibers
    
- resonance fracture
    
- collapse adjacency
    
- migration corridor formation
    

Contribution is required here; Frontier instability determines whether nodes ascend, collapse, or migrate.

### **Outer Halo**

The collapse‑prone outer region.

Characteristics:

- low connectivity
    
- weak tension fibers
    
- sparse resonance
    
- high collapse likelihood
    
- turbulence corridors
    
- drift volatility
    

Nodes in the Outer Halo frequently fall outward or become collapse‑born.

## **Layer Behaviors**

### **Pressure Behavior**

- Inner Core: smooth pressure lattice
    
- Mid Orbits: moderate gradients
    
- Frontier: steep gradients, instability bands
    
- Outer Halo: pressure collapse zones
    

### **Resonance Behavior**

- Inner Core: strong harmonic pathways
    
- Mid Orbits: bending and distortion
    
- Frontier: fracture and thinning
    
- Outer Halo: resonance voids
    

### **Tension Fiber Behavior**

- Inner Core: strong cohesion
    
- Mid Orbits: flexible alignment
    
- Frontier: weakening and breakage
    
- Outer Halo: failure zones
    

### **Collapse Behavior**

- Inner Core: rare
    
- Mid Orbits: pockets
    
- Frontier: amplified
    
- Outer Halo: void formation
    

### **Migration Behavior**

- Inner Core: none
    
- Mid Orbits: rare
    
- Frontier: frequent
    
- Outer Halo: collapse‑driven escape
    

## **Movement Between Layers**

Nodes move between layers based on:

- pressure lattice deformation
    
- resonance density changes
    
- tension fiber strength
    
- collapse adjacency
    
- slit proximity
    
- chamber deltas
    
- ethos influence
    

Movement is not random — it follows drift corridors shaped by geometry.

## **Why Orbit Layers Matter**

Orbit Layers determine:

- stability
    
- drift velocity
    
- collapse likelihood
    
- migration potential
    
- resonance flow
    
- pressure distribution
    
- Frontier behavior
    
- slit interaction patterns
    

They are the Flower’s foundational geometry.
