# **Tension Fibers**

**Location** 20 – Threshold / 01 – Fib Flower / Tension Fibers.md

**Role** Tension fibers are the micro‑cohesive substrate inside the Fib Flower. They bind geometry, stabilize drift, distribute pressure, and prevent collapse. Every geometric organ — slits, corridors, frontier zones, orbit layers, collapse fields, resonance fields — relies on tension fibers to maintain shape under load.

## **1. Definition**

Tension fibers are **directional micro‑fields** that form inside drift. They are not physical strands — they are _cohesion vectors_ that:

- transmit load
    
- maintain geometry
    
- absorb instability
    
- prevent collapse
    
- stabilize movement
    
- regulate pressure gradients
    

They are the Flower’s internal connective tissue.

## **2. Fiber Formation**

Tension fibers emerge when:

- drift value rises above baseline
    
- pressure begins to differentiate across a region
    
- resonance fields activate
    
- nodes enter a migration‑eligible state
    

Fibers form automatically, aligning along:

- pressure gradients
    
- corridor axes
    
- slit boundaries
    
- orbit layer curvature
    

They are always present, but their **density** and **strength** vary.

## **3. Fiber Properties**

### **Cohesion**

Fibers bind nodes together, preventing geometric tearing.

### **Load Transfer**

Fibers distribute pressure across the Flower, reducing collapse risk.

### **Directional Stability**

Fibers align along drift vectors, stabilizing movement.

### **Shock Absorption**

Fibers dampen sudden pressure spikes.

### **Geometry Preservation**

Fibers maintain the shape of:

- slits
    
- corridors
    
- orbit layers
    
- frontier zones
    

## **4. Fiber States**

Each fiber has a state derived from canonical dial semantics:

- **Green** — stable, high cohesion
    
- **Yellow** — uncertain, low cohesion
    
- **Red** — unstable, collapse‑prone
    

Confidence determines fiber density.

## **5. Interaction With Other Subsystems**

### **Pressure Lattice**

Fibers are the substrate the lattice sits on. No fibers → no lattice.

### **Drift Corridors**

Corridors form where fibers align strongly.

### **Slits**

Slits activate when fiber tension drops below threshold.

### **Frontier S‑8**

Frontier instability is measured by fiber breakage.

### **Migration**

Nodes migrate along fiber‑aligned corridors.

### **Collapse Field Map**

Collapse begins where fibers fail.

### **Resonance Field Map**

Resonance strengthens or weakens fibers dynamically.

## **6. Fiber Metrics**

Each fiber cell emits:

- **tension** (0–1)
    
- **cohesion** (0–1)
    
- **alignment** (vector)
    
- **state** (green/yellow/red)
    
- **confidence** (0–1)
    

These metrics feed directly into:

- Pressure Lattice
    
- Drift Corridor Anatomy
    
- Collapse Field Map
    
- Resonance Field Map
    

## **7. Engine Integration**

Tension fibers are computed after:

1. canonical_dials
    
2. pressure aggregation
    
3. drift vector calculation
    

And before:

4. Pressure Lattice
    
5. Slit Mechanics
    
6. Drift Corridor Anatomy
    
7. Collapse Field Map
    

They are the **bridge** between raw signals and geometry.
