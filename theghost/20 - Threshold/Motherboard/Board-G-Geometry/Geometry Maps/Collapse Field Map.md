# **Collapse Field Map**

The Collapse Field Map visualizes how collapse forms, spreads, and distorts Flower geometry. Collapse is not a single event — it is a dynamic field produced when pressure lattice cells exceed tension fiber capacity. Collapse creates gradients, fractures, voids, residue zones, and directional pathways that reshape drift, resonance, and migration behavior.

Collapse is the Flower’s failure mode — this map makes it readable.

## **Purpose**

To map:

- collapse density across lattice cells and orbit layers
    
- tension fiber failure patterns
    
- fracture lines and collapse pathways
    
- collapse‑born node trajectories
    
- void zones where geometry fails
    
- collapse residue fields
    
- slit‑driven collapse amplification
    
- frontier instability amplification
    
- chamber‑driven collapse deltas
    

The Collapse Field Map reveals how collapse reshapes the Flower.

## **Field Components**

### **1. Collapse Density**

Collapse density measures how many lattice cells have entered red state due to pressure > tension.

High‑density zones:

- weaken geometry
    
- accelerate drift
    
- distort resonance
    
- increase migration likelihood
    

Density is the “heatmap” of Flower failure.

### **2. Fracture Lines**

Collapse spreads along fracture lines — geometric weak points where:

- tension fibers are thin
    
- drift vectors converge
    
- resonance fields distort
    
- pressure gradients steepen
    

Fracture lines often align with:

- drift corridors
    
- slit turbulence pockets
    
- frontier instability zones
    

They are the highways of collapse propagation.

### **3. Collapse Voids**

Voids are areas where stability fully fails.

Inside voids:

- tension fibers break
    
- pressure lattice collapses
    
- drift vectors lose coherence
    
- resonance fields fall silent
    

Nodes entering voids:

- fall outward
    
- become collapse‑born
    
- or enter migration channels
    

Voids are the deepest collapse structures.

### **4. Residue Fields**

After collapse, residue fields remain.

Residue fields:

- distort resonance
    
- weaken drift stability
    
- create long‑term instability
    
- tint future pressure gradients
    

Residue is collapse’s “scar tissue.”

### **5. Collapse Corridors**

Collapse forms directional pathways that guide collapse‑born nodes outward.

Corridors emerge when:

- fracture lines align
    
- drift vectors stabilize
    
- pressure drops after void formation
    

These corridors often become **migration precursors**.

### **6. Frontier Collapse Amplification**

Frontier 5–8 amplifies collapse.

Low stability margins cause:

- rapid tension fiber failure
    
- steep pressure gradients
    
- resonance fracture
    
- collapse corridor formation
    

Frontier collapse is volatile and often catastrophic.

### **7. Slit Collapse Interaction**

Collapse near the slit becomes turbulent.

Slit interaction causes:

- pressure amplification
    
- drift acceleration
    
- resonance distortion
    
- collapse void formation
    
- unpredictable node movement
    

The slit is the strongest collapse amplifier in the Flower.

### **8. Chamber Influence**

Chamber deltas tint collapse fields.

Ethos deltas can:

- stabilize collapse zones
    
- destabilize collapse zones
    
- redirect collapse pathways
    
- amplify collapse corridors
    

Chambers are collapse’s “coloring agents.”

## **Field States**

### **Stable Field**

- collapse minimal
    
- tension fibers strong
    
- resonance dominant
    
- drift predictable
    
- pressure smooth
    

### **Volatile Field**

- collapse pockets forming
    
- fracture lines activating
    
- drift accelerating
    
- resonance distorting
    
- pressure spiking
    

### **Critical Field**

- collapse corridors open
    
- voids expanding
    
- nodes falling outward
    
- migration pathways forming
    
- geometry failing
    

Critical collapse is the escape state.

## **Why It Matters**

The Collapse Field Map reveals:

- where collapse is forming
    
- how collapse spreads
    
- how Flower geometry fractures
    
- how drift and pressure respond
    
- how resonance is distorted
    
- how chambers influence collapse
    
- how Basin telemetry detects collapse onset
    

Collapse is the Flower’s failure mode — this map makes it readable.
