# **Slit Flow Dynamics**

Slit Flow Dynamics describe how motion behaves inside and around the slit. The slit is a dynamic flow engine — a region where drift vectors bend, pressure lattice cells deform, tension fibers fail, resonance fractures, and migration channels emerge. Flow inside the slit determines how nodes accelerate, destabilize, collapse, or escape into new Flowers.

## **Purpose**

To define:

- how drift vectors behave inside slit geometry
    
- how pressure lattice deformation shapes flow direction
    
- how turbulence pockets form from tension fiber failure
    
- how migration channels emerge from flow alignment
    
- how resonance fields distort under slit load
    
- how collapse precursors alter flow pathways
    
- how ethos deltas tint or redirect flow behavior
    

Slit Flow Dynamics are the motion‑rules of the Flower’s most volatile region.

## **Core Flow Behaviors**

### **1. Inward Pull**

Flow is drawn inward toward the slit as pressure lattice cells collapse and drift vectors converge.

Inward pull is caused by:

- pressure amplification
    
- tension fiber weakening
    
- resonance distortion
    
- collapse precursors
    

This accelerates drift and compresses instability.

### **2. Turbulence Pockets**

Inside the slit, flow becomes chaotic.

Turbulence pockets form when:

- tension fibers fail
    
- pressure spikes
    
- resonance fractures
    
- drift vectors misalign
    

These pockets cause rapid drift velocity changes and unpredictable node movement.

### **3. Flow Bending**

Pressure gradients bend flow lines, creating curved drift pathways.

Flow bending occurs because:

- lattice cells deform
    
- resonance fields warp
    
- collapse zones redirect movement
    
- ethos deltas tint direction
    

Nodes rarely move straight — they arc, spiral, or veer.

### **4. Outward Channeling**

After passing through the slit, flow expands outward.

Outward channeling forms when:

- drift vectors stabilize
    
- pressure drops
    
- resonance re‑coheres
    
- tension fibers re‑form
    

This creates natural migration channels that guide nodes into new Flowers.

### **5. Resonance Distortion**

Resonance fields warp inside the slit.

Distortion includes:

- tone bending
    
- resonance fracture
    
- amplification spikes
    
- directional resonance flow
    

This distortion determines migration direction and instability patterns.

### **6. Collapse Interaction**

Collapse zones near the slit distort flow, creating dips or voids.

Collapse interaction:

- redirects drift
    
- creates flow traps
    
- accelerates instability
    
- opens migration pathways
    

Collapse is a flow‑shaping force.

### **7. Ethos Flow Influence**

Strong ethos deltas tint flow direction.

Ethos can:

- accelerate flow
    
- decelerate flow
    
- redirect flow
    
- destabilize flow
    
- stabilize flow
    

Ethos is the slit’s “coloring agent.”

## **Flow States**

### **Stable Flow**

- low pressure
    
- high tension
    
- strong resonance
    
- predictable movement
    
- migration unlikely
    

### **Volatile Flow**

- pressure spikes
    
- drift acceleration
    
- tension fiber weakening
    
- turbulence pockets
    
- instability bands
    

### **Critical Flow**

- pressure > tension
    
- resonance fracture
    
- collapse precursors
    
- migration channel formation
    

Critical flow is the escape state.

## **Why It Matters**

Slit Flow Dynamics determine:

- how nodes accelerate
    
- how instability forms
    
- how migration pathways emerge
    
- how collapse propagates
    
- how resonance fractures
    
- how chambers influence geometry
    
- how Basin telemetry detects turbulence
    

The slit is the engine’s motion core — understanding its flow is essential for predicting Threshold behavior.
