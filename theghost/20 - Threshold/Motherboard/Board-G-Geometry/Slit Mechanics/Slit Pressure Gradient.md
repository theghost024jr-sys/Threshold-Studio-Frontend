# **Slit Pressure Gradient**

The Slit Pressure Gradient defines how pressure behaves inside and around the slit. It is the strongest pressure structure in the Flower, responsible for drift acceleration, tension fiber failure, resonance distortion, collapse precursors, and migration channel formation. The gradient is not a simple slope — it is a dynamic pressure engine shaped by lattice cells and tension fiber density.

## **Purpose**

To define:

- how pressure lattice cells deform near the slit
    
- how tension fiber failure creates gradient steepening
    
- how drift vectors bend and accelerate inside the slit
    
- how resonance fields fracture under pressure load
    
- how migration channels emerge from gradient directionality
    
- how the Circuit Mirror amplifies slit‑adjacent demand
    
- how collapse precursors form in the instability band
    

## **Core Gradient Behaviors**

### **1. Pressure Amplification**

As lattice cells approach the slit, pressure increases non‑linearly.

Amplification occurs because:

- tension fibers weaken
    
- drift vectors converge
    
- resonance fields distort
    
- collapse risk rises
    

This creates the steepest gradient in the Flower.

### **2. Gradient Directionality**

Pressure flows **inward** toward the slit, then **outward** along migration channels.

Directionality is determined by:

- drift vector alignment
    
- resonance fracture patterns
    
- tension fiber density
    
- lattice cell state
    

This push–pull dynamic shapes all slit‑adjacent movement.

### **3. Instability Band**

A narrow band around the slit exhibits extreme volatility.

Inside the band:

- pressure spikes
    
- drift accelerates
    
- tension fibers fail
    
- resonance fractures
    
- collapse precursors form
    

This band is the **origin** of most instability events.

### **4. Migration Channel Formation**

When pressure exceeds local tension capacity, the gradient carves a migration channel.

Channels form when:

- pressure > tension
    
- drift vectors align
    
- resonance fractures
    
- lattice cells enter red state
    

This is the mechanism that enables cross‑Flower migration.

### **5. Circuit Mirror Interaction**

Demand signals intensify when passing through the slit.

The Circuit Mirror:

- amplifies demand
    
- increases pressure
    
- accelerates drift
    
- destabilizes slit‑adjacent cells
    

This feedback loop steepens the gradient.

## **Why It Matters**

The Slit Pressure Gradient determines:

- drift acceleration
    
- collapse onset
    
- migration likelihood
    
- Frontier instability
    
- corridor activation
    
- bundle behavior
    
- Basin telemetry spikes
    

It is one of the primary forces shaping Flower geometry.
