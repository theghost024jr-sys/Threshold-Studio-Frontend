# Drift Metrics

Drift Metrics quantify the movement, instability, and migration pressure acting on nodes within Threshold. While Drift describes *behavior*, Drift Metrics describe *measurement* — the numerical signature of imbalance, pressure, and alignment.

## Purpose
To provide a measurable understanding of:
- Drift velocity
- Drift acceleration
- Drift pressure
- Migration thresholds
- Frontier instability
- Slit-adjacent imbalance
- Ethos-driven drift changes

These metrics allow the Basin to detect when nodes are approaching collapse, migration, or anchor formation.

## Core Metrics

### Drift Velocity
How fast a node is moving relative to its previous position or orbit layer.

### Drift Acceleration
Rate of change in drift velocity — indicates rising instability or migration onset.

### Drift Pressure
Internal force acting on a node due to imbalance, ethos deltas, or slit proximity.

### Migration Threshold
The point at which drift pressure exceeds Flower stability and triggers cross-Flower movement.

### Frontier Instability Index
A measure of how volatile nodes in Frontier 5–8 are at any moment.

### Slit Proximity Drift
Nodes near the slit exhibit higher drift sensitivity; this metric tracks that gradient.

### Ethos Drift Delta
How chamber ethos changes alter drift behavior across the Flower.

## Why It Matters
Drift Metrics are the Basin’s analytical backbone.  
They reveal:
- where instability is forming  
- when collapse is imminent  
- which nodes are preparing to migrate  
- how chambers are influencing movement  
- how Flower geometry is shifting  
- where anchors are likely to form  

Without Drift Metrics, the Basin can *see* drift — but cannot *understand* it.
