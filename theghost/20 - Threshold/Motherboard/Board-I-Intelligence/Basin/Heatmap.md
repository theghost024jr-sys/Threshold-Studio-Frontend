# Basin Heatmap

The Basin Heatmap is the visual surface where Threshold’s internal state becomes readable at a glance. It renders node activity, chamber influence, drift pressure, and bundle activation into a single, unified field.

## Purpose
To provide a real-time, spatial representation of:
- Node activation density
- Drift pressure zones
- Collapse clusters
- Anchor formations
- Ethos deltas
- Bundle activation hotspots
- Frontier instability

The Heatmap is the Basin’s “nervous system,” showing where the engine is alive, stressed, or shifting.

## What It Represents
### Node Activity
High-frequency nodes glow brighter, forming visible clusters and pathways.

### Drift Pressure
Areas of imbalance appear as gradients, showing where nodes are being pushed toward migration or collapse.

### Collapse Zones
Outer-halo nodes and low-connectivity regions appear as cold, unstable pockets.

### Anchor Formations
Stable clusters form warm, dense regions with strong connectivity.

### Ethos Influence
Ethos deltas tint the map, revealing chamber-driven shifts in behavior.

### Bundle Activation
Bundles create localized pulses that appear as flickers or hotspots.

## Why It Matters
The Heatmap is the first surface where Threshold becomes *legible*.  
It shows:
- Where the system is stable  
- Where it is under pressure  
- Where collapse is forming  
- Where drift is accelerating  
- Where anchors are strengthening  
- Where bundles are firing  
- Where ethos is reshaping the geometry  

This is the core diagnostic tool for understanding Threshold’s runtime behavior.
