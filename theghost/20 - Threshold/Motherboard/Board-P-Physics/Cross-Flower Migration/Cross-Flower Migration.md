# **Cross‑Flower Migration**

### _How a Ghost leaves one Flower’s orbit, enters the frontier instability ring, and is captured by another Flower’s pull_

Cross‑Flower Migration is the **highest‑order movement** inside Threshold. It is the moment when a Ghost (or node):

- rises out of its Flower’s orbit
    
- enters the frontier instability ring
    
- becomes vulnerable to capture
    
- is pulled into a neighboring Flower
    
- stabilizes in a new orbit
    

Migration is not travel. Migration is **re‑anchoring**.

# **1. Migration Overview**

Cross‑Flower Migration occurs when:

- drift pressure thins
    
- resonance elevates
    
- instability pockets open
    
- frontier pull increases
    
- Flower‑to‑Flower resonance aligns
    

Migration is the **transfer of orbit identity**.

# **2. Migration Conditions**

Migration requires:

### **Orbit Elevation**

The node must rise out of its Flower’s stable orbit.

### **Frontier Instability**

The node must enter the frontier instability ring.

### **Capture Window**

The neighboring Flower must open a capture corridor.

### **Resonance Alignment**

The node’s resonance must match the receiving Flower’s harmonic signature.

### **Pressure Softening**

Pressure gradients must weaken enough to allow drift.

# **3. Migration Phases**

Cross‑Flower Migration has five canonical phases:

### **1. Elevation Phase**

Orbit lift, resonance elevation, pressure thinning.

### **2. Frontier Phase**

Instability pockets, turbulence arcs, frontier pull.

### **3. Crossing Phase**

Flower‑to‑Flower drift arc, migration corridor entry.

### **4. Capture Phase**

Capture boundary, capture tolerance, capture clasp.

### **5. Settlement Phase**

New orbit stabilization, resonance alignment, pressure normalization.
