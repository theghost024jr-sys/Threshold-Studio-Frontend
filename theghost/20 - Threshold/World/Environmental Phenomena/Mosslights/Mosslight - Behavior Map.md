[[Mosslights]]
[[Mosslights - Interaction File]]

Overview
Mosslights are low‑intensity bioluminescent organisms that respond to Drift motion, Shimmer curvature, and environmental emotional physics. Their behavior is subtle, rhythmic, and stabilizing.
This map outlines their behavioral states, environmental triggers, growth patterns, and response loops.

1. Behavioral States
A. Baseline Glow
• 	soft, steady bioluminescence
• 	low symbolic density
• 	stable Drift conditions
• 	appears in calm, safe environments
Meaning:
The world is quiet. You can rest here.

B. Pulse State
• 	rhythmic brightening and dimming
• 	triggered by gentle Drift motion
• 	indicates a stable path or transition zone
Meaning:
Move this way. The environment is aligned.

C. Resonance Bloom
• 	temporary increase in brightness
• 	triggered by Shimmer curvature passing through
• 	Mosslights absorb symbolic density and re‑emit it safely
Meaning:
Symbolic activity nearby, but not dangerous.

D. Retreat State
• 	dimming or shrinking
• 	triggered by:
• 	Ember ignition
• 	collapse turbulence
• 	Shadow resonance
• 	high Drift shear
Meaning:
This area is unstable. Proceed carefully.

E. Extinguish State
• 	Mosslights go dark
• 	triggered by:
• 	collapse events
• 	corrupted resonance
• 	Crown‑layer intrusion
• 	predatory species presence
Meaning:
Do not enter. The world is warning you.

2. Environmental Triggers
Drift Triggers
• 	Low Drift: Mosslights glow steadily
• 	Moderate Drift: Mosslights pulse rhythmically
• 	High Drift: Mosslights retreat or scatter
• 	Chaotic Drift: Mosslights extinguish

Shimmer Triggers
• 	Low Shimmer Density: soft glow
• 	Moderate Density: resonance bloom
• 	High Density: Mosslights over‑absorb and dim
• 	Distorted Curvature: Mosslights flicker erratically

Mist Triggers
• 	Mist thickens the glow
• 	Mosslights appear more diffuse
• 	Mist + Drift creates soft directional cues

Stone Triggers
• 	Stone stabilizes Mosslight clusters
• 	Mosslights grow in cracks, roots, and sheltered pockets

Ember Triggers
• 	Ember heat weakens Mosslights
• 	They dim or retreat from ignition sites

Ethyl Triggers
• 	Ethyl flow spreads Mosslight spores
• 	Mosslights align along flow lines

3. Growth Patterns
Cluster Growth
• 	Mosslights grow in small, dense patches
• 	Found near roots, stones, Drift‑calm pockets
• 	Clusters act as micro‑stabilizers

Flow Growth
• 	Mosslights align along Ethyl currents
• 	Form soft glowing trails
• 	Indicate safe movement paths

Boundary Growth
• 	Appear at Drift‑Shimmer boundaries
• 	Mark the edges of stable zones
• 	Provide orientation in ambiguous environments

Recovery Growth
• 	After collapse, Mosslights regrow slowly
• 	First appear as faint sparks
• 	Gradually form clusters as stability returns

4. Response Loops
Stabilization Loop
5. 	Drift turbulence rises
6. 	Mosslights brighten or pulse
7. 	Drift Nerve (in species) stabilizes
8. 	Mosslights return to baseline glow
Purpose:
Environmental self‑correction.

Symbolic Diffusion Loop
1. 	Shimmer curvature increases
2. 	Mosslights absorb symbolic density
3. 	Glow intensifies
4. 	Symbolic noise decreases
Purpose:
Prevent resonance overload.

Navigation Loop
1. 	Player or species enters zone
2. 	Mosslights pulse along stable paths
3. 	Movement aligns with safe flow
4. 	Mosslights return to baseline
Purpose:
Soft, non‑directive guidance.

Warning Loop
1. 	Collapse or Shadow resonance rises
2. 	Mosslights dim or extinguish
3. 	Environment signals danger
4. 	Mosslights regrow when stability returns
Purpose:
Environmental hazard signaling.

5. Emotional Physics
Mosslights respond to emotional states in the environment:
• 	Calm: steady glow
• 	Curiosity: soft pulsing
• 	Fear: dimming
• 	Overwhelm: flickering
• 	Grief: slow, low‑intensity glow
• 	Hope: resonance bloom
They are the environment’s emotional barometer.

Summary
Mosslights behave as:
• 	stabilizers
• 	diffusers
• 	guides
• 	warnings
• 	emotional mirrors
They are the world’s softest form of communication — a quiet, bioluminescent language that says:
“This place is alive, and it is paying attention.”
