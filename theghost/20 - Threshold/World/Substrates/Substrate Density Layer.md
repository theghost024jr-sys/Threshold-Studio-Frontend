Layer Name
Substrate Density Layer
Layer Type
Deep Structural Map / Foundational System Layer
Purpose
The Substrate Density Layer defines the deep, non-visible architecture of Threshold.
It maps how mythic substrate accumulates, compresses, fractures, and resonates beneath all other layers.
This layer governs:
• 	collapse susceptibility
• 	mythic discharge likelihood
• 	residue formation
• 	boundary instability
• 	emotional weather amplification
• 	actor-phase visibility
Substrate density is the root condition from which all higher-layer phenomena emerge.

1. Substrate Strata
The substrate is organized into three primary strata:
A. Deep Substrate
• 	highest density
• 	slowest movement
• 	stores long-term symbolic pressure
• 	source of substrate pulses
• 	rarely interacts with surface layers
B. Mid Substrate
• 	medium density
• 	active resonance zone
• 	prone to symbolic shear
• 	interacts with collapse fronts
• 	generates substrate fault lines
C. Surface Substrate
• 	lowest density
• 	most reactive
• 	interacts with emotional weather
• 	produces mythic blooms
• 	sensitive to boundary instability

2. Density Gradients
Substrate density varies across space, forming gradients that influence system behavior.
High-Density Zones
• 	strong mythic charge
• 	high collapse susceptibility
• 	increased discharge events
• 	unstable boundaries
Medium-Density Zones
• 	active resonance
• 	moderate collapse risk
• 	stable symbolic behavior
Low-Density Zones
• 	quiet substrate
• 	minimal mythic activity
• 	low collapse risk

3. Fault Lines
Substrate density differences create fault lines, which act as structural seams.
Fault line behaviors:
• 	symbolic shear
• 	resonance amplification
• 	collapse propagation
• 	boundary fracture
Fault lines are the primary predictors of large-scale instability.

4. Density Inversions
A density inversion occurs when lower strata become less dense than upper strata.
Effects:
• 	substrate pulses
• 	mythic discharge
• 	collapse front formation
• 	residue turbulence
Density inversions are rare but highly volatile.

5. Resonance Pockets
Localized zones where substrate density amplifies symbolic or emotional charge.
Characteristics:
• 	high symbolic density
• 	increased mythic bloom likelihood
• 	emotional turbulence amplification
• 	actor-phase visibility increases
Resonance pockets are often mistaken for mythic events.

6. Substrate–Collapse Coupling
Substrate density directly influences collapse behavior.
High Density → High Collapse Susceptibility
• 	collapse fronts form easily
• 	collapse vectors strengthen
• 	vortex formation more likely
Low Density → Collapse Resistance
• 	collapse dissipates quickly
• 	vectors weaken
• 	residue remains minimal

7. Substrate–Boundary Interaction
Boundary instability increases when substrate density is uneven.
Effects:
• 	threshold flashes
• 	liminal arcs
• 	edge pulses
• 	role-boundary fractures
Boundary behavior is a surface expression of deep substrate tension.
