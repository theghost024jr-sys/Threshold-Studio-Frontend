Overview
Threshold Weather Navigation is the discipline of moving through the world by reading and responding to emotional‑symbolic weather systems.
These weather systems are not atmospheric — they are made of:
• 	Drift turbulence
• 	Shimmer curvature
• 	Mist density
• 	Stone resonance
• 	Ember ignition fields
• 	Ethyl flow
• 	Heat zones
• 	Collapse pressure
Threshold Weather Navigation teaches how to interpret these forces and move safely, efficiently, and meaningfully through them.
It is the practical counterpart to Whisper Cartography.

Purpose
Threshold Weather Navigation exists to:
• 	avoid collapse zones
• 	find stable paths
• 	interpret environmental signals
• 	navigate emotional physics
• 	read symbolic density
• 	track Drift and Shimmer interactions
• 	understand narrative pressure
• 	move through Heat zones safely
It is the survival skill of Threshold.

Navigation Techniques
1. Drift Riding
Using Drift currents to move efficiently.
Requires reading turbulence and spiral direction.
2. Shimmer Shielding
Positioning oneself to avoid symbolic overload.
Uses Mist, Stone, or low‑density zones as buffers.
3. Heat Crossing
Moving through emotionally intense zones without collapse.
Requires grounding and resonance control.
4. Collapse Avoidance
Reading collapse fronts early and rerouting.
Uses Warden‑type signals and Stone echoes.
5. Flow Following
Using Ethyl currents to travel along coherent paths.
Often paired with Whisper Cartography.
6. Veil Reading
Interpreting Mist density to find hidden transitions.
Useful for liminal zones and soft thresholds.
