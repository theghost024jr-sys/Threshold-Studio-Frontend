Map Name
Substrate Density Map
Map Type
Deep Structural Density Map / System-Level Reference
Purpose
This map visualizes the distribution, gradients, and structural features of mythic substrate across Threshold.
It is the foundational diagnostic map for interpreting collapse behavior, discharge likelihood, residue formation, and boundary instability.
The Substrate Density Map is non-geographic.
It represents conditions, not places.

1. Density Zones
Substrate density is divided into three primary zones:
A. High-Density Zones
Characteristics:
• 	strong mythic charge
• 	high collapse susceptibility
• 	frequent discharge events
• 	unstable boundaries
• 	deep resonance pockets
Indicators:
• 	symbolic overload
• 	substrate pulses
• 	fault line clustering

B. Medium-Density Zones
Characteristics:
• 	active resonance
• 	moderate collapse risk
• 	stable symbolic behavior
• 	predictable discharge patterns
Indicators:
• 	mild symbolic shear
• 	occasional resonance amplification

C. Low-Density Zones
Characteristics:
• 	quiet substrate
• 	minimal mythic activity
• 	low collapse risk
• 	stable boundaries
Indicators:
• 	absence of residue
• 	low emotional turbulence

2. Density Gradients
Density transitions form gradients that influence system behavior.
Gradient Types
• 	Steep Gradients: high instability, fault line formation
• 	Shallow Gradients: stable transitions, low turbulence
• 	Inverted Gradients: density inversion, volatile behavior
Gradients determine how substrate moves, not where it is.

3. Fault Lines
Fault lines are structural seams where density differences accumulate tension.
Fault Line Behaviors
• 	symbolic shear
• 	resonance amplification
• 	collapse propagation
• 	boundary fracture
Fault Line Indicators
• 	diagonal density discontinuities
• 	sudden symbolic noise
• 	substrate pulses
Fault lines are the primary predictors of large-scale instability.

4. Resonance Pockets
Localized zones where substrate density amplifies symbolic or emotional charge.
Characteristics
• 	high symbolic density
• 	increased mythic bloom likelihood
• 	emotional turbulence amplification
• 	actor-phase visibility increases
Formation Conditions
• 	density compression
• 	fault line intersections
• 	substrate inversion zones
Resonance pockets are highly reactive.

5. Density Inversion Zones
A density inversion occurs when lower strata become less dense than upper strata.
Effects
• 	substrate pulses
• 	mythic discharge
• 	collapse front formation
• 	residue turbulence
Indicators
• 	upward symbolic drift
• 	inverted gradient curves
• 	resonance pocket instability
Density inversions are rare and volatile.

6. Collapse Susceptibility Overlay
The map includes a collapse susceptibility overlay derived from density data.
High Susceptibility
• 	high-density zones
• 	steep gradients
• 	fault line intersections
• 	inversion zones
Low Susceptibility
• 	low-density zones
• 	shallow gradients
• 	stable boundaries
Collapse susceptibility is a derived metric, not a density measure.

7. Boundary Instability Overlay
Boundary instability correlates with substrate density irregularities.
Instability Indicators
• 	threshold flashes
• 	liminal arcs
• 	edge pulses
• 	role-boundary fractures
Boundary instability is strongest where:
• 	gradients are steep
• 	density is uneven
• 	resonance pockets cluster

8. Map Structure
The Substrate Density Map includes:
• 	density zones (high, medium, low)
• 	density gradients
• 	fault lines
• 	resonance pockets
• 	inversion zones
• 	collapse susceptibility overlay
• 	boundary instability overlay
This map is multi-layered and non-spatial.
