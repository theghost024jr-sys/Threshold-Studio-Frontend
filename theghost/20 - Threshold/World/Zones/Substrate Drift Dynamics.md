Map Name
Substrate Drift Dynamics
Map Type
Deep Substrate Motion Map / Structural Drift Model
Purpose
Substrate drift describes the slow, continuous movement of mythic substrate across density fields.
It is not collapse, not discharge, not turbulence — it is the baseline motion of the substrate layer itself.
Substrate drift determines:
• 	how density zones migrate
• 	how fault lines shift
• 	how resonance pockets form and dissolve
• 	how collapse susceptibility evolves over time
• 	how symbolic pressure redistributes
• 	how emotional weather patterns change
This is the tectonic motion of Threshold.

1. Drift Definition
Substrate drift is the gradual, directional movement of substrate density across the deep layer.
Drift is driven by:
• 	density gradients
• 	symbolic pressure
• 	emotional turbulence
• 	collapse history
• 	resonance pocket migration
• 	boundary instability
Drift is slow, but its effects accumulate.

2. Drift Forces
A. Gradient Pull
Density gradients exert a pull on substrate.
Effects:
• 	high-density zones migrate toward lower-density regions
• 	gradients flatten over time
• 	fault lines shift laterally
Gradient pull is the primary driver of drift.

B. Symbolic Pressure Flow
Symbolic charge moves through substrate like heat.
Effects:
• 	symbolic pressure redistributes
• 	resonance pockets drift
• 	symbolic shear zones migrate
Symbolic pressure flow is subtle but persistent.

C. Emotional Turbulence Push
Emotional turbulence pushes substrate outward.
Effects:
• 	turbulence pockets displace substrate
• 	drift accelerates near emotional storms
• 	boundaries thin
This force is irregular and unpredictable.

D. Collapse Aftershock Drift
Collapse leaves behind aftershock waves in the substrate.
Effects:
• 	density ripples
• 	fault line reorientation
• 	resonance pocket destabilization
Aftershock drift is strongest immediately after collapse.

3. Drift Modes
A. Linear Drift
• 	moves along density gradients
• 	predictable
• 	low turbulence
• 	slow and steady
B. Radial Drift
• 	expands outward from resonance pockets
• 	medium turbulence
• 	moderate symbolic distortion
C. Spiral Drift
• 	rotates around fault line intersections
• 	high turbulence
• 	strong symbolic shear
Spiral drift is the most complex and least predictable.

4. Drift Speed
Substrate drift is slow, but measurable.
Speed Factors
• 	gradient steepness
• 	symbolic pressure
• 	emotional turbulence
• 	collapse proximity
• 	resonance pocket density
Speed Ranges
• 	Slow Drift: stable zones, low turbulence
• 	Moderate Drift: active resonance zones
• 	Fast Drift: collapse-adjacent regions
Fast drift indicates structural instability.
