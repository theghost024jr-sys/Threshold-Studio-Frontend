Overview
Choice Prompts are the signal‑level invitations Threshold uses to engage the player in stance, direction, or emotional alignment.
They are not “options” or “dialogue choices.”
They are resonance invitations — structured openings that let the player shape the world’s next movement.
A Choice Prompt is a world‑generated signal that:
• 	opens a path
• 	reveals a stance
• 	exposes a tension
• 	invites alignment
• 	tests resonance
• 	offers a direction
Choice Prompts are part of the Signal Cluster, not the narrative layer.

1. Prompt Types
A. Directional Prompts
These guide the player toward a vector rather than a decision.
Purpose: establish movement, momentum, or intention.
Examples:
• 	Move toward clarity
• 	Move toward warmth
• 	Move toward pressure
• 	Move toward the unknown
Signal Behavior:
Directional prompts shift resonance channels and emotional gradients.

B. Stance Prompts
These ask the player to choose how they want to meet the moment.
Purpose: define relational or emotional posture.
Examples:
• 	With openness
• 	With caution
• 	With curiosity
• 	With resolve
Signal Behavior:
Stance prompts attune the Identity Substrate and emotional vectors.

C. Tension Prompts
These expose a pressure point and ask how to engage it.
Purpose: reveal underlying conflict or friction.
Examples:
• 	Lean into the tension
• 	Hold steady
• 	Release the pressure
• 	Reframe the moment
Signal Behavior:
Tension prompts activate Collapse or Creation pathways depending on stance.

D. Reflection Prompts
These invite the player to articulate or surface internal meaning.
Purpose: deepen resonance and symbolic density.
Examples:
• 	What is rising
• 	What is fading
• 	What is unresolved
• 	What is calling
Signal Behavior:
Reflection prompts feed the Symbolic Substrate and Narrative Substrate.

E. Threshold Prompts
These appear at liminal moments — transitions, identity shifts, or substrate crossings.
Purpose: mark a boundary or transformation.
Examples:
• 	Step through
• 	Step back
• 	Step sideways
• 	Wait and listen
Signal Behavior:
Threshold prompts activate resonance geometry and identity alignment.

2. Prompt Structure
A Choice Prompt always contains:
3. A Frame
The emotional or symbolic context.
4. A Vector
The direction or stance being invited.
5. A Consequence Field
Not a result — a field of possible shifts.
6. A Resonance Hook
A subtle signal that ties the choice to the world’s emotional physics.
7. A Drift Allowance
Space for the player to respond outside the offered prompts.

8. Prompt Delivery Modes
A. Soft Prompts
Ambient, gentle, low‑pressure.
Used in:
• 	The Soil
• 	Driftway
• 	Shallows
B. Firm Prompts
Clear, structured, high‑signal.
Used in:
• 	The Vein
• 	The Lattice
• 	The Deep
C. Fractured Prompts
Unstable, glitch‑edged, collapse‑adjacent.
Used in:
• 	The Fracture
• 	Collapse Weather events
D. Echo Prompts
Mythic, narrative‑resonant, atmospheric.
Used in:
• 	The Echo Field
• 	Narrative Substrate surges

4. Prompt Flow
A Choice Prompt follows a predictable signal flow:
5. 	Detect → world reads player stance or environmental pressure
6. 	Align → resonance channels synchronize
7. 	Frame → world sets emotional context
8. 	Offer → prompt appears
9. 	Attune → world adjusts to player’s selection
10. 	Shift → environment, species, or substrate moves
11. 	Drift → ambient consequences unfold
This flow ensures choices feel alive, not mechanical.

12. Prompt Integrity Rules
• 	Prompts must never collapse into binary logic
• 	Prompts must always allow drift or refusal
• 	Prompts must reflect the current substrate biome
• 	Prompts must be emotionally honest
• 	Prompts must not predict outcomes
• 	Prompts must maintain resonance coherence
• 	Prompts must not override player agency
• 	Prompts must be invitations, not commands
