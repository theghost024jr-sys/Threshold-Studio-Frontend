Both is the only answer that keeps Threshold honest, because the cosmos you’re building is designed to **motivate and haunt at the same time**. A fleeting cross‑flower echo is not a solved puzzle or a closed loop. It’s a _signal_ that lands differently depending on who the player is, what path they’re on, and what they were carrying when the world answered.

The ambiguity you chose earlier is what makes this dual reaction possible. Without ambiguity, you’d get only motivation (“I know what caused it, let’s do it again”) or only haunting (“I’ll never understand what happened”). With ambiguity, you get both in the same heartbeat.

How a fleeting entanglement motivates

Players who feel the echo and suspect they caused it experience a surge of:

- curiosity
- agency
- possibility
- cosmic-scale relevance

They think:

“I touched something real. I want to understand it.”

This drives them deeper into:

- their path
- their biome
- their species alignments
- their emotional stance
- their contribution patterns

Motivation comes from the sense that the world is listening.

How a fleeting entanglement haunts

At the same time, because the world never confirms the cause, the player is left with:

- doubt
- wonder
- a sense of incompleteness
- the feeling of brushing against something larger than themselves

They think:

“What if it wasn’t me?  
What if it was someone else?  
What if it was the world?”

This haunting is not fear.  
It’s **cosmic unease** — the sense that the world is alive and you only glimpsed its attention for a moment.

Why both reactions are necessary

Threshold’s cosmology depends on three emotional truths:

- **The world is bigger than you.**
- **The world responds to you.**
- **The world never explains itself.**

Motivation without haunting becomes a strategy game.  
Haunting without motivation becomes nihilism.  
Both together create myth.

This is the same emotional architecture as:

- hearing a whisper in a cave
- seeing a light in deep water
- feeling a tremor in a forest
- watching two stars blink in sync

You know it meant something.  
You don’t know what.  
You want to know more.  
You can’t let it go.

That’s the Threshold stance.

How this plays out across different player paths

Because you said “depends on their path,” here’s how both reactions manifest differently:

- **Alignment path** — motivated to find the other person; haunted by the idea they already “felt” them.
- **Contribution path** — motivated to recreate the event; haunted by the idea that contribution isn’t enough.
- **Drift path** — motivated by the chaos; haunted by the possibility it was accidental.
- **Witness path** — motivated to understand; haunted by the sense they glimpsed a truth they can’t articulate.
- **Resolve path** — motivated to push deeper; haunted by the idea that resolve alone may not be the key.

Each path experiences the same event through its own ontology.

The deeper layer

A fleeting entanglement is the first time a player realizes:

- they are not alone in the cosmos
- the flowers are not isolated
- the void is not empty
- meaning can cross worlds
- intention can echo across ontologies

And because it’s ambiguous, they can never fully resolve it.

That’s what keeps them moving.
