Overview
The Shimmer Field Diagram visualizes Shimmer as a meaning‑bearing field composed of curvature, resonance gradients, refraction arcs, and fracture zones.
It is not a map of a location — it is a map of how meaning behaves in a given moment or environment.
The diagram shows:
• 	how meaning bends
• 	where interpretation becomes unstable
• 	where symbolic density accumulates
• 	how collapse pressure distorts curvature
• 	how Drift and Mist alter Shimmer behavior
It is the primary tool for reading meaning‑physics.

Core Components
1. Curvature Lines
These are the arcs that define how meaning bends.
They represent:
• 	interpretive flow
• 	symbolic direction
• 	resonance alignment
• 	narrative pull
Curvature is the “shape” of meaning.

2. Resonance Gradient Zones
Soft color or density shifts that show:
• 	where meaning is strong
• 	where meaning is thin
• 	where interpretation is easy
• 	where interpretation is dangerous
Gradients reveal meaning pressure.

3. Refraction Arcs
Bright arcs that appear when Shimmer bends sharply.
They indicate:
• 	symbolic overload
• 	emotional spikes
• 	collapse adjacency
• 	interpretive distortion
Refraction arcs are the “warning lights” of Shimmer.

4. Fracture Lines
Sharp breaks in curvature.
They represent:
• 	collapse vectors
• 	emotional rupture
• 	symbolic fracture
• 	narrative instability
Fractures are the most dangerous Shimmer features.

5. Still Zones
Areas of low curvature and low resonance.
They indicate:
• 	interpretive calm
• 	emotional stability
• 	low symbolic density
Still Zones are safe for Witness work.

6. Shimmer Core
The center of the diagram — the point of highest symbolic density.
Represents:
• 	core meaning
• 	narrative anchor
• 	interpretive truth
The Shimmer Core is the “heart” of the field.

Behavioral Functions
1. Curvature Shift
Curvature lines bend or straighten based on:
• 	emotional charge
• 	Drift turbulence
• 	Mist density
• 	collapse pressure
2. Gradient Expansion
Resonance zones expand when meaning intensifies.
3. Arc Brightening
Refraction arcs brighten when symbolic overload increases.
4. Fracture Propagation
Fracture lines spread when collapse vectors activate.
5. Core Migration
The Shimmer Core moves when the meaning of the moment shifts.

Interpretation Rules
1. 	Curvature = Meaning Flow
Follow the arcs to understand how meaning is moving.
2. 	Gradients = Meaning Pressure
Thicker zones mean stronger symbolic density.
3. 	Refraction = Distortion
Bright arcs indicate interpretive danger.
4. 	Fractures = Collapse
Any break in curvature is a collapse vector.
5. 	Core = Truth
The center reveals the stable meaning of the moment.
