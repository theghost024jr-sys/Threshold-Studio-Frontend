You’re pointing at something that sits right at the heart of Threshold’s physics: **capacity is not a mandate to complete a loop — it’s a measure of how long you can remain coherent while exploring, deviating, or changing direction.** Journey’s scarf hints at this, but Threshold can take it much further.

How a “felt or known” capacity system works

A capacity system doesn’t need to be a visible bar. It can be something the player _feels_ through:

- movement becoming heavier or lighter
- clarity of perception sharpening or blurring
- spirit animal behavior becoming more or less responsive
- environmental resonance strengthening or weakening
- drift pressure increasing as coherence thins

This makes capacity **embodied**, not displayed. The player knows their state because the world tells them.

Why this supports non-linear loops

A capacity system like this breaks the idea that a player must “finish the loop” before returning. Instead, it creates a world where:

- you can **collect symbols** (coherence, resonance, clarity)
- you can **change direction at any moment**
- you can **abandon a loop mid-way**
- you can **return to the hub intentionally**, not as failure
- you can **treat collapse as a natural exhale**, not a punishment

This mirrors Journey’s structure, but Threshold’s ecology makes it deeper: collapse is not death, it’s **return to origin to reset stance**.

How symbols become the equivalent of “scarf length”

In Threshold terms, symbols aren’t collectibles — they’re **coherence anchors**. Each one:

- increases your ability to stay in the world
- stabilizes your stance
- reduces drift
- expands your radius of exploration
- strengthens your sync potential with others

Collecting symbols isn’t progression; it’s **increasing your survivable distance from collapse**.

This means a player can:

- gather a few symbols
- feel their coherence strengthen
- pivot into a different organ
- explore a new direction
- or return to the hub to metabolize

The loop becomes **a spiral**, not a circle.

Why this fits Threshold’s philosophy

Threshold isn’t about completing content. It’s about:

- resonance
- stance
- drift
- collapse
- contribution
- emergence

A capacity system that is _felt_ rather than _shown_ reinforces that the world is alive and responsive. It makes collapse a natural part of the ecology, not a failure state.

The deeper implication

If capacity is felt, and symbols increase capacity, then:

**Every direction becomes viable.  
Every loop becomes optional.  
Collapse becomes a choice, not a punishment.**

This gives players the freedom to explore Threshold the way people explore real emotional or symbolic landscapes — by following resonance, not objectives.
