1. Humans experience harm before they understand it
A child feels:
• 	pain
• 	fear
• 	abandonment
• 	betrayal
• 	chaos
But they don’t have:
• 	moral categories
• 	metaphysical explanations
• 	social frameworks
So the raw experience is just overwhelm.
“Evil” doesn’t exist yet.

2. Humans create stories to make sense of suffering
Once language and culture enter the picture, humans start asking:
• 	Why did this happen
• 	Who caused it
• 	What force is behind this
• 	How do we prevent it
And because early humans didn’t have:
• 	psychology
• 	neuroscience
• 	sociology
…they used the tools they did have:
myth, symbol, story, personification.
This is where you get:
• 	demons
• 	devils
• 	tricksters
• 	chaos spirits
• 	malevolent gods
These aren’t literal beings.
They’re narrative containers for experiences that were too big to integrate.

4. The devil is the ultimate personification
The devil isn’t a being.
He’s a symbolic compression of:
• 	fear
• 	betrayal
• 	cruelty
• 	chaos
• 	temptation
• 	suffering
• 	the shadow self
He’s the narrative shorthand for everything humans can’t integrate.
Humans didn’t discover the devil.
They invented him to explain the parts of themselves they couldn’t face.

5. So yes — humans create myths to define evil
But here’s the deeper structural truth:
**Myths don’t create evil.
Myths create the concept of evil.**
The harm exists.
The suffering exists.
The cruelty exists.
But the category “evil” —
that’s a human construction.
A baby proves it.
A baby has no concept of evil.
It only has raw experience.
The concept comes later, through:
• 	culture
• 	story
• 	symbol
• 	religion
• 	social norms
• 	moral frameworks
Myths are the scaffolding humans build to make sense of harm.
