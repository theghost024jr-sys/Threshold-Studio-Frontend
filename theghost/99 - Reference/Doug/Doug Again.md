Oh this is a _perfect_ question, theghost — because **Non‑Critical Safety Concerns** is exactly the kind of bureaucratic nonsense that small towns invent when they need to justify a budget line but don’t actually want to do anything important.

Let’s define it cleanly, with the exact tone this world deserves.

**Non‑Critical Safety Concerns (NCSC)**

_A municipal category for things that technically aren’t dangerous but someone complained loudly enough that the town had to pretend to care._

**Definition**

**Non‑Critical Safety Concerns** are issues that:

- pose no real threat
- affect no measurable part of public safety
- cannot cause injury unless someone is extremely determined
- exist mostly because a resident filed a strongly worded letter
- are easier to assign to Doug than to explain why they don’t matter

They are “safety concerns” in the same way a lukewarm cup of coffee is a “thermal hazard.”

**Examples of Non‑Critical Safety Concerns**

- Triangles
- Overly enthusiastic squirrels
- Sidewalk chalk drawings that “look aggressive”
- Benches that are “too inviting”
- A mailbox that leans at a “worrisome angle”
- A fountain that splashes “in a suggestive pattern”
- A wind chime that is “audibly suspicious”

These are the kinds of things that get put on a list because the town council doesn’t want to argue with Mrs. Kline from Maple Street again.

**Who Would Be Concerned?**

This is the best part — the people concerned are not experts, not officials, not inspectors.

They are:

**1. Retired residents with too much time**

They notice everything.  
They report everything.  
They believe the town is one oddly shaped planter away from collapse.

**2. The Town Council Safety Subcommittee**

They are not concerned because they care.  
They are concerned because they don’t want to deal with complaints.

**3. Doug**

Doug is concerned because he believes this is his calling.  
He takes every report seriously.  
He once spent an afternoon measuring the “aggressiveness” of a triangle someone drew on a napkin.

**4. Absolutely no one else**

Most residents:

- don’t know the department exists
- don’t know Doug exists
- don’t know triangles were banned
- don’t know why Doug keeps taping corners in the library

But the town keeps the department because it’s easier than dismantling it.
