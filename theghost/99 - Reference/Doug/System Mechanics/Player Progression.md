- it grows only through play
- the graph itself becomes the progression system
- players shape their own powers
- the world and the rules evolve together

Let me lay it out cleanly so you can see the full shape.

🌱 **THE STARTING STATE: 5×5 ROOT GRID**

At the beginning:

- There are **five cards**
- Each card has **five attributes**
- These attributes are **fixed**
- They define the **only legal actions** in the world

This is the “physics of turn zero.”

The five attributes per card are the ones we already defined:

1. Click Target
2. Action
3. Creation
4. Linking
5. Limiter

That’s the whole engine.

🔓 **THE MOMENT AFTER: Players Create Their Own Attributes**

Once the game begins, something beautiful happens:

**Players start earning new attributes by contributing to the graph.**

Contribution =

- adding a node
- linking something
- filling a blank
- spawning a biome
- attaching to a layer
- or any action allowed by their root card

Every contribution is a “move,” and after a certain number of moves (you choose the threshold), the player unlocks a new attribute.

This attribute is:

- invented by the player
- approved by the group
- added to their card
- permanently part of their power set

This is where the game becomes _alive_.

🧩 **Example of how it plays out**

Let’s say the **Yellow Operator** has contributed 3 times.

They unlock a new attribute.

They propose:

“I want to be able to link `.md` files to biomes too.”

The group says yes or no.  
If yes, it becomes:

**New Attribute:**  
“You may link any `.md` to any biome.”

Now the Yellow Operator has **six attributes**, and the world shifts.

🎮 **Why this works socially**

Because the moment someone clicks something, the table erupts:

- “That’s mine — I can link that.”
- “Wait, I unlocked a new attribute, I can act here too.”
- “If you do that, I can follow up with my power.”
- “Let me fill that blank before you link it.”

The game becomes a **conversation**, not a turn order.

The cards become **permissions**, not roles.

The unlocks become **identity**, not upgrades.

🧱 **The structure is simple, but the emergent behavior is deep**

You start with:

**5 cards × 5 attributes = 25 total rules.**

But after 20 minutes of play, you might have:

- 5 cards × 7 attributes
- or 5 cards × 10 attributes
- or 5 cards × 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 attributes

The system grows like a coral reef.

And because the graph is the board, the rules evolve _in response to the world_.
