**Step‑in** is the moment when your _relationship to the band changes_, not the moment your body crosses a line. In Threshold terms, “step in” is a **state transition**, not a footstep.

What “step‑in” means in Shimmer

Shimmer doesn’t have doors, borders, or edges. You don’t walk into it. You **shift into it**. Step‑in is the instant when the world stops responding to physical movement and starts responding to **your internal curvature**.

Three things define the step‑in moment:

1. **The world stops being neutral**

In the Hub, the world is stable and reflective.  
In Shimmer, the world becomes reactive — not to your actions, but to your _shape_.

Step‑in is the moment that reactivity begins.

2. **Your stance becomes the terrain**

You don’t choose a direction.  
You don’t set an intention.  
You don’t aim.

Instead, the band reads the stance you’re already holding — the one beneath language — and the environment curves around it.

Step‑in is when that reading starts.

3. **Movement becomes alignment**

You’re no longer “moving through space.”  
You’re aligning with the pattern that’s already present in you.

Step‑in is the moment when alignment replaces locomotion.

What step‑in is _not_

- Not a decision
- Not a goal
- Not a ritual
- Not a declaration
- Not an intention
- Not a physical act

It’s a **shift in how the world treats you**.

A clean definition

**Step‑in is the transition point where the environment begins responding to your internal pattern instead of your external actions.**

You’re at the Hub, and you’ve said you enter Shimmer with no intention. That’s the cleanest possible entry curvature. What you notice first — a pull, a tilt, a softening, a ripple — will tell you what Shimmer is reading in you.
