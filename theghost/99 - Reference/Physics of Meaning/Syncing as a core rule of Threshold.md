A syncing mechanic can sit at the **core-rule level** in Threshold because it expresses something fundamental about how your world works: _alignment creates capacity_. Not as a bonus, not as a buff, but as a law of the physics of being.

The cleanest way to articulate it as a core rule is to treat syncing as a **universal ecological principle** that every organ, every phenomenon, and every player action participates in.

Syncing as a core rule of Threshold

Syncing becomes a rule on the same tier as your existing structural laws (lineage, reciprocity, threshold, roles, gifts). It would read something like:

**When two or more entities align in stance, rhythm, or intention, the world recognizes them as a single coherent signal and increases their effective capacity.**

This rule is not about cooperation. It’s about **coherence**.

It means the world responds to _alignment_ the way physics responds to resonance.

What the core rule actually governs

A core rule must be universal, so syncing would apply across the entire architecture:

🧭 1. Identity

Two identities in sync reduce drift, stabilize boundaries, and amplify clarity.  
Identity organs become more legible to the environment.

🌿 2. Environment

Biomes respond differently to aligned vs. misaligned signals.  
Some phenomena only open when multiple signals resonate.

🐾 3. Spirit animals

Spirit animals read synced players as a single, stronger presence.  
Their behavior shifts accordingly.

🔥 4. Contribution

In Fib 5–8, syncing becomes survival physics:  
aligned players generate more contribution, stabilize storms, and unlock deeper layers.

🌀 5. Drift

Syncing collapses drift states faster.  
Misalignment amplifies drift.

🧩 6. Organ activation

Some organs require resonance to activate.  
Syncing becomes the key to unlocking higher-order mechanics.

🛠️ 7. Crafting, rituals, traversal

Any mechanic that involves timing, rhythm, or stance becomes more powerful when synced.

This is why syncing works as a core rule: it’s not a feature — it’s a **law of the world**.

Why syncing belongs at the core level

A core rule must satisfy three criteria:

- **It must be universal** — applies everywhere.
- **It must be ecological** — affects relationships, not just individuals.
- **It must be ontological** — defines what the world _is_, not what players _do_.

Syncing satisfies all three.

It says:  
**The world of Threshold is built to reward coherence.**  
Not obedience. Not strategy. Not optimization.  
Coherence.

This is deeply aligned with your architecture: organs, resonance, drift, contribution, stance.

How this changes the feel of the entire game

Once syncing is a core rule, Threshold becomes a world where:

- alignment is power
- rhythm is communication
- presence is contribution
- coherence is survival
- resonance is identity
- drift is the cost of misalignment

This gives the world a living, breathing quality.  
Players don’t just “team up” — they **attune**.

A natural next step

Do you want syncing to be:

- **always on** (a background law like gravity), or
- **contextual** (stronger in some organs, weaker in others), or
- **threshold-based** (only activates when alignment crosses a certain clarity level)?

Each version creates a different feel for the world.
