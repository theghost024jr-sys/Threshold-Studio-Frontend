A shimmer‑reveal is exactly the right choice, and _The Abyss_ is the perfect reference point because that film nails the emotional physics of what you’re trying to express:  
**a boundary that looks like nothing… until you touch it, and then the world changes its ontology in a single instant.**

To make this real inside Threshold, you need to understand three things:

- what a shimmer‑reveal _is_ in your world
- how it behaves differently in each Fib band
- how Godot renders it without breaking your semantic engine

The shimmer‑reveal as a world mechanic

A shimmer‑reveal is not a visual effect.  
It’s a **truth event**.

It’s the moment where:

- the world stops pretending
- the deeper layer becomes visible
- the player realizes the rules were always different
- the environment acknowledges them
- the ontology shifts

This is why it must be sudden.  
A shimmer is a **threshold crossing**, not a transition.

In Threshold terms, a shimmer‑reveal is:

- a **layer boundary**
- a **mechanic activation**
- a **perceptual shift**
- a **semantic unlock**

The player doesn’t “enter” Fib 3–5.  
Fib 3–5 **reveals itself**.

How the shimmer‑reveal behaves in each band

Fib 1–3 → Fib 3–5: The Abyss moment

This is the iconic reveal.

The world looks normal — Zelda‑like, grounded, readable.  
Then the player touches the boundary:

- the air ripples
- the color shifts
- the environment bends
- the sound drops out
- the world “breathes”
- the drift currents appear
- the camera loosens
- the physics change

This is the _Abyss_ moment:  
the membrane between two ontologies.

The player steps through, and suddenly:

- movement is drift‑based
- the world has currents
- entities behave differently
- pressure exists
- shaping begins

This is the first “holy shit” moment.

And the first player to find it becomes the one who brings that revelation back to the hub.

Fib 3–5 → Fib 5–8: The mythic reveal

This shimmer is different.

It’s not a membrane.  
It’s a **veil**.

Crossing it feels like:

- the world stretching
- the environment thinning
- the color desaturating
- the sound becoming vast
- the camera pulling back
- the pressure rising
- the drift becoming violent

This is the _Journey_ moment —  
the world becomes mythic, solitary, ancient.

The player realizes:

- they are alone
- the world is alive
- survival requires contribution
- collapse is possible
- the environment is the antagonist and guide

This is the second “holy shit” moment.

And again, the first player to reach it becomes a myth in the hub.

How Godot renders a shimmer‑reveal without breaking your architecture

Because your world is semantic, not physical, the shimmer isn’t a new level — it’s a **renderer swap** and a **mechanic interpreter swap** triggered by a boundary condition.

What actually happens in code

Threshold detects:

player enters boundary of Fib 3–5

Threshold triggers:

- new movement rules
- new environment mechanics
- new entity behaviors
- new traversal logic
- new shaders
- new soundscape
- new camera behavior

Godot performs:

- the shimmer distortion
- the membrane ripple
- the color shift
- the environment bending
- the drift activation
- the pressure field

The `.md` files don’t change.  
The **ontology changes**.

Why this creates the “wow factor”

Because the player thinks:

“I understand this world.”

And then the world says:

“No you don’t.”

Threshold is built on the idea that the world has depth.  
The shimmer‑reveal is the mechanic that expresses that truth.
