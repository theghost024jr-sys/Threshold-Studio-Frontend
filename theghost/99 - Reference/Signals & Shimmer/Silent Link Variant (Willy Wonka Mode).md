Oh that’s _exactly_ the flavor of mischief that makes your systems feel alive, theghost.  
You’re basically introducing **asymmetric invitations** — some explicit, some silent — and letting the world decide who notices.

Let me articulate the mechanic cleanly, because what you just described is actually a **two‑tiered recognition trial**:

**The Silent Link Variant (Willy Wonka Mode)**

**1. You tell someone: “I’ll put you in the game.”**

That’s it.  
No name.  
No hint.  
No species.  
No shard.  
No coordinates.

Just the promise.

**2. You still create their character.**

You anchor them in the world with:

- a username‑based name
- a stance
- a role
- a location
- a pattern they _might_ recognize if they ever stumble across it

But they don’t know any of this.

**3. They are technically “linked,” but dormant.**

The link exists, but it’s inert — like a seed that hasn’t been watered.

**4. If they ever find themselves, the link activates.**

This is the Wonka part.

They’re wandering the world, and suddenly they see:

- a character with their username
- or a character whose behavior feels eerily familiar
- or a detail that only _they_ would notice
- or a pattern that mirrors their own stance

And if they say, even privately, “Wait… is that me?”  
Boom.  
Ultra Shimmer.

**5. The rarity becomes absurdly high.**

Because now Ultra Shimmer is only reachable by:

- people you chose
- who were put in the world
- who were _not_ told their name
- who still managed to find themselves anyway

That’s mythic scarcity.

**Why this is so good**

**It rewards curiosity, not compliance.**

Most people will forget you even said it.  
A few will look.  
One or two will _notice_.

**It creates a living folklore.**

Players will whisper:

- “I think I found myself.”
- “Did he put me in?”
- “How would I know?”
- “What if I already passed myself and didn’t realize?”

That’s pure Wonka energy — the golden ticket hidden in plain sight.

**It makes the world feel bigger than the rules.**

Because the system isn’t just mechanical.  
It’s relational.  
It’s perceptual.  
It’s about who _sees_ themselves reflected back.
