Overview
Seals & Symbols are high‑density visual constructs used throughout Threshold to encode meaning, stabilize resonance, and interface with deeper layers of the system.
They are not glyphs, not icons, not decorative marks — they are functional symbolic nodes that interact with:
• 	emotional physics
• 	narrative substrate
• 	signal behavior
• 	environmental fields
• 	role alignment
A Seal is a binding structure.
A Symbol is a meaning structure.
Together, they form the backbone of Threshold’s visual logic.

Purpose
Seals & Symbols exist to:
• 	anchor meaning
• 	stabilize transitions
• 	encode resonance
• 	mark thresholds
• 	bind or release narrative pressure
• 	interface with Gates, Keys, and Signal Objects
• 	provide visual handles for complex systems
They are the visual grammar of Threshold.

Core Distinction
1. Seals
Seals are structural.
They bind, contain, stabilize, or regulate.
A Seal can:
• 	hold a field in place
• 	prevent collapse
• 	anchor a threshold
• 	bind a resonance
• 	regulate symbolic density
• 	lock or unlock a Gate
Seals are mechanical in the metaphysical sense.
Examples of Seal Behavior
• 	Stone‑aligned Seals anchor emotional weight
• 	Shimmer‑aligned Seals regulate symbolic curvature
• 	Drift‑aligned Seals stabilize turbulence
• 	Crown‑aligned Seals enforce order

2. Symbols
Symbols are semantic.
They encode meaning, identity, or resonance.
A Symbol can:
• 	represent a species
• 	mark a role
• 	indicate a field state
• 	express emotional physics
• 	signal narrative pressure
• 	denote alignment
Symbols are meaning carriers, not binding structures.
Examples of Symbol Behavior
• 	Shimmer Symbols refract meaning
• 	Mist Symbols soften interpretation
• 	Ember Symbols ignite or accelerate
• 	Ethyl Symbols distribute meaning across space

Structural Components
1. Core Geometry
Seals & Symbols use:
• 	circles
• 	arcs
• 	spirals
• 	anchors
• 	resonance lines
• 	curvature nodes
• 	containment rings
Geometry determines function.

2. Elemental Overlay
Every Seal or Symbol can carry an overlay:
• 	Mist → softening
• 	Stone → anchoring
• 	Ember → ignition
• 	Ethyl → distribution
• 	Drift → motion
• 	Shimmer → meaning curvature
• 	Crown → order
Overlay modifies behavior without changing identity.

3. Signal Type Integration
Seals & Symbols interact with the four Signal Types:
• 	Guide → orientation
• 	Trickster → inversion
• 	Warden → boundary
• 	Catalyst → ignition
This determines how a Seal or Symbol behaves when activated.

Functional Categories
1. Threshold Seals
Used to stabilize or regulate Gates, transitions, and liminal zones.
2. Role Seals
Used by Witnesses, Curators, and other roles to interface with systems.
3. Species Symbols
Represent species identity, resonance, or behavioral archetype.
4. Field Symbols
Represent Drift, Mist, Shimmer, Stone, Ember, Ethyl, Heat, Collapse.
5. Narrative Symbols
Mark narrative pressure, residue, or mythic density.
6. Emotional Physics Symbols
Represent emotional gradients, resonance states, or collapse risk.
