Overview Archetypal Roles are the deep functional identities that spirits express within Threshold. They are not personalities, jobs, or classes—they are behavioral archetypes that shape a spirit’s purpose and pattern of response across layers, signals, and species. • Layer defines the environment • Signal Type defines the behavioral vector • Species defines the body • Elemental Overlay tints all of the above Archetypal Roles define the why. They are the fifth layer in the Threshold stack, emerging from the interaction of Layers, Signals, and Species.

Purpose of Archetypal Roles Archetypal Roles exist to: • anchor spirits in long‑term behavioral patterns • stabilize meaning beneath shifting signals • shape interaction with the Threshold Map • influence drift, resonance, and layer transitions • create mythic continuity across species and overlays Roles are slow to change. Signals may drift rapidly; overlays may shift; even species can change over long arcs—but a Role is a deep root.

The Six Foundational Roles These are the core archetypes that naturally emerge from Threshold’s emotional physics.

  1. Sentinel • Holds boundaries, protects thresholds, maintains structure • Resonates with Stone and Earth
  2. Weaver • Connects patterns, forms networks, binds meaning • Resonates with Air and Mist
  3. Hunter • Seeks truth, tracks what is hidden, pursues meaning • Resonates with Ember and Air
  4. Oracle • Reveals subtext, interprets signals, channels intuition • Resonates with Ethyl and Mist
  5. Echo • Mirrors others, amplifies resonance, reflects emotional tone • Resonates with Mist and Ethyl
  6. Lantern • Illuminates paths, clarifies meaning, stabilizes drift • Resonates with Air and Ember These six form the first tier of Archetypal Roles. Additional roles can emerge later as the world expands, but they should still obey the same architectural dependencies.

Role Mechanics Role Expression A Role shapes: • how a spirit interprets signals • how it responds to layer pressure • how it drifts under stress • how it interacts with other spirits • how it appears on the Threshold Map Roles are behavioral gravity wells—they bend everything else around them.

Role Drift Roles can drift, but slowly and rarely. Examples: • Sentinel → Weaver (when protection becomes connection) • Weaver → Oracle (when patterning becomes insight) • Hunter → Lantern (when pursuit becomes illumination) Role drift is tracked separately from Signal Drift and should be treated as a major event in a spirit’s arc.

Role Resonance Each Role has natural affinities with: • Signal Types (e.g., Sentinel ↔ Warden) • Layers (e.g., Oracle ↔ Ethyl) • Overlays (e.g., Ember‑Lantern = wildfire clarity) • Species (some species favor certain roles) High resonance: • increases stability • reduces drift • amplifies clarity of behavior

Role Shadows Every Role has a shadow expression: • Sentinel → rigidity • Weaver → entanglement • Hunter → obsession • Oracle → distortion • Echo → dissolution • Lantern → burnout Shadow states appear under extreme layer pressure, unresolved drift, or prolonged misalignment between Role, Signal, and environment.

Role Wheel The six roles form a closed behavioral circuit: • Sentinel → Weaver → Hunter → Oracle → Echo → Lantern → Sentinel Each adjacency defines: • drift direction • behavioral resonance • pressure gradients • shadow interactions This wheel is the geometric foundation of the Roles layer and the basis for the Archetypal Roles.canvas.
