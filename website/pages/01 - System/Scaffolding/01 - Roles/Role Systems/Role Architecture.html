The structural blueprint of how Roles exert pressure, shape behavior, and interact with identity, signal, and environment within Threshold.

Purpose Role Architecture defines the core mechanics of Roles in Threshold. It explains: • what a Role fundamentally is • how Roles generate pressure • how Roles shape identity and behavior • how Roles interact with species and environments • how Roles distort, collapse, or stabilize systems • how Roles integrate into the larger worldstate Roles are not jobs, archetypes, or personalities — they are directional forces that shape the physics of meaning and behavior.

The Four Pillars of Role Architecture Every Role is built from four structural pillars:

  1. Vector The Role’s directional force. Components: • orientation • behavioral trajectory • relational stance • environmental posture Function: Determines how the Role moves through the world.

  2. Pressure The Role’s intensity and influence. Components: • amplitude • density • collapse threshold • stabilization potential Function: Determines how strongly the Role affects identity, species, and environment.

  3. Expression The Role’s outward manifestation. Components: • movement signature • signal output • behavioral pattern • interaction style Function: Determines how the Role appears in action.

  4. Resonance The Role’s harmonic alignment with identity and lineage. Components: • lineage affinity • identity compatibility • signal harmonics • collapse resistance Function: Determines how the Role integrates or destabilizes.

Role States Roles exist in four canonical states:

  1. Aligned Role Vector, pressure, expression, and resonance are coherent. • stable • predictable • identity‑supportive This is the state of Role Resonance.

  2. Distorted Role One or more pillars misaligned. • warped vector • unstable pressure • noisy expression • weak resonance This is the state of Role Drift.

  3. Collapsed Role Role loses structural integrity. • pressure spikes • vector inversion • expression breakdown • resonance rupture This is the state of Role Collapse.

  4. Reformed Role Role rebuilt after collapse or distortion. • restored vector • recalibrated pressure • coherent expression • renewed resonance This is the state of Role Reformation.

Role Pressure Mechanics Role pressure interacts with systems in predictable ways: On Identity • stabilizes or destabilizes boundaries • aligns or misaligns identity geometry • triggers drift or collapse On Species • amplifies expression patterns • triggers sensitivity thresholds • alters movement and signal patterns On Environment • shifts pressure fields • alters drift vectors • creates resonance pockets or collapse wells On Signals • amplifies harmonics • distorts drift • inverts or stabilizes patterns Roles are pressure engines.

Role Interaction Logic Roles interact through three mechanisms:

  1. Vector Interaction Vectors align, cross, or oppose. • alignment → resonance • crossing → tension • opposition → collapse risk

  2. Pressure Interaction Pressures amplify or cancel. • amplification → escalation • cancellation → stabilization • mismatch → distortion

  3. Resonance Interaction Harmonics synchronize or clash. • synchronization → clarity • clash → identity rupture • oscillation → drift loops

Role–Identity Coupling Identity Architecture and Role Architecture are co‑dependent: • identity determines role expression • role determines identity posture • misalignment produces drift • collapse in one triggers collapse in the other This coupling is the basis of Role Identity Drift.

Role–Environment Coupling Roles reshape environments: • Anchor stabilizes • Bridge connects • Flame escalates • Warden compresses • Seer reveals • Harbor absorbs • Strider disrupts Environments reshape Roles: • Mist distorts • Branch stabilizes • Deep compresses • Shore oscillates • Ruin fractures

Role Failure Modes Roles fail when: • pressure exceeds capacity • vector misaligns • expression becomes unstable • resonance collapses Failure produces: • drift • distortion • collapse • identity rupture
