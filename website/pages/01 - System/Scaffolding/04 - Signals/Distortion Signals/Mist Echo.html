  1. Signal Identity Core Nature Mist Echo is a distortion signal created when sound, motion, or identity interacts with high distortion pressure. It is not a reflection — it is a curved repetition of an event, delayed and bent by the environment. Signature • soft • delayed • slightly off‑angle • sometimes doubled • sometimes ahead of the source Mist Echo is the sound of drift curvature made audible.

  2. Signal Behavior 2.1 Oscillation Mist Echo oscillates in amplitude and direction. It never repeats the same way twice. 2.2 Curvature The echo bends around corners, through mist, and sometimes toward the listener. 2.3 Delay Drift The delay between source and echo varies with: • drift momentum • distortion pressure • identity stability 2.4 Multiplicity Under high pressure, the echo splits into: • primary echo • secondary echo • phantom echo (identity‑aligned)

  3. Effects on Drift 3.1 Drift Curvature Increase Mist Echo increases drift curvature by subtly pulling motion sideways. 3.2 Drift Velocity Variance Drift becomes uneven — bursts of speed followed by slow, syrupy motion. 3.3 Drift Arc Multiplication A single drift arc may split into two or three parallel arcs. 3.4 Drift Loop Formation Repeated Mist Echo exposure can create oscillating drift loops.

  4. Effects on Identity 4.1 Perception Drift Identity begins to misread direction and distance. 4.2 Boundary Softening Edges blur; self‑location becomes uncertain. 4.3 Echo‑Alignment Identity may “lean” toward the echo instead of the source. 4.4 Identity Splitting (High Pressure) A faint double‑image of the self appears — harmless unless collapse pressure rises.

  5. Collapse Interaction Mist Echo is collapse‑adjacent, not collapse‑aligned. 5.1 Spiral Collapse Amplification If collapse begins, Mist Echo tightens curvature into a spiral. 5.2 Recursive Collapse Trigger Repeated echoes can create a feedback loop that leads to recursive collapse. 5.3 Collapse Spread Mist Echo can carry collapse pressure along its path, extending collapse zones.

  6. Role Interaction Roles Strengthened • Listener — reads oscillation patterns • Weaver — detects structure in noise • Trickster — amplifies distortion Roles Weakened • Anchor — boundaries dissolve • Guardian — perimeter logic breaks • Scholar — pattern clarity collapses

  7. Species Interaction Mistwalkers • generate Mist Echo unintentionally • navigate using echo curvature • communicate through echo‑patterns • follow phantom echoes instinctively Crows • mimic Mist Echo • use it to disorient predators • thrive in oscillating sound fields

  8. Environmental Interaction Strongest In • Mist Corridor • Spiral Grove • Mirage Plain Weakest In • Deep Snowfield • Stone Basin • High Branch Mist Echo requires distortion pressure to exist.

  9. Variants 9.1 Soft Echo Low distortion, gentle oscillation. Common in early corridor regions. 9.2 Split Echo Two echoes diverge at different angles. Occurs under medium distortion. 9.3 Phantom Echo Identity‑aligned echo that appears ahead of the listener. Occurs under high distortion. 9.4 Spiral Echo Echo curves inward toward collapse. Occurs near Spiral Fields.

  10. Description A soft, delayed repetition of sound that seems to come from the wrong direction. Sometimes it arrives early. Sometimes it arrives late. Sometimes it arrives twice. It is the sound of the world bending — the audible signature of drift curvature. Mist Echo is the heartbeat of distortion environments.
