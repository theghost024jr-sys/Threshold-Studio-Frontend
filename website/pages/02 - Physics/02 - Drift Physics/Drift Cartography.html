Overview Drift Cartography is the diagrammatic system used to map Drift as a dynamic motion field. It visualizes: • micro‑currents • turbulence zones • shear fields • spiral flows • lift pockets • collapse drag • emotional‑motion gradients Drift Cartography is not a map of space — it is a map of motion, showing how movement behaves in a given moment or environment. It is the primary tool for reading motion‑physics in Threshold.

Core Components

  1. Flow Lines Smooth lines showing the direction and strength of Drift. They represent: • baseline motion • current direction • flow stability • navigable paths Flow lines are the “roads” of Drift.

  2. Turbulence Zones Chaotic, swirling regions where Drift becomes unstable. Indicate: • emotional spikes • collapse adjacency • Shimmer interference • Mist thickening Turbulence zones are dangerous for navigation.

  3. Shear Fields Sharp directional splits in Drift flow. They represent: • sudden emotional shifts • collapse vectors • boundary transitions • field misalignment Shear fields can tear scripts, arcs, or trajectories.

  4. Spiral Currents Circular or logarithmic spirals in the Drift field. They indicate: • recursive motion • symbolic density • emotional recursion • collapse‑adjacent loops Spiral currents can trap or guide travelers depending on alignment.

  5. Lift Pockets Upward or outward Drift zones. They represent: • emotional lightness • clarity • Golden Drift emergence • safe passage Lift pockets are ideal for navigation.

  6. Drag Fields Heavy, downward Drift zones. They indicate: • collapse residue • emotional heaviness • symbolic overload • environmental stagnation Drag fields slow movement and distort scripts.

Behavioral Functions

  1. Flow Shift Flow lines bend or reroute based on: • emotional charge • Mist density • Shimmer curvature • collapse pressure
  2. Turbulence Expansion Turbulence zones grow when emotional fields destabilize.
  3. Shear Propagation Shear fields spread when collapse vectors activate.
  4. Spiral Tightening Spiral currents tighten under symbolic overload.
  5. Lift Bloom Lift pockets expand during Golden Drift states.

Interpretation Rules

  1. Flow = Motion Follow flow lines to understand how movement wants to occur.
  2. Turbulence = Instability Avoid or stabilize turbulence zones.
  3. Shear = Danger Shear fields indicate collapse vectors or emotional rupture.
  4. Spirals = Recursion Spiral currents show looping or recursive behavior.
  5. Lift = Safety Lift pockets indicate clarity and safe passage.
  6. Drag = Weight Drag fields reveal collapse residue or emotional heaviness.

Use Cases Drift Cartography is used for: • navigation • collapse avoidance • emotional‑motion diagnostics • script stabilization • Drift state prediction • Golden Drift identification • Ruins micro‑current mapping • Witness Spiral movement analysis It is the primary diagram for motion‑physics.
