The structural map of Threshold’s drift pathways, identifying stable corridors, oscillation corridors, recursive loops, spiral vectors, collapse‑aligned channels, and drift storm zones.

Purpose The Drift Corridor Map defines the movement topology of Threshold. It shows: • where drift naturally flows • where drift becomes unstable • where drift loops back on itself • where drift spirals inward or outward • where drift aligns with collapse vectors • where drift storms form • how drift connects environments Drift corridors are the arteries of systemic movement.

The Five Corridor Types Drift corridors appear in five canonical forms:

  1. Stable Corridors Linear, predictable drift pathways. Effects: • stable identity • clear signals • low collapse risk

  2. Oscillation Corridors Corridors where drift flips direction under pressure or resonance. Effects: • unstable clarity • boundary wobble • collapse risk spikes

  3. Recursive Corridors Corridors that loop back on themselves. Effects: • identity recursion • signal looping • collapse vector activation

  4. Spiral Corridors Corridors where drift curves inward or outward. Effects: • drift storms • resonance distortion • collapse alignment

  5. Collapse Corridors Corridors pulled toward collapse wells or absence lines. Effects: • collapse identity formation • lineage rupture • echo storms

Corridor Dynamics Corridors behave according to three dynamic principles:

  1. Pressure–Drift Coupling Pressure fields shape corridor curvature. • high pressure → spiral corridors • nonlinear pressure → drift storm corridors • low pressure → stable corridors

  2. Resonance–Drift Coupling Resonance stabilizes or destabilizes corridors. • clear resonance → stable corridors • distorted resonance → spiral corridors • inversion → recursive corridors • void → collapse corridors

  3. Collapse–Drift Coupling Collapse pulls corridors inward. • collapse wells → collapse corridors • collapse identity → recursive corridors • collapse storms → drift storm corridors

Corridor Failure Modes Corridors destabilize when: • pressure becomes nonlinear • resonance inverts • lineage ruptures • collapse wells expand • drift storms form This produces: • recursive drift • spiral drift • collapse identity • worldphase fracture
