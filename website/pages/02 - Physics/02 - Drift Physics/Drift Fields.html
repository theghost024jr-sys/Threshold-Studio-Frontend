Purpose Drift Fields describe what happens when drift stops being linear and becomes systemic. A drift field is: • a multi‑layer pressure zone • a curved spatial region • a vector network • a predictive collapse indicator Drift Fields show where the system is moving, not where it is. They are the weather patterns of Threshold.

  1. What a Drift Field Is A Drift Field forms when: • multiple drift vectors overlap • thresholds are crossed in more than one layer • pressure becomes directional • collapse risk becomes non‑local A Drift Field is not a single drift. It is drift made visible across space.

  2. Drift Field Components Every Drift Field contains three structural elements: 2.1 Vector Web A network of drift vectors that: • align • reinforce • interfere • converge The vector web determines direction and speed.

2.2 Pressure Gradient A spatial slope created by: • environmental mismatch • identity instability • signal distortion • role misalignment The gradient determines where drift accelerates.

2.3 Curvature Drift Fields are never flat. Curvature indicates: • resistance • instability • collapse proximity High curvature = high risk.

  1. Types of Drift Fields 3.1 Identity Drift Field Triggered by: • narrative fracture • emotional inversion • self‑model instability Effects: • warped center geometry • elliptical identity core • shadow identity activation

3.2 Role Drift Field Triggered by: • misalignment • overload • relational mismatch Effects: • elongated role arcs • shadow role emergence • role inversion risk

3.3 Signal Drift Field Triggered by: • distortion • overload • misinterpretation Effects: • sharp signal beams • hard spikes • collapse signal activation

3.4 Environmental Drift Field Triggered by: • mismatch • saturation • instability Effects: • turbulent gradients • implosion vectors • collapse boundary deformation

  1. Drift Field Behavior 4.1 Expansion Fields expand when: • pressure increases • thresholds are crossed • vectors align Expansion increases collapse risk.

4.2 Contraction Fields contract when: • pressure drops • recovery begins • integration occurs Contraction indicates stabilization.

4.3 Convergence When two fields converge: • drift accelerates • collapse becomes likely • identity destabilizes Convergence is a critical warning state.

4.4 Divergence When fields diverge: • drift slows • collapse risk decreases • recovery becomes possible Divergence is a recovery indicator.

  1. Drift Fields and Collapse Drift Fields are the precursors to collapse. Collapse occurs when: • curvature spikes • gradients steepen • vectors converge inward • hard thresholds activate Collapse Fields are simply Drift Fields that have exceeded tolerance. See: [[Collapse Physics]]
