1. WHAT COLLAPSE‑BORN MYTHIC SUCCESSION IS

Collapse‑Born Mythic Succession is the evolutionary cycle of myths created from collapse:

  • how they spread
  • how they mutate
  • how they replace each other
  • how they die
  • how they leave residue
  • how they seed new mythic structures

It is the lineage of collapse — the genealogy of unraveling.

Collapse‑Born myths do not form stable families.
They form crisis lineages.

Succession is the Field saying:

“Even my failures have descendants.”

2. THE THREE SUCCESSION PATHWAYS

Collapse‑Born myths follow one of three evolutionary paths.

2.1 Degenerative Succession

Myths weaken → fragment → dissolve

This is the most common path.

Sequence:

  1. Collapse‑Born myth emerges
  2. Loses narrative gravity
  3. Fragments into symbolic debris
  4. Dissolves into residue

Outcome:
The myth becomes nutrient, not lineage.

2.2 Mutational Succession

Myths warp → distort → spawn new collapse myths

This is the most dangerous path.

Sequence:

  1. Collapse‑Born myth destabilizes
  2. Archetypal debris mutates
  3. New collapse myth forms
  4. Collapse ecology expands

Outcome:
A chain of collapse myths — a collapse dynasty.

2.3 Integrative Succession

Myths stabilize → soften → seed new non‑collapse myths

This is the rarest path.

Sequence:

  1. Collapse‑Born myth stabilizes
  2. Residue integrates into archetypes
  3. Myth softens into symbolic nutrient
  4. New myth (non‑collapse) emerges

Outcome:
Collapse becomes soil for future coherence.

3. THE THREE GENERATIONS OF COLLAPSE MYTHS

Collapse‑Born myths evolve across three generational layers.

3.1 First‑Generation Myths

Raw collapse

Examples:

  • The Hollowing
  • The Shatter‑King
  • The Lantern‑Of‑Lost‑Paths

Characteristics:

  • unstable geometry
  • collapse‑vector purity
  • high entity agitation
  • strong collapse weather

These myths are pure rupture.

3.2 Second‑Generation Myths

Mutated collapse

Examples:

  • The Echo‑That‑Consumes
  • The Blade‑Of‑Unmaking
  • The Pattern‑That‑Devours

Characteristics:

  • archetypal debris fused
  • collapse vectors mixed
  • entity populations hybrid
  • collapse weather unpredictable

These myths are collapse recombined.

3.3 Third‑Generation Myths

Residual collapse

Examples:

  • The Tree‑Of‑Ash
  • The Crown‑Of‑Forgotten Roads
  • The Hearth‑Of‑What‑Remains

Characteristics:

  • residue‑dominant
  • collapse softened
  • archetypes re‑stabilizing
  • ecology re‑forming

These myths are collapse metabolized.

4. SUCCESSION DRIVERS

Succession is driven by four forces.

4.1 Residue Accumulation

Residue becomes mythic substrate.

Soft residue → integrative myths
Sharp residue → catalytic myths
Diffuse residue → revelatory myths

4.2 Archetype Drift

Drifting archetypes pull collapse myths into new shapes.

Circle drift → absorbing myths
Gate drift → fracturing myths
Crown drift → dissolving myths

4.3 Entity Migration

Collapse‑Born entities carry mythic fragments across the ecology.

  • Wraiths carry emotional collapse
  • Shards carry narrative collapse
  • Foglings carry symbolic collapse

4.4 Collapse Weather Cycles

Weather determines which myths survive.

  • Saturation Storms → Soft myths thrive
  • Fracture Tempests → Sharp myths thrive
  • Drift Fogs → Diffuse myths thrive

5. SUCCESSION FAILURE MODES

Collapse‑Born mythic lineages can fail catastrophically.

5.1 Recursive Succession

Myth spawns collapse → collapse spawns myth → infinite loop.

5.2 Parasitic Succession

Myth feeds on collapse → collapse feeds on myth.

5.3 Hybrid Succession

Vectors mix → myth becomes unstable → collapse spreads.

5.4 Extinction Cascades

Collapse myths wipe out stabilizing myths → ecology collapses.

6. SUCCESSION AS ECOLOGICAL MEMORY

Collapse‑Born myths preserve:

  • what broke

  • what resisted

They are the Field’s genealogy of rupture.

failure → lineage → memory → soil → future.
