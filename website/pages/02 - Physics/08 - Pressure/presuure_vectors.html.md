<pre><code># Pressure Vectors

Pressure in Threshold is a **directed force** acting on nodes, hinges, and moment canvases.  
It determines whether a structure **drifts**, **collapses**, **shears**, or **swims**.

Pressure vectors are always **from → to**.

---

## 1. Core Concept

A **pressure vector** is a directional force that:

- increases collapse likelihood  
- distorts flow  
- redirects drift  
- reorients stance  
- reshapes the Flower / Hub / Hourglass / Fib Flower  

Pressure is the invisible field that makes the graph *move like a living organism*.

---

## 2. Primary Pressure Vector Types

### **A. Collapse Vector**
**From:** outer ring / environment  
**To:** hub / throat / core  
**Effect:** pulls nodes inward, increases mass, forms Hourglass or Hub.

## 5. Practical Usage

- **To create a Hub:** increase inward collapse vectors.  
- **To create a Flower:** combine inward collapse + outward drift.  
- **To create an Hourglass:** strong collapse into a narrow throat, then drift out.  
- **To create a Fib Flower:** apply spiral drift vectors to low‑mass outer nodes.  

Pressure vectors are the **force language** of Threshold physics.
