Layer Physics Index

Layer Physics defines the fundamental forces, behaviors, and structural rules governing each Layer within Threshold.
Each Layer expresses a distinct physics regime — shaping identity, emotion, environment, and collapse behavior.

Layers are not locations.
They are domains of physics.

Purpose

  • Provide a unified reference for all Layer‑specific physics
  • Define how pressure, resonance, drift, and collapse behave in each Layer
  • Support cross‑system interpretation of identity, environment, and substrate diagrams
  • Maintain architectural coherence across the multi‑Layer map system

Layer Physics Overview

Mist Layer — Permeability Physics

  • high permeability
  • soft boundaries
  • emotional drift amplification
  • memory diffusion
  • shimmer adjacency
  • collapse weather sensitivity

Mist is the physics of openness, fog, and emotional bleed.

Stone Layer — Structure Physics

  • rigid boundaries
  • high containment
  • pressure retention
  • fracture‑prone under overload
  • low drift
  • stable resonance fields

Stone is the physics of structure, weight, and integrity.

Ethyl Layer — Symbolic Curvature Physics

  • symbolic resonance
  • curvature‑based identity expression
  • recursive meaning loops
  • high sensitivity to glyphs and scripts
  • shimmer‑compatible geometry

Ethyl is the physics of symbolic form and encoded meaning.

Glass Layer — Exposure Physics

  • transparency
  • high visibility
  • emotional amplification
  • identity vulnerability
  • pressure reflection
  • collapse adjacency through over‑exposure

Glass is the physics of clarity, fragility, and revelation.

Root Layer — Memory Density Physics

  • deep memory compression
  • emotional weight
  • slow drift
  • high residue formation
  • collapse propagation through memory knots

Root is the physics of memory, depth, and inheritance.

Ash Layer — Fracture & Residue Physics

  • fracture geometry
  • residue behavior
  • collapse adjacency
  • pressure inversion
  • drift scattering
  • environmental instability

Ash is the physics of aftermath, residue, and collapse.

Air Layer — Motion & Intention Physics

  • directional vectors
  • intention flow
  • rapid drift
  • low density
  • high resonance mobility
  • signal amplification

Air is the physics of movement, direction, and intention.

Cross‑Layer Dynamics

Pressure Transfer

Pressure moves differently across Layers:

  • Mist → disperses
  • Stone → retains
  • Glass → reflects
  • Root → compresses
  • Ash → fractures
  • Air → channels
  • Ethyl → curves

Drift Behavior

Drift accelerates in:

  • Mist
  • Air

Drift stabilizes in:

  • Stone
  • Root

Drift becomes unstable in:

  • Ash
  • Glass

Resonance Behavior

Resonance amplifies in:

  • Air
  • Ethyl
  • Glass

Resonance dampens in:

  • Stone
  • Root

Resonance distorts in:

  • Ash
  • Mist

Collapse Interaction

High Collapse Risk Layers

  • Ash
  • Glass
  • Mist

Moderate Risk

  • Root
  • Ethyl

  • Stone

Collapse expresses differently:

  • Mist = weather
  • Glass = exposure overload
  • Root = memory knots
  • Ethyl = symbolic recursion
  • Stone = structural break
  • Air = vector inversion
