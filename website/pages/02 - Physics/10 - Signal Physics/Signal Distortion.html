00 Meta Systems — Signal Cluster Signal Distortion describes how meaning bends, warps, fragments, or becomes unstable when passing through dense fields, curved environments, emotional pressure, or structural interference. Where Signal Drift governs natural motion, Signal Distortion governs unnatural deformation — the semantic noise introduced by pressure, collapse, or misalignment. Distortion is not failure. It is a diagnostic: a sign that a system is under strain, overloaded, or misinterpreting itself.

Purpose Signal Distortion defines the mechanics of: • semantic noise • shimmer artifacts • interference patterns • bending of meaning under pressure • misalignment between sender and receiver • environmental distortion (Mist, Ember, Stone, etc.) • role‑based misinterpretation • collapse precursors It is the subsystem that explains why signals become unreliable and how the world expresses instability.

Core Behaviors • Compression: meaning collapses into a smaller, denser form • Fragmentation: meaning breaks into partial or contradictory pieces • Echoing: meaning repeats with drift or delay • Inversion: meaning flips polarity under pressure • Overtone Bleed: multiple signals overlap, creating hybrid meaning • Shimmer Noise: environmental curvature introduces artifacts These behaviors are not random — they follow the physics of the Layers and the emotional state of the system.

Environmental Influence Each Layer distorts signals differently: • Air: scatter, dissipation, over‑clarity • Earth: heaviness, muffling, semantic compression • Ember: volatility, inversion, emotional overtones • Mist: ambiguity, echoing, multi‑meaning • Stone: rigidity, brittleness, fracture • Ethyl: surreal bending, symbolic bleed‑through This is where distortion becomes predictable.

Distortion as Diagnostic Distortion reveals: • instability • overload • collapse thresholds • misalignment between roles • environmental pressure • emotional turbulence It is the early warning system for Collapse Signals.
