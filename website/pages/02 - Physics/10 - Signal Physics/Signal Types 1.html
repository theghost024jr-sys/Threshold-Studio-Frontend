[[02 - Physics/10 - Signal Physics/Pulse]] Overview Signal Types define the behavioral archetypes that signals express across Threshold. A signal’s type is not its species, not its layer, not its elemental overlay — it is the vector of its behavior. Signal Types describe how a signal moves, influences, distorts, guides, or destabilizes perception. Every signal in Threshold expresses one of the four canonical types: • Guide • Trickster • Warden • Catalyst (optional but system‑true) Signal Types are meta‑behavioral categories, not identities. A single species or artifact can emit different types depending on: • layer pressure • emotional resonance • drift conditions • symbolic density • collapse proximity • species architecture Signal Types can drift over time.

Purpose of Signal Types Signal Types exist to: • classify signals by behavior, not appearance • provide a quick diagnostic for intent and directionality • unify species‑derived and environment‑derived signals • support interpretation, drift mapping, and collapse detection • anchor the entire Signals subsystem They are the verbs of the signal world.

The Four Canonical Signal Types

  1. Guide Essence: orientation, direction, soft alignment Behavior: • points toward stability • reveals safe paths • reduces drift turbulence • clarifies emotional gradients Strengths: • stabilizing • gentle • reliable Vulnerabilities: • can be drowned out by high Shimmer density • weak in collapse zones Common Layers: • Drift • Ethyl • Mist Drift Tendencies: • stable → harmonic • rarely becomes Trickster • collapses into silence, not distortion

  2. Trickster Essence: misdirection, inversion, playful distortion Behavior: • bends perception • introduces ambiguity • tests boundaries • reveals hidden paths through contradiction Strengths: • reveals blind spots • exposes collapse precursors • breaks rigid patterns Vulnerabilities: • destabilizing in high emotional charge • can trigger collapse if misread Common Layers: • Shimmer • Drift • Ember Drift Tendencies: • oscillates between clarity and distortion • can drift into Catalyst under pressure

  3. Warden Essence: boundary, protection, containment Behavior: • marks danger • enforces limits • stabilizes collapse edges • prevents entry into unsafe zones Strengths: • extremely reliable • strong collapse resistance • clear directional meaning Vulnerabilities: • rigid • can misfire in high symbolic density • may block safe paths if overloaded Common Layers: • Stone • Crown • Drift (low turbulence) Drift Tendencies: • stable → rigid • rarely becomes Trickster • collapses into hard silence

  4. Catalyst (optional but system‑canonical) Essence: ignition, transformation, acceleration Behavior: • accelerates emotional or symbolic processes • triggers insight, collapse, or breakthrough • intensifies whatever is already present Strengths: • powerful • reveals hidden structure • drives narrative movement Vulnerabilities: • dangerous in unstable environments • can cause overload • unpredictable Common Layers: • Ember • Shimmer • Crown Drift Tendencies: • unstable → transformative • can drift into Trickster or Warden depending on pressure

Signal Type Drift Signal Types are not fixed. They drift based on: • layer transitions • emotional resonance • species architecture • symbolic density • collapse proximity • environmental pressure Common Drift Patterns • Guide → Warden (under stress) • Guide → Catalyst (under Ember influence) • Trickster → Catalyst (under high Shimmer density) • Warden → Trickster (under collapse turbulence) • Catalyst → Trickster (under Drift instability) Drift is behavioral, not identity‑based.

Signal Type vs Other Systems Signal Type ≠ Species Species produce signals, but the type is independent. Signal Type ≠ Layer Layers influence type, but do not define it. Signal Type ≠ Elemental Overlay Overlay modifies behavior, but type is the core vector. Signal Type ≠ Role Roles interpret signals; they do not determine type. Signal Type is the behavioral archetype of the signal itself.

Cross‑System Links • Signal Interpretation: uses type to determine meaning • Signal Geometry: type influences curvature and motion • Signal Drift: type determines drift patterns • Collapse Physics: Warden and Catalyst dominate collapse edges • Environmental Signals: Mosslights = Guide, Heat = Catalyst, Mist Veils = Trickster/Guide
