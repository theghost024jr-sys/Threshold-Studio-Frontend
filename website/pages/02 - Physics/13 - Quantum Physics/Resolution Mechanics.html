Definition Resolution Mechanics govern how motion, tension, drift, and activated vectors come to completion within the Field. They determine how and when force settles, transforms, or returns to equilibrium. Resolution is not optional. Resolution is an invariant requirement of the system.

Purpose To ensure that: • no force remains unresolved • no drift persists indefinitely • no threshold stays half‑crossed • no gift remains unbalanced • no role stays in an unstable state • no consequence loops remain open Resolution Mechanics guarantee closure and prevent runaway force.

Resolution Pathways Every activated vector resolves through one of the following pathways:

  1. Completion The action reaches its natural end. Examples: • a gift is fully exchanged • a role completes its cycle • a threshold is fully crossed Completion is the cleanest form of resolution.

  2. Transformation The force changes form and becomes a new state. Examples: • drift becomes a crossing • tension becomes a gift • imbalance becomes lineage motion Transformation resolves by changing the nature of the force.

  3. Propagation The force moves to another node or vector. Examples: • reciprocity returns force to the giver • lineage passes force down a chain • zones redirect motion Propagation resolves by moving the force elsewhere.

  4. Dissipation The force resolves through cost. Examples: • attention fades • energy is spent • a role collapses Dissipation resolves by expenditure.

  5. Collapse The system forces resolution when a state cannot be maintained. Examples: • unresolved drift collapses a role • unbalanced gifts collapse into tension • incomplete crossings collapse into drift Collapse is the system enforcing resolution.

Resolution Sequence When a vector activates, resolution follows this sequence:

  1. Detect Unresolved Force The Field identifies tension, drift, imbalance, or open loops.
  2. Apply Invariants Invariants constrain how resolution can occur: • cost must be paid • transformation must occur • identity must remain coherent • consequence must be honored • motion must be conserved
  3. Determine Pathway The system selects the appropriate resolution pathway: • completion • transformation • propagation • dissipation • collapse
  4. Execute Resolution The Consequence Engine applies the pathway to the active force.
  5. Update Matrix The Matrix of Force records the new state.
  6. Return to Stability The Field settles until the next activation.

Properties of Resolution Inevitable All force must resolve. Nothing remains open. Conserved Resolution does not erase force; it transforms or redirects it. Deterministic Given the same conditions, resolution follows the same pathway. Contextual Resolution depends on: • vector type • node state • zone • lineage • role • drift level Recursive Resolution can trigger new activation events.

Function Resolution Mechanics: • prevent infinite drift • maintain system stability • ensure closure • enforce invariants • complete consequence loops • return the Field to neutral • keep the Matrix coherent Without resolution, the system would accumulate unresolved force until collapse. With resolution, the system remains alive, stable, and interpretable.

Relationship to Other Meta Files • Binding Law → universal participation • Field → environment of motion • Vectors → directional forces • Invariants → stabilizing laws • Matrix of Force → structural map • Consequence Engine → interpreter of motion • Activation Logic → triggers of motion • Resolution Mechanics → completion of motion Resolution Mechanics close the loop. They are the final piece of the core engine.
