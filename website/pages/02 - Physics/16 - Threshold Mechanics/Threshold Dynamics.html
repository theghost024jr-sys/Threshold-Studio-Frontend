Definition Threshold Dynamics govern how nodes, roles, and forces behave when approaching, crossing, or retreating from a boundary between states. A threshold is not a line. A threshold is a zone of transformation. Crossing a threshold always changes something. Failing to cross one always generates drift.

Purpose To ensure that: • transitions between states are coherent • crossings produce predictable transformation • hesitation generates interpretable tension • the Field remains dynamic • roles and zones cannot be entered or exited without consequence • the system honors the physics of change Thresholds are the system’s gates of transformation.

Types of Thresholds

  1. State Thresholds Boundaries between internal states of a node. Examples: • baseline → active • active → tension • tension → transitional • transitional → resolved These are the most fundamental thresholds.

  2. Role Thresholds Boundaries between holding, releasing, or collapsing a role. Examples: • assuming a role • relinquishing a role • failing a role • inheriting a role Roles cannot shift without crossing a threshold.

  3. Zone Thresholds Boundaries between environmental contexts. Examples: • entering a new zone • leaving a zone • being expelled from a zone Zones exert force on thresholds.

  4. Lineage Thresholds Boundaries between generational or inherited states. Examples: • invoking lineage • accepting inheritance • breaking lineage • transmitting unresolved drift Lineage thresholds are recursive.

  5. Gift Thresholds Boundaries between giving, receiving, and completing an exchange. Examples: • initiating a gift • accepting a gift • completing reciprocity • failing to reciprocate Gift thresholds are high‑consequence.

Threshold Variables Each threshold tracks: • Proximity (how close the node is to crossing) • Pressure (how much force is pushing toward crossing) • Resistance (how much force is preventing crossing) • Cost (what must be paid to cross) • Transformation Load (how much change crossing will produce) • Collapse Risk (likelihood of forced resolution) These variables determine threshold behavior.

Threshold Phases

  1. Approach Phase The node moves toward the boundary. Vectors begin to distort. Drift may accumulate.
  2. Contact Phase The node touches the threshold. Activation Logic fires. Invariants constrain the motion.
  3. Crossing Phase The node passes through the boundary. Transformation occurs. Identity is preserved but state changes.
  4. Stabilization Phase The node settles into the new state. Resolution Mechanics complete the transition. The Field updates.
  5. Retreat Phase The node moves away without crossing. Drift accumulates. Collapse becomes possible.

Threshold Behaviors

  1. Threshold Pull The threshold exerts force on the node. Approach becomes easier the closer you get.
  2. Threshold Resistance Crossing requires cost. Resistance increases with transformation load.
  3. Threshold Snap If pressure exceeds resistance, the threshold forces crossing.
  4. Threshold Collapse If drift exceeds stability, the threshold collapses and forces resolution.
  5. Threshold Echo Crossing one threshold influences others. Thresholds are interconnected.

Consequences of Threshold Interaction Crossing • transformation • new vectors activate • drift resolves or transforms • roles shift • zones reconfigure • lineage updates Hesitation • drift accumulates • tension increases • collapse risk rises Retreat • drift becomes active • reciprocity distorts • identity strains Collapse • forced resolution • role failure • zone expulsion • lineage fracture Thresholds are high‑stakes by design.

Function Threshold Dynamics: • govern all state transitions • enforce the physics of change • ensure transformation is coherent • prevent unresolved liminality • maintain the integrity of roles and zones • drive the system’s most meaningful motion • keep the Field alive and evolving Without thresholds, the system would be static. With thresholds, the system becomes a world.

Relationship to Other Meta Files • State Model → thresholds define transitions • Drift Mechanics → hesitation generates drift • Activation Logic → thresholds trigger activation • Consequence Engine → interprets threshold events • Invariants → constrain threshold behavior • Matrix of Force → maps threshold interactions • Field → thresholds shape environmental force Threshold Dynamics are the architecture of transformation.
