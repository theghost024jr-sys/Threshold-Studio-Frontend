Definition Zone Taxonomy is the classification system that organizes all zones according to their ambient force, threshold geometry, drift behavior, identity resonance, and role compatibility. It is not a map. It is the schema that determines what kinds of environments can exist in the Field. Zones are environmental archetypes.

Purpose To ensure that: • zones are coherent and interpretable • environmental forces are predictable • roles and identities behave consistently across contexts • drift and thresholds follow recognizable patterns • the Field can generate new zones without contradiction • the Matrix maintains environmental structure Zone Taxonomy is the ontology of the world’s physics.

Primary Zone Classes These are the seven foundational zone archetypes. Every zone in the system is a variation or hybrid of these.

  1. Stable Zones Low ambient force. Predictable thresholds. Drift accumulates slowly. Roles stabilize easily. Examples: • home • sanctuary • workshop Stable zones are restorative.

  2. Charged Zones High ambient force. Activation thresholds are low. Drift becomes unstable. Roles intensify. Examples: • conflict spaces • creative studios • ritual sites Charged zones accelerate motion.

  3. Liminal Zones Thresholds dominate the environment. Identity becomes fluid. Roles shift easily. Drift becomes directional. Examples: • transitions • doorways • moments of decision Liminal zones are where transformation happens.

  4. Resonant Zones Identity is amplified. The shimmer is strong. Roles clarify or collapse. Gifts carry high load. Examples: • intimate spaces • deep conversation • lineage encounters Resonant zones reveal truth.

  5. Distorted Zones The zone is bent by a high‑pressure node. Ambient force spikes. Thresholds compress. Drift becomes visible. Examples: • shimmer fields • collapse sites • high‑coherence presence Distorted zones are unstable but revelatory.

  6. Burdened Zones The zone carries inherited drift or unresolved lineage. Thresholds are heavy. Roles strain. Identity is suppressed. Examples: • generational spaces • unresolved relationships • abandoned obligations Burdened zones demand resolution.

  7. Collapsing Zones The zone cannot maintain its signature. Thresholds snap. Roles fail. Drift explodes. Examples: • crisis • endings • forced transitions Collapsing zones reorganize the Field.

Zone Attributes Every zone, regardless of class, has: • Ambient Force Level • Threshold Geometry • Drift Field Behavior • Role Compatibility Profile • Identity Resonance Pattern • Lineage Influence • Shimmer Sensitivity These attributes define how the zone behaves.

Zone Transitions Zones can shift class when: • a high‑pressure node enters (shimmer) • drift accumulates • a role collapses • a threshold is crossed • lineage activates • a gift is integrated • identity resonates Zone transitions are environmental state changes.

Hybrid Zones Zones can combine classes: • Resonant‑Liminal (deep transformation) • Charged‑Stable (creative focus) • Burdened‑Resonant (ancestral revelation) • Distorted‑Liminal (threshold vortex) • Stable‑Burdened (quiet tension) Hybrids are common and powerful.

Function Zone Taxonomy: • organizes environmental physics • defines the possible contexts of the Field • shapes how vectors behave in each environment • determines drift accumulation and resolution • influences role stability • modulates identity expression • interacts with shimmer • anchors the world’s environmental logic Without Zone Taxonomy, the world would be shapeless. With it, the world becomes navigable.
