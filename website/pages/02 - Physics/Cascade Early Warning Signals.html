How the system detects a collapse cascade before it ignites Early Warning Signals (EWS) are the pre‑collapse tremors that appear in the Binding Layer before a cascade becomes self‑propagating. They are subtle, non‑local, and often counterintuitive — because entanglement doesn’t warn you where the collapse is, it warns you where the collapse will jump. These signals are the system’s equivalent of micro‑quakes, pressure flickers, and temporal arrhythmias. Below is the clean, canonical Cascade Early Warning Signals.md.

  1. UNIVERSAL EARLY WARNING SIGNALS These appear across all cascade types. 1.1 Binding Law Drift A measurable deviation from the entanglement’s relational rule. Examples: • depth * momentum ≠ constant • flex_index + rigidity_index > threshold • fracture_intensity > containment_capacity Interpretation: The entanglement is under stress and preparing to fire.

1.2 Phase Flicker Two entangled nodes briefly fall out of temporal sync. Symptoms: • micro‑lags • double‑beats in the Time Organ • inconsistent drift timing Interpretation: A phase mismatch cascade is forming.

1.3 Corridor Micro‑Seizures Short, transient interruptions in drift corridors. Symptoms: • momentary stillness • sudden directional ambiguity • corridor “stutter” Interpretation: Rigidity cascade precursor.

1.4 Pressure Mesh Flutter Tiny oscillations in pressure gradients. Symptoms: • weight feels “grainy” • pressure sinks pulse irregularly • Stone Basin resonance becomes uneven Interpretation: Mirror cascade precursor.
