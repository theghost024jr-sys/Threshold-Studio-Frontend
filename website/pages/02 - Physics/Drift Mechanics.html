Drift Mechanics

Invariant Anchor This module expresses how drift behaves when cohesion is under pressure. See: [[Invariant00 – Cohesion Under Pressure]]

Definition

Drift is the passive movement of nodes, clusters, and Flowers through the cohesion field due to pressure gradients, resonance patterns, and spatial deformation. Drift is not intentional motion — it is pressure‑shaped flow.

Drift is the Garden’s background motion, the constant shifting of structure within dynamic space.

Core Mechanical Laws

1. Drift follows pressure gradients

Drift always moves:

  • from high pressure → low pressure

  • from contradiction → alignment

  • from unstable zones → stable basins

Drift is the Garden’s pressure equilibration.

2. Drift increases when cohesion weakens

When cohesion is low:

  • drift accelerates

  • boundaries soften

  • clusters slide

  • Flowers wobble

When cohesion is high:

  • drift slows

  • structures stabilize

  • gradients sharpen

Drift is cohesion‑dependent.

3. Drift is shaped by spatial deformation

When space compresses:

  • drift slows

  • structures pack tightly

  • gradients steepen

When space expands:

  • drift increases

  • structures spread

  • gradients flatten

Drift is geometry‑responsive.

4. Drift interacts with resonance

Resonance can:

  • accelerate drift

  • suppress drift

  • redirect drift

  • synchronize drift across Flowers

Drift is resonance‑guided.

5. Drift precedes collapse

Before collapse:

  • drift spikes

  • boundaries destabilize

  • contradictions accumulate

  • identity diffuses

Drift is the early warning signal of structural failure.

6. Drift reorganizes identity

After drift:

  • clusters re‑sort

  • contradictions eject

  • new basins form

  • identity condenses

Drift is the Garden’s pre‑identity sorting mechanism.

Drift Modes (structural, not lore)

  • Linear Drift — steady movement along a gradient

  • Turbulent Drift — chaotic movement caused by contradictions

  • Resonant Drift — synchronized movement across structures

  • Collapse Drift — rapid movement preceding failure

  • Field Drift — diffuse movement in low‑cohesion space

These modes emerge from pressure and resonance.

Behavior Across the Origin Cycle

Distortion

Drift becomes chaotic; contradictions push structures around.

Drift

Drift accelerates; boundaries soften; identity diffuses.

Field

Drift spreads; structures lose definition.

Collapse

Drift spikes; collapse waves propagate.

Identity

Drift slows; new basins form; structures condense.

Role

Drift aligns to directional cohesion.

Signal

Drift stabilizes; resonance channels form.

Summary Rule

Drift is pressure‑shaped motion that:

  • follows gradients

  • increases when cohesion weakens

  • responds to spatial deformation

  • interacts with resonance

  • precedes collapse

  • reorganizes identity

Drift is the Garden’s background motion and pre‑collapse dynamics.
