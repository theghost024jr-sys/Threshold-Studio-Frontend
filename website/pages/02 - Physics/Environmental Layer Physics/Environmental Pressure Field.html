Purpose The Environmental Pressure Field defines how place exerts force on identity, drift, and collapse. Every environment expresses a unique pressure signature, but all pressure behaviors follow the same underlying physics defined here. This file is the template that all environment nodes inherit from.

  1. Pressure Components Environmental pressure is composed of five interacting forces: 1.1 Gradient Pressure Directional force created by: • heat rising • cold sinking • brine descending • wind shear • canopy compression Determines movement bias and drift direction.

1.2 Density Pressure How tightly space is packed: • thick forest canopy • deep water • ruins with narrow corridors • brinicle tubes • caves High density increases: • identity compression • collapse susceptibility • drift resistance

1.3 Distortion Pressure How much the environment bends or warps: • heat shimmer • ice refraction • echo chambers • fog diffusion • spiral brinicles High distortion affects: • signal clarity • perception • drift curvature

1.4 Memory Pressure How much the environment remembers: • old forests • ruins • deep ice • sediment layers High memory pressure stabilizes identity but slows drift.

1.5 Recovery Pressure How much the environment restores: • groves • oases • warm pockets • sheltered coves High recovery pressure reduces collapse risk.

  1. Pressure Expression Types Pressure expresses itself in four modes: 2.1 Static Pressure Constant, unchanging force. Examples: • tundra cold • desert heat • deepwater weight

2.2 Dynamic Pressure Shifts over time or with movement. Examples: • tide • wind • drifting fog

2.3 Rotational Pressure Pressure that spirals or curves. Examples: • brinicle spirals • whirlpools • dust devils

2.4 Collapse Pressure Pressure that accelerates collapse. Examples: • brittle ice • unstable ruins • volcanic vents

  1. Pressure → Drift Interaction Pressure modifies drift in four ways: 3.1 Drift Acceleration High gradient pressure speeds drift: • downhill • downward brine • heat updrafts

3.2 Drift Resistance High density slows drift: • forests • caves • ruins

3.3 Drift Curvature Distortion bends drift: • spirals • mirages • echo corridors

3.4 Drift Compression Memory pressure tightens drift: • old forests • deep ice • sediment layers

  1. Pressure → Collapse Interaction Pressure determines collapse style: • brittle collapse (ice) • implosive collapse (caves) • dispersive collapse (desert) • recursive collapse (ruins) • spiral collapse (brinicle spiral) High pressure increases collapse risk.

  2. Pressure → Identity Interaction Pressure affects identity stability: • high memory pressure stabilizes • high distortion destabilizes • high density compresses • high gradient pulls identity off‑center Identity is most stable in: • groves • warm pockets • sheltered ruins Identity is most unstable in: • spirals • brittle ice • high distortion zones

  3. Pressure → Signals Environmental pressure can generate Environmental Signals when: • pressure becomes patterned • drift becomes rhythmic • collapse leaves residue • recovery creates stillness Examples: • Winter Crown • Heat Mirage • Flood Pulse • Ashfall • Stillwatch (if environmental) These are Signals, not environments.
