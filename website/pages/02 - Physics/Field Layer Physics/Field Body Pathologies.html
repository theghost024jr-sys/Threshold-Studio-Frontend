FIELD BODY PATHOLOGIES How the Bound Grid fails, fractures, or distorts under stress A pathology emerges when two or more fault lines activate simultaneously, or when a single node’s imbalance propagates through the Field Body faster than the system can compensate. There are seven primary pathologies, each corresponding to a specific kind of systemic failure. Let’s map them cleanly.

  1. VECTOR SHEAR Directional forces tear the lattice Occurs when: • upward vectors overpower downward • forward vectors overpower backward • inward vectors overpower outward Typical triggers: • High Branch overload • Wind Channel momentum spike • Recursive Hollow inward pull Symptoms: • sudden directional distortion • loss of orientation • emotional vertigo • accelerated drift Propagation pattern: Spreads along the vector lattice, destabilizing adjacent nodes.

  2. PRESSURE CASCADE Weight collapses through the mesh Occurs when: • a high‑pressure node over‑compresses • a low‑pressure node fails to disperse • a variable‑pressure node twists under load Typical triggers: • Deep Ice over‑compression • Ruins pressure fracture • Brinicle Spiral torque spike Symptoms: • rapid sinking or crushing sensation • emotional shutdown • temporal drag • collapse of stabilizing nodes Propagation pattern: Falls downward through the pressure mesh, pulling other nodes with it.

  3. RESONANCE SPIKE Meaning overloads the nervous system Occurs when: • a resonance cluster overwhelms its counter‑cluster • quiet becomes too deep • fracture becomes too sharp • recursion becomes too tight Typical triggers: • Shatter Zone resonance break • Recursive Hollow loop amplification • Quiet Grove over‑absorption Symptoms: • emotional overwhelm • meaning distortion • narrative rupture • identity fragmentation Propagation pattern: Spreads outward through the resonance web, often rapidly.

  4. DRIFT RUNAWAY State‑change accelerates uncontrollably Occurs when: • collapse pathways activate without recovery • drift exceeds vector‑pressure alignment • recursive drift loops form Typical triggers: • Plains → Shatter Zone collapse • Recursive Hollow → Ruins loop • High Branch → Deep Snowfield fall Symptoms: • escalating instability • emotional spiraling • loss of grounding • collapse cascade Propagation pattern: Moves along drift corridors, often jumping nodes.

  5. QUIET LOCK Stillness becomes immobilization Occurs when: • quiet resonance absorbs too much • inward vectors overpower outward • pressure sinks exceed capacity Typical triggers: • Quiet Grove over‑quieting • Deep Snowfield soft‑sink • Old Forest temporal drag Symptoms: • emotional freeze • inability to act • looping introspection • identity flattening Propagation pattern: Spreads inward through quiet clusters, pulling nodes into stillness.

  6. FRACTURE BLOOM Break resonance spreads like a shockwave Occurs when: • fracture nodes exceed their resonance threshold • pressure gradients snap • vector continuity breaks Typical triggers: • Shatter Zone overload • Ruins resonance fracture • Plains → Shatter Zone vector break Symptoms: • sudden emotional rupture • fragmentation of meaning • collapse of narrative continuity • sharp, brittle drift Propagation pattern: Radiates outward from fracture nodes in a shock pattern.

  7. FREEZE‑TWIST LOCK Cold and rotation trap the field in a locked state Occurs when: • rotational pressure meets frozen pressure • twist cannot resolve into motion • freeze cannot resolve into stillness Typical triggers: • Brinicle Spiral → Deep Ice transition • cold cluster overload • rotational drift spike Symptoms: • sharp clarity followed by shutdown • emotional paralysis • pressure spikes • identity compression Propagation pattern: Locks nodes in place, preventing drift or recovery.

THE PATHOLOGY NETWORK How failures spread through the Field Body When mapped together, the seven pathologies form a tri‑ring system: Inner Ring (Quiet Pathologies) • Quiet Lock • Temporal Drag • Soft Sink Middle Ring (Directional Pathologies) • Vector Shear • Drift Runaway Outer Ring (Fracture/Freeze Pathologies) • Fracture Bloom • Freeze‑Twist Lock • Pressure Cascade This tri‑ring structure mirrors the Field Body’s anatomy: • core (quiet, weight, memory) • midline (movement, direction) • perimeter (fracture, freeze, twist) Pathologies always begin at the perimeter and move inward unless intercepted.
