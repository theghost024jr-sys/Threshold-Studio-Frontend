Field Dynamics

Subsystem Type: Field Behavior Engine

Field Dynamics defines how fields behave over time and under pressure. Where Field.md defines what a field is, Field Dynamics defines how a field moves.

Every field in Threshold expresses:

  • curvature

  • pressure

  • drift

  • deformation

  • collapse

  • resonance

  • interaction patterns

Field Dynamics is one of the core engines of the Physics Cluster.

I. Physics Layer — Governing Field Behavior

1. Definition

A field is not static. It bends, compresses, expands, distorts, resonates, and collapses based on:

  • tension

  • density

  • curvature

  • drift

  • identity stability

  • environmental pressure

Field Dynamics describes the motion architecture of fields.

2. Field Structure

Every field has three structural components:

2.1 Field Core

The stable center of the field. Defines identity and baseline behavior.

2.2 Field Gradient

Directional pressure that shapes drift and motion.

2.3 Field Boundary

The outer edge where the field interacts with other fields or environments. Fields are continuous, not discrete.

3. Canonical Field Behaviors

3.1 Curvature

Fields bend under pressure. Curvature types:

  • shallow

  • tight

  • spiral

  • oscillating

  • compressive

Curvature determines drift arc shape.

3.2 Expansion

Occurs when:

  • pressure decreases

  • identity stabilizes

  • recovery signals activate

Expansion increases radius, drift distance, and influence.

3.3 Contraction

Occurs when:

  • pressure increases

  • collapse signals activate

  • identity destabilizes

Contraction increases density, drift compression, and collapse risk.

3.4 Distortion

Occurs when:

  • distortion pressure rises

  • Mist‑aligned signals appear

  • Trickster geometry interacts

Distortion produces oscillation, curvature, and collapse spirals.

3.5 Stabilization

Occurs when:

  • memory pressure rises

  • Stillwatch or Deep Quiet activate

  • identity re‑coheres

Stabilization straightens drift arcs and reduces curvature.

4. Field Pressure Types

4.1 Gradient Pressure

Directional force that pulls drift along a path. Produces long arcs and predictable motion.

4.2 Density Pressure

Compression force that slows drift. Produces tight arcs and identity compression.

4.3 Distortion Pressure

Curvature force that bends drift. Produces oscillation and spirals.

4.4 Memory Pressure

Stabilizing force that smooths drift. Prevents collapse.

5. Field Drift (Physics)

Fields generate drift in three modes:

5.1 Passive Drift

Drift follows natural gradient. Common in plains, stable forests, low‑pressure zones.

5.2 Active Drift

Field actively pushes drift. Common in storms, spiral zones, Ember/Mist interactions.

5.3 Resonant Drift

Drift synchronizes with field oscillation. Common in Listener roles, rhythmic environments.

6. Field Interaction Patterns

Fields interact using spatial analogs of role/signal patterns:

  • Alignment — gradients reinforce

  • Tension — gradients oppose

  • Overlap — fields blend

  • Opposition — fields push apart

  • Resonance — curvature synchronizes

  • Interference — fields distort each other

7. Field Collapse

Fields collapse in predictable modes:

7.1 Brittle Collapse

Boundary fractures inward. (Stone‑aligned)

7.2 Spiral Collapse

Curvature tightens into a vortex. (Mist/Trickster‑aligned)

7.3 Dispersive Collapse

Gradient dissolves outward. (Air/Ember‑aligned)

7.4 Implosive Collapse

Core collapses inward. (Ethyl‑aligned)

7.5 Recursive Collapse

Loops collapse into themselves. (Memory‑aligned)

Collapse style determines drift behavior.

8. Field Recovery

Recovery occurs through:

  • Stabilization — memory pressure smooths curvature

  • Re‑Coherence — core realigns

  • Expansion — radius increases

  • Forward Drift — motion resumes

Recovery signals include:

  • Stillwatch

  • Grove Pulse

  • Deep Quiet

  • Ember Rest

9. Field Networks

Fields form networks:

  • Drift Fields — multi‑arc regions

  • Drift Corridors — stable paths

  • Field Loops — closed fields

  • Collapse Fields — collapse‑aligned regions

These networks define the world’s motion architecture.

II. Canonical Summary

Field Dynamics is the engine that governs how fields move, bend, collapse, and recover. It is the behavioral layer of the Field subsystem and the foundation of Drift Fields, Collapse Fields, and Field Maps.
