Node Dynamics — Internal Physics of Threshold Nodes

Node Dynamics defines the internal physics that govern every node in Threshold. Nodes are not static points — they are living state machines with:

  • memory

  • fatigue

  • resonance

  • drift

  • collapse

  • recovery

  • pressure response

  • migration behavior

Every node expresses a dynamic signature that determines how it interacts with:

  • Flowers

  • Clusters

  • Drift fields

  • Pressure gradients

  • Echo signals

  • Crown/Seam transitions

Core Dynamic Properties

1. Resonance (R)

A node’s harmonic alignment with its neighbors.

  • High R → stability

  • Low R → drift

  • Negative R → collapse risk

Links: [[Echo Protocol]] • [[Harmony Emergence]]

2. Pressure (P)

Accumulated structural tension inside the node.

  • P increases with misalignment

  • P decreases with reintegration

  • P spikes cause collapse or migration

Links: [[Pressure System]] • [[Reduction Cycle]]

3. Drift (D)

Directional movement caused by imbalance.

  • D = first derivative of node’s vector field

  • High D → orbit change

  • Extreme D → cross‑Flower migration

Links: [[Drift System]] • [[Cross-Flower Migration]]

4. Collapse (C)

Structural failure mode.

  • Triggered by pressure spikes

  • Causes node contraction

  • Leads to fragmentation or reintegration

Links: [[Fragmentation]] • [[Reintegration]]

5. Recovery (Rc)

Node’s ability to regain stability.

  • Rc increases with resonance

  • Rc decreases with fatigue

  • Rc determines reintegration success

Links: [[Harmony Emergence]] • [[EchoNode State Machine]]

Node State Engine

Every node cycles through five internal states:

  1. Idle — stable, low pressure

  2. Activation — receiving signals

  3. Drift — moving due to imbalance

  4. Collapse — structural failure

  5. Recovery — reintegration attempt

This engine runs continuously and determines the node’s behavior inside Flowers and Clusters.

## **Dynamic Laws**

### **Law 1 — Nodes seek resonance**

Nodes naturally move toward harmonic alignment.

### **Law 2 — Pressure determines motion**

High pressure → drift Extreme pressure → collapse

### **Law 3 — Drift determines topology**

Drift changes:

- orbit
    
- cluster position
    
- Flower membership
    

### **Law 4 — Collapse is reversible**

Nodes can reintegrate unless fatigue exceeds threshold.

### **Law 5 — Recovery is probabilistic**

Recovery success depends on:

- resonance
    
- fatigue
    
- environmental stability
