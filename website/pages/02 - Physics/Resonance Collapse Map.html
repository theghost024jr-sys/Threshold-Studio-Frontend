Resonance Collapse Map.md

Map of Resonance Behavior During Collapse Events

Overview

Resonance collapse is the moment when tension exceeds structural capacity, causing resonance fields to break, invert, or discharge. This map describes:

  • how resonance destabilizes

  • how collapse propagates

  • what patterns emerge

  • where residue flows

  • how the system recovers

Resonance collapse is not destruction. It is reformatting under pressure.

1. Collapse Initiation Zones

Collapse begins in one of three zones:

1.1 Tension Peaks

Where resonance density becomes too high to maintain coherence. Signature: sharp spikes, harmonic distortion, lattice strain.

1.2 Boundary Fractures

Where identity boundaries crack under drift or overload. Signature: edge‑splintering, boundary flicker, resonance leakage.

1.3 Drift Saturation Points

Where drift pressure overwhelms local resonance. Signature: directional push, gradient collapse, drift‑weather fronts.

These zones determine the shape of the collapse.

2. Collapse Patterns

Resonance collapse expresses itself in four canonical patterns:

2.1 Radial Collapse

Resonance implodes inward toward a central point. Produces:

  • high‑density residue

  • strong compost yield

  • clean collapse signature

2.2 Shear Collapse

Resonance tears sideways along tension lines. Produces:

  • long fracture paths

  • identity splitting

  • drift‑echo trails

2.3 Spiral Collapse

Resonance rotates as it collapses. Produces:

  • vortex residue

  • recursive identity fragments

  • collapse‑weather spirals

2.4 Inversion Collapse

Resonance flips polarity before collapsing. Produces:

  • inverted residue

  • shimmer distortion

  • unstable emergence seeds

Each pattern has its own recovery path.

3. Collapse Propagation

Collapse spreads through resonance fields in predictable ways:

3.1 Gradient Flow

Collapse moves along the steepest tension gradient.

3.2 Boundary Jump

Collapse leaps across weak identity boundaries.

3.3 Drift Push

Drift currents accelerate collapse in their direction.

3.4 Harmonic Chain Reaction

Nearby resonance fields collapse in sequence.

Propagation speed depends on:

  • resonance density

  • drift pressure

  • boundary strength

  • identity integrity

4. Collapse Outputs

Every collapse produces three outputs:

4.1 Collapse Residue

Raw, unstructured material. Sent to: Compost Engine

4.2 Drift Echo

Residual motion left behind after collapse. Sent to: Drift Layer

4.3 Shimmer Distortion

Temporary instability in the Shimmer Layer. Resolved by: Signal System

These outputs determine the recovery path.

5. Collapse Recovery Paths

Recovery depends on collapse type:

5.1 Radial → Compost‑Return

Residue is dense and clean → ideal for composting.

5.2 Shear → Drift‑Return

Identity fragments follow drift currents back to stability.

5.3 Spiral → Lantern‑Return

Spiral collapse produces intermittent clarity events.

5.4 Inversion → Ember‑Return

Identity must re‑ignite internally to stabilize.

These correspond to the Return Path Index.

7. Integration Points

7.1 Drift Layer

Collapse feeds drift‑echo into the layer.

7.2 Compost Engine

Residue is metabolized into substrate.

7.3 Shimmer Layer

Distortion is smoothed by shimmer equilibrium.

7.4 Identity System

Identity fragments follow Return Paths.

7.5 Collapse Weather

Collapse patterns shape local weather conditions.

Summary

The Resonance Collapse Map defines:

  • where collapse begins

  • how collapse spreads

  • what patterns emerge

  • what residue is produced

  • how the system recovers

Collapse is not failure. Collapse is conversion.
