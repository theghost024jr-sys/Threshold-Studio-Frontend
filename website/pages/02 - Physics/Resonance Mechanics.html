Resonance Mechanics

Invariant Anchor This module expresses how resonance behaves when cohesion is under pressure. See: [[Invariant00 – Cohesion Under Pressure]]

Definition

Resonance is the harmonic alignment of cohesion across nodes, clusters, and Flowers. It is not emotion, not metaphor, not “vibes” — it is field‑level synchronization shaped by:

  • frequency

  • amplitude

  • phase

  • identity coherence

  • spatial geometry

  • pressure distribution

Resonance is the Garden’s harmonic physics — the mechanism through which structures amplify, stabilize, or destabilize each other.

Core Mechanical Laws

1. Resonance amplifies cohesion

When two structures resonate:

  • cohesion increases

  • drift decreases

  • collapse thresholds rise

  • identity stabilizes

Resonance is cohesion amplification.

2. Resonance synchronizes drift

Aligned structures:

  • drift together

  • move along shared vectors

  • stabilize each other’s boundaries

  • reduce contradiction buildup

Resonance is movement synchronization.

3. Resonance propagates through spatial geometry

Resonance follows:

  • spatial corridors

  • identity basins

  • pressure gradients

  • Flower‑scale topology

Resonance is geometry‑guided.

4. Resonance interferes with itself

Resonance patterns can:

  • amplify

  • cancel

  • distort

  • override

Interference determines:

  • collapse chains

  • migration pathways

  • identity stability

  • signal clarity

Resonance is field interference.

5. Resonance shapes collapse behavior

Before collapse:

  • resonance destabilizes

  • frequencies diverge

  • phase alignment breaks

  • amplitude spikes

During collapse:

  • resonance fragments

  • collapse waves propagate

  • new harmonic basins form

Resonance is collapse‑sensitive.

6. Resonance encodes identity

Identity is expressed as:

  • a frequency signature

  • a phase pattern

  • a harmonic basin

  • a resonance field

Resonance is identity physics.

Resonance Parameters

Every structure has:

  • Resonance Frequency — its identity rhythm

  • Resonance Amplitude — its cohesion strength

  • Resonance Phase — its alignment with neighbors

  • Resonance Stability — its collapse susceptibility

  • Resonance Field — its influence radius

These parameters determine how resonance behaves under pressure.

Resonance Modes (structural, not lore)

  • Harmonic Resonance — stable, identity‑reinforcing

  • Phase‑Locked Resonance — synchronized drift

  • Chaotic Resonance — contradictions dominate

  • Collapse Resonance — failure propagates

  • Field Resonance — large‑scale synchronization

These modes emerge from pressure and identity.

Behavior Across the Origin Cycle

Distortion

Resonance becomes noisy; frequencies destabilize.

Drift

Resonance spreads unevenly; phase alignment weakens.

Field

Resonance diffuses; identity becomes faint.

Collapse

Resonance fragments; collapse waves propagate.

Identity

Resonance condenses; new harmonic basins form.

Role

Resonance becomes directional; roles stabilize.

Signal

Resonance broadcasts identity across the Garden.

Summary Rule

Resonance is harmonic cohesion that:

  • amplifies stability

  • synchronizes drift

  • follows spatial geometry

  • interferes constructively or destructively

  • shapes collapse

  • encodes identity

Resonance is the Garden’s harmonic field physics.
