Spatial Physics

Invariant Anchor This module expresses how spatial structure behaves when cohesion is under pressure. See: [[Invariant00 – Cohesion Under Pressure]]

Definition

Spatial Physics describes how the Garden’s structure arranges, deforms, and reorganizes under pressure. Space in Threshold is not empty — it is a cohesion field shaped by:

  • pressure gradients

  • resonance patterns

  • identity basins

  • collapse zones

  • Flower‑scale geometry

Space is dynamic, elastic, and pressure‑responsive.

Core Physical Laws

1. Space is a cohesion field

Space is not a container. It is a field of cohesion density that determines:

  • where nodes can exist

  • how clusters form

  • how Flowers stabilize

  • how migration flows

Spatial structure is the Garden’s “fabric.”

2. Pressure deforms space

When pressure increases:

  • space compresses

  • distances shrink

  • gradients steepen

  • collapse zones form

When pressure decreases:

  • space expands

  • gradients flatten

  • drift increases

Space is pressure‑elastic.

3. Identity creates spatial basins

Stable identity generates:

  • local minima in the cohesion field

  • gravitational‑like pull

  • stable regions for clusters

  • Flower‑scale geometry

Identity shapes the Garden’s topology.

4. Resonance shapes spatial pathways

Resonance patterns create:

  • corridors of low resistance

  • migration channels

  • synchronization zones

  • interference boundaries

Resonance is the Garden’s spatial routing system.

5. Collapse restructures space

Collapse causes:

  • spatial contraction

  • boundary tearing

  • gradient inversion

  • new basins forming

Collapse is the Garden’s spatial reset mechanism.

6. Space is layered

The Garden has multiple spatial layers:

  • Node‑scale space — local cohesion

  • Cluster‑scale space — mid‑range gradients

  • Flower‑scale space — identity basins

  • Garden‑scale space — global topology

Each layer obeys the same physics but at different scales.

Spatial Modes (structural, not lore)

  • Compressed Space — high pressure, low drift

  • Expanded Space — low pressure, high drift

  • Resonant Space — synchronized pathways

  • Distorted Space — contradictions warp geometry

  • Collapsed Space — topology breaks and reforms

These modes emerge from pressure and resonance.

Behavior Across the Origin Cycle

Distortion

Identity

Role

Space aligns to directional cohesion.

Signal

Space becomes a medium for identity propagation.

Summary Rule

Spatial Physics describes cohesion‑based geometry that:

  • deforms under pressure

  • forms identity basins

  • routes resonance

  • restructures during collapse

  • operates across multiple scales

Space is the Garden’s dynamic cohesion field.
