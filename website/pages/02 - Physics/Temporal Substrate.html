Temporal Substrate

Category: 03 – Substrates Color: #FFD93D (Sunflower Gold) Role: The rhythm engine of Threshold — the substrate that governs oscillation, coherence, phase behavior, and the seam between states.

The Temporal Substrate is not time. It is the behavior of time — the underlying oscillation field that keeps Threshold from drifting into collapse.

It defines:

  • rhythm

  • pacing

  • phase

  • coherence

  • transitions

  • temporal pressure

Everything that moves, shifts, or becomes must pass through this substrate.

1. Definition

The Temporal Substrate is:

  • a persistent oscillation field

  • a stabilizing rhythm beneath all layers

  • a regulator of expansion ↔ contraction cycles

  • the anchor for [[Time Particles]]

  • the timing organ for system transitions

  • the coherence spine for the world

It provides the world with a pulse.

2. Core Behaviors

The Temporal Substrate:

  • maintains cycle rhythm

  • resists drift‑induced distortion

  • synchronizes the [[Wind (Attunement System)|Wind]]

  • regulates the Choice Layer hum → whisper → wind sequence

  • anchors directional temperament shifts

  • stabilizes collapse recovery

  • defines the “feel” of time

It is the metronome of Threshold.

3. Temporal Modes

The substrate expresses five canonical modes:

1. Stable Mode

Clean oscillation, low drift, high coherence.

2. Agitated Mode

Increased frequency, turbulence influence, emotional pressure.

3. Collapsed Mode

Rhythm breaks, time smears, phase boundaries dissolve.

4. Crown Mode

Maximum definition, highest coherence, perfect rhythm.

5. Seam Mode

Minimum definition, pure potential, pre‑temporal state.

Modes determine how the world feels, not how it moves.

4. Cycle Mechanics

The Temporal Substrate governs:

  • expansion → peak → contraction → seam

  • buildup and release of temporal pressure

  • pacing of environmental shifts

  • rhythm of player stance transitions

  • timing of narrative beats

  • collapse → recovery cycles

Cycles are the heartbeat of Threshold.

With Drift Layer

Drift thickens or thins the rhythm. High drift → high temporal friction. Low drift → clean oscillation.

  • re‑sequencing

  • re‑phasing

6. Temporal Distortions

Distortions occur when:

  • drift pressure exceeds threshold

  • collapse tears the substrate

  • directional fields misalign

  • particle agitation spikes

  • the world enters a seam state

Distortions are not errors. They are opportunities for redefinition.
