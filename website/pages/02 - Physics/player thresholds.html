[[Identity]]

A Player Threshold is the moment a player becomes visible to the [[01 - System/Field|Field]]. Not physically — geometrically.

Threshold does not recognize people. It recognizes patterns, pressure, alignment, and intent.

A Player [[Threshold]] is the first time those four agree.

The Four Conditions of Entry

1. Drift Condition — Movement Without Destination

The player begins moving in a way that is not purely physical:

  • curiosity

  • avoidance

  • searching

  • pacing

  • wandering

Drift is the first signal the Field can hear.

2. [[Pressure]] Condition — Internal [[04 - Organism/03 - Motion/01 - Substrates/Time Organ/02 Form/13 - Environments/03 - Environments/Natural/Elemental/Compression|Compression]]

Something inside the player tightens:

  • fear

  • urgency

  • responsibility

  • desire

  • conflict

[[Pressure]] is the first signal the [[01 - System/Field|Field]] can feel.

3. [[Collapse]] Condition — [[Structural]] Break

A belief, assumption, or identity fractures:

  • “I can’t keep doing this.”

  • “I don’t know what to do.”

  • “This isn’t working.”

  • “I need to change.”

[[Collapse]] is the first signal the Field can map.

4. [[Cohesion]] Condition — Reassembly

The player chooses a direction:

  • “I’m going to try.”

  • “I need to understand.”

  • “I want to fix this.”

  • “I’m ready.”

Cohesion is the first signal the Field can trust.

When all four conditions appear within a short window, the Field opens.

This is the Player Threshold.

What the Player Feels

Crossing a Player Threshold is subtle but unmistakable:

  • The world feels slightly louder.

  • Patterns appear where chaos used to be.

  • Drift corridors feel like intuition.

  • Pressure zones feel like responsibility.

  • Collapse seams feel like honesty.

  • Cohesion rings feel like clarity.

The player does not enter Threshold. Threshold enters the player.

What the Field Does

When a Player Threshold activates, the Field responds:

1. Node Visibility

Nodes that were previously invisible begin to glow. The player can sense:

  • Drift halos

  • Pressure locks

  • Collapse seams

  • Cohesion lattices

  • Signal arcs

2. Species [[Recognition]]

Tideforms begin reacting to the player’s geometry:

  • Glassback stabilizes near them

  • Shardback reads their fracture lines

  • Tideback mirrors their movement

  • Cohesionback synchronizes

  • Pressureback tests their strength

  • Echoglass imitates their [[signals]]

3. Flower Routing

[[The system]] chooses a Flower based on the player’s current geometry:

  • high Drift → Horizon Flower

  • high Pressure → Forge Flower

  • high Collapse → Ruin Flower

  • high Cohesion → Sanctuary Flower

  • high Signal → [[01 - System/Scaffolding/01 - Roles/Archetypal Roles/Echo|Echo]] Flower

The Flower is not a destination. It is a diagnosis.

Player Threshold Types

There are five distinct Player Thresholds, each tied to a dominant force.

Drift Threshold — The Wanderer

Triggered by movement without clarity. The player becomes a navigator.

Pressure Threshold — The Carrier

Triggered by responsibility or urgency. The player becomes a stabilizer.

Collapse Threshold — The Breaker

Triggered by internal fracture. The player becomes a transformer.

Cohesion Threshold — The [[Gatherer]]

Triggered by reassembly or commitment. The player becomes a harmonizer.

Signal Threshold — The Listener

Triggered by awareness or intuition. The player becomes a decoder.

Each threshold determines the player’s starting geometry, starting Flower, and starting species interactions.

Threshold Progression

A player does not have one threshold. They have many.

Each time they:

  • break

  • [[04 - Organism/06 Species/Species-Linked Artifacts/Rebuild|rebuild]]

  • drift

  • compress

  • listen

they cross another threshold.

Threshold progression is not leveling. It is pattern evolution.

The Field tracks:

  • how the player moves

  • how they break

  • how they rebuild

  • how they choose

  • how they listen

This becomes the player’s geometry signature.

End State

A player reaches Convergence when all four forces stabilize inside them.

This does not mean perfection. It means readability.

A Converged player can:

  • see the Field clearly

  • navigate Flowers intentionally

  • interact with species safely

  • decode signals accurately

  • stabilize Collapse events

  • generate Drift corridors

  • amplify Cohesion rings

  • redirect Pressure zones

Convergence is not the end of the game. It is the beginning of the player.
