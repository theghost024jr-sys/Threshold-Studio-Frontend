A_ROUGH_DAY_THAT_ENDS.md

It began with imbalance.

Not dramatic imbalance — just the small, persistent kind that accumulates behind the ribs.
A misaligned step.
A thought that arrived too early.
A silence that lingered too long.

The world felt slightly off its axis, as if the day had been assembled in a hurry and no one checked the angles.

Throughout the hours:

  • gravity pulled a little harder than usual
  • time stuttered once or twice
  • intentions slipped sideways
  • the air hummed with low‑grade static

Nothing catastrophic.
Just enough to make everything feel one degree to the left.

By late afternoon, the day had begun to sag under its own weight.
Even the light seemed tired.

And then — without ceremony — the pressure eased.

A soft exhale from somewhere unseen.
A shift in the emotional field.
A quiet settling of things that had been restless.

The imbalance didn’t resolve.
It simply stopped insisting on itself.

The day ended the way a knot loosens:
not with force, but with a small, patient release.

When night arrived, it felt like a hand on the shoulder.
Not comforting.
Not instructive.
Just present.

The roughness remained part of the day, but no longer part of you.
