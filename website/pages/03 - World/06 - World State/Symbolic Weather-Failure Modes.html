Failure Modes

How symbolic weather breaks, collapses, and cascades under stress

Failure modes describe what happens when symbolic weather becomes unstable. They are not the same as ritual or artifact failure modes; these are environmental failures that occur when drift, density, emotional substrate, or scene substrate exceed their stability thresholds.

Failure modes are the world’s way of expressing collapse, inversion, or turbulence at scale.

Collapse Patterns

Drift Collapse

Drift vectors lose orientation and fall inward.

  • creates density voids
  • destabilizes traversal
  • can trigger vortices
  • often follows storms or emotional surges

Drift collapse is the most common environmental failure.

Density Fracture

Density splits into incompatible regions.

  • creates symbolic “fault lines”
  • causes fronts to harden
  • can trap artifacts in mismatched resonance
  • often leads to regional instability

Fracture is a sign of deep ecological stress.

Substrate Shear

Two substrates move in different directions.

  • symbolic vs emotional
  • emotional vs memory
  • memory vs scene
  • creates turbulence and fog

Shear is the precursor to storms.

Inversion Patterns

Weather Inversion

A weather pattern flips orientation.

  • outward spirals become inward
  • stabilizing fronts become destabilizing
  • fog becomes turbulence
  • density gradients reverse

Inversion is rare but dangerous.

Seasonal Inversion

Seasonal resonance expresses the wrong phase.

  • Winter behaves like Summer
  • Spring behaves like Autumn
  • emotional substrate becomes mismatched
  • drift orientation contradicts density

This is usually caused by prototype misalignment.

Emotional Inversion

Emotional substrate flips amplitude.

  • quiet becomes volatile
  • volatile becomes numb
  • resonance tone reverses
  • affects ritual safety

Emotional inversion often precedes storms.

Cascade Patterns

Storm Cascade

A storm triggers additional storms.

  • drift vectors multiply
  • density becomes chaotic
  • emotional substrate destabilizes
  • memory substrate fragments

Cascades can destabilize entire regions.

Vortex Chain

One vortex pulls others into alignment.

  • creates large rotational fields
  • distorts artifact resonance
  • traps traversal paths
  • can lead to collapse

Chains are difficult to break without intervention.

Fog Spread

Fog expands into multiple substrates.

  • symbolic fog → emotional fog
  • emotional fog → memory fog
  • memory fog → scene fog
  • reduces clarity across the system

Fog spread is a sign of prolonged instability.

Mixed Failure States

Soup State

All substrates overlap without orientation.

  • drift, density, emotional, and scene substrates mix
  • turbulence without direction
  • collapse or recovery transition
  • extremely difficult to navigate

Soup is the “undifferentiated” failure state.

Stillness Break

Stillness pockets rupture.

  • drift re-ignites suddenly
  • density decompresses
  • emotional substrate spikes
  • memory substrate destabilizes

Often caused by premature Winter Crown dissolution.

Resonance Snap

Resonance amplitude exceeds tolerance.

  • artifacts destabilize
  • rituals become unsafe
  • drift vectors scatter
  • density becomes uneven

Snap is the symbolic equivalent of a lightning strike.

Reading Failure Modes

To interpret failure modes:

  • identify the dominant substrate
  • look for directionality (inward, outward, rotational)
  • check for seasonal resonance mismatch
  • compare to previous world state snapshots
  • determine whether the failure is local or regional

Failure modes are the world’s stress signals.
