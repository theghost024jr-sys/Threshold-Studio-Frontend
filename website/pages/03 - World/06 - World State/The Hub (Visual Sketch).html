THE HUB — VISUAL SKETCH (IN WORDS) Imagine the screen goes dim, like someone gently lowered the lights in a quiet room. A soft charcoal background settles in — not flat black, but textured, like paper in low light. There’s a faint warmth to it, almost like the glow of a lamp behind you. And then:

  1. The Center In the middle of the screen sits a small, calm circle. Not bright. Not glowing. Just present. A soft, warm gray. A single point of orientation. It feels like the “breath” of the system — the still point everything else orbits.

  2. The North Arc — Environments Above the center, arranged in a gentle arc, are the Environment tiles. They’re shaped like smooth stones — rounded rectangles with softened edges. Each one has its own muted color tone: • a deep teal • a warm amber • a dusty rose • a slate blue • a moss green Nothing neon. Nothing loud. When you hover over one, it lifts slightly — just a few pixels — and a soft glow blooms around it, like a candle being brought closer. They feel like rooms waiting to be entered.

  3. The South Arc — Artifacts Below the center, mirroring the arc above, are the Artifact icons. Smaller. More symbolic. More mysterious. They look like: • runes • tokens • small carved pieces • glyphs etched into stone Each one has a subtle texture, like something you could pick up between your fingers. They don’t glow on hover — they hum. A faint pulse, like a heartbeat. They feel like objects with stories.

  4. The Left Column — Substrate Spine On the left side of the screen, running vertically, is the Substrate Spine. A thin vertical line, soft and warm, like a thread of light. Along it are small nodes — circles or glyphs — each representing a layer of the substrate. They’re arranged with generous spacing, like steps on a ladder. Hovering over one makes it brighten slightly, as if acknowledging your presence. It feels like the structural backbone of the world.

  5. The Right Column — Node Browser On the right side, mirroring the spine, is the Node Column. Instead of a line, this side feels more like a constellation. Small floating chips. Tiny symbols. Each one representing a node — a concept, a connection, a piece of wiring. They drift just a hair — barely perceptible — like dust motes in a sunbeam. Hovering over one draws a thin line from it to the center, showing its relationship to the Hub. It feels like the nervous system of the world.

  6. The Background The background isn’t static. It breathes. A slow, almost imperceptible gradient shift: • charcoal to slate • slate to deep gray • deep gray back to charcoal Like the room is alive, but calm.

  7. The Movement When you click anything: • the rest of the Hub dims • the selected element rises • the screen slides gently sideways or downward • the new view appears like a curtain being drawn aside No sharp cuts. No sudden jumps. Everything moves like it’s respecting your nervous system.

THE FEELING Standing in the Hub feels like: • being in a quiet observatory • or a dim library with tall ceilings • or the foyer of a temple built for thinking • or the center of a symbolic map It’s not a UI. It’s a place. A place that holds you. A place that waits for you. A place that feels like it’s been there longer than you have.
