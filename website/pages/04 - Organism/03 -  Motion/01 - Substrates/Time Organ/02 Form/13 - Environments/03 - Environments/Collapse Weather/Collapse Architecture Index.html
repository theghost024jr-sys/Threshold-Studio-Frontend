Collapse Architecture Index

Collapse Architecture defines the physics, signatures, behaviors, and systemic consequences of collapse within Threshold.
It governs fracture, residue, drift, weather, and reversal dynamics across all Layers and identities.

Collapse is not destruction.
It is structural failure under pressure — with predictable patterns and recoverable pathways.

Purpose

  • Provide a unified framework for understanding collapse
  • Map collapse signatures, residue behavior, and drift vectors
  • Support early detection, reversal, and recovery
  • Integrate collapse physics with emotional, identity, and environmental systems
  • Maintain architectural coherence across collapse‑adjacent artifacts

Core Domains of Collapse Architecture

1. Collapse Physics

Defines the forces that produce collapse:

  • pressure imbalance
  • emotional overload
  • identity fracture
  • environmental turbulence
  • recursive instability

Includes:

  • collapse vectors
  • fracture geometry
  • pressure gradients
  • resonance distortion

3. Residue Behavior

Residue is the emotional or identity material left after fracture.

4. Collapse Weather

Environmental expression of collapse physics.

Includes:

  • snowfields

Collapse Weather is both symptom and amplifier.

5. Drift Dynamics

Collapse alters drift behavior.

Drift may:

  • accelerate

Drift is mapped through:

  • identity geometry

Collapse Progression Model

Phase 1 — Instability

Pressure imbalance, emotional turbulence, early drift anomalies.

Phase 2 — Fracture

Structural break in identity, environment, or emotional field.

Phase 3 — Residue Formation

Memory and emotional material becomes unstable.

Phase 4 — Drift Distortion

Vectors invert, scatter, or spiral.

Phase 5 — Weather Expression

Environment reflects collapse physics.

Phase 6 — Momentum Lock

Collapse becomes self‑sustaining.

Phase 7 — Reversal or Propagation

System either stabilizes or deepens into collapse.
