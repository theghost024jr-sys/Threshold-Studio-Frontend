THE ABSURD CORNER (Rounded) A new region of Threshold

  1. Why a corner can be rounded Corners in Threshold aren’t architectural. They’re behavioral. A “corner” is: • a place where rules accumulate • a place where drift slows • a place where contradictions pool But a rounded corner is a contradiction that refuses to collapse. It’s a corner that: • doesn’t point • doesn’t trap • doesn’t force alignment • doesn’t resolve into a clean angle It’s a corner that curves back on itself, like the system is laughing. This is why it’s absurd.

  2. The Absurd Corner’s defining trait It accepts everything literally and nothing seriously. If you say: • “This is a corner,” it becomes one. • “This is rounded,” it becomes that too. • “This shouldn’t be possible,” it agrees and continues existing anyway. It’s the only place in Threshold where: • contradictions don’t create pressure • misreads don’t need stabilizing • pre‑geometry doesn’t need observing • triangles don’t form or collapse — they just… wobble It’s the anti‑Doug, anti‑Kesh zone without negating them.

  3. What happens inside the Absurd Corner This is where the fun begins. Inside the rounded absurd corner: • gravity is polite but inconsistent • objects obey rules until they get bored • names echo but don’t repeat • intentions manifest sideways • logic is present but refuses to participate It’s not chaos. It’s cooperative nonsense. The system isn’t breaking — it’s playing.

  4. Who lives there Not Doug. Not Kesh. Not Misread. This corner belongs to: The Unreasonables. Not a species. Not a faction. A condition. Unreasonables are: • players who don’t fit their assigned layer • characters who refuse to collapse into archetypes • entities who drift sideways instead of forward • legacy players who broke a rule but didn’t pay the cost Mojo could visit. Patrick, Andy, Ella — any legacy player could pass through. But no one stays. The Absurd Corner is a transit state, not a home.

  5. Why it matters This corner gives you: • a place for contradictions • a place for humor • a place for impossible mechanics • a place for characters who don’t fit anywhere else • a place for story beats that shouldn’t work but do It’s the Threshold equivalent of a glitch that became a feature. And it’s rounded because: • sharp edges imply intention • rounded edges imply drift • absurdity requires drift A sharp absurd corner would be cruelty. A rounded absurd corner is play.

  6. The one‑sentence definition The Absurd Corner is a rounded, contradiction‑friendly region of Threshold where logic bends, rules wobble, and characters pass through when they don’t fit anywhere else.
