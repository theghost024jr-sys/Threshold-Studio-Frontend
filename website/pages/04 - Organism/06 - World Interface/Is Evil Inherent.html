Those instincts include: • fear • territoriality • resource protection • in‑group preference • fight/flight responses When these instincts get: • distorted • amplified • unregulated • traumatized • disconnected from empathy …they can produce behaviors we label as “evil.” So in this model: • evil is not inherent • but the capacity for harm is inherent • and “evil” is what we call that capacity when it collapses into destructive patterns This is the psychological origin of evil.

  1. Is “evil” environmental? (The external-origin hypothesis) This is the idea that: • trauma • deprivation • abuse • neglect • social collapse • cultural conditioning • dehumanization • power structures …shape people into doing harm. And structurally, this is true. Environments can produce “evil” behavior by breaking the systems that regulate empathy and identity. Examples: • chronic fear • humiliation • isolation • indoctrination • scarcity • violence • unstable attachment These conditions can: • shut down empathy • distort identity • normalize cruelty • reward domination • collapse moral reasoning In this model: • evil is emergent • evil is environmental • evil is a failure mode of human systems This is the sociological origin of evil.

  2. Is “evil” supernatural? (The metaphysical-origin hypothesis) This is the oldest human explanation. Before psychology, before sociology, before neuroscience, humans needed a way to explain: • suffering • chaos • betrayal • cruelty • natural disasters • internal impulses they didn’t understand So they externalized it into: • demons • spirits • dark gods • cosmic forces • metaphysical opposites This is not “wrong” — it’s a symbolic framework. Supernatural evil is a metaphor humans created to make sense of the parts of themselves and the world they couldn’t integrate. It’s not about literal demons. It’s about: • fear • chaos • the unknown • the shadow • the unintegrated self This is the mythic origin of evil.

So which one is it? Here’s the structural synthesis — the one that actually holds up: Evil is not inherent. Evil is not supernatural. Evil is environmental — and interpreted through myth. Humans are born with: • survival instincts • emotional needs • vulnerability • potential for harm But “evil” — the concept — emerges when: • environments distort those instincts • systems break • empathy collapses • identity fractures • meaning dissolves And then humans explain that collapse using: • myth • religion • narrative • symbolism So the real answer is: Evil is a human label for the destructive behaviors that emerge when internal systems and external environments fail — and myth is the language we use to make sense of that failure. Not a force. Not an essence. Not a metaphysical opposite. A pattern of collapse.
