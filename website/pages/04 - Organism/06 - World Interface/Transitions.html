Transitions.md

Overview

Transitions are the structural moments where one stable state yields to another. They are not events but alignment shifts that occur when internal and external conditions cross a threshold. All transitions follow the same underlying mechanics: drift accumulates, tension rises, and the system reconfigures into a new equilibrium.

Transitions operate across multiple layers of the system, including cycles, seasons, eras, and thresholds. Each layer expresses the same underlying pattern with different scales and consequences.

Transition Types

Cycle Transitions

Cycle transitions mark the shift from one phase of the cycle to the next. They occur when accumulated drift reaches a point where the system can no longer maintain its current configuration.

Transition Mechanics

Drift Accumulation

All transitions begin with drift — the gradual misalignment between the system’s internal state and its external demands.
See: [[Roles Drift Table]], [[Drift Thresholds]]

Tension Formation

As drift accumulates, tension forms within the system’s structure. This tension is not emotional; it is geometric and energetic.
Related: [[Signal Architecture]]

Alignment Event

A transition occurs when tension resolves into a new alignment. This is not a collapse but a reconfiguration.
Related: [[Shift]]

Stabilization

After the transition, the system stabilizes into a new configuration with new rules, boundaries, and behaviors.
Related: [[Era Transitions]], [[Cycles]]
