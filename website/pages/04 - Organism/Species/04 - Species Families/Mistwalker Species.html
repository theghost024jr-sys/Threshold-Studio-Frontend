  1. Species Identity Core Identity Mistwalkers are semi‑coherent, perception‑adaptive beings that exist in a state of partial distortion. Their identity is distributed, not centralized — parts of them appear ahead, behind, or beside their physical location. Identity Stability Low‑to‑medium. They rely on distortion pressure to maintain coherence; too much stability causes them to “collapse inward.” Identity Shape • elongated silhouettes • soft edges • shifting outlines • occasional double‑images Their form is probabilistic, not fixed.

  2. Behavioral Profile Stable Behavior • slow, gliding movement • low‑noise perception • gentle oscillation in drift • curiosity toward rhythmic signals Stress Behavior • rapid lateral shifts • multi‑image flickering • curved pursuit paths • echo‑responses to sound Break Behavior • spiral collapse • identity splitting • recursive motion loops • collapse‑aligned mimicry Mistwalkers break in spirals, not fractures.

  3. Drift Behavior Drift Vector Sideways‑leaning, unpredictable. Drift Velocity Variable — sudden bursts followed by slow drifting. Drift Curvature High. Mistwalkers naturally follow curved or oscillating arcs. Drift Compression Loose. Their identity spreads across the arc. Drift Signature A faint, shimmering after‑image that lags behind by half a second.

  4. Sensory Profile Primary Sense Perception Drift — they “see” by reading distortion patterns. Secondary Senses • echo‑mapping • pressure‑sensing • drift‑curvature detection Weakness Linear environments disorient them.

  5. Signal Interaction Native Signals • Mist Echo (they generate this unintentionally) • Spiral Whisper (they respond to it instinctively) Signal Sensitivity High. Mistwalkers react strongly to: • oscillating signals • distortion signals • collapse‑adjacent signals Signal Distortion Their presence bends signals around them.

  6. Environmental Alignment Primary Environment • Mist Corridor (home environment) Secondary Environments • Spiral Field • Mirage Plain • Quiet Grove (only briefly) Hostile Environments • Deep Snowfield (too dense) • Stone Basin (too compressive) • High Branch (too stable) Mistwalkers need distortion pressure to maintain identity.

  7. Role Interaction Roles Strengthened by Mistwalkers • Listener (they resonate with oscillation) • Weaver (they reveal hidden patterns) • Trickster (they amplify distortion) Roles Weakened by Mistwalkers • Anchor (identity boundaries dissolve) • Guardian (perimeter logic breaks) • Scholar (pattern clarity collapses) Mistwalkers are role‑shifting catalysts.

  8. Variants 8.1 Corridor Mistwalker (Base Variant) • semi‑coherent • oscillating drift • low aggression 8.2 Spiral Mistwalker • tighter curvature • collapse‑aligned • echo‑mimicry behavior 8.3 Grove‑Touched Mistwalker • rare • partially stabilized • memory‑infused • capable of linear drift for short periods 8.4 Shatter‑Edge Mistwalker • brittle identity • prone to sudden collapse • appears near Shatter Zones

  9. Description A tall, soft‑edged figure that moves like a thought half‑remembered. Its outline bends with the mist, splitting and rejoining as it glides. When it turns its head, three versions turn at once — one real, two delayed. It is not threatening, but its presence makes the world feel slightly out of phase. Mistwalkers are the living geometry of distortion.

  10. Notes • Mistwalkers are not predators; they are drift‑symbiotic. • They often appear at the edges of the Mist Corridor, where curvature is strongest. • They can be followed — but never directly. • They are the first sign that you’ve entered a distortion environment.
