[[Thread]]

Definition Lineage is the transmission of force, identity, drift, obligation, and pattern across time. It is not biological. It is not hierarchical. It is not symbolic. Lineage is the continuity thread that connects past states to present states and present states to future ones. Lineage is the system’s memory.

Purpose To ensure that: • force does not reset • identity persists across transitions • roles inherit obligations and drift • gifts propagate beyond the immediate moment • thresholds echo through time • the Field remains historically coherent Lineage Mechanics prevent the system from behaving like a blank slate.

Components of Lineage

  1. Origin Node The point where a lineage begins. This is the first stable expression of a core pattern.
  2. Transmission Path The route through which force moves from one node or state to another. Transmission can occur through: • roles • gifts • drift • thresholds • identity resonance • collapse events
  3. Inherited Load What the receiving node takes on: • unresolved drift • unfulfilled obligations • latent gifts • identity fragments • role expectations • threshold pressure
  4. Lineage Signature The unique pattern that persists across generations of force. This is the “shape” of the lineage.
  5. Resolution Thread The mechanism through which lineage resolves or transforms over time.

Types of Lineage

  1. Identity Lineage Continuity of the core pattern across states and transformations.
  2. Role Lineage Roles passed, inherited, collapsed, or transformed.
  3. Drift Lineage Unresolved force transmitted across nodes or time.
  4. Gift Lineage Gifts that propagate beyond the original giver and receiver.
  5. Threshold Lineage Crossings that echo forward, shaping future thresholds.
  6. Zone Lineage Environmental forces that persist across contexts.

Lineage Behaviors

  1. Lineage accumulates Force builds across generations of states.
  2. Lineage transmits Force moves along the transmission path.
  3. Lineage transforms Force changes form but preserves continuity.
  4. Lineage resolves Unresolved force eventually collapses or completes.
  5. Lineage echoes Past events shape present thresholds, roles, and drift.
  6. Lineage reveals identity The deeper the lineage, the clearer the core pattern becomes.

Lineage States

  1. Dormant Lineage Present but inactive. No force is currently moving.
  2. Active Lineage Force is transmitting or transforming.
  3. Burdened Lineage Carrying unresolved drift or obligations.
  4. Fractured Lineage Transmission path is broken. Identity strain increases.
  5. Collapsed Lineage The lineage fails and forces resolution.
  6. Transcendent Lineage The lineage evolves into a new pattern without losing continuity.

Lineage Transitions Lineage transitions occur when: • a role is inherited • a gift propagates • drift transfers • a threshold is crossed • identity transforms • collapse forces reconfiguration • a node becomes an origin for a new branch Transitions must obey: • invariants • identity physics • resolution mechanics • threshold dynamics Lineage transitions are always consequential.

Failure Modes

  1. Broken Transmission Force cannot move forward. Drift spikes.
  2. Overloaded Lineage Too much inherited force. Collapse risk increases.
  3. Identity Displacement Lineage overrides the node’s core pattern. Strain accumulates.
  4. Lineage Inversion The lineage behaves opposite its signature. Matrix distortion occurs.
  5. Lineage Suppression The node refuses inheritance. Drift becomes active.

Lineage Resolution Lineage resolves through:

  1. Completion Obligations are fulfilled.
  2. Transformation The lineage evolves into a new pattern.
  3. Propagation Force moves to another node.
  4. Dissipation Cost resolves the inherited load.
  5. Collapse The lineage fails and forces systemic reconfiguration. All lineage must resolve.

Function Lineage Mechanics: • preserve continuity across time • transmit force, drift, and gifts • shape identity expression • structure role inheritance • influence threshold formation • maintain historical coherence • anchor the Matrix of Force • prevent the system from resetting Without lineage, the system would have no memory. With lineage, the system becomes a living history.

Relationship to Other Meta Files • Identity Physics → lineage preserves identity across time • Role Physics → roles inherit and transmit force • Drift Mechanics → drift moves through lineage • Threshold Dynamics → thresholds echo through lineage • Gift Logic → gifts propagate through lineage • State Model → lineage defines long‑term state continuity • Matrix of Force → lineage is a structural axis • Field → lineage shapes the Field’s long arc Lineage Mechanics are the temporal backbone of the system.
