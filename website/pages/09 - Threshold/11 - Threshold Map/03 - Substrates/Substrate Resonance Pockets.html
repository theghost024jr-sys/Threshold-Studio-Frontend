Map Name Substrate Resonance Pockets Map Type Localized Density Amplification Map / High-Activity Substrate Zones Purpose Substrate resonance pockets are localized zones where substrate density compresses, amplifies, or destabilizes symbolic and emotional charge. They are the reactive organs of the substrate layer — small, intense, and capable of influencing multiple higher-layer systems. Resonance pockets are not geographic. They are structural conditions that can appear anywhere density, tension, and symbolic pressure converge.

  1. Resonance Pocket Definition A resonance pocket is a localized amplification zone formed when substrate density: • compresses • aligns • destabilizes • or becomes symbolically saturated These pockets act as resonance chambers, intensifying whatever passes through them.

  2. Formation Conditions Resonance pockets form when three or more of the following conditions overlap: • steep density gradients • fault line proximity • symbolic pressure accumulation • emotional turbulence • density inversion onset • collapse adjacency • mythic substrate activation The more conditions present, the more volatile the pocket.

  3. Resonance Pocket Types A. Compression Pockets Formed by density compression. Characteristics: • high symbolic density • strong amplification • stable shape • predictable behavior These pockets are powerful but not volatile.

B. Turbulence Pockets Formed by emotional or symbolic turbulence. Characteristics: • unstable boundaries • fluctuating intensity • unpredictable amplification • high discharge likelihood These pockets are volatile and reactive.

C. Inversion Pockets Formed during density inversion events. Characteristics: • extreme amplification • rapid formation • high collapse susceptibility • strong discharge signatures These pockets are rare and dangerous.

D. Fault-Line Pockets Formed at fault line intersections. Characteristics: • directional amplification • collapse conduction • symbolic shear • resonance drift These pockets act as amplifiers along structural seams.

  1. Resonance Behaviors A. Symbolic Amplification Resonance pockets amplify symbolic charge. Effects: • mythic bloom likelihood increases • symbolic noise intensifies • residue becomes more legible

B. Emotional Amplification Emotional turbulence is magnified. Effects: • drift destabilization • emotional weather intensification • boundary thinning

C. Collapse Amplification Collapse behavior becomes stronger and more coherent. Effects: • collapse fronts sharpen • collapse vectors strengthen • vortex formation becomes more likely

D. Discharge Amplification Mythic discharge becomes more frequent and more intense. Effects: • sprite formation • jet formation • convergence discharge events

  1. Pocket Stability States A. Stable Pocket • predictable amplification • low discharge risk • stable boundaries B. Semi-Stable Pocket • fluctuating amplification • moderate discharge risk • boundary drift C. Unstable Pocket • high amplification • high discharge risk • collapse susceptibility D. Critical Pocket • extreme amplification • density inversion imminent • collapse formation almost guaranteed Critical pockets are the most dangerous substrate structures.

Pocket Indicators Structural Indicators • density compression • gradient steepening • fault line clustering Symbolic Indicators • symbolic noise • mythic bloom precursors • residue alignment shifts Emotional Indicators • turbulence spikes • drift instability • pressure fluctuations These indicators allow early detection.

Pocket Mapping Components A resonance pocket map includes: • pocket locations (structural, not spatial) • pocket types • stability states • amplification profiles • fault line adjacency • collapse susceptibility overlays • discharge likelihood overlays This map is essential for predicting high-activity zones.
