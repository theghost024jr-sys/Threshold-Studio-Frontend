Map Name Substrate–Collapse Coupling Map Type System Interaction Map / Deep Structural Coupling Purpose This map defines how substrate density and collapse physics interact. Collapse does not originate from the surface layer — it emerges from density tension, fault line instability, and resonance amplification within the substrate. Substrate–collapse coupling determines: • where collapse forms • how collapse propagates • what shape collapse takes • how collapse interacts with residue and boundaries • how collapse dissipates This is a root-level interaction map.

  1. Coupling Overview Collapse is a surface expression of deep substrate tension. Coupling strength depends on: • density magnitude • gradient steepness • fault line activity • resonance pocket proximity • inversion presence High coupling = collapse is likely, strong, and structurally coherent. Low coupling = collapse is weak, diffuse, or fails to form.

  2. Coupling Factors A. Density Magnitude High substrate density increases collapse susceptibility. Effects: • collapse fronts form more easily • collapse vectors strengthen • vortex formation becomes more stable Low density resists collapse formation.

B. Gradient Steepness Steep gradients create directional collapse behavior. Effects: • collapse propagates along gradient direction • collapse speed increases • collapse fronts sharpen Shallow gradients produce slow, diffuse collapse.

C. Fault Line Activity Fault lines act as collapse conduits. Effects: • collapse travels along fault lines • collapse intensity increases at intersections • collapse can jump between strata Fault line intersections are high-risk zones.

D. Resonance Pockets Resonance pockets amplify collapse behavior. Effects: • collapse becomes symbolically charged • residue density increases • emotional turbulence intensifies Resonance pockets create volatile collapse signatures.

E. Density Inversions Density inversions are the strongest coupling condition. Effects: • collapse fronts form spontaneously • collapse vectors become unstable • discharge events become likely • residue becomes turbulent Inversions are rare but extremely dangerous.

Fault Line Collapse • triggered by fault line instability • fast propagation • high structural coherence • medium symbolic charge C. Inversion Collapse • triggered by density inversion • explosive formation • high symbolic charge • strong discharge likelihood Inversion collapse is the most volatile.

High Density • dense residue • slow decay • high symbolic clarity Medium Density • moderate residue • predictable decay • stable symbolic behavior Low Density • minimal residue • fast decay • low symbolic clarity Residue is a density echo.

  4. Collapse Dissipation Collapse dissipates when coupling weakens. Dissipation triggers: • gradient flattening • fault line stabilization • resonance pocket discharge • inversion resolution Dissipation is rapid once coupling drops below threshold.

  5. Coupling Index The Substrate–Collapse Coupling Index (SCCI) is derived from: • density magnitude • gradient steepness • fault line activity • resonance pocket proximity • inversion presence High SCCI = high collapse likelihood. Low SCCI = collapse-resistant region.
