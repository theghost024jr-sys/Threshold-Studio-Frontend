Essence Ruins are environments defined by exposed memory, fractured structure, and lingering resonance. They are what remains when a place has outlived its original purpose but not its emotional weight. Ruins hold echoes, pressure lines, and half‑intact pathways that reveal how the world used to think. A Ruin is not decay. It is the architecture of what was, still trying to speak. Environmental Physics • Visibility: Broken sightlines; partial walls and frames • Sound Behavior: Reverberates unpredictably; pockets of silence • Pressure: Uneven; old vectors still active in fragments • Stability: Low to medium; depends on age and emotional load • Temperature: Cooler in shadowed cavities; warmer in exposed stone Field Dynamics • Movement follows old pathways even when they no longer exist • Emotional resonance clings to surfaces, especially corners and thresholds • Memory accumulates in cracks, hollows, and collapsed beams • Light behaves strangely around broken geometry Phenomena

  1. Echo Residue Faint emotional impressions left by past events. Touching certain surfaces triggers: • flashes of memory • emotional echoes • déjà vu
  2. Collapse Drift Loose stones or beams shift subtly in response to emotional pressure. The Ruin “breathes” with the traveler.
  3. Ghost Lines Faint outlines of former structures visible in peripheral vision. They guide movement without being fully real.
  4. Resonance Pockets Small zones where sound or emotion becomes amplified. Often used by Guides to read the Ruin’s history. Flora & Fauna • Stoneforms: Anchor species; stabilize fractured geometry • Ruin Crows: Nest in high cavities; track emotional residue • Dust Hares: Move through collapsed corridors • Mosslights: Glow softly in high‑memory zones Emotional Physics • Vector: Inward → Downward • Resonance: Fragmented coherence • Effect: Draws attention to unresolved memory or past choices • Instability: Over‑immersion in echoes; emotional collapse • Signature: A hollow, pulling sensation behind the sternum Role Interaction • Guide Roles: Read old vectors; reconstruct partial pathways • Trickster Roles: Exploit blind corners and ghost lines • Healer Roles: Stabilize resonance pockets; soothe echoes • Wanderer Roles: Risk getting lost in memory loops Drift Behavior • Early Drift: Attention pulled toward broken geometry • Mid Drift: Emotional echoes feel personal • Late Drift: Identity merges with the Ruin’s memory • Collapse Mode: Ruin‑sink — falling into old patterns as if they were present Mythic Fragment

They say Ruins form when a place remembers too much and can no longer hold its shape.

The walls fall, the beams crack, but the memory stays, waiting for someone to listen without fear. Integration Notes • Use Ruins for scenes involving memory, aftermath, or rediscovery • Ideal for characters confronting old choices or broken structures • Pairs naturally with Shatter Zones (fracture), Groves (healing), and Branch (choice) • Works as a counterpoint to Plains (clarity) and Warm Pocket (comfort) • Excellent for mapping the emotional archaeology of a narrative arc
