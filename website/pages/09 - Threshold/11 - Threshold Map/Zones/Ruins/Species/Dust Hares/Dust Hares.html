Overview Dust Hares are light‑boned, drift‑responsive ruin mammals that inhabit collapsed structures, stone hollows, and dust‑heavy corridors. They are not prey animals, not scavengers, not rodents — they are drift‑aligned resonance creatures whose bodies are adapted to micro‑currents, collapse residue, and symbolic dust. Dust Hares are one of the primary movement indicators in Ruins ecology.

Purpose in the Ecosystem Dust Hares exist to: • redistribute symbolic dust • maintain micro‑drift circulation • prevent stagnation in collapse‑adjacent zones • reveal hidden turbulence • soften emotional fields • keep Ruins “breathing” They are the circulators of Ruins.

Core Properties

  1. Lightweight, Dust‑Coated Bodies Dust Hares have: • hollow, stone‑resonant bones • soft particulate fur • Shimmer‑responsive whiskers • Mist‑absorbing undercoat Their bodies are tuned to the physics of Ruins.

  2. Drift‑Aligned Locomotion Dust Hares do not run. They glide, hop‑float, and spiral‑leap using Drift currents. Movement patterns include: • soft arcs • low‑gravity hops • drift‑assisted glides • collapse‑avoidance zigzags Their motion reveals the invisible structure of the air.

  3. Collapse Sensitivity Dust Hares detect collapse long before it becomes visible. Signs include: • sudden stillness • ears flattening • whiskers vibrating • dust shedding • retreat into stone hollows They are early‑warning systems for collapse fronts.

  4. Emotional Field Reactivity Dust Hares respond to emotional physics: • calm → slow, drifting hops • fear → rapid scatter • grief → low, heavy movement • anticipation → upward leaps • overwhelm → chaotic spirals They mirror the emotional state of the environment.
