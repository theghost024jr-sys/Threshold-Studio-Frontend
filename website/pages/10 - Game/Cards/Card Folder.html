⭐ THE CARD FOLDER (Core Categories)

Right now, based on everything we’ve built, the game naturally wants four major card types:

  1. Helper Cards
  2. Attribute Addition Cards
  3. One‑Time Creation Cards
  4. Permission / Meta‑System Cards

These four categories cover every kind of move a player might need.

Let’s define each one cleanly.

⭐ 1. HELPER CARDS

These are the “ask for clarity, guidance, or execution help” cards.

They do NOT give new abilities.
They help you use the abilities you already have.

Examples:

  • ASK theghost
    Use this when you have an ability but don’t know how to perform it.
    After use, shuffle back into the deck.
  • Show Me the Move
    Demonstrates a single example of how your ability works.
  • Translate This
    Converts a confusing Obsidian action into a simple step‑by‑step.

These cards keep the game flowing and prevent ability‑lock.

⭐ 2. ATTRIBUTE ADDITION CARDS

These are the “level up” cards.

They add new abilities to your Player Card.

Examples:

  • Gain 1 Attribute
    Draw from the Attribute Deck and add it to your Player.
  • Upgrade an Existing Attribute
    Turns a basic ability into an advanced one.
  • Swap an Attribute
    Replace one ability with another.

These cards are the progression engine.

⭐ 3. ONE‑TIME CREATION CARDS

These let you create something once, and then the card is discarded.

They’re powerful but temporary.

Examples:

  • Create a New Note
    Make a new Obsidian note as part of the game world.
  • Spawn a Path
    Create a new path in the grid or canvas.
  • Generate a Token
    Add a temporary object, marker, or rule.
  • Manifest an Artifact
    Create a one‑off object with a special effect.

These cards shape the world.

⭐ 4. PERMISSION / META‑SYSTEM CARDS

These modify the rules of the game itself.

They don’t give abilities.
They give permissions.

Examples:

  • You May Place an .md in a Folder
    Allows file‑level organization.
  • You May Rearrange the Grid
    Lets you restructure the play area.
  • You May Ask Another Player for Their Card
    Social permission.
  • You May Break One Rule (Once)
    Controlled chaos.

These cards are the “physics” of the game.

⭐ Putting It All Together: The Folder Structure

Here’s how your Obsidian vault should look:

/Cards /Players Player1.md Player2.md ... Player10.md /Helpers AskTheGhost.md ShowMeTheMove.md TranslateThis.md /Attributes LinkPath.md ColorCode.md SpawnNode.md ... (20+ attribute cards) /OneTime CreateNote.md SpawnPath.md ManifestArtifact.md /Permissions PlaceMDinFolder.md RearrangeGrid.md BreakOneRule.md

This is clean, modular, and expandable.
