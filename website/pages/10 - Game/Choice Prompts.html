Overview Choice Prompts are the signal‑level invitations Threshold uses to engage the player in stance, direction, or emotional alignment. They are not “options” or “dialogue choices.” They are resonance invitations — structured openings that let the player shape the world’s next movement. A Choice Prompt is a world‑generated signal that: • opens a path • reveals a stance • exposes a tension • invites alignment • tests resonance • offers a direction Choice Prompts are part of the Signal Cluster, not the narrative layer.

  1. Prompt Types A. Directional Prompts These guide the player toward a vector rather than a decision. Purpose: establish movement, momentum, or intention. Examples: • Move toward clarity • Move toward warmth • Move toward pressure • Move toward the unknown Signal Behavior: Directional prompts shift resonance channels and emotional gradients.

B. Stance Prompts These ask the player to choose how they want to meet the moment. Purpose: define relational or emotional posture. Examples: • With openness • With caution • With curiosity • With resolve Signal Behavior: Stance prompts attune the Identity Substrate and emotional vectors.

C. Tension Prompts These expose a pressure point and ask how to engage it. Purpose: reveal underlying conflict or friction. Examples: • Lean into the tension • Hold steady • Release the pressure • Reframe the moment Signal Behavior: Tension prompts activate Collapse or Creation pathways depending on stance.

D. Reflection Prompts These invite the player to articulate or surface internal meaning. Purpose: deepen resonance and symbolic density. Examples: • What is rising • What is fading • What is unresolved • What is calling Signal Behavior: Reflection prompts feed the Symbolic Substrate and Narrative Substrate.

E. Threshold Prompts These appear at liminal moments — transitions, identity shifts, or substrate crossings. Purpose: mark a boundary or transformation. Examples: • Step through • Step back • Step sideways • Wait and listen Signal Behavior: Threshold prompts activate resonance geometry and identity alignment.

  1. Prompt Structure A Choice Prompt always contains:

  2. A Frame The emotional or symbolic context.

  3. A Vector The direction or stance being invited.

  4. A Consequence Field Not a result — a field of possible shifts.

  5. A Resonance Hook A subtle signal that ties the choice to the world’s emotional physics.

  6. A Drift Allowance Space for the player to respond outside the offered prompts.

  7. Prompt Delivery Modes A. Soft Prompts Ambient, gentle, low‑pressure. Used in: • The Soil • Driftway • Shallows B. Firm Prompts Clear, structured, high‑signal. Used in: • The Vein • The Lattice • The Deep C. Fractured Prompts Unstable, glitch‑edged, collapse‑adjacent. Used in: • The Fracture • Collapse Weather events D. Echo Prompts Mythic, narrative‑resonant, atmospheric. Used in: • The Echo Field • Narrative Substrate surges

  8. Prompt Flow A Choice Prompt follows a predictable signal flow:

  9. Detect → world reads player stance or environmental pressure

  10. Align → resonance channels synchronize

  11. Frame → world sets emotional context

  12. Offer → prompt appears

  13. Attune → world adjusts to player’s selection

  14. Shift → environment, species, or substrate moves

  15. Drift → ambient consequences unfold

This flow ensures choices feel alive, not mechanical.
