Engine Overview

The Engine is the runtime of Threshold — the underlying physics, logic, and behavioral system that governs how every node moves, reacts, aligns, collapses, and navigates through the world. Species, Humanoids, Organs, Chambers, Glyphs, Maps, and Seasonal Systems all operate within the constraints of the Engine.

The Engine defines six fundamental forces:

Drift

Pressure

Resonance

Collapse

Navigation

Role Gravity

These forces interact to create the dynamic behavior of Threshold’s nodes, Flowers, Organs, and Chambers.

Purpose of the Engine

The Engine provides:

A consistent physics layer for all world systems

A unified rule set for node behavior

A foundation for Organ and Chamber design

A runtime loop that keeps Threshold stable

A framework for Drift Zones, Collapse Zones, and Resonance Clusters

A semantic backbone for species behavior and glyph interpretation

Without the Engine, Threshold would drift into chaos — systems would behave inconsistently, species would lose their roles, and Organs would fail to coordinate.

The Six Forces

Drift

The natural movement of nodes from high pressure to low pressure. Drift creates currents, flows, and migration paths.

Pressure

The invisible field generated by Organs, Chambers, and Flowers. Pressure shapes Drift and determines stability.

Resonance

Harmonic alignment between nodes. Resonance accelerates Drift, stabilizes clusters, and reduces Collapse risk.

Collapse

The failure state of node stability. Collapse resets Drift direction and creates Fault Lines.

Navigation

The decision-making layer that determines how nodes choose paths through Threshold.

Role Gravity

The force that pulls nodes toward their intended function, stabilizing Drift and preventing runaway Collapse.
