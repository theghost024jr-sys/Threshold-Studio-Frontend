Snail Band Clearing

Follow the stone stair south of the Waterfall until the falling water becomes a pulse in the roots. A small porch waits where mist meets the Old Forest.

At dusk, the Snail drops his debris bag, sits down, and picks the first notes on a banjo tuned to the Forest's hum. The Beetle waits, then taps a precise rhythm on wood and shell. The frogs listen for the groove before laying down a slow root-bass and answering chorus.

The clearing is not a stage and the gathering is not a concert. Roots pulse, branches sway, spores turn in warm spirals, and passing travelers stop only as long as they need. The band is the Forest speaking to itself.

Sinker Sound

A traveler may sit at the porch and answer with one heavy, grounded beat. On alignment, the bass deepens, the Beetle locks to the pulse, and an old path opens farther south into the Old Forest. Missing the beat causes no harm; the band continues until the traveler is ready to listen again.

Random System Check

On some nights, a low-priority monitoring task routes Doug through the clearing. He arrives with a clipboard to verify an unassigned pressure log, checks the porch pulse against the Waterfall's drift delta, and records the band as stable. He does not interrupt the music or interpret it. He closes the ticket, follows the path back north, and the clearing's collapse probability quietly drops by 0.7 percent.
