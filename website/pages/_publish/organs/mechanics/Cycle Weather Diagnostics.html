2. THE FIVE WEATHER STATES OF THE CYCLE

Each phase produces a distinct atmospheric system.

A. Coherence Weather (Circle)

Stable, warm, inward‑gathering weather systems

Atmospheric signatures:

  • warm, even temperatures
  • low wind variance
  • soft, radial cloud patterns
  • stable humidity

Pressure signatures:

  • mild inward pressure
  • low vertical gradient

Drift signatures:

  • inward drift currents
  • low turbulence

Resonance signatures:

  • harmonic, low‑variance hum

Diagnostic summary:
Weather feels settled, centered, and aligned.

Transition indicator:
Warmth thickens; cloud edges sharpen → Tension forming.

B. Tension Weather (Gate)

Compressed, directional, high‑contrast weather systems

Atmospheric signatures:

  • sharp temperature gradients
  • dry air
  • linear cloud formations
  • wind funnels

Pressure signatures:

  • strong vertical pressure
  • rising atmospheric density

Drift signatures:

  • linear compression
  • drift corridors forming

Resonance signatures:

  • narrow‑band, directional resonance

Diagnostic summary:
Weather feels tight, pressured, and on the verge.

Transition indicator:
Vertical gradients spike; wind corridors destabilize → Collapse imminent.

C. Collapse Weather (Crown)

Unstable, scattering, rapidly shifting weather systems

Atmospheric signatures:

  • sudden gusts
  • erratic temperature drops
  • chaotic cloud fracturing
  • bursts of precipitation

Pressure signatures:

  • outward pressure bursts
  • pressure pockets collapsing

Drift signatures:

  • scatter drift
  • turbulence and micro‑eddies

Resonance signatures:

  • diffuse, unstable resonance

Diagnostic summary:
Weather feels broken open, unpredictable, and dissolving.

Transition indicator:
Turbulence slows; temperature stabilizes → Void forming.

D. Void Weather (Tree)

Still, cool, muted weather systems

Atmospheric signatures:

  • near‑total stillness
  • cool, even temperatures
  • fog, frost, or low‑lying mist
  • muted light

Pressure signatures:

  • downward settling
  • minimal variance

Drift signatures:

  • drift sinks
  • low velocity

Resonance signatures:

  • muted, low‑frequency hum

Diagnostic summary:
Weather feels empty, quiet, and suspended.

Transition indicator:
Subtle warming; mist begins to lift → Renewal awakening.

E. Renewal Weather (Lantern)

Warm, rising, flickering weather systems

Atmospheric signatures:

  • warm pockets
  • rising air columns
  • shimmering light gradients
  • early cloud re‑formation

Pressure signatures:

  • upward lift
  • soft outward expansion

Drift signatures:

  • spark drift
  • upward flicker

Resonance signatures:

  • rising, flickering resonance

Diagnostic summary:
Weather feels alive, awakening, and ready to begin.

Transition indicator:
Warm pockets merge; cloud patterns stabilize → Coherence returning.

3. TRANSITION WEATHER — THE HINGE CONDITIONS

Transitions have their own atmospheric signatures.

Coherence → Tension

  • warmth thickens
  • radial clouds become linear
  • humidity drops

Tension → Collapse

  • wind corridors break
  • sudden gusts
  • cloud fracturing

Collapse → Void

  • turbulence dissipates
  • fog or still air forms
  • temperature stabilizes

Void → Renewal

  • warm pockets appear
  • mist lifts
  • upward drift begins

Renewal → Coherence

  • warmth spreads evenly
  • cloud patterns harmonize
  • drift gathers inward

Transition weather is the most diagnostically important.

4. WEATHER DRIFT ECOLOGY

Drift determines weather behavior.

Coherence: inward drift → stable systems

Tension: compressed drift → pressure corridors

Collapse: scatter drift → turbulence

Void: downward drift → stillness

Renewal: upward drift → rising warmth

Weather is drift made visible.

5. WEATHER RESONANCE ECOLOGY

Resonance determines atmospheric tone.

Coherence: harmonic

Tension: sharp

Collapse: diffuse

Void: muted

Renewal: flickering

Weather is resonance made physical.

6. WEATHER PATHOLOGIES — WHEN THE CYCLE MISFIRES

1. Saturation Lock Weather

  • overly warm, overly stable
  • no pressure shift

2. Pressure Loop Weather

  • chronic vertical gradients
  • constant dryness

3. Collapse Cascade Weather

  • repeated turbulence
  • unstable precipitation

4. Deep Void Weather

  • prolonged stillness
  • no warming

5. Premature Renewal Weather

  • early warm pockets
  • fragile atmospheric forms

Weather pathologies reveal systemic imbalance.

7. DIAGNOSTIC CHECKLIST — RAPID WEATHER IDENTIFICATION

A quick five‑point diagnostic:

  1. Is the air gathering, compressing, scattering, settling, or rising?
  2. Is the temperature stable, gradient‑driven, chaotic, still, or warming?
  3. Is the wind inward, linear, erratic, absent, or upward?
  4. Is the light soft, sharp, diffuse, dim, or shimmering?
  5. Is the resonance harmonic, narrow, unstable, muted, or flickering?

The answers reveal the phase.
