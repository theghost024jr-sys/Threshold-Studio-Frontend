Definition Role Physics govern how roles behave as force‑bearing structures within the Field. A role is not a title. A role is a position in the Matrix of Force that shapes: • how vectors act • how drift accumulates • how thresholds form • how gifts move • how identity stabilizes • how lineage transmits Roles are the system’s structural scaffolding.

Purpose To ensure that: • roles carry force • roles shape motion • roles constrain behavior • roles generate thresholds • roles accumulate and resolve drift • roles maintain identity through change Role Physics keep the system coherent and interpretable.

Role States Every role exists in one of the following states:

  1. Dormant The role exists but is not currently held. No vectors are active.
  2. Held The role is actively occupied. Vectors engage. State Model updates.
  3. Strained The role is held under tension. Drift accumulates. Threshold proximity increases.
  4. Transitional The role is being assumed, released, or inherited. Identity is in motion.
  5. Collapsed The role fails due to unresolved drift or threshold overload. Forced resolution occurs.

Role Variables Each role tracks: • Role Load (how much force it can carry) • Role Integrity (how stable the role is) • Role Alignment (how well the holder fits the role) • Drift Load (how much unresolved force the role carries) • Threshold Pressure (how close the role is to shifting states) • Lineage Weight (how much inherited force the role carries) These variables determine how the role behaves.

Role Behaviors

  1. Roles shape vector behavior The same vector behaves differently depending on the role holding it. Examples: • Reciprocity in a leadership role differs from reciprocity in a novice role. • Drift in a guardian role behaves differently than drift in a witness role. Roles modulate force.

  2. Roles generate thresholds Every role has entry, exit, and collapse thresholds. Crossing them always produces transformation.

  3. Roles accumulate drift Roles carry unresolved obligations, inherited tensions, and structural burdens. Drift can be: • personal • relational • structural • lineage‑based Roles are drift magnets.

  4. Roles transmit force Roles pass force through: • lineage • gifts • obligations • thresholds • collapse events Roles are conduits.

  5. Roles stabilize identity Roles preserve identity through change. Identity is preserved; state is transformed.

Role Transitions A role transitions when: • it is assumed • it is released • it is inherited • it collapses • it is transformed • it is abandoned • it is forced open or closed by thresholds Transitions follow the State Model and Threshold Dynamics.

Role Failure Modes

  1. Misalignment The holder does not match the role’s requirements. Drift accumulates rapidly.
  2. Neglect The role is held without fulfilling its obligations. Collapse risk increases.
  3. Overload The role carries more force than its Role Load allows. Threshold collapse becomes likely.
  4. Inversion The role behaves opposite to its intended vector. Identity strain occurs.
  5. Abandonment The role is dropped without resolution. Drift propagates through lineage and relationships.

Role Resolution Roles resolve through: • Completion (role cycle ends cleanly) • Transformation (role becomes a new role) • Propagation (role passes through lineage) • Dissipation (role loses force through cost) • Collapse (forced resolution due to overload or neglect) All roles must resolve.

Function Role Physics: • define how force is held • shape vector behavior • generate thresholds • accumulate and resolve drift • stabilize identity • structure relationships • maintain coherence in the Field Without Role Physics, the system would have motion but no structure. With Role Physics, the system becomes architecture.
