Overview The Archetypal Roles Wheel is the geometric structure that defines how the six foundational roles relate to one another. It is the behavioral circuit of Threshold — a closed loop of resonance, drift, pressure, and adjacency. The wheel establishes: • drift direction • resonance pathways • shadow pressure points • role adjacency • the spatial logic used in the Roles canvas and Threshold Map This is the structural backbone of the Roles layer.

The Six‑Role Circuit The wheel forms a continuous loop: Sentinel → Weaver → Hunter → Oracle → Echo → Lantern → Sentinel Each adjacency is meaningful: • Sentinel → Weaver Protection becomes connection • Weaver → Hunter Patterning becomes pursuit • Hunter → Oracle Precision becomes insight • Oracle → Echo Interpretation becomes resonance • Echo → Lantern Reflection becomes illumination • Lantern → Sentinel Guidance becomes structure This circuit defines the behavioral physics of the Roles layer.

Drift Geometry Role drift follows the wheel’s direction: • Primary drift moves clockwise • Shadow drift occurs under pressure and may jump across the wheel • Collapse drift tends to move backward or inward Examples: • Sentinel → Weaver • Weaver → Hunter • Hunter → Oracle • Oracle → Echo • Echo → Lantern • Lantern → Sentinel Drift outside this pattern is rare and indicates extreme layer pressure or misalignment.

Resonance Pathways Each role resonates most strongly with its neighbors: • Sentinel ↔ Weaver • Weaver ↔ Hunter • Hunter ↔ Oracle • Oracle ↔ Echo • Echo ↔ Lantern • Lantern ↔ Sentinel These pathways define: • stabilizing relationships • supportive dynamics • shared elemental tendencies • compatible signal patterns Roles opposite each other on the wheel tend to create tension or shadow pressure.

Shadow Pressure Points Oppositional pairs generate the strongest shadow states: • Sentinel ↔ Oracle Structure vs. interpretation • Weaver ↔ Echo Connection vs. reflection • Hunter ↔ Lantern Pursuit vs. illumination These tensions shape: • collapse patterns • conflict arcs • layer‑pressure responses Shadow pressure is not inherently negative — it is a source of transformation.

Use in the Threshold Map The wheel provides the spatial logic for placing roles on the Threshold Map: • adjacency determines proximity • drift direction determines flow • shadow pressure determines fault lines • resonance determines stabilizing zones The wheel is the template for the Roles canvas and the future Map layer.
