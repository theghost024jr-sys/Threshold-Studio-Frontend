Signals that deform meaning, warp perception, and destabilize drift within an environment or identity.

Purpose Distortion Signals describe the class of signals that introduce semantic noise, perceptual bending, or structural deformation into Threshold’s signal field. They are not inherently harmful — distortion is a pressure phenomenon, not a moral one. Distortion Signals emerge when: • environmental pressure spikes • drift becomes unstable • identity boundaries thin • collapse fields form • species with distortion affinity are present • Mistborne or Echo‑aligned entities interact Distortion Signals are the signature language of instability.

The Three Distortion Families Distortion Signals fall into three canonical families:

  1. Echo‑Curved Signals Distortion through repetition, delay, or reflection. • Meaning loops back altered • Signals return with shifted emphasis • Perception splits into multiple interpretations • Time feels staggered or doubled Common Effects: • Echo drift • Perception splitting • Recursive thought patterns Example Signals: • Mist Echo • Hollow Return • Split‑Tone Pulse

  2. Spiral‑Aligned Signals Distortion through rotation, tightening, or inward collapse. • Meaning compresses • Boundaries twist • Direction becomes ambiguous • Drift vectors curve inward Common Effects: • Spiral collapse • Directional confusion • Pressure intensification Example Signals: • Spiral Knot • Inward Pull • Collapse Helix

  3. Mirage‑Pattern Signals Distortion through overlay, shimmer, or false patterning. • Meaning appears where none exists • Signals overlay each other • Perception becomes suggestive rather than literal • Boundaries blur Common Effects: • Mirage drift • Pattern hallucination • Boundary thinning Example Signals: • Shimmer Veil • Mirage Thread • False‑Pattern Bloom
