Overview Layer Drift 1 is the surface expression of Drift physics — the shallowest, most accessible layer where motion, emotion, and environmental resonance begin to interact. It is not turbulent, not deep, not destabilizing. It is the entry layer of Drift: gentle, directional, and readable. Drift 1 governs: • soft environmental motion • emotional gradients • low‑intensity turbulence • early drift cues • surface‑level resonance patterns It is the layer most species can perceive without training.

Core Properties

  1. Low Turbulence Motion is soft, slow, and predictable. Currents form gentle spirals and directional flows.
  2. Emotional Sensitivity Drift 1 responds to: • calm • curiosity • mild tension • low‑grade fear • anticipation It does not respond strongly to grief, collapse, or high emotional charge.
  3. Surface Resonance Resonance patterns are: • shallow • readable • low‑density • stable These patterns form the basis of Whisper Cartography.
  4. Soft Guidance Drift 1 provides: • path suggestions • orientation cues • environmental “lean” • subtle warnings It never forces movement.

Behavior Baseline State Calm, slow, directional motion. Feels like a gentle pull or soft wind. Pulse State Occurs when emotional charge rises slightly. Creates rhythmic motion patterns. Ripple State Triggered by symbolic density. Creates small waves or oscillations. Disturbance State Triggered by: • sudden fear • narrative pressure • minor collapse residue Creates brief turbulence but stabilizes quickly.

Environmental Interactions With Mist Mist thickens Drift 1 motion, making cues easier to read. With Shimmer Shimmer curvature bends Drift 1 currents slightly, creating soft arcs. With Stone Stone anchors Drift 1, reducing turbulence. With Ember Ember heat disrupts Drift 1, causing flicker or retreat. With Ethyl Ethyl flow aligns Drift 1 currents, increasing coherence.

Species Perception Most species can perceive Drift 1 through: • body orientation • emotional shifts • subtle environmental cues • Mosslight behavior • Mist density changes Drift‑aligned species perceive it directly as motion.

Navigation Use Drift 1 is used for: • Whisper Cartography • Threshold Weather Navigation • emotional orientation • safe pathfinding • early collapse detection It is the training ground for deeper Drift layers.

Drift 1 vs Drift 2 A critical distinction:

Drift 1 is the accessible layer. Drift 2 is the danger layer.

Layer Drift

Layer Drift describes how identity, emotion, signal, and residue move between Layers within Threshold.
It is not collapse, not travel, not transition.
It is physics leakage — the tendency of one Layer’s rules to bleed into another.

Layer Drift is the primary mechanism by which instability spreads across the Layer Stack.

Purpose

  • Define how drift behaves across Layer boundaries
  • Map cross‑Layer pressure transfer and resonance distortion
  • Support collapse detection and identity stabilization
  • Provide a framework for multi‑Layer interpretation
  • Maintain coherence across the Layer Stack

Core Principles

1. Drift Follows Pressure

Drift moves from:

  • high pressure → low pressure
  • dense memory → open fields
  • fracture zones → stable resonance

Pressure gradients determine drift direction.

2. Drift Is Layer‑Dependent

Each Layer modifies drift differently:

  • Mist: amplifies drift
  • Air: accelerates drift
  • Stone: resists drift
  • Root: slows drift
  • Glass: distorts drift
  • Ethyl: curves drift
  • Ash: scatters drift
3. Drift Is Not Movement

Drift is a change in physics, not location.
Identity remains where it is — but the rules acting on it shift.

4. Drift Is Reversible

Drift can be stabilized or redirected through:

  • pressure balancing
  • resonance tuning
  • boundary reinforcement
  • collapse reversal protocols

Drift Types

Vertical Drift

Movement up or down the Layer Stack.
Examples:

  • Root → Mist (memory bleed)
  • Glass → Stone (pressure retreat)
  • Mist → Air (permeability escape)
Lateral Drift

Movement within a Layer’s internal physics.
Examples:

  • Stone fracture → Stone compression
  • Mist diffusion → Mist turbulence
Curvature Drift

Symbolic drift shaped by Ethyl Layer physics.
Examples:

  • recursive loops
  • meaning curvature
  • glyph‑driven drift
Fracture Drift

Drift triggered by collapse adjacency.
Examples:

  • Ash scattering
  • Glass shatter drift
  • Mist collapse weather

Drift Indicators

  • boundary thinning
  • resonance distortion
  • vector misalignment
  • emotional turbulence
  • memory bleed
  • pressure inversion
  • shimmer onset

These indicators appear before collapse signatures.

Cross‑Layer Drift Patterns

Mist ↔ Air
  • high permeability
  • rapid drift
  • emotional bleed
Glass ↔ Ethyl
  • symbolic distortion
  • exposure recursion
  • meaning curvature
Root ↔ Ash
  • memory compression
  • residue formation
  • collapse adjacency
Stone ↔ Root
  • pressure retention

  • structural weight

Drift Under Collapse Conditions

Collapse accelerates drift through:

  • pressure spikes
  • residue turbulence
  • fracture propagation
  • environmental instability

Drift becomes:

  • scattered in Ash
  • distorted in Glass
  • amplified in Mist
  • inverted in Air

Stabilization Methods

Pressure Rebalancing

Reduce gradients that pull drift across Layers.

Boundary Reinforcement

Strengthen identity containment.

Resonance Tuning

Align identity with Layer physics.

Counter‑Drift Vectors

Redirect drift into stable channels.

Environmental Dampening

Reduce turbulence in collapse weather.

Interpretation Framework

Relational Drift

Drift caused by adjacency to another Layer.

Contextual Drift

Drift shaped by emotional state or collapse proximity.

Systemic Drift

Drift across the entire Layer Stack.
