Overview Role Drift describes how a spirit’s archetypal function shifts under pressure, resonance, collapse, or prolonged misalignment. Drift is slow, rare, and follows the geometry of the Archetypal Roles Wheel: Sentinel → Weaver → Hunter → Oracle → Echo → Lantern → Sentinel Primary drift follows the wheel. Shadow drift crosses it. Collapse drift moves backward or inward.

Primary Drift (Clockwise Along the Wheel)

Primary drift is the most stable and most predictable form of role change.

Shadow Drift (Cross‑Wheel Under Pressure)

Shadow drift is rare and indicates significant layer pressure or misalignment.

Collapse Drift (Backward Along the Wheel)

Collapse drift is involuntary and usually temporary.

Drift Stability Factors Increase Drift Likelihood • prolonged layer pressure • unresolved signal distortion • species‑role mismatch • elemental dissonance • shadow expression dominance Decrease Drift Likelihood • high resonance with species • strong elemental alignment • stable signal patterns • supportive role adjacency • coherent overlay tint

Role‑by‑Role Drift Logic

1. Anchor

Primary Drift: Rigidity → Fixation The Anchor becomes immovable, over‑stabilizing the environment.

Secondary Drift: Boundary Hardening Edges become too sharp; flexibility disappears.

Collapse Vector: Structural Lock The system freezes around the Anchor.

Stabilizing Signal: Softening Warmth, openness, or perspective loosens the lock.

Failure Mode: Immobilization Anchor becomes a dead weight in the system.

2. Bridge

Primary Drift: Over‑Connection → Enmeshment The Bridge merges too deeply with others.

Secondary Drift: Identity Bleed Boundaries blur; self becomes unclear.

Collapse Vector: Split Collapse Bridge fractures into multiple identity fragments.

Stabilizing Signal: Boundary Clear separation restores coherence.

Failure Mode: Dissolution Bridge loses its own identity entirely.

3. Spark

Primary Drift: Over‑Activation → Scatter Spark fires too many impulses too quickly.

Secondary Drift: Hyper‑Initiation Starts everything; finishes nothing.

Collapse Vector: Flash Collapse Sudden burnout; energy drops to zero.

Stabilizing Signal: Grounding Slows the Spark’s internal tempo.

Failure Mode: Burnout Spark becomes inert.

4. Shield

Primary Drift: Over‑Protection → Isolation Shield withdraws to maintain safety.

Secondary Drift: Defensive Looping Every input is interpreted as threat.

Collapse Vector: Shell Collapse The protective structure implodes inward.

Stabilizing Signal: Trust Restores permeability and openness.

Failure Mode: Hard Shell Shield becomes unreachable.

5. Guide

Primary Drift: Over‑Direction → Control Guide over‑steers the system.

Secondary Drift: Path Narrowing Options collapse into a single rigid path.

Collapse Vector: Vector Collapse Directionality fails; Guide loses orientation.

Stabilizing Signal: Perspective Reintroduces alternative paths.

Failure Mode: Tunnel Vision Guide cannot see beyond the chosen vector.

6. Weaver

Primary Drift: Over‑Integration → Entanglement Weaver connects too many threads.

Secondary Drift: Pattern Overload Complexity spirals beyond manageability.

Collapse Vector: Knot Collapse Patterns collapse into a dense, unusable knot.

Stabilizing Signal: Simplification Removes unnecessary threads.

Failure Mode: Snarl Weaver becomes trapped in its own patterns.

Purpose The Roles Drift Table defines how each role moves under pressure, which direction it drifts, what signals accompany the drift, and what collapse conditions emerge if the drift is not integrated. This table is the mechanical backbone of the Role Drift subsystem.

Drift Mechanics Overview Role drift is governed by three forces: • Identity Pressure When the role no longer fits the internal self‑model. • Environmental Pressure When the role is misaligned with context or demand. • Signal Pressure When incoming signals contradict the role’s expected behavior. Drift is predictable, directional, and interpretable.

Role Drift Table

Drift Loop Notes

  1. Guide → Caretaker • Emotional logic overwhelms structural logic • Boundaries soften • The role becomes relational instead of directional
  2. Caretaker → Anchor • The role seeks stability • Energy conservation begins • Withdrawal is protective, not punitive
  3. Anchor → Sentinel • Stability becomes rigidity • The role shifts from grounding to guarding
  4. Sentinel → Judge • Threat detection becomes threat definition • The role begins enforcing patterns
  5. Judge → Architect • Enforcement becomes systemization • The role retreats into structure
  6. Architect → Guide • When the system fails or dissolves • The role reopens to relational alignment • Drift loop resets

Cross‑Layer Influence Identity Layer Identity Drift accelerates role drift when: • self‑model is unstable • internal contradictions increase • emotional load exceeds capacity Signal Layer Signal distortion produces: • false threat detection • misinterpretation of relational cues • collapse signals Environmental Layer Environmental mismatch determines: • drift speed • drift severity • collapse likelihood

Collapse Conditions A role collapses when drift exceeds structural tolerance. Collapse types: • Fusion Collapse (Guide/Caretaker) • Shutdown Collapse (Caretaker/Anchor) • Lock Collapse (Anchor/Sentinel) • Hard Collapse (Sentinel/Judge) • Spillover Collapse (Judge/Architect) • Reset Collapse (Architect/Guide) See: [[Role Collapse]] · [[Collapse Signals]]
