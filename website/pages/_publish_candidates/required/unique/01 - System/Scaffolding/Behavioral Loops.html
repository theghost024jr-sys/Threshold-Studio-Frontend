Re00 Meta Systems — Behavioral Layer / Identity Cluster Behavioral Loops describe the repeating action‑patterns that emerge when identity, emotion, environment, and signal pressure interact. Where Resonance Loops describe recursive meaning, Behavioral Loops describe recursive action — the visible, embodied cycles that beings fall into when under strain, alignment, or transformation. A Behavioral Loop is not habit. It is behavior shaped by systemic forces.

Purpose Behavioral Loops define: • how actions repeat under emotional or environmental pressure • how identity drift becomes visible in behavior • how roles manifest through repeated patterns • how species tendencies reinforce or destabilize loops • how collapse or healing becomes embodied • how players and NPCs reveal internal state through motion They are the behavioral physics of Threshold.

Primary Loop Types

  1. Stabilizing Loops Behavior repeats in a grounding, predictable pattern. • consistent actions • low drift • emotional regulation • identity reinforcement Common in Earth, Air, and Stone environments.

  2. Escalation Loops Behavior intensifies with each cycle. • rising emotional charge • overexpression • role amplification • drift acceleration Common in Ember and Ethyl environments.

  3. Avoidance Loops Behavior circles around a pressure point without engaging it. • dissociation • withdrawal • displacement • underexpression Common in Air and Mist environments.

  4. Inversion Loops Behavior flips polarity on each cycle. • contradictory actions • shadow activation • unstable identity • collapse precursors Common in Mist–Ember boundary zones.

  5. Fragmentation Loops Behavior breaks into inconsistent or conflicting patterns. • erratic motion • fractured identity • signal distortion • emotional overload Common in Stone environments or high‑pressure states.

  6. Restorative Loops Behavior cycles toward integration and repair. • grounding actions • emotional release • identity realignment • relational repair Common in Healer‑aligned species and Earth environments.

Loop Mechanics Trigger A Behavioral Loop begins when: • emotional load spikes • identity drift activates • resonance signals propagate • environmental curvature bends behavior • role pressure increases Cycle The behavior repeats with: • increased intensity • increased distortion • increased clarity • or increased integration depending on the loop type. Exit Conditions A loop ends when: • emotional charge dissipates • environment shifts • identity stabilizes • a role realigns • a restorative signal intervenes Behavioral Loops are state‑dependent, not permanent.

Environmental Influence Each Layer shapes loops differently: • Air: dissipation, avoidance loops • Earth: grounding, stabilizing loops • Ember: escalation loops • Mist: inversion and avoidance loops • Stone: fragmentation loops • Ethyl: surreal escalation or inversion loops Behavior becomes predictable once the environment is known.

Species Influence Species alter loops through: • instinctive tendencies • sensory bias • emotional resonance • drift susceptibility This is where the Species–Role Sense Table and Species–Role Expression Table plug in.

Role Influence Roles shape loops through: • archetypal stance • emotional posture • relational position • drift vectors This is where Role Drift and Role Identity Drift connect.

Downstream Effects Behavioral Loops lead to: • visible drift • role misalignment • identity strain • collapse signals • threshold events • restorative cycles They are the embodied expression of deeper systemic forces.
