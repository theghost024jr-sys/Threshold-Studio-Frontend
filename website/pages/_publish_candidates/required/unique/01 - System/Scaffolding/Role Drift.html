00 Meta Systems — Roles Layer / Signal Cluster Role Drift describes the natural directional movement of a Role within the archetypal lattice when exposed to emotional pressure, environmental curvature, species resonance, or signal distortion. Every Role has inherent drift vectors — predictable directions it tends to move when destabilized. Role Drift is not collapse. It is the first motion of a Role under strain.

Purpose Role Drift defines: • the natural drift vectors between Roles • how Roles shift under environmental pressure • how emotional states bend Role expression • how species resonance alters Role behavior • how signals accelerate or distort drift • how drift leads to inversion, displacement, or collapse It is the navigation map for understanding how Roles behave in motion.

Primary Drift Types

  1. Primary Drift The default direction a Role moves when lightly destabilized. This is the “nearest neighbor” in the archetypal lattice. Examples (generic, not species‑specific): • Guide → Maker • Witness → Healer • Trickster → Maker • Keeper → Warden Primary Drift is predictable and reversible.

  2. Secondary Drift A deeper shift into a more distant archetype. Occurs under moderate emotional or environmental pressure. Examples: • Guide → Witness • Maker → Trickster • Warden → Keeper Secondary Drift is less stable and often accompanied by distortion.

  3. Shadow Drift Movement toward the Role’s shadow form. This is the beginning of inversion. Examples: • Guide → Misleader • Witness → Absorber • Maker → Destroyer • Trickster → Saboteur Shadow Drift is dangerous but not yet collapse.

  4. Collapse Drift The Role moves toward its collapse vector — the archetype it becomes when overwhelmed. Examples: • Warden → Oppressor • Healer → Martyr • Trickster → Chaos Agent Collapse Drift is the final stage before Role Collapse.

Environmental Influence Each Layer shapes Role Drift differently: • Air: underexpression, dissipation, slow drift • Earth: grounding, resistance to drift • Ember: intensification, overexpression drift • Mist: ambiguity, inversion drift • Stone: rigidity, fracture drift • Ethyl: symbolic bleed‑through, surreal drift Drift becomes predictable once the environment is known.

Species Influence Species alter Role Drift through: • sensory bias • emotional resonance • instinctive tendencies • drift susceptibility This is where the Species–Role Sense Table and Species–Role Expression Table plug in.

Signal Influence Signals accelerate or bend drift through: • distortion • drift arcs • resonance loops • curvature types This is the bridge between the Roles Layer and the Signal Cluster.

Drift Thresholds A Role enters Drift when: • emotional load exceeds stability • identity drift begins • species resonance spikes • environmental curvature bends meaning • signals distort or invert Drift is the early warning system for Role Collapse.

Drift Outcomes Role Drift leads to: • temporary misalignment • partial inversion • displacement into neighboring roles • shadow expression • collapse vectors • threshold events Drift is the motion, collapse is the destination.
