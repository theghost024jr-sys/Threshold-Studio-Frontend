00 Meta Systems — Signal Cluster / Field Dynamics Curvature Types describe the different ways space, meaning, and motion bend within Threshold. Curvature determines how signals travel, distort, drift, or collapse when moving through an environment with emotional, elemental, or structural pressure. Curvature is not visual. It is behavioral geometry — the shape of influence.

Purpose Curvature Types define: • how signals bend under pressure • how fields warp meaning • how drift arcs form • how shimmer behaves • how collapse propagates • how environments alter motion • how roles misinterpret or amplify signals This file establishes the taxonomy of curvature so the rest of the Signal Cluster can reference it cleanly.

Primary Curvature Types

  1. Linear Curvature Meaning moves in a straight, predictable line. • low distortion • low drift • high clarity • common in Air and Stone environments Used as the baseline for comparison.

  2. Concave Curvature Meaning bends inward toward a center. • compression • intensification • emotional gravity • common in Ember and Earth environments Signals become denser and more charged.

  3. Convex Curvature Meaning bends outward, away from center. • dissipation • scattering • over‑clarity or over‑exposure • common in Air and Mist environments Signals become thin, stretched, or over‑interpreted.

  4. Spiral Curvature Meaning rotates as it moves. • echoing • repetition • recursive interpretation • common in Mist and Ethyl environments Signals return altered, often with layered meaning.

  5. Shear Curvature Meaning splits along a plane. • fragmentation • polarity inversion • semantic fracture • common in Stone and Ember environments Signals break into contradictory pieces.

  6. Turbulent Curvature Meaning moves through chaotic, unstable fields. • shimmer noise • unpredictable drift • collapse precursors • common in Ember–Mist boundary zones Signals become volatile and difficult to interpret.

Curvature as Diagnostic Curvature reveals: • environmental instability • emotional pressure • role misalignment • drift thresholds • collapse vectors • shimmer fields It is one of the earliest indicators that a system is approaching a threshold event.
