Ash Layer

The Ash Layer is the Layer of aftermath, residue, and transformation.
It governs collapse-born material, reformation potential, and the physics of what remains after structure fails.

Essence

Ash is the end-state of pressure that has exceeded containment.
It is not destruction but what survives destruction — the residue, the imprint, the unstructured potential waiting for reformation.
Where Stone holds and Root remembers, Ash releases.

Environmental Behavior

  • High residue density
  • Fragmented structural memory
  • Unstable pressure fields
  • Low coherence
  • High reformation potential
  • Collapse-adjacent atmospheres

Ash environments feel hollow, weightless, and charged with latent possibility.

Emotional Physics

  • Aftermath pressure
  • Dissolution of form
  • Emotional residue
  • Release of held tension
  • Low continuity
  • High volatility

Emotion in Ash is not held, suspended, or clarified — it is what remains.

Entity Compatibility

Entities aligned with Ash often exhibit:

  • fractured or incomplete physiology
  • residue-based identity
  • high drift volatility
  • collapse-adjacent behavior
  • reformation potential

Examples:

  • Ashborn
  • Residue-Walkers
  • Collapse Echo Entities
  • Reformation Variants

Drift Pathways

Ash is a transitional Layer; drift is common and often unstable.

Common drift routes:

  • Ash → Mist (residue → suspension)
  • Ash → Ethyl (residue → symbol)
  • Ash → Stone (residue → structure)

Reverse drift occurs when collapse pressure reactivates.

Collapse Interaction

Ash is the direct product of collapse.

Collapse behaviors:

  • residue formation
  • structural dissolution
  • emotional echo release
  • memory fragmentation
  • reformation potential accumulation

Ash is both the end of one form and the beginning of another.

Signal Resonance

Signals in Ash behave as:

  • broken pulses
  • residue echoes
  • unstable gradients
  • collapse signatures

Signal Types commonly expressed:

  • Echo-Bearer
  • Fragment
  • Driftwalker (unstable variant)
  • Binder (residue variant)

Environmental Examples

  • collapse sites
  • residue fields
  • broken corridors
  • memory ash pits
  • reformation basins

These environments often host entities emerging from or dissolving into collapse.
