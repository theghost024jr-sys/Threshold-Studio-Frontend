Map Name Substrate Fault Lines Map Type Deep Structural Seam Map / System Instability Reference Purpose Substrate fault lines are structural seams formed by uneven substrate density. They are the primary conduits for: • collapse propagation • symbolic shear • resonance amplification • density inversion events • mythic discharge • boundary fracture Fault lines are not geographic. They are structural relationships between density zones.

  1. Fault Line Definition A substrate fault line is a density discontinuity where: • substrate tension accumulates • gradients steepen • symbolic pressure increases • resonance becomes unstable Fault lines are the deepest predictors of systemic instability.

  2. Fault Line Types A. Primary Fault Lines Characteristics: • major density discontinuities • long-range influence • high collapse susceptibility • strong resonance amplification Primary fault lines define the macro-structure of Threshold’s instability.

B. Secondary Fault Lines Characteristics: • moderate density differences • localized influence • predictable behavior • medium collapse risk Secondary lines often feed into primary ones.

C. Micro-Fault Lines Characteristics: • small-scale density fractures • short-lived • low collapse risk • high symbolic noise Micro-faults often precede larger instability events.

C. Collapse Conduction Fault lines act as pathways for collapse. Effects: • collapse travels along fault lines • propagation speed increases • collapse fronts sharpen • vortex formation becomes more likely
