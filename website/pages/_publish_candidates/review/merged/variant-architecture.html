The lawful divergence of a single canonical system A Variant is not an alternate universe. A Variant is a lawful transformation of the Core, governed by strict rules: • It must preserve the canonical physics • It must preserve the canonical layers • It must preserve the canonical identity grammar • It must preserve the canonical emotional physics • It must diverge only along defined axes A Variant is a fork, not a contradiction. Think of it as: Threshold → expressed through a different curvature.

  1. The Four Canonical Components of a Variant Every Variant has exactly four architectural components: 1.1 Variant Frame The initial conditions that define the Variant’s identity. Examples of Frame parameters: • Environmental curvature (season, scarcity, abundance, decay) • Signal density (quiet world, loud world, fractured world) • Identity tension (roles compressed, roles expanded, roles inverted) • Emotional climate (warm, brittle, volatile, numb) • Map deformation (tight, stretched, fragmented, nested) The Frame is the seed.

1.2 Variant Drift How the Variant moves away from the Core over time. Drift is lawful. It follows: • Primary Drift — the intended direction of divergence • Shadow Drift — the unintended consequence • Collapse Drift — the failure mode if the Variant becomes unstable This mirrors your Role Drift system, but at the world scale.

1.3 Variant Thresholds The points where the Variant must make a choice. These are the “forking events”: • A gift is refused • A lineage breaks • A collapse accelerates • A signal overloads • A role inverts • A map boundary is crossed • A species violates its own physics • A refuge is abandoned or corrupted Each Threshold forces the Variant to either: • Re-align with the Core, or • Diverge further, or • Enter Collapse This is the Variant’s “decision tree.”

1.4 Variant Collapse or Reintegration Every Variant ends in one of three canonical outcomes: A. Reintegration The Variant returns to the Core, bringing back: • new gifts • new scars • new lineage • new physics • new memory This is how Threshold evolves without breaking. B. Stable Divergence The Variant becomes a parallel but lawful expression of the Core. It is not “alternate canon.” It is adjacent canon. C. Collapse The Variant fails and becomes: • a dead world • a broken map • a corrupted signal • a collapsed lineage • a ghost environment Collapsed Variants become warnings and lessons for future Frames.

Variant Architecture

Source: 01 - System/Scaffolding/Variant Architecture.md

Purpose Variant Architecture defines the system‑level rules governing how variations emerge, stabilize, diverge, collapse, and propagate across all layers of Threshold. It ensures that every variant — whether biological, behavioral, identity‑based, or role‑based — follows the same underlying physics. Variants are not exceptions. They are expressions of systemic pressure.

  1. Origin Conditions A variant emerges when three pressures align: • Environmental Pressure A mismatch between the entity and its environment. • Signal Pressure Persistent distortion, overload, or misinterpretation. • Identity Pressure A conflict between internal structure and external demand. When all three reach threshold, a variant forms.

  2. Divergence Rules Once a variant emerges, it diverges according to: • Layer Priority The dominant layer (Species, Identity, Role, Signal) dictates the direction of divergence. • Drift Load The accumulated unresolved pressure determines the rate of divergence. • Overlay Influence Elemental overlays bias the variant toward specific behaviors or expressions. Variants do not drift randomly — they drift predictably based on these forces.

  3. Stabilization Rules A variant stabilizes when: • Drift load decreases • Environmental mismatch resolves • Signal distortion reduces • Identity conflict integrates Stabilization produces a coherent variant type, not a temporary anomaly.

  4. Collapse Conditions Variants collapse when: • Drift load exceeds structural capacity • Role or Identity cannot integrate the divergence • Signals become non‑interpretable • Environmental pressure becomes absolute Collapse produces: • Collapse Signals • Role Collapse • Identity Fracture • Species Regression Collapse is not failure — it is a reset mechanism.

  5. Inheritance Rules Variants propagate through: • Species Lineage • Identity Templates • Role Patterns • Signal Loops • Environmental Imprinting A variant that stabilizes becomes heritable across its domain.

  6. Cross‑Layer Integration Variants interact across layers using the Universal Integration Engine: • Species variants influence Identity • Identity variants influence Roles • Role variants influence Signals • Signals influence Environment • Environment feeds back into Species This creates variant ecosystems, not isolated anomalies.

  7. Variant Classes All variants fall into one of four classes: • Structural Variants (Species, physiology, architecture) • Behavioral Variants (Role expression, loops, drift) • Interpretive Variants (Signal processing, meaning‑making) • Identity Variants (Self‑model, drift, fracture, integration) Each class obeys the same meta‑rules but expresses them differently.
