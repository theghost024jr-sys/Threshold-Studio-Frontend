Purpose The Behavioral Index defines the core behavioral physics of Threshold. It is the upstream registry that organizes: • resonance pairs • behavioral modes • stress behaviors • break behaviors • drift‑behavior coupling • collapse‑behavior coupling • interaction patterns This file does not describe roles or species. It describes the behavioral forces that everything in the world expresses.

  1. Behavioral Structure Every behavior in Threshold follows a three‑state model: 1.1 Stable Behavior Identity is coherent. Drift is linear or rhythmic. Collapse risk is low. 1.2 Stress Behavior Identity stretches or compresses. Drift accelerates or curves. Collapse becomes possible. 1.3 Break Behavior Identity fractures or inverts. Drift becomes turbulent or spiral. Collapse becomes active. This tri‑state model applies to: • roles • species • signals • environments • collapse events • emotional states

  2. The Seven Resonance Pairs (Behavioral Axes) These are the seven fundamental behavioral axes. Each pair defines a behavioral spectrum with stable, stress, and break modes.

2.1 Stillness ↔ Motion Stable: grounded presence Stress: agitation, pacing Break: sudden collapse, snap movement

2.2 Pattern ↔ Noise Stable: clarity, meaning Stress: overload, confusion Break: fragmentation collapse

2.3 Connection ↔ Isolation Stable: healthy bonding Stress: cling or withdrawal Break: overconnection collapse or severance collapse

2.4 Order ↔ Chaos Stable: structure, predictability Stress: tension, misalignment Break: spiral collapse

2.5 Expansion ↔ Contraction Stable: healthy reach Stress: overextension or overcompression Break: dispersive or implosive collapse

2.6 Memory ↔ Forgetting Stable: continuity, narrative Stress: rumination or drift loss Break: recursive collapse

2.7 Vulnerability ↔ Protection Stable: openness with boundaries Stress: defensiveness or overexposure Break: defensive collapse
