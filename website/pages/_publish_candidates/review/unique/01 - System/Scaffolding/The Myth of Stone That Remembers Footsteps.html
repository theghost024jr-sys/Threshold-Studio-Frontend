They say there is a stone somewhere in the world — no one agrees where — that remembers every footstep ever placed upon it. Not the people. Not their names. Not their stories. Just the weight. The first to step on it was a child chasing a bird. The stone kept the lightness of that moment, the way joy barely touches the earth. The next was an old man carrying a grief he never spoke aloud. The stone kept that too — a heaviness that settled into its grain like rainwater into cracks. Over centuries, travelers crossed it without knowing. Some hurried. Some wandered. Some paused just long enough for the stone to feel the tremble of a decision being made. The stone never judged. It only remembered. But here’s the part people argue about: Some say if you stand on the stone long enough, it will lend you a memory that isn’t yours. Not a vision. Not a voice. Just a weight — the echo of someone else’s moment. A sudden lightness that makes you breathe easier. A heaviness that makes you understand something you didn’t have words for. A steadiness that wasn’t yours when you arrived. The oldest storytellers say the stone isn’t magical at all. They say it simply knows the truth that people forget: Every step changes the ground, and sometimes the ground gives something back. [[Whisperhawk Signal]]
