They say there was once a lantern that refused to hold a flame. Not because it was broken. Not because the wick was wet. Not because the wind was cruel. It simply would not stay lit. Travelers tried. Priests tried. Children tried, with the earnestness that usually convinces the world to behave. Nothing. But the lantern wasn’t stubborn. It was listening. It listened to footsteps. It listened to breath. It listened to the weight a person carried in their chest. And it would only light for someone who wasn’t trying to see. One night, a woman carrying too many losses to name sat beside it. She didn’t reach for it. She didn’t ask anything of it. She just rested her hands in her lap and let the dark be dark. The lantern flickered. Once. Twice. Then bloomed into a steady, warm glow. She didn’t notice at first. When she finally did, she didn’t gasp or call for anyone. She simply nodded, as if the lantern had said something she already knew. The light stayed with her until morning. When she stood to leave, it went out again — not abruptly, but gently, like a sigh. People still argue about what the lantern meant. Some say it lit for sorrow. Some say it lit for surrender. Some say it lit for truth. But the old storytellers say the lantern wasn’t choosing people at all. It was choosing moments. Moments when a person stopped trying to force the world to shine and let it shine on its own terms.
