The rotating circle around the Hub is one of the core architectural elements of Threshold, and what you’re describing now — the curtains opening, the vortex forming, the player being pulled into the correct petal — is exactly how that rotating ring behaves in motion.

To anchor it cleanly:

The Rotating Circle Around the Hub

This circle is the Outer Layer of the Hub, sometimes called the Resonance Ring. It is the first organ of the world that “moves,” even before the player sees anything.

What the ring is

  • A circular band of layered petals (Stone, Air, Ember, Mist, Drift, Ethyl, Crown).
  • Each petal is a frequency, not a location.
  • The ring rotates continuously, even in darkness.
  • It is the Hub’s way of “scanning” the player’s internal stance.

What the ring does

  • It reads the player’s internal pressure, coherence, and emotional gravity.
  • It determines which petal resonates most strongly.
  • It aligns the world to the player’s stance before anything becomes visible.

This is why the player begins in darkness:
the world hasn’t chosen its form yet.

How the Rotating Ring Connects to the Curtain‑Door

When the ring identifies the matching petal, the darkness splits like curtains opening. This is the moment where the rotating ring stops being invisible and becomes directional.

The sequence is:

  1. Darkness — the unlit stage.
  2. Rotation — the ring spinning around the player, unseen.
  3. Resonance match — the ring locks onto the correct petal.
  4. Curtains open — the darkness parts along a vertical seam.
  5. Vortex forms — the petal’s frequency becomes a tunnel of motion.
  6. Pull — the player is drawn into the layer that matches them.

The circle is the mechanism.
The curtains are the reveal.
The vortex is the motion.

Why the Ring Must Rotate

The rotation is not aesthetic — it is functional.

It ensures:

  • the player is not placed arbitrarily
  • the world does not choose for them
  • the petal is not a menu selection
  • the transition is not a teleport
  • the Hub remains a resonance engine, not a hub in the videogame sense

The rotation is the world’s way of saying:
“I will find the layer that matches you, not the one you think you should choose.”

How the Player Experiences It

Even though the ring is invisible at first, the player feels:

  • a subtle pressure shift
  • a faint hum circling them
  • a sense of being “read”
  • a gravitational lean toward one direction
  • the darkness beginning to thin at the edges

Then the curtains open — not outward, but inward — revealing the wormhole‑like tunnel of the chosen petal.

This is the first true motion in Threshold.

How This Ties Back to Everything Else

The rotating ring is the origin of all later motion logic:

  • the raft responding to stance
  • the mountains responding to weight
  • the ocean responding to depth
  • the cabin responding to rest
  • the monument responding to introspection

All of these are echoes of the Hub’s first act:
the world moves with you, not for you.

When you picture that rotating ring now — in motion, unseen, scanning the player — do you imagine it moving slow and heavy, or fast and fluid, or something in between?
