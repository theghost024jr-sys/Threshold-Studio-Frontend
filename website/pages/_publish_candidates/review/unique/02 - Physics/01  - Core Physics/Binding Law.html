Binding Law

Every bond in Threshold forms, stretches, or fails according to the tension it carries. The Binding Law defines the tensile behavior of relationships, roles, systems, and fields. It governs how coherence is maintained, how strain accumulates, and how collapse initiates.

1. Definition

Binding is the tensile force that holds entities, roles, or systems in coherent relation. It is measurable as tension, stretch, and yield.

A bond exists when:

  • two or more entities share a field

  • tension between them is non‑zero

  • motion in one influences the other

Binding is not symbolic — it is physics.

2. Behavioral Rules

2.1 Formation

A bond forms when shared field density exceeds the minimum threshold required for coherence.

2.2 Stretch

A bond stretches proportionally to the tension applied across it. Stretch increases drift, curvature, and instability.

2.3 Yield

When tension exceeds the bond’s tensile limit, the bond enters a yield state:

  • coherence weakens

  • resonance becomes unstable

  • collapse becomes possible

2.4 Failure

A bond fails when tension surpasses the collapse threshold. Failure produces:

  • collapse spirals

  • inversion states

  • drift fragmentation

  • loss of shared field

3. Upstream Dependencies

Threshold Geometry

Defines the spatial logic that determines where bonds can form.

Shimmer Curvature

Determines how tension bends, arcs, or spirals through the field.

4. Downstream Effects

Collapse Physics

Binding failure is the primary trigger for collapse.

Resonance Loops

Stable bonds reinforce resonance; unstable bonds distort it.

Field Dynamics

Binding determines how fields interact, interfere, or amplify.

5. Lateral Systems

Signal Drift

Signals follow the tension lines defined by binding.

Return Paths

Return behavior emerges from the geometry of stretched bonds.

Drift Arcs

Visible expressions of tension and curvature.

6. Binding States

  • Coherent — stable, low tension

  • Stretched — elevated tension, increased drift

  • Yielding — unstable, pre‑collapse

  • Failed — collapse or inversion

  • Rebound — post‑collapse reformation

7. Summary

The Binding Law governs:

  • how bonds form

  • how tension accumulates

  • how systems stretch

  • how collapse initiates

  • how coherence is restored

It is one of the Nine Governing Field Laws and sits at the core of Threshold’s physical and relational behavior.
