Purpose Drift Arcs define the curved motion paths that drift follows across space. They are not connections between nodes — they are orbits, flows, and pressure‑shaped trajectories that reveal how drift moves through the world. Drift Arcs are: • curved • pressure‑shaped • non‑linear • identity‑responsive • environment‑dependent They are the visible geometry of drift.

  1. What a Drift Arc Is A Drift Arc is a curved path created when drift moves through: • pressure gradients • environmental curvature • identity fields • role influence • collapse residues Drift Arcs show: • where drift wants to go • how drift bends • how drift avoids collapse • how drift orbits identity structures They are the motion grammar of the Threshold Map.

  2. Arc Components Every Drift Arc has four components: 2.1 Origin Point Where drift begins bending. Often caused by: • pressure shift • identity shift • environmental boundary • signal influence 2.2 Curvature The shape of the arc: • shallow • tight • spiral • oscillating Curvature is determined by distortion pressure and drift momentum. 2.3 Vector Bias The direction the arc leans: • toward stability • toward collapse • toward motion • toward pattern This bias comes from Resonance Pairs. 2.4 Dissipation Point Where the arc ends or transitions: • into another arc • into a drift corridor • into a collapse zone • into a stable region

  3. Types of Drift Arcs There are five canonical arc types.

3.1 Primary Drift Arc The main, stable arc that defines the world’s flow. Characteristics: • smooth • predictable • low curvature • follows pressure gradients Used for: • Threshold Map outer ring • stable environments • role drift under low pressure

3.2 Shadow Drift Arc The arc drift takes under stress. Characteristics: • sharper curvature • lower stability • closer to collapse zones Used for: • role shadow drift • environmental stress paths • collapse adjacency

3.3 Spiral Drift Arc A tightening arc that approaches collapse. Characteristics: • inward curvature • increasing momentum • collapse‑aligned Used for: • spiral environments • Trickster geometry • collapse contagion

3.4 Oscillating Drift Arc A rhythmic arc that alternates curvature. Characteristics: • wave‑like • patterned • resonance‑aligned Used for: • Listener role • patterned signals • rhythmic drift fields

3.5 Compressive Drift Arc A tight, dense arc created by high density or memory pressure. Characteristics: • short radius • slow movement • identity‑tightening Used for: • deep forests • ruins • Stone/Ethyl interactions

  1. Arc Behavior Under Pressure Drift Arcs respond to pressure in predictable ways: 4.1 Gradient Pressure Increases arc length. Drift travels farther before bending. 4.2 Density Pressure Increases arc compression. Arcs become tighter and slower. 4.3 Distortion Pressure Increases curvature. Arcs bend more sharply. 4.4 Memory Pressure Stabilizes arcs. Arcs become smoother and more predictable. 4.5 Recovery Pressure Straightens arcs. Arcs lose curvature and become linear.

  2. Arc → Identity Interaction Identity interacts with arcs in three ways: 5.1 Alignment Identity follows the arc naturally. 5.2 Resistance Identity pushes against curvature. 5.3 Collapse Identity falls inward along the arc. Roles express these differently: • Hunter follows arcs • Guardian resists arcs • Weaver branches arcs • Scholar straightens arcs • Listener oscillates arcs • Trickster spirals arcs • Anchor halts arcs

  3. Arc → Environment Interaction Environments shape arcs: • spirals → spiral arcs • groves → stabilizing arcs • ruins → compressive arcs • plains → linear arcs • storms → oscillating arcs • deep snow → slow arcs Arcs reveal the pressure fingerprint of an environment.

  4. Arc → Collapse Interaction Collapse interacts with arcs in three ways: 7.1 Collapse Attraction Arcs bend toward collapse zones. 7.2 Collapse Deflection Arcs curve around collapse zones. 7.3 Collapse Spiral Arcs tighten into collapse. Collapse style determines arc behavior.

  5. Arc Networks Drift Arcs form networks: • Drift Corridors (stable paths) • Drift Fields (multi‑arc regions) • Drift Loops (closed arcs) • Collapse Spirals (inward arcs) These networks define the motion architecture of the world.
