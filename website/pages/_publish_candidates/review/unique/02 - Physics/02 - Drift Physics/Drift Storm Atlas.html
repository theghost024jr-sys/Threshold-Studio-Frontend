The comprehensive index of drift storm types, origins, behaviors, propagation patterns, collapse interactions, and environmental signatures across Threshold.

Purpose The Drift Storm Atlas documents the extreme movement events that occur when drift becomes nonlinear, recursive, or collapse‑aligned. It explains: • how drift storms form • where they originate • how they propagate • how they interact with pressure, resonance, lineage, and collapse • how they destabilize worldstate • how they resolve (or fail to) Drift storms are not weather. They are systemic movement failures.

The Five Drift Storm Classes Drift storms fall into five canonical classes, each with distinct mechanics and failure modes.

Class I — Oscillation Storms Drift flips direction rapidly and unpredictably. Origin: Shore, Mist, Field Mechanics: • oscillation corridors overload • resonance destabilizes • boundaries wobble Effects: • identity instability • signal fragmentation • collapse risk spikes Failure Mode: Recursive drift

Class II — Recursive Storms Drift loops back on itself, creating movement recursion. Origin: Mist, Shore, Ruin Mechanics: • recursive corridors expand • signals loop • identity recursion begins Effects: • collapse vector activation • lineage interference • resonance distortion Failure Mode: Collapse corridor formation

Class III — Spiral Storms Drift curves inward or outward in accelerating spirals. Origin: Deep, Forge, Ruin Mechanics: • pressure curvature • resonance bending • collapse alignment Effects: • drift curvature • collapse identity formation • echo storms Failure Mode: Collapse well ignition

Class IV — Collapse Storms Drift is pulled violently toward collapse wells. Origin: Ruin, Deep, Lost Myth–aligned zones Mechanics: • collapse corridors merge • resonance inverts • lineage ruptures Effects: • collapse identity • worldphase fracture • systemic instability Failure Mode: Systemic collapse

Class V — Absence Storms Drift collapses into voids created by the Lost Myth. Origin: Ruin, Deep, absence lines Mechanics: • resonance voids expand • lineage silence spreads • collapse wells deepen Effects: • mythic silence • collapse propagation • substrate rupture Failure Mode: Long‑cycle destabilization

Storm Formation Mechanics Drift storms form through three primary mechanisms:

  1. Pressure Overload • nonlinear pressure • compression spikes • pressure storms Produces: spiral storms

  2. Resonance Inversion • distorted resonance • inversion pockets • resonance voids Produces: recursive or collapse storms

  3. Lineage Rupture • thread severing • mythic silence • substrate fracture Produces: absence storms

Storm Propagation Patterns Drift storms propagate through:

  1. Drift Corridors Storms follow existing movement pathways.
  2. Collapse Corridors Storms accelerate toward collapse wells.
  3. Rupture Lines Storms spread along lineage fractures.
  4. Pressure Fronts Storms ride pressure gradients.

Storm Resolution Mechanics Storms resolve through: • resonance return • pressure equalization • lineage reconnection • boundary stabilization • collapse dissipation Absence storms rarely resolve fully.

Storm Failure Modes Storms destabilize worldstate when: • recursive loops merge • spiral vectors accelerate • collapse wells expand • resonance voids spread • lineage ruptures propagate This produces: • collapse identity • worldphase fracture • long‑cycle instability • mythic silence
