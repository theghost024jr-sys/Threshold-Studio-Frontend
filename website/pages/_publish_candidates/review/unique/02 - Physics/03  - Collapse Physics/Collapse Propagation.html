Definition Collapse Propagation describes how the force released during a role, threshold, lineage, or zone collapse moves outward through the Field, altering nodes, roles, drift states, thresholds, and environmental signatures. Collapse is not an endpoint. Collapse is a redistribution of force. Propagation is how the system absorbs, redirects, and resolves that force.

Purpose To ensure that: • collapse does not create force voids • unresolved load finds a new path • the Field remains coherent • lineage and roles reorganize • drift is redistributed or resolved • thresholds adapt to new conditions • identity stabilizes after rupture Collapse Propagation is the system’s way of maintaining continuity after structural failure.

Sources of Collapse Propagation Propagation begins when any of the following collapse:

  1. Role Collapse A role fails under drift, misalignment, or threshold overload.
  2. Threshold Collapse A boundary snaps due to pressure exceeding resistance.
  3. Lineage Collapse A transmission path breaks, forcing inherited force outward.
  4. Zone Collapse An environmental signature fails to maintain coherence.
  5. Identity Collapse The boundary layer protecting the core pattern ruptures. Each source produces a different propagation signature.

Propagation Phases

  1. Release Phase The collapsed structure releases stored force: • drift • obligations • identity strain • threshold pressure • lineage load This is instantaneous.
  2. Shockwave Phase Force radiates outward through: • relationships • roles • zones • lineage • the Matrix The shockwave reveals hidden structure.
  3. Redistribution Phase Force finds new pathways: • new roles • new thresholds • new lineage branches • new drift vectors • new zone signatures The system reorganizes.
  4. Stabilization Phase The Field settles into a new configuration. Identity reasserts itself. Drift resolves or transforms.

Propagation Vectors Collapse propagates through five primary vectors:

  1. Drift Propagation Unresolved force moves to nearby nodes or roles. Often becomes active drift.
  2. Threshold Propagation Thresholds shift, compress, or appear in new locations.
  3. Role Propagation Roles reorganize, transfer, or reform.
  4. Lineage Propagation Inherited force finds a new transmission path.
  5. Zone Propagation Environmental signatures distort or collapse. Propagation is multi‑vector by default.

Propagation Behaviors

  1. Amplification Force increases as it moves through aligned structures.
  2. Attenuation Force decreases when absorbed by compatible roles or zones.
  3. Reflection Force bounces back when encountering resistance or misalignment.
  4. Refraction Force changes direction when passing through different zone signatures.
  5. Convergence Multiple propagation paths meet and intensify.
  6. Divergence Force splits into multiple pathways. Propagation behaves like physics because it is physics.

Propagation Outcomes

  1. New Thresholds Collapse often creates fresh boundaries that must be crossed.
  2. Role Reformation Roles rebuild themselves in new configurations.
  3. Identity Revelation Collapse exposes the core pattern beneath the role.
  4. Drift Redistribution Unresolved force spreads across the system.
  5. Zone Transformation Environmental signatures shift to accommodate new force.
  6. Lineage Rewiring Transmission paths reorganize.
  7. Shimmer Intensification High‑pressure nodes amplify the propagation.

Failure Modes Propagation can destabilize the system when:

  1. Force Bottlenecks Too much force tries to pass through a narrow role or threshold.
  2. Propagation Loops Force circulates without resolving.
  3. Identity Overload A node receives more force than its boundary layer can handle.
  4. Zone Fracture A zone splits into incompatible signatures.
  5. Lineage Collapse Cascade One collapse triggers another, recursively. These are rare but high‑consequence.

Propagation Resolution Propagation resolves through:

  1. Absorption Roles or zones take on the force.
  2. Transformation Force changes form (drift → threshold, threshold → gift).
  3. Dissipation Cost resolves excess load.
  4. Integration Identity incorporates the new force.
  5. Collapse Cascade Further collapse forces full resolution. All propagation must resolve.

Function Collapse Propagation: • redistributes force after structural failure • prevents force voids • reorganizes roles and thresholds • reveals hidden structure • stabilizes identity • reshapes zones • updates the Matrix • maintains continuity in the Field Without propagation, collapse would destroy coherence. With propagation, collapse becomes transformation.
