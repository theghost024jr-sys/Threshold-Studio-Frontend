Layer Interaction Protocols.md Overview Layer Interaction Protocols define the rules, constraints, and pathways by which Threshold’s layers exchange information, influence each other, and maintain coherence. Each layer has its own physics, but no layer operates in isolation. These protocols ensure that: • signals move cleanly • meaning remains stable • collapse does not propagate uncontrollably • creation does not overwhelm lower layers • identity and environment remain interpretable • substrate and surface layers stay aligned Layer Interaction Protocols are the backbone of Threshold’s vertical and horizontal coherence.

  1. Interaction Principles

  2. Vertical Integrity Layers may influence layers above or below them, but never overwrite them. Influence is gradient‑based, not absolute.

  3. Horizontal Isolation Layers at the same level may exchange signals, but only through resonance channels, not direct mutation.

  4. One‑Way Binding Lower layers may bind to higher layers, but higher layers may not bind downward. This prevents symbolic or narrative contamination of substrate physics.

  5. Drift Allowance All interactions must allow for drift — no interaction is fully deterministic.

  6. Collapse Containment Collapse events must remain within the originating layer unless explicitly escalated.

  7. Creation Permission Creation events require substrate readiness and resonance alignment before propagating upward.

  8. Vertical Interaction Model Vertical interactions follow a five‑step cascade:

  9. Signal Emission A layer emits a signal (emotional, symbolic, narrative, environmental, identity).

  10. Interpretation The receiving layer interprets the signal according to its own physics.

  11. Modulation The receiving layer modulates the signal (attenuation, amplification, translation).

  12. Response The receiving layer generates a response signal.

  13. Integration Both layers update their local state based on the exchange. This cascade prevents direct mutation and ensures interpretive coherence.

  14. Horizontal Interaction Model Layers at the same level interact through resonance channels, not direct state changes. Horizontal Exchange Rules • Only resonant signals may cross horizontally • Emotional signals may drift horizontally • Symbolic signals require alignment • Narrative signals require shared context • Collapse signals are blocked unless thresholds are exceeded • Creation signals require mutual readiness Horizontal interactions are peer‑to‑peer, not hierarchical.

  15. Interaction Types A. Influence Soft modulation of another layer’s state. Examples: • Emotional → Symbolic (viscosity affects condensation) • Symbolic → Narrative (density affects echo formation) B. Binding Stable attachment of meaning across layers. Examples: • Symbolic → Environmental (glyphs shaping atmosphere) • Identity → Species (stance shaping behavior) C. Attunement Resonance alignment between layers. Examples: • Emotional → Identity (stance shifts) • Narrative → Symbolic (mythic alignment) D. Escalation A signal moves upward due to intensity. Examples: • Emotional spike → Collapse • Symbolic overload → Creation event E. Dissipation A signal moves downward to release pressure. Examples: • Narrative residue → Substrate memory • Identity tension → Emotional substrate

  16. Interaction Constraints

  17. No Direct Overwrite A layer cannot directly change another layer’s internal state.

  18. No Forced Alignment Layers may align, but alignment cannot be imposed.

  19. No Infinite Loops Signals must degrade or resolve after a finite number of exchanges.

  20. No Cross‑Physics Mutation Symbolic physics cannot mutate emotional physics, etc.

  21. Collapse Containment Collapse cannot propagate upward without explicit permission.

  22. Creation Throttling Creation cannot propagate downward without substrate readiness.

  23. Interaction Pathways A. Downward Pathways • Narrative → Symbolic • Symbolic → Emotional • Emotional → Substrate Downward pathways condense meaning. B. Upward Pathways • Substrate → Emotional • Emotional → Symbolic • Symbolic → Narrative Upward pathways express meaning. C. Lateral Pathways • Identity ↔ Species • Environment ↔ Narrative • Emotional ↔ Environmental Lateral pathways stabilize meaning.

  24. Interaction Failure Modes

  25. Resonance Dissonance Layers cannot align; signals distort.

  26. Symbolic Overdensity Too much meaning condenses; collapse risk increases.

  27. Emotional Turbulence Viscosity spikes; tension cannot dissipate.

  28. Narrative Fracture Echoes misalign; story‑weather becomes unstable.

  29. Identity Drift Stance becomes unanchored; species behavior destabilizes. Each failure mode has its own containment protocol.

  30. Integration Notes Layer Interaction Protocols govern: • Choice Prompts • Moment Canvases • Collapse Flow • Creation Flow • Substrate Biomes • Identity Geometry • Environmental Physics They ensure that Threshold remains: • coherent • interpretable • stable • alive Even under pressure.
