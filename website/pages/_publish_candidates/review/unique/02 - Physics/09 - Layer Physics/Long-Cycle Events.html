Purpose Long‑Cycle Events describe the slowest, deepest movements in Threshold. They operate across: • multiple Layers • entire environments • species migrations • lineage activation cycles • worldphase transitions • collapse and reformation epochs They are the tectonic plates of the system — the forces that define eras.

The Four Classes of Long‑Cycle Events Long‑Cycle Events fall into four structural classes:

  1. Pressure Epochs Long‑term shifts in global pressure fields. Characteristics: • slow rise or fall in baseline pressure • environment‑wide compression or expansion • species adaptation or destabilization • drift vector reorientation Examples: • The Great Compression • The Low‑Pressure Drift Era • The Branch Stabilization Epoch Outcome: Worldstate tension resets.

  2. Resonance Eras Long‑term alignment or misalignment of environmental resonance. Characteristics: • synchronized resonance across multiple environments • lineage activation waves • collapse risk reduction or amplification • signal clarity or distortion dominance Examples: • The Harmonic Era • The Shimmering Drift • The Deep Resonance Cycle Outcome: Meaning‑physics shifts.

  3. Lineage Cycles Long‑term activation, dormancy, rupture, or reformation of lineage threads. Characteristics: • ancestral resonance waves • mythic substrate reconfiguration • identity inheritance shifts • species‑lineage realignment Examples: • The Lantern Cycle • The Stone Memory Surge • The Lost Thread Emergence Outcome: Mythic substrate reshapes.

  4. Collapse/Reformation Cycles Long‑term oscillations between collapse and rebuilding. Characteristics: • collapse vectors spanning multiple environments • systemic drift instability • worldphase fracture • slow reformation and stabilization Examples: • The Spiral Collapse Era • The Great Rebinding • The Post‑Collapse Drift Cycle Outcome: The world reorganizes itself.

Propagation Mechanics Long‑Cycle Events propagate through the system in three ways:

  1. Layer Propagation Events move through the Layer stack. • Mythic → Identity → Role → Signal → Species → Environment → Worldstate • or the reverse This determines how deep the event cuts.

  2. Spatial Propagation Events move across environments. • drift‑carried • pressure‑driven • resonance‑linked • lineage‑anchored This determines where the event manifests.

  3. Temporal Propagation Events unfold over long arcs. • slow buildup • tipping point • cascade • stabilization • long tail This determines when the event becomes visible.

Event States Long‑Cycle Events move through four predictable states:

  1. Emergence Subtle shifts begin. • pressure anomalies • resonance flickers • lineage echoes • drift curvature changes

  2. Expansion The event becomes dominant. • environmental shifts • species migration • signal distortion or clarity • identity drift patterns

  3. Saturation The event defines the era. • stable pressure epoch • synchronized resonance • lineage activation wave • collapse or reformation dominance

  4. Dissipation The event loses influence. • pressure equalizes • resonance breaks • lineage threads quiet • collapse vectors fade

Interaction With Other Systems Identity Long‑Cycle Events reshape identity architecture and drift patterns. Role Roles intensify or weaken depending on the event’s vector. Signal Signal geometry and distortion patterns shift globally. Species Species adapt, migrate, or destabilize. Environment Environments enter new resonance states. Worldstate Worldphase transitions occur. Mythic Layer Long‑Cycle Events often reveal or conceal myths.
