Definition Vectors are the directional forces that generate motion within the Field. They determine how meaning moves, why it moves, and what direction it takes when acted upon. A vector is not content. A vector is a rule of motion.

Purpose To provide the system with consistent, interpretable pathways for: • activation • transformation • consequence • drift • exchange • crossing Vectors ensure the Field is dynamic rather than static.

Core Vectors

  1. Reciprocity Motion generated by exchange. Every action creates a return path. Nothing moves one‑way.
  2. Lineage Motion generated by inheritance. Meaning travels along established paths of origin and descent.
  3. Drift Motion generated by neglect, imbalance, or unresolved force. Drift always seeks resolution.
  4. Threshold Motion generated by crossing from one state, role, or zone into another. Crossing always transforms.
  5. Gift Motion generated by voluntary transfer of value, attention, or care. Gifts alter both giver and receiver.
  6. Role Motion generated by the activation of a position within the system. Roles shape the direction and type of force applied.
  7. Zone Motion generated by environmental context. Zones influence how vectors behave and how force propagates.

Properties of Vectors Directional Vectors always point somewhere. They define the path of motion. Consistent A vector behaves the same way across the entire Field. No exceptions. Interacting Vectors combine, reinforce, or counteract each other. Complex motion emerges from simple interactions. Consequence‑Producing Every vector produces an outcome. No vector is inert. Conserved Vector‑driven motion cannot disappear. It must resolve, transform, or propagate.

Activation Conditions A vector activates when: • a role is invoked • a gift is given • a threshold is crossed • a drift accumulates • a lineage is referenced • a zone exerts influence • a force is applied Vectors do not activate spontaneously. They require cause.

Function Vectors provide the Field with: • direction • momentum • structure • consequence • interpretive clarity Without vectors, the Field would be static. With vectors, the Field becomes a living system of force.
