The master index of global conditions across Threshold, tracking pressure, resonance, drift, lineage, collapse, and environmental states.

Purpose The Worldstate Index defines the current global condition of Threshold. It is the output of: • Global System Logic • Integration Engine • Long‑Cycle Events • Era Transitions • Mythic Substrate dynamics • Collapse/Reformation cycles The Worldstate Index is the highest‑order state variable in the system.

The Seven Worldstate Axes Worldstate is measured across seven axes — one for each Layer, but expressed at the global scale.

  1. Pressure Axis Tracks global pressure distribution. States include: • low pressure • stable pressure • rising pressure • high pressure • nonlinear pressure • pressure storm

  2. Drift Axis Tracks global drift behavior. States include: • stable drift • oscillating drift • recursive drift • spiral drift • drift storm

  3. Resonance Axis Tracks global harmonic alignment. States include: • clear resonance • mixed resonance • distorted resonance • resonance inversion • resonance silence

  4. Boundary Axis Tracks global boundary integrity. States include: • strong boundaries • porous boundaries • thinning boundaries • inverted boundaries • boundary collapse

  5. Lineage Axis Tracks global thread continuity. States include: • active lineage • dormant lineage • ruptured lineage • lineage storm • mythic silence

  6. Collapse Axis Tracks global collapse activity. States include: • no collapse • localized collapse • collapse vectors active • recursive collapse • systemic collapse

  7. Reformation Axis Tracks global recovery behavior. States include: • stabilization • reorientation • reintegration • incomplete reformation • failed reformation

Worldstate Calculation The Worldstate Index is computed through three steps:

  1. Integration Engine Aggregation Collects all active system inputs: • pressure fields • drift vectors • resonance patterns • boundary states • lineage threads • collapse vectors • environmental conditions

  2. Dominance Logic Resolution Determines which forces override others. Examples: • collapse > boundary • resonance > distortion • pressure > drift • lineage > identity

  3. Worldstate Output Produces the global condition across all seven axes. This output becomes the current Worldstate.

Worldstate Categories The Index rolls up into five high‑level categories:

  1. Stable Worldstate • stable pressure • clear resonance • strong boundaries • active lineage • no collapse • stabilization active

  2. Drift‑Dominant Worldstate • oscillating or recursive drift • porous boundaries • mixed resonance • localized collapse

  3. Distortion‑Dominant Worldstate • nonlinear pressure • distorted resonance • thinning boundaries • ruptured lineage • collapse vectors active

  4. Collapse‑Dominant Worldstate • pressure storm • resonance inversion • inverted boundaries • lineage rupture • recursive collapse

  5. Reformation‑Dominant Worldstate • pressure equalizing • resonance returning • boundaries reforming • lineage reconnecting • collapse resolving

Worldstate Transitions Worldstate shifts occur when: • pressure fields reorganize • resonance patterns realign • lineage threads activate or rupture • collapse vectors propagate • environments deform • species adapt • myths reconfigure These transitions correspond to Era Transitions.

Worldstate and Long‑Cycle Events Long‑Cycle Events define the macro‑scale shape of Worldstate: • Pressure Epochs • Resonance Eras • Lineage Cycles • Collapse/Reformation Cycles The Worldstate Index is the snapshot; Long‑Cycle Events are the trajectory.

Worldstate and Mythic Substrate The Substrate determines: • which axes destabilize • how collapse propagates • how reformation behaves • how lineage reconnects The Lost Myth is the permanent instability in the Worldstate.

Worldstate Failure Modes Worldstate destabilizes when: • collapse becomes recursive • resonance inverts • lineage ruptures • boundaries collapse • drift becomes self‑sustaining • pressure becomes nonlinear This produces: • worldphase fracture • collapse storms • distortion fields • mythic silence • long‑cycle instability
