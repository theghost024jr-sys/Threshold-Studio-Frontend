Where the Field Body is most likely to fracture, distort, or slip Fault lines appear where two or more stability balances fail at the same time: • vector imbalance • pressure imbalance • resonance imbalance • drift imbalance Each fault line is a boundary between incompatible forces. Let’s map them cleanly.

  1. HIGH BRANCH → DEEP SNOWFIELD The Height‑to‑Weight Fault Line Forces in conflict: • upward vector (High Branch) • downward/quiet pressure (Deep Snowfield) Why it fails: • height sharpens awareness • snowfield softens and sinks it • the transition creates vertigo, hesitation, and emotional drop Fault Type: Vector–Pressure Shear Symptoms: • sudden loss of clarity • emotional sinking • balance distortion This is one of the most common collapse points.

  2. PLAINS → SHATTER ZONE The Linearity‑to‑Fracture Fault Line Forces in conflict: • linear vector (Plains) • break vector (Shatter Zone) Why it fails: • linearity wants continuity • fracture wants discontinuity • the transition snaps the vector lattice Fault Type: Vector–Resonance Break Symptoms: • sudden disorientation • narrative rupture • emotional fragmentation This is the system’s sharpest fault.

  3. RECURSIVE HOLLOW → RUINS The Loop‑to‑Fragment Fault Line Forces in conflict: • recursive resonance (Recursive Hollow) • fractured resonance (Ruins) Why it fails: • recursion repeats • ruins break • the transition creates self‑shattering loops Fault Type: Resonance–Drift Amplification Symptoms: • déjà vu turning into emotional collapse • memory loops breaking into fragments • identity echoing and then cracking This is the system’s most psychologically dangerous fault.

  4. BRINICLE SPIRAL → DEEP ICE The Twist‑to‑Freeze Fault Line Forces in conflict: • rotational pressure (Brinicle Spiral) • frozen pressure (Deep Ice) Why it fails: • twist wants motion • freeze wants stillness • the transition locks and fractures simultaneously Fault Type: Pressure–Resonance Lock Symptoms: • sudden emotional freeze • sharp clarity followed by shutdown • pressure spikes This is the system’s coldest fault.

  5. WIND CHANNEL → OLD FOREST The Momentum‑to‑Memory Fault Line Forces in conflict: • forward vector (Wind Channel) • backward/temporal vector (Old Forest) Why it fails: • momentum pushes forward • memory pulls backward • the transition creates temporal drag Fault Type: Vector–Temporal Tension Symptoms: • slowed thought • emotional lag • difficulty choosing direction This is the system’s most subtle fault.

  6. GROVE → SHATTER ZONE The Stillness‑to‑Break Fault Line Forces in conflict: • inward stillness (Grove) • fracture resonance (Shatter Zone) Why it fails: • stillness absorbs • fracture explodes • the transition overwhelms the Grove’s quiet Fault Type: Resonance Overload Symptoms: • quiet turning brittle • emotional overwhelm • sudden collapse This is the system’s most emotionally volatile fault.

  7. STONE BASIN → HIGH BRANCH The Weight‑to‑Height Fault Line Forces in conflict: • downward weight (Stone Basin) • upward vector (High Branch) Why it fails: • weight grounds • height exposes • the transition creates tension vertigo Fault Type: Pressure–Vector Opposition Symptoms: • heaviness turning into fear of falling • emotional tension • instability in decision‑making This is the system’s most physically felt fault.

  8. QUIET GROVE → RECURSIVE HOLLOW The Deep‑Quiet‑to‑Loop Fault Line Forces in conflict: • inward quiet (Quiet Grove) • inward recursion (Recursive Hollow) Why it fails: • quiet deepens • recursion repeats • the transition creates quiet loops Fault Type: Quiet–Recursive Drift Symptoms: • stillness turning into stuckness • introspection turning into looping • emotional softening turning into emotional recursion This is the system’s most deceptively gentle fault.

THE FAULT LINE NETWORK How they connect When you map all fault lines together, they form a ring of instability around the Stability Diamond (Grove, Old Forest, Stone Basin, Deep Snowfield). The pattern is: • Core: stable • Ring: fault lines • Outer membrane: collapse zones This is the Field Body’s tectonic structure.
