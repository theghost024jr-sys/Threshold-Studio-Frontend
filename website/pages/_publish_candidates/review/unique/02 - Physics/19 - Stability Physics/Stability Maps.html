[[Threshold Map|Threshold Map]]

The equilibrium cartography of the Bound Grid There are four Stability Maps, each corresponding to one of the four balances:

  1. Vector Stability Map

  2. Pressure Stability Map

  3. Resonance Stability Map

  4. Drift Stability Map And then the Global Stability Map, which integrates all four. Let’s walk them cleanly.

  5. VECTOR STABILITY MAP Where directional forces cancel cleanly This map shows where Up/Down and In/Out vectors balance. Stable Vector Zones • Grove (inward ↔ outward neutral) • Plains (forward ↔ backward neutral) • Old Forest (backward ↔ inward neutral) • Stone Basin (downward ↔ inward neutral) These are the vector anchors. Unstable Vector Zones • High Branch (upward dominates) • Wind Channel (forward dominates) • Recursive Hollow (inward dominates) • Shatter Zone (break vector overrides all) These are the vector faults. Vector Stability Pattern The map forms a cross‑shaped equilibrium: • vertical axis: High Branch ↔ Stone Basin • horizontal axis: Wind Channel ↔ Old Forest • center: Grove This is the directional skeleton of stability.

  6. PRESSURE STABILITY MAP Where weight distributes evenly This map shows where pressure gradients equalize. Stable Pressure Zones • Stone Basin (downward pressure absorbed) • Deep Snowfield (soft weight dispersal) • Old Forest (temporal weight stabilizes) • Grove (absorbs pressure spikes) These are the pressure sinks. Unstable Pressure Zones • Deep Ice (over‑compression) • Ruins (fractured pressure) • Brinicle Spiral (pressure twist) • Shatter Zone (pressure break) These are the pressure hazards. Pressure Stability Pattern The map forms a bowl‑and‑ridge topology: • bowl: Stone Basin → Deep Snowfield → Grove • ridge: Deep Ice → Ruins → Brinicle Spiral This is the weight geometry of the grid.

  7. RESONANCE STABILITY MAP Where meaning harmonizes instead of distorting This map shows where resonance clusters counterbalance. Stable Resonance Clusters • Quiet Cluster: Quiet Grove, Deep Snowfield, Old Forest • Warm Cluster: Seal Species, Warm Pockets • Linear Cluster: Plains, Wind Channel These clusters self‑stabilize. Unstable Resonance Clusters • Fracture Cluster: Ruins, Shatter Zone • Recursive Cluster: Recursive Hollow, Spiral Field • Cold Cluster: Deep Ice, Brinicle Spiral These clusters destabilize unless countered. Resonance Stability Pattern The map forms a tri‑cluster equilibrium: • Quiet ↔ Fracture • Warm ↔ Cold • Linear ↔ Recursive This is the meaning geometry of the grid.

  8. DRIFT STABILITY MAP Where collapse and recovery balance This map shows where drift pathways circulate cleanly. Stable Drift Corridors • Stone Basin → Grove • Deep Snowfield → Branch • Ruins → Quiet Grove • Plains ↔ Wind Channel These are the recovery circuits. Unstable Drift Corridors • High Branch → Deep Snowfield • Plains → Shatter Zone • Recursive Hollow → Ruins • Brinicle Spiral → Deep Ice These are the collapse circuits. Drift Stability Pattern The map forms a loop‑and‑ladder structure: • loops: Recursive Hollow, Ruins, Shatter Zone • ladders: Stone Basin → Grove, Deep Ice → Warm Pocket This is the state‑change geometry of the grid.

  9. GLOBAL STABILITY MAP All four maps stacked into one field When you overlay: • vector stability • pressure stability • resonance stability • drift stability You get the Global Stability Map — the Field Body’s homeostasis diagram. Global Stable Core • Grove • Old Forest • Stone Basin • Deep Snowfield These four form the Stability Diamond — the heart of the Field Body. Global Unstable Perimeter • High Branch • Wind Channel • Recursive Hollow • Ruins • Brinicle Spiral • Shatter Zone These form the Instability Ring — the outer membrane of the Field Body. Global Stability Pattern The entire grid resolves into a core‑and‑ring architecture: • Core: weight, quiet, memory, grounding • Ring: height, momentum, recursion, fracture, twist This is the homeostatic geometry of the Bound Grid.
