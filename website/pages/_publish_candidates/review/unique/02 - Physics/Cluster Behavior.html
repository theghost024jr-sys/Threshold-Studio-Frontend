[[Invariant00 - Cohesion Under Pressure]]

Cluster Behavior

Invariant Anchor This module expresses how clusters behave when cohesion is under pressure. See: [[Invariant00 – Cohesion Under Pressure]]

Definition

A cluster is a temporary or persistent aggregation of nodes whose cohesion is higher between each other than with the surrounding field. Clusters form, drift, stabilize, collapse, or merge based on pressure distribution.

Clusters are not entities — they are pressure‑shaped structures.

Core Behavioral Laws

1. Clusters redistribute pressure internally

When pressure increases:

  • cohesion flows toward aligned nodes

  • contradictions are pushed outward

  • unstable nodes drift to the perimeter

  • stable nodes condense toward the core

A cluster is a pressure‑sorting machine.

2. Clusters collapse in chains, not all at once

Cluster collapse follows a predictable sequence:

  • outer contradictions fail first

  • mid‑layer nodes destabilize

  • core nodes collapse last

This is why clusters have collapse signatures, not collapse events.

3. Clusters drift as a single body

Clusters move through the Garden according to:

  • external pressure gradients

  • internal cohesion distribution

  • resonance with nearby clusters

  • Flower‑scale currents

A drifting cluster behaves like a cohesion‑weighted organism.

4. Clusters merge when their cohesion signatures align

Two clusters will merge if:

  • their internal pressure patterns resonate

  • their contradictions are compatible

  • their collapse thresholds overlap

Merging is not additive — it produces a new identity basin.

5. Clusters repel when their contradictions amplify

Repulsion occurs when:

  • pressure pushes contradictions inward

  • resonance patterns interfere

  • collapse thresholds diverge

Repulsion is a stability‑preserving behavior.

6. Clusters stabilize Flowers

Clusters are the structural scaffolding of Flowers.

They:

  • anchor identity

  • regulate collapse

  • distribute pressure

  • maintain Flower‑scale coherence

A Flower without clusters is just a cloud of nodes.

Behavior Under Each Origin Phase

Distortion

Clusters wobble; cohesion becomes uneven.

Drift

Clusters slide relative to each other; boundaries soften.

Field

Clusters expand; cohesion diffuses.

Collapse

Clusters fail in chains; contradictions eject.

Identity

Clusters reform around new stable cores.

Role

Clusters align to directional cohesion.

Signal

Clusters propagate resonance outward.

Cluster Types (Structural, not lore)

  • Core Clusters — high cohesion, low drift

  • Boundary Clusters — high contradiction, high drift

  • Transitional Clusters — mediate pressure between Flowers

  • Collapse Clusters — unstable, high‑pressure, short‑lived

  • Resonant Clusters — synchronize Flowers

These types emerge from pressure, not classification.

Summary Rule

A cluster is a cohesion‑seeking structure that:

  • sorts pressure

  • stabilizes identity

  • collapses in chains

  • drifts as a body

  • merges when aligned

  • repels when contradictory

Clusters are the Garden’s structural metabolism.
