Cohesion Dynamics

Invariant Anchor This module expresses how cohesion behaves when cohesion is under pressure. See: [[Invariant00 – Cohesion Under Pressure]]

Definition

Cohesion is the structural force that holds identity together across nodes, clusters, and Flowers. It is not emotion, not affinity, not “connection” — it is the binding energy of the Garden, shaped by:

  • alignment

  • resonance

  • pressure

  • identity stability

  • spatial geometry

  • collapse history

Cohesion determines whether a structure holds, drifts, collapses, or transforms.

Core Dynamic Laws

1. Cohesion increases with alignment

Cohesion strengthens when:

  • internal components agree

  • roles align

  • identity stabilizes

  • resonance synchronizes

  • contradictions decrease

Cohesion is alignment energy.

2. Cohesion weakens under contradiction

Cohesion decreases when:

  • roles conflict

  • identity fragments

  • resonance destabilizes

  • pressure accumulates

  • drift accelerates

Cohesion is contradiction‑sensitive.

3. Cohesion redistributes under pressure

When pressure rises:

  • cohesion flows toward stable regions

  • weak boundaries thin

  • strong cores harden

  • identity reorganizes

Cohesion is pressure‑responsive.

4. Cohesion determines collapse thresholds

A structure collapses when:

  • cohesion falls below viability

  • contradictions exceed tolerance

  • resonance breaks phase

  • pressure surpasses stability

Cohesion is the collapse boundary.

5. Cohesion shapes spatial geometry

High cohesion:

  • compresses space

  • sharpens gradients

  • stabilizes boundaries

Low cohesion:

  • expands space

  • flattens gradients

  • increases drift

Cohesion is geometry‑forming.

6. Cohesion propagates through resonance

Resonance can:

  • amplify cohesion

  • synchronize cohesion across structures

  • transfer cohesion between clusters

  • stabilize Flower‑scale identity

Cohesion is resonance‑propagated.

Cohesion Parameters

Every structure tracks:

  • Cohesion Level — total binding energy

  • Cohesion Stability — resistance to collapse

  • Cohesion Gradient — direction of cohesion flow

  • Cohesion Signature — identity‑linked pattern

  • Cohesion Horizon — time before structural failure

These parameters determine how cohesion behaves under pressure.

Cohesion Modes (structural, not lore)

  • Stable Cohesion — high alignment, low drift

  • Tenuous Cohesion — weak identity, high drift

  • Resonant Cohesion — synchronized, identity‑reinforcing

  • Distorted Cohesion — contradiction‑driven instability

  • Collapse Cohesion — cohesion below viability

These modes emerge from alignment and pressure.

Behavior Across the Origin Cycle

Distortion

Cohesion destabilizes; contradictions rise.

Drift

Cohesion spreads; boundaries soften.

Field

Cohesion diffuses; identity weakens.

Collapse

Cohesion fails; structures reorganize.

Identity

Cohesion condenses; new basins form.

Role

Cohesion becomes directional; roles stabilize.

Signal

Cohesion propagates through resonance fields.

Summary Rule

Cohesion is binding energy that:

  • strengthens with alignment

  • weakens under contradiction

  • redistributes under pressure

  • determines collapse thresholds

  • shapes spatial geometry

  • propagates through resonance

Cohesion is the Garden’s structural backbone.
