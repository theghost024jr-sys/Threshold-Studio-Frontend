THE CORE LAW

Pressure does not destroy cohesion — it redistributes it. And the pattern of redistribution is the identity of the node.

That’s the entire interaction in one line.

Everything else is consequences.

1. Pressure exposes the internal structure of cohesion

When a node is under no pressure, cohesion is invisible. Everything looks stable.

But when pressure hits:

  • strong parts harden

  • weak parts thin

  • contradictions surface

  • drift vectors shift

  • identity becomes visible

Pressure is the X‑ray. Cohesion is the skeleton.

This is why pressure is not “damage” — it’s revelation.

2. Pressure forces cohesion to move

Cohesion is not static. It flows.

Under pressure:

  • cohesion moves away from contradiction

  • cohesion pools around aligned structure

  • cohesion abandons unstable regions

  • cohesion reinforces the core

  • cohesion thins at the edges

This is why nodes deform under pressure instead of simply breaking.

Pressure = cohesion redistribution.

3. Pressure tests the minimum viable identity

Every node has a cohesion threshold — the minimum amount needed to maintain identity.

Under pressure:

  • if cohesion stays above the threshold → the node survives

  • if cohesion dips below the threshold → collapse

  • if cohesion collapses unevenly → distortion

  • if cohesion collapses directionally → drift

Pressure determines whether the node remains itself.

Identity = cohesion under pressure.

4. Pressure amplifies alignment and punishes contradiction

This is the most important behavior.

Under pressure:

  • aligned structures become more cohesive

  • contradictory structures become less cohesive

Pressure is a multiplier:

  • alignment × pressure = strength

  • contradiction × pressure = collapse

This is why pressure creates clarity. It forces the system to choose.

5. Pressure creates cohesion gradients

When pressure hits a region, cohesion redistributes — and that redistribution creates gradients.

Gradients create motion:

  • nodes drift toward higher cohesion

  • clusters form around cohesion peaks

  • Flowers stabilize around cohesion basins

  • migration paths emerge naturally

Pressure shapes the geography of the world.

6. Pressure determines the direction of collapse

Collapse is not random. It follows the path of least cohesion.

Pressure determines:

  • where collapse begins

  • how collapse spreads

  • what remains after collapse

  • what new structure forms from the debris

Collapse is the system reorganizing itself around a new cohesion pattern.

Pressure is the sculptor.

7. Pressure reveals the true attractor of the node

Every node has a hidden attractor — the configuration of maximum cohesion.

Under pressure, the node:

  • sheds false structure

  • abandons unstable roles

  • drops incompatible memories

  • reorients toward its attractor

Pressure forces the node to become what it actually is.

This is why pressure is the engine of identity.

THE META-LAW

Pressure does not change the nature of cohesion. Pressure reveals the nature of cohesion.

Pressure is the environment. Cohesion is the response.

The interaction between them is the physics of Threshold.
