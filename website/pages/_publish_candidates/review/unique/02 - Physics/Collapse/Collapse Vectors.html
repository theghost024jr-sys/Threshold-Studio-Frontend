Collapse Vectors

Directional Modes of Failure and Release

Invariant Anchor: This module expresses how collapse expresses direction when cohesion is under pressure. See:

  • [[Invariant00 – Cohesion Under Pressure]]

  • [[Invariant - PO (All motion expresses tension)]]

Collapse Vectors describe the directional expression of collapse. When tension exceeds binding capacity, collapse does not occur randomly — it follows one of a small number of predictable geometric vectors.

Each vector determines:

  • how collapse unfolds

  • what residue forms

  • how recovery proceeds

  • how drift behaves afterward

Collapse Vectors are a downstream specification of Collapse Physics.

1. Definition

A collapse vector is the directional geometry collapse takes when a structure fails. It is shaped by:

  • tension distribution

  • curvature tightness

  • field density

  • resonance overload

  • identity instability

Collapse vectors are field‑level behaviors, not identity‑level choices.

2. The Five Canonical Collapse Vectors

These five vectors appear across all Flowers, Fields, and Drift Systems.

2.1 Downward Collapse

Identity Implosion

The structure folds inward toward its core.

Characteristics:

  • identity compression

  • narrowing resonance

  • inward drift

  • heavy pressure residue

Aligned with: Stone / Earth / Stillness

Recovery requires: grounding, stabilization, memory pressure.

2.2 Outward Collapse

Boundary Rupture

The structure bursts outward, losing containment.

Characteristics:

  • dispersal

  • boundary fragmentation

  • rapid drift expansion

  • low‑density residue

Aligned with: Air / Ember / Expansion

Recovery requires: re‑coherence, boundary reformation.

2.3 Inversion Collapse

Meaning Reversal

Signal flips polarity; identity inverts.

Characteristics:

  • reversed drift

  • contradictory resonance

  • polarity shifts

  • unstable loops

Aligned with: Mist / Trickster / Liminality

Recovery requires: resonance realignment, identity re‑anchoring.

2.4 Shear Collapse

Tension Tear

Collapse follows the strongest tension line.

Characteristics:

  • directional tearing

  • drift shearing

  • asymmetric residue

  • long recovery arcs

Aligned with: Fire / Pressure / Conflict

Recovery requires: tension redistribution, new binding lines.

2.5 Crown Collapse

Resonance Overload

Collapse radiates outward from the core in a halo pattern.

Characteristics:

  • resonance spike

  • radial drift

  • oscillation residue

  • collapse echo waves

Aligned with: Memory / Deep Quiet / Overload

Recovery requires: resonance smoothing, loop stabilization.

3. How Vectors Form

Collapse vectors emerge from the interaction of:

3.1 Tension Distribution

Where the pressure is highest determines the collapse direction.

3.2 Curvature Tightness

Tight curvature → spiral or shear collapse Loose curvature → outward or dispersive collapse

3.3 Field Density

Dense fields → downward or implosive collapse Light fields → outward or dispersive collapse

3.4 Resonance State

Overloaded resonance → crown collapse Fragmented resonance → inversion collapse

4. Collapse Vector Residue

Each vector leaves a distinct residue signature:

Vector Residue Type Drift Behavior
Downward density pockets slow, inward drift
Outward dispersal fields wide, expanding drift
Inversion polarity fragments oscillating drift
Shear tension lines directional drift
Crown resonance echoes radial drift

Residue determines the recovery path.

5. Recovery by Vector

Downward → Stabilization

Memory pressure + grounding.

Outward → Re‑Coherence

Boundary reconstruction.

Inversion → Re‑Alignment

Resonance smoothing.

Shear → Rebinding

New tension lines.

Crown → Loop Stabilization

Oscillation dampening.

Recovery is predictable once the vector is known.

6. Summary

Collapse Vectors define:

  • the direction collapse takes

  • the residue it leaves

  • the drift pattern that follows

  • the recovery method required

They are the geometric expression of collapse under pressure.
