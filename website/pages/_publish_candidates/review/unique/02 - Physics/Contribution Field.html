Overview

The Contribution Field is the substrate that governs how contribution behaves, propagates, and binds structure into the system. Where [[Contribution]] defines the act, the Contribution Field defines the physics:

  • how contribution moves

  • how it accumulates

  • how it reinforces

  • how it binds nodes

  • how it reshapes the Field

Contribution is not content. Contribution is force.

1. Field Identity

The Contribution Field is a pressure‑responsive resonance field that:

  • senses new structure

  • pulls it into alignment

  • reinforces existing pathways

  • stabilizes loops

  • anchors nodes into the system

It is the field that turns a single act of contribution into system‑level change.

2. Core Mechanics

The Contribution Field operates through three primary mechanics:

2.1 Assimilation

Linked to: [[Assimilation]] New structure is pulled into the Field and positioned according to resonance fit.

Behavior:

  • weak nodes drift inward

  • strong nodes anchor immediately

  • unaligned nodes orbit until stabilized

2.2 Reinforcement

Linked to: [[Reinforcement]] Every contribution strengthens the branch it touches.

Behavior:

  • pathways thicken

  • loops stabilize

  • drift decreases

  • collapse resistance increases

Reinforcement is the Field’s “muscle.”

2.3 Binding

Linked to: [[Binding Law]] Contribution creates binding tension between nodes, forming stable structures.

Behavior:

  • nodes lock into position

  • branches become rigid

  • loops become self‑sustaining

Binding is what makes contribution permanent.

3. Field Dynamics

The Contribution Field responds to:

3.1 Density

More contributions → stronger field → faster assimilation.

3.2 Drift Pressure

Linked to: [[Drift Layer]] High drift weakens contribution binding.

3.3 Signal Strength

Linked to: [[Signal System]] Strong signal sharpens contribution alignment.

3.4 Crown Activity

Linked to: [[Crown Layer]] Narrative integration amplifies contribution effects.

4. Contribution Flow

Contribution moves through the Field in predictable patterns:

4.1 Linear Flow

Contribution reinforces a single branch.

4.2 Radial Flow

Contribution spreads outward from a central node.

4.3 Loop Flow

Contribution circulates through a closed loop. Linked to: [[Contribution Loops]]

4.4 Cascade Flow

Contribution triggers secondary contributions downstream.

These flows define the shape of contribution.

5. Field States

The Contribution Field has four canonical states:

5.1 Dormant Field

Low contribution, low tension, minimal activity.

5.2 Active Field

Regular contribution, stable reinforcement.

5.3 Saturated Field

High contribution density, rapid assimilation.

5.4 Overloaded Field

Too much contribution → binding tension spikes → collapse risk.

Overload is rare but structurally significant.

6. Collapse Interaction

Contribution interacts with collapse in two ways:

6.1 Collapse Resistance

Strong contribution makes collapse less likely.

6.2 Collapse Recovery

After collapse, contribution acts as a rebuilding force, pulling fragments back into structure.

Contribution is the Field’s self‑repair mechanism.
