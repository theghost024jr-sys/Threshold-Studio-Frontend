Purpose A Field is a continuous region of influence that shapes drift, identity behavior, and collapse patterns. Fields are not objects or locations — they are spatial substrates that exert pressure, curvature, and motion bias. Where environments describe place, and drift describes motion, fields describe the invisible forces that shape motion and identity across space. Fields are the geometry of influence in Threshold.

  1. What a Field Is A Field is defined by three core properties: 1.1 Influence The ability to shape drift, identity, and collapse. 1.2 Continuity Fields are continuous — they have no hard edges unless shaped by another field. 1.3 Pressure Fields exert pressure that determines: • drift direction • drift curvature • identity stability • collapse likelihood Fields are invisible architectures that determine how the world moves.

  2. Field Components Every Field contains three structural components: 2.1 Field Core The stable center of the field. Defines the field’s identity and baseline behavior. 2.2 Field Gradient Directional pressure that shapes drift and motion. 2.3 Field Boundary The outer region where the field interacts with other fields or environments. Boundaries are soft, not rigid.

  3. Field Types There are four canonical Field types in Threshold.

3.1 Drift Fields Fields that shape the motion of drift. • define drift arcs • create drift corridors • influence role movement • shape environmental flow

3.2 Pressure Fields Fields that exert force on identity and drift. • density • gradient • distortion • memory Defined in: [[Environmental Pressure Field]]

3.3 Collapse Fields Fields aligned with collapse physics. • spiral collapse fields • brittle collapse fields • recursive collapse fields • dispersive collapse fields Collapse Fields override normal field behavior.

3.4 Recovery Fields Fields aligned with re‑coherence. • Stillwatch fields • Grove Pulse fields • Deep Quiet fields • Ember Rest fields Recovery Fields straighten drift and stabilize identity.

  1. Field Behavior Fields behave according to Field Dynamics, but the core behaviors are: 4.1 Curvature Fields bend under pressure. 4.2 Expansion Fields grow when pressure decreases or identity stabilizes. 4.3 Contraction Fields shrink when pressure increases or collapse activates. 4.4 Distortion Fields warp under Mist‑aligned influence. 4.5 Stabilization Fields smooth under memory or recovery pressure. Fields are living geometries.

  2. Field Interaction Fields interact using the same patterns as roles and signals, but expressed spatially. Alignment Fields reinforce each other’s gradient. Tension Fields pull drift in different directions. Overlap Fields blend and share territory. Opposition Fields push against each other. Resonance Fields synchronize curvature. Interference Fields distort each other’s shape. Field interaction determines the shape of the world’s motion.

  3. Field Influence on Systems Fields influence every major subsystem: 6.1 Drift Fields determine: • drift direction • drift curvature • drift velocity • drift compression 6.2 Identity Fields stabilize or destabilize identity. 6.3 Collapse Fields determine collapse style and spread. 6.4 Signals Signals express differently depending on field substrate. 6.5 Environments Environments are shaped by the fields they sit within.

  4. Field Networks Fields form networks that define the world’s architecture: • Drift Fields (multi‑arc regions) • Drift Corridors (stable paths) • Field Loops (closed fields) • Collapse Fields (collapse‑aligned regions) • Recovery Fields (stabilizing regions) These networks are the motion skeleton of Threshold.
