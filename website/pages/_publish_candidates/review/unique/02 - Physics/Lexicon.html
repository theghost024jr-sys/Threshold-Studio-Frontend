Lexicon.md Canonical Vocabulary of Threshold

  1. Purpose The Lexicon defines the official vocabulary of Threshold. It ensures that every architectural term has: • one meaning • one function • one place in the system The Lexicon prevents conceptual drift and keeps the architecture coherent as it grows. This is not lore. This is not flavor. This is system language.

  2. Core Terms

Archetype A deep symbolic pattern that shapes roles, environments, and artifacts. Archetypes are structural mythic forms, not characters.

Artifact A dense symbolic object that carries emotional weight, resonance, and meaning. Artifacts are objects of memory and symbolic gravity.

Binding The mechanism by which elements adhere, connect, or fuse across layers. Binding is structural adhesion, not narrative attachment.

Breath The slow oscillation of presence, light, and emotional tone across the system. Breath is the life force of Threshold.

Collapse The convergence of states into a single outcome or stabilized form. Collapse is resolution physics, not destruction.

Density The emotional and symbolic weight of an element. Density determines motion speed, transition timing, and cognitive gravity.

Drift The slow movement of meaning, resonance, or state across the system over time. Drift is structural motion, not animation.

Environment A spatial and emotional room that holds temperature, breath, and symbolic context. Environments are places, not screens.

Environment Architecture The structural rules governing how environments behave, breathe, and hold meaning. Defines room physics.

Field A spatial or conceptual zone that influences behavior, resonance, or placement. Fields are invisible influence maps.

Forces The deep currents that move through the system — gravity, resonance, drift, breath, density. Forces are the physics engine of meaning.

Gravity of Center The pull toward orientation, grounding, and stillness. The Hub is the center of gravity.

Hub The central orientation surface of Threshold. A still point where all navigation begins and returns.

Interface Architecture The spatial, visual, and atmospheric expression of the system’s deeper physics. Defines surfaces, transitions, color fields, and layout.

Interaction Architecture The behavioral logic of how the user and system touch each other. Defines movement, contact, pacing, and return.

Meta‑Physics The deepest laws of the system — emotional physics, containment, orientation, resonance, density. Meta‑Physics is the substrate of meaning.

Mythic The layer of symbolic resonance, archetypal structure, and deep narrative physics. Mythic is not story — it is symbolic architecture.

Node A light, resonant point of connection that links elements and transmits meaning. Nodes are the nervous system of Threshold.

Node Architecture The structural rules governing nodes — drift, resonance, connection, activation. Defines wiring physics.

Resonance The propagation of emotional charge when an element is touched or activated. Resonance is meaning in motion.

Return The one‑step movement back to the Hub. Return is a safety law, not a navigation feature.

Role A functional and symbolic identity within the system. Roles are positions in the architecture, not characters.

Soil The substrate layer from which environments, artifacts, and symbolic structures grow. Soil is the ground of the system.

Symbolic Density The concentration of meaning within an element. Artifacts have the highest symbolic density.

System The totality of Threshold’s architecture, physics, surfaces, and symbolic substrate. The system is the organism.

System Architecture The master structural logic of Threshold. Defines layers, relationships, and governing principles.

Temperature The emotional climate of an element — warm, cool, neutral, shifting. Temperature shapes tone, pacing, and resonance.

Temporal The layer governing time, state progression, and rhythm. Temporal is system time, not narrative time.

Variant A branching form or alternate structural expression of an element. Variants are structural possibilities, not alternate timelines.

World The macro‑scale spatial logic of Threshold — the totality of environments. The world is the container of rooms.

World State The dynamic condition of the world at a given moment. World State is global emotional and structural configuration.

  1. Cross‑Layer Terms

Containment The system’s responsibility to hold, soften, and ground the user. Containment is a meta‑physical requirement.

Orientation The system’s ability to show where center is and how to return. Orientation is gravitational logic.

Presence The sense that the system is alive, aware, and breathing. Presence is ambient responsiveness.

Quiet The absence of demand, noise, or cognitive spikes. Quiet is a design law, not an aesthetic.

Transition A soft, density‑based movement between states or surfaces. Transitions are emotional physics, not animations.

  1. Purpose of the Lexicon The Lexicon ensures: • clarity • consistency • architectural integrity • semantic stability • long‑term coherence It is the dictionary of the system.
