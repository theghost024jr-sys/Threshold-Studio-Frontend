[[Invariant00 - Cohesion Under Pressure]]

Role Ecology

Invariant Anchor This module expresses how roles behave when cohesion is under pressure. See: [[Invariant00 – Cohesion Under Pressure]]

Definition

A role is a stable pattern of directional cohesion within a Flower. Roles are not jobs, archetypes, or personalities — they are pressure‑aligned functions that emerge when identity becomes directional.

Roles regulate collapse, stabilize clusters, and propagate signal.

Roles are the Flower’s functional anatomy.

Core Behavioral Laws

1. Roles emerge when cohesion becomes directional

A role forms when:

  • identity condenses

  • cohesion aligns along a stable axis

  • pressure gradients become predictable

  • resonance patterns synchronize

Roles are the shape of stabilized identity.

2. Roles regulate collapse thresholds

Roles increase Flower stability by:

  • absorbing pressure

  • redistributing contradictions

  • reinforcing cluster boundaries

  • maintaining identity coherence

A Flower with strong roles collapses later and reforms faster.

3. Roles coordinate cluster behavior

Roles influence clusters by:

  • guiding drift

  • stabilizing boundaries

  • amplifying resonance

  • suppressing contradictions

Roles are the Flower’s internal governance.

4. Roles propagate signal

Roles emit structured signals that:

  • synchronize Flowers

  • guide migration

  • reinforce identity

  • trigger activation logic

Signal is the Flower’s communication metabolism.

5. Roles compete and cooperate under pressure

When pressure increases:

  • aligned roles reinforce each other

  • contradictory roles destabilize

  • dominant roles absorb pressure

  • weak roles collapse

Role ecology is pressure‑driven functional selection.

Role Types (structural, not lore)

  • Core Roles — stabilize identity; low drift

  • Boundary Roles — regulate contradictions; high pressure

  • Transitional Roles — mediate cluster interactions

  • Resonant Roles — amplify Flower‑scale signal

  • Collapse Roles — emerge during failure; short‑lived

These types emerge from cohesion patterns, not classification.

Behavior Across the Origin Cycle

Distortion

Roles blur; directional cohesion weakens.

Drift

Roles shift; functional boundaries soften.

Field

Roles diffuse; identity becomes non‑directional.

Collapse

Roles fail; contradictions spike; signal fragments.

Identity

New roles condense around stable axes.

Role

Directional cohesion strengthens; Flower stabilizes.

Signal

Roles broadcast identity across the Garden.

Summary Rule

A role is a directional cohesion function that:

  • emerges from stabilized identity

  • regulates collapse

  • coordinates clusters

  • propagates signal

  • competes and cooperates under pressure

Roles are the Flower’s functional ecology.
