Particle Behavior Map

Purpose

This map describes how particles (small, mobile units of presence, memory, or tension) behave inside the world/system. It’s a way to track where things are trying to go, even before they fully arrive or manifest.

Particle types

  • Anchor Particles:
    Tend toward dense regions, major nodes, or stable structures. They reinforce existing patterns.

  • Drift Particles:
    Move toward the frontier, edges, and low‑density regions. They seek the unknown, the unformed, the unresolved.

  • Bridge Particles:
    Travel along connections between clusters. They carry influence, rumor, memory, or tension from one region to another.

  • Void Particles:
    Hover in empty pockets. They mark places where something could exist but doesn’t yet.

Behavioral rules

  • Attraction to density:
    Particles are more likely to move toward clusters with many links or strong coherence.

  • Repulsion from saturation:
    When a region becomes overfull, particles begin to drift outward, searching for new space.

  • Path‑following:
    Bridge particles prefer existing connections (links, routes, relationships) rather than jumping randomly.

  • Frontier seeking:
    Drift particles accumulate at the edges of the known world, forming halos or soft boundaries.

  • Memory pooling:
    Void particles can accumulate into a “potential” that later condenses into a new node, event, or myth.

Uses in the world

  • World‑state diagnostics:
    Where particles cluster tells you where attention, tension, or memory is gathering.

  • Story seeds:
    High particle activity in a quiet region suggests something is about to happen there.

  • Cycle tracking:
    Particle movement can show where you are in a cycle (build‑up, drift, collapse, renewal).

  • Threshold mapping:
    Particles at boundaries indicate active thresholds—places where crossing or change is likely.

Design questions

  • What kind of particles dominate this region (anchor, drift, bridge, void)?
  • Where are particles trying to go, and what’s stopping them?
  • What happens when particle flow is blocked or forced?
  • Which cycles are visible in particle movement right now?
  • What new node, event, or myth is implied by current particle behavior?
