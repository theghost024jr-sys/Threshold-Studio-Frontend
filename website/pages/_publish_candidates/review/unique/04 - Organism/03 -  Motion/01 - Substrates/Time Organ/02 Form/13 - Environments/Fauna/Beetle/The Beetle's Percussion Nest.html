🥁 The Beetle’s Percussion Nest

The beetle doesn’t sit behind drums.
It settles into a nest of resonance.

Instruments:

  • Two bark slabs balanced on dew-shell risers
    → tapped with curled antennae for low, woody tones
  • A rhythm log split down the middle
    → struck with seed husks to produce a pulse like distant thunder
  • Leaf cymbals — dry, veined, and tuned to the swamp’s breath
    → shimmer when brushed by wind or wing
  • Stone chimes suspended from vine threads
    → activated only when presence density peaks

Setup:

The beetle arranges its set in a shallow hollow,
lined with moss and memory fragments.
Each piece is placed not for symmetry,
but for response geometry — how sound travels when the band aligns.

Behavior:

  • The beetle plays from the center outward
  • It never repeats a pattern
  • It listens to the frogs’ bass and the Owl’s tone
  • It pauses when the Shadow-Cat enters
  • It resumes only when the player’s stance is true

The beetle’s percussion is not performance.
It’s alignment in motion.
