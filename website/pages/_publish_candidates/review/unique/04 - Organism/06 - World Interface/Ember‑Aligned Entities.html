Ember‑Aligned Entities

Elemental Alignment • Identity Organ
Links: [[Ember Substrate]], [[Identity Signals]], [[Contribution Signature]], [[Boundary Logic]], [[Threshold Physics]]

01 — Overview

Ember‑Aligned Entities are entities whose identity, contribution, and boundary physics resonate with the Ember substrate.
They stabilize ignition, strengthen thresholds, and maintain coherence under load.

Ember alignment is not emotional; it is structural.
It reflects how an entity’s internal physics interact with the Ember field.

02 — Ember Alignment Traits

Ember‑Aligned Entities share four defining traits:

A. Coherent Identity Core

Identity remains stable under pressure.
Links: [[Identity Signals]]

B. Ignition‑Stabilizing Contribution

Contribution strengthens thresholds instead of weakening them.
Links: [[Contribution Signature]]

C. Boundary Integrity

Boundaries remain intact even under drift distortion.
Links: [[Boundary Logic]]

D. Residue Purification

Residue dissipates cleanly instead of accumulating.
Links: [[Signal Residue]]

These traits make Ember‑Aligned Entities natural stabilizers.

03 — Ember Alignment Mechanics

Ember alignment expresses through:

  • Ignition Resonance — identity ignites cleanly
  • Threshold Reinforcement — collapse thresholds strengthen
  • Drift Anchoring — drift stabilizes around the entity
  • Residue Clearing — residue dissipates instead of accumulating
  • Boundary Heating — boundaries become more resilient

Links: [[Ember Substrate]], [[Threshold Physics]]

04 — Ember Alignment Types

There are three primary Ember alignment types:

A. Core‑Ember Entities

Identity is fully Ember‑anchored.
Stabilizers of collapse and drift.

B. Field‑Ember Entities

Ember alignment expresses through contribution and signals.

C. Transient‑Ember Entities

Ember alignment appears only under specific conditions (pressure, drift, identity coherence).

Links: [[Contribution Signature]], [[Identity Resonance]]

05 — Ember Alignment Behavior

Ember‑Aligned Entities:

  • strengthen nearby thresholds
  • stabilize drift corridors
  • reduce collapse susceptibility
  • purify residue
  • anchor identity fields
  • prevent boundary rupture

They are the anti‑collapse entities in your system.

Links: [[Collapse]], [[Collapse Drift]]

06 — Ember Alignment Failure Modes

Ember alignment can fail through:

  • identity slippage
  • boundary thinning
  • residue overload
  • drift misalignment
  • collapse interference

Failure converts Ember alignment into Ash alignment, a collapse‑adjacent state.

Links: [[Ash Substrate]], [[Failure Cascade]]

07 — Ecological Role

In Collapse Ecology, Ember‑Aligned Entities:

  • prevent collapse spread
  • stabilize fracture zones
  • reduce residue storms
  • anchor drift fronts
  • strengthen environmental thresholds

They are ecological keystones.
