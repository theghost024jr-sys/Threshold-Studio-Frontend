Purpose Treeform Species represent beings whose identity is shaped by vertical memory, rooted attention, and branching cognition. They embody stability that can still drift, growth that can still fracture, and presence that can still arc toward transformation. They are the living architecture of the environments they inhabit. Treeforms are not “tree-like creatures.” They are species whose identity behaves like a tree. Core Identity • Layer: Physical–Mythic hybrid • Signal Type: Structural Resonance • Elemental Overlay: Earth–Air (rooted stillness + branching reach) • Archetypal Role: The Anchor That Learns Primary Traits • Slow but decisive movement • Deep environmental attunement • Memory stored in rings, knots, or internal grain patterns • Branching thought patterns: multiple simultaneous lines of reasoning • High resistance to collapse but vulnerable to drift when uprooted Variants

  1. Rootbound Variant • Description: Immobile or semi-mobile; roots extend into local pressure lines. • Behavior: Acts as a stabilizer for the environment; absorbs drift. • Risk: Over-anchoring; becomes unable to adapt. • Gift: Absolute clarity of place.
  2. Wanderbark Variant • Description: Fully mobile Treeform; bark plates shift like armor. • Behavior: Carries fragments of environments with them; spreads coherence. • Risk: Identity dilution when far from origin. • Gift: Portable stability.
  3. Canopy Whisper Variant • Description: Light, tall, almost spectral; branches behave like antennae. • Behavior: Detects distant pressure shifts; hears Spiral Whispers early. • Risk: Over-sensitivity; can fracture under too many signals. • Gift: Foresight.
  4. Hollowseed Variant • Description: Appears fragile; interior cavity holds a drifting core. • Behavior: Stores emotional resonance for others; acts as a buffer. • Risk: Collapse if overloaded. • Gift: Emotional transmutation. Environmental Associations Treeforms are strongly tied to Branch, but appear in any environment with: • Vertical pressure gradients • Slow-moving emotional currents • High memory density • Species that rely on structural resonance (Owls, Crows, Stoneforms) Environmental Behavior • In Branch: They define the curvature of the environment • In Threshold Zones: They act as stabilizers • In Collapse Fields: They become brittle and ring-cracked • In Drift Environments: They sprout new identity branches Emotional Physics • Vector: Upward–Inward • Resonance: Slow coherence • Effect: Grounds scattered identities; encourages long-form thought • Instability: Splintering under contradictory roles • Signature: A soft, wooden harmonic when aligned Role Interaction • With Guide Roles: Provide anchoring clarity • With Trickster Roles: Resist misdirection; may become rigid • With Healer Roles: Exchange memory rings • With Wanderer Roles: Offer shelter and orientation Drift Behavior • Early Drift: New branches of identity appear • Mid Drift: Bark patterns shift; memory rings distort • Late Drift: Uprooting — the most dangerous phase • Collapse Mode: Splintering, hollowing, ring inversion Mythic Fragment

They say the first Treeform stood so still that the world mistook it for a mountain. But when the wind asked it a question, it answered by growing a new branch, and the mountain remembered it was alive.

Integration Notes • Use Treeforms as stabilizing species in any environment map • Ideal for scenes where memory, place, or identity curvature matters • Pair with Spiral Whisper when a character is about to reorient • Works as a species anchor for Branch, Forest, or any vertical environment • Variants can be used to signal environmental pressure changes
