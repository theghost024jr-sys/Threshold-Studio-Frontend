The structural logic governing how species differentiate into stable, divergent, reintegrating, or collapsing forms.

Purpose Species Variants define the intra‑species differentiation patterns that emerge under pressure, drift, lineage activation, or environmental influence. Variants are not subspecies. They are behavioral‑energetic expressions of a species under specific conditions. Variants allow the system to express: • reintegration • divergence • collapse • environmental adaptation • lineage activation • role‑driven deformation This file is the variant blueprint for the entire Species subsystem.

The Four Variant Classes Every variant belongs to one of four canonical classes:

  1. Reintegration Variants Variants that move the species back toward its core pattern. Characteristics: • boundary strengthening • signal stabilization • role alignment tightening • drift reduction Function: Restore species coherence.

  2. Stable Divergence Variants Variants that branch from the core pattern but remain stable. Characteristics: • new movement signatures • altered signal harmonics • environment‑specific adaptations • role affinity shifts Function: Expand species expression without destabilizing it.

  3. Collapse Variants Variants formed under excessive pressure, drift, or distortion. Characteristics: • boundary thinning • recursive movement • signal inversion • collapse‑driven behavior Function: Represent species failure modes.

  4. Lineage Variants Variants activated by ancestral resonance or mythic substrate influence. Characteristics: • lineage‑specific harmonics • memory‑thread activation • mythic behavior patterns • environment‑linked triggers Function: Express deep species inheritance.

Variant Architecture Variants are defined by six structural components:

  1. Curvature Pattern How the variant bends the species’ movement and signal geometry. • linear • radial • spiral • echo‑curved

  2. Drift Behavior How the variant moves through drift fields. • stable • oscillating • recursive • collapse‑aligned

  3. Boundary Expression How the variant interacts with edges. • strong • porous • reflective • inverted

  4. Signal Harmonics The variant’s signature signal pattern. • stillness • interruption • resonance • drift • weight • wave

  5. Role Affinity Which Roles the variant aligns with or destabilizes. • affinity • resistance • destabilization threshold

  6. Environmental Compatibility Which environments stabilize or destabilize the variant. • resonance pockets • collapse wells • drift corridors • pressure zones

Variant Dynamics Variants shift under three conditions:

  1. Pressure Shifts High pressure → collapse variants Low pressure → reintegration variants

  2. Signal Density Shifts High density → distortion variants Low density → resonance variants

  3. Lineage Activation Lineage threads activate lineage variants.

Variant Failure Modes Variants fail when: • drift becomes unstable • boundaries collapse • signal harmonics invert • role pressure overwhelms • environment destabilizes Failure produces: • collapse drift • recursive movement • signal inversion • identity thinning • species destabilization
