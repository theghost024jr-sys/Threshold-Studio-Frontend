Here’s how a first session actually plays out — not theoretical, not abstract, but exactly how it feels at the table with five humans, five root cards, and a blank Obsidian canvas.

I’ll walk you through it beat‑by‑beat, the way it really unfolds.

⭐ SESSION ONE — “THE FIRST CLICK”

The canvas is open.
Five players sit there staring at it.
No one knows what to do.

This is perfect.

PHASE 1 — Confusion (the intended starting state)

Player One:

“What am I supposed to click?”

Player Two:

“I don’t see any blanks.”

Player Three:

“Are those images or PNG.md pairs?”

Player Four:

“Where are the Layers?”

Player Five:

“I don’t think anything is connected yet.”

Everyone is lost.
This is the correct opening.

Because the world hasn’t spoken yet.

PHASE 2 — The First Recognition

Someone finally notices something.

Player Four squints at the canvas:

“Wait… that node says Mist.
That’s a Layer.
I’m allowed to connect something to those.”

Boom.
The first spark.

Everyone turns to look at the Mist node.

Player Four suddenly has a domain.
The world has given them a place to act.

PHASE 3 — The First Move

Player Four clicks the Mist layer.

Player Four:

“Okay… I can attach an existing .md file to this.”

Player Two:

“There’s a blank .md right there.
But I can’t touch it yet — it’s not blank anymore.”

Player One:

“That PNG.md is close to another PNG.md.
I could link those.”

Player Five:

“Those two .md files have content.
I could connect them.”

But Player Four has the floor.

They pick an .md file with content.
They link it to Mist.

The graph changes.
Contribution #1 for Player Four.

Everyone sees the world shift.

PHASE 4 — The Chain Reaction

Now that something is attached to Mist:

Player Two:

“Oh! That .md is no longer blank.
But that one over there is blank.
I can fill that.”

Player Two clicks the blank .md.

Adds one sentence.

Contribution #1 for Player Two.

The world shifts again.

PHASE 5 — Player One’s Moment

Player One notices two PNG.md pairs sitting close together.

Player One:

“Those two are close.
I can link them…
if I can remember the second one.”

Everyone watches.

Player One closes their eyes.
Points.
Links the two PNG.md files.

Contribution #1 for Player One.

The table cheers.

PHASE 6 — Player Three Enters

Player Three sees the newly linked PNG.md pair.

Player Three:

“That’s an image.
I can biome that.”

Clicks the image.
Declares:

“This is Hollow Orchard.”

Creates a biome node.
Links it to the image.

Contribution #1 for Player Three.

The world now has its first place.

PHASE 7 — Player Five Finds Their Opening

Two .md files now have content and context.

Player Five:

“Those two are related.
I can connect them.”

Clicks one.
Declares the relationship.
Links them.

Contribution #1 for Player Five.

The graph now has its first conceptual bridge.

⭐ THE MOMENT EVERYONE FEELS IT

Someone steps back and says:

“Oh.
I get it now.”

Because the world is no longer empty.
The cards make sense.
The verbs are alive.
The players see their domains.

⭐ PHASE 8 — The First Unlock Approaches

After a few more moves:

  • Player Two fills two more blanks → 3 Contributions → unlock
  • Player One links two more PNG.md pairs → 3 Contributions → unlock
  • Player Three spawns two more biomes → 3 Contributions → unlock
  • Player Four attaches two more .md files → 3 Contributions → unlock
  • Player Five connects two more .md pairs → 3 Contributions → unlock

And then the magic moment:

Player Two:

“I think that was my third meaningful move.”

The table checks.
Nods.

Player Two unlocks a new attribute.

Player Two:

“I want to be able to add two sentences instead of one.”

Approved.

The world expands.
