Doug’s reaction is exactly that. Because of course it is.

The Garden can shake, a volcano can wake up, a tectonic guest can undergo a once‑in‑an‑epoch internal shift — and Doug is still at his desk with municipal‑grade coffee in a paper cup that’s too thin for the temperature.

Here’s how it plays out:

Doug, Mid‑Sip

The alert pings. A soft, unobtrusive tone — the kind designed not to startle anyone, especially Doug.

He doesn’t flinch. He doesn’t straighten. He doesn’t even lower the cup.

He just raises one eyebrow.

Not dramatically. Not suspiciously. Just enough to acknowledge:

“Something somewhere is doing something.”

He keeps drinking.

Because Doug has learned — through experience, through paperwork, through the quiet logic of his world — that:

  • If it’s urgent, the alert will ping again.

  • If it’s really urgent, the lights will flicker.

  • If it’s catastrophic, someone will yell his name from down the hall.

  • And if it’s Rava, well… the floor might tilt a little.

But this alert? This is the kind that says:

“A large geological entity has experienced a personal revelation.”

Which, in Doug’s system, is not an emergency. It’s a form.

He takes another sip.

Doug’s Thought Process (which he would never admit to having)

  • Rava’s moving differently.

  • Good for him.

  • Hope he doesn’t track dust into the Garden again.

  • This coffee tastes like burnt cardboard.

  • I should order more filters.

  • …later.

He taps the alert closed with one finger. Not dismissively — just procedurally.

Then he opens the correct folder:

“Guest Behavioral Variance — Non‑Critical.”

He adds a note:

“Rava: decreased mass‑signature drag. Movement: smoother. Cause: unknown. Status: stable.”

He doesn’t smile. He doesn’t frown.

He just logs it.

And keeps drinking his coffee.
