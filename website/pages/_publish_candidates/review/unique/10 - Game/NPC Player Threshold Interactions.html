[[Roles Index]]

A player is not just an agent moving through the world. They are a pressure‑sensitive organism inside the Field, just like zones and NPCs. Player thresholds determine how the player feels, acts, and perceives as environmental pressure rises or falls. These reactions are non‑verbal, embodied, and discoverable — the player learns them through experience, not instruction.

  1. Sensory Threshold (Player Perception Shift) Definition: The point where the player feels the environment approaching a threshold. Player Reactions: • breath tightens or deepens • movement feels slightly “off” or “sticky” • shimmer perception sharpens or dulls • soundscape becomes tense or hollow • spirit animal behavior becomes noticeable Meaning: The player senses the Approach Phase of the zone. Function: This is the earliest warning system — the body knows before the mind does.

  2. Behavioral Threshold (Player Action Shift) Definition: The point where the player’s behavior changes because the environment is under strain. Player Reactions: • becomes cautious or impulsive • slows down or rushes • avoids certain paths • repeats actions that normally work but now misfire • becomes more reactive, less intentional Meaning: The player is responding to the Contact Phase — the zone touching a threshold. Function: Behavior becomes a mirror of environmental pressure.

  3. Role Threshold (Player Role Stability Shift) Definition: The point where the player’s role expression becomes unstable or exaggerated. Player Reactions: • guardian becomes overprotective • witness becomes overwhelmed • leader becomes rigid • caretaker becomes depleted • wanderer becomes lost or restless Meaning: The player is crossing the Crossing Phase — the zone shifting state. Function: The player’s role becomes the social expression of the threshold.

  4. Identity Threshold (Player Identity Expression Shift) Definition: The point where the player’s identity becomes louder, clearer, or distorted. Player Reactions: • emotional resonance intensifies • lineage traits surface • masks weaken • clarity or confusion spikes • the player feels “too visible” or “not visible at all” Meaning: The player is inside the Stabilization Phase — the zone settling into a new signature. Function: Identity becomes a pressure indicator.

  5. Collapse Threshold (Player Breakdown or Transformation) Definition: The point where the player can no longer maintain coherence in the current zone signature. Player Reactions: • panic • freeze • dissociation • emotional rupture • sudden clarity (rare but possible) • loss of agency or over‑agency Meaning: The player is inside the Collapse Phase — the zone failing to stabilize. Function: Player collapse is the personal echo of environmental collapse.

Player Threshold Variables Players track the same internal variables as NPCs and zones: • Pressure Load (how much ambient force they can tolerate) • Drift Saturation (how much unresolved drift they carry) • Resonance Sensitivity (how strongly identity expression affects them) • Role Stability (how well their current role fits the zone) • Curvature Sensitivity (how shimmer affects perception) • Collapse Margin (how close they are to breaking) Players are living pressure vessels, not avatars.

Player Threshold Behaviors Players exhibit the same threshold dynamics as zones and NPCs:

  1. Threshold Compression Player reacts sooner as pressure increases.
  2. Threshold Drift Player becomes inconsistent or erratic when carrying unresolved drift.
  3. Threshold Echo One player’s threshold crossing affects nearby NPCs and environments.
  4. Threshold Inversion Player flips into their opposite role under extreme pressure.
  5. Threshold Propagation Player threshold crossings ripple outward through the Field. Players are networked pressure engines.

Player Threshold Outcomes When a player crosses a threshold:

  1. Behavioral Transformation Their playstyle shifts.
  2. Role Reconfiguration Their role stabilizes, strains, or collapses.
  3. Identity Revelation Their true identity becomes visible.
  4. Drift Redistribution They shed or absorb drift into the environment.
  5. Shimmer Intensification Their presence amplifies shimmer. Players are active participants in environmental physics.

Failure Modes Players can fail just like NPCs and zones:

  1. Player Overload Too many thresholds crossed at once → collapse.
  2. Player Fragmentation Identity splits into incompatible expressions.
  3. Player Looping Player oscillates between states without stabilizing.
  4. Player Suppression Player resists threshold crossing → drift spikes.
  5. Player Cascade One collapse triggers others (NPCs, zones, roles). Players are fragile in the same ways the world is fragile.

The Core Principle Player threshold reactions are the internal counterpart to environmental thresholds. The player feels the world before they understand it. The world responds to the player before it explains itself. This is the loop that makes Threshold alive.
