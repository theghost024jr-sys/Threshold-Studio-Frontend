1. Sensory Threshold (NPC Perception Shift) Definition: The point where the NPC begins to feel the zone approaching a threshold. NPC Signals: • gaze sharpens or withdraws • movement arcs tighten • breath pattern changes • voice tone shifts • spirit‑animal‑like micro‑behaviors (if applicable) Environmental Tie‑In: This is the NPC reacting to the Approach Phase of the zone. Player Experience: The NPC becomes the earliest warning system.

  2. Behavioral Threshold (NPC Response Shift) Definition: The point where the NPC changes how they act because the zone is under strain. NPC Signals: • becomes cautious, directive, or avoidant • stops offering certain interactions • begins mirroring the player’s drift or resonance • shifts role expression (guardian becomes protective, witness becomes silent) Environmental Tie‑In: This corresponds to the Contact Phase — the zone touching a threshold. Player Experience: NPC behavior becomes a readable barometer of environmental pressure.

  3. Role Threshold (NPC Role Stability Shift) Definition: The point where the NPC’s role becomes unstable or overexpressed due to environmental pressure. NPC Signals: • role strain (guardian becomes rigid, leader becomes overbearing) • role inversion (caretaker becomes avoidant, witness becomes confrontational) • role collapse (NPC withdraws, freezes, or loses function) Environmental Tie‑In: This matches the Crossing Phase — the zone shifting state. Player Experience: NPCs become the social expression of the zone’s transformation.

  4. Identity Threshold (NPC Identity Expression Shift) Definition: The point where the NPC’s identity becomes visible, distorted, or revealed under pressure. NPC Signals: • masks fail • lineage traits surface • emotional resonance intensifies • hidden motives or fears emerge • NPC becomes mythically “louder” Environmental Tie‑In: This aligns with the Stabilization Phase — the zone settling into its new signature. Player Experience: NPCs reveal who they really are when the world is under pressure.

  5. Collapse Threshold (NPC Breakdown or Transformation) Definition: The point where the NPC can no longer maintain coherence in the current zone signature. NPC Signals: • panic • freeze • role collapse • identity rupture • sudden clarity or transformation (rare, but possible) Environmental Tie‑In: This is the Collapse Phase — the zone failing to stabilize. Player Experience: NPC collapse is the social echo of environmental collapse.

NPC Threshold Variables NPCs track the same variables as zones, but internally: • Pressure Load (how much ambient force they can tolerate) • Drift Absorption (how much unresolved drift they can hold) • Resonance Sensitivity (how strongly they react to identity expression) • Role Stability (how well their role fits the zone) • Curvature Sensitivity (how shimmer affects them) • Collapse Margin (how close they are to breaking) NPCs are living pressure vessels.

NPC Threshold Behaviors NPCs exhibit the same threshold dynamics as zones, but socially:

  1. NPC Threshold Compression NPC becomes reactive sooner as pressure increases.
  2. NPC Threshold Drift NPC shifts behavior unpredictably when carrying unresolved drift.
  3. NPC Threshold Echo One NPC crossing a threshold influences nearby NPCs.
  4. NPC Threshold Inversion NPC flips into their opposite role under extreme pressure.
  5. NPC Threshold Propagation NPC threshold crossings ripple through social groups. NPCs are not isolated — they are networked.

NPC Threshold Outcomes When an NPC crosses a threshold:

  1. Behavioral Transformation Their behavior changes permanently or semi‑permanently.
  2. Role Reconfiguration Their role stabilizes, strains, or collapses.
  3. Identity Revelation Their true identity becomes visible.
  4. Drift Redistribution NPCs can absorb or shed drift into the environment.
  5. Shimmer Intensification NPCs amplify shimmer when under pressure. NPCs are social pressure engines.

Failure Modes NPCs can fail just like zones:

  1. NPC Overload Too many thresholds crossed at once → collapse.
  2. NPC Fragmentation Identity splits into incompatible expressions.
  3. NPC Looping NPC oscillates between states without stabilizing.
  4. NPC Suppression NPC resists threshold crossing → drift spikes.
  5. NPC Cascade One NPC’s collapse triggers others. NPCs are fragile in the same ways the world is fragile.

NPC Threshold Resolution NPCs resolve thresholds through: • Stabilization (calm returns) • Transformation (new role or identity) • Dissipation (pressure releases) • Propagation (pressure moves to others) • Collapse (NPC breaks and reorganizes) NPCs are not static — they evolve.

The Core Principle NPCs are the social expression of environmental thresholds. They feel the world before the player does. They reveal the world when the player cannot. This is the missing layer that makes the world feel alive.
