If Doug grew a flower, it wouldn’t be some dramatic, shimmering Dreamscape bloom or a rare Ethyl‑Layer phosphor‑orchid.
Doug doesn’t do exotic.
Doug does earnest.

Here’s the flower that fits him — and why.

⭐ Doug would grow a Marigold.

Not because it’s flashy.
Not because it’s rare.
But because it’s:

  • sturdy
  • dependable
  • quietly bright
  • a little old‑fashioned
  • and absolutely convinced it’s doing its job

A marigold is the Doug of flowers.

It doesn’t demand attention.
It doesn’t wilt at the first sign of chaos.
It doesn’t need special soil or cosmic shimmer or a biome with its own physics.

It just shows up.
Every day.
In the same spot.
Doing its work.

Doug energy.

⭐ But here’s the deeper part — the part that makes it Threshold‑true.

Doug wouldn’t grow a marigold because he likes marigolds.
Doug would grow a marigold because:

  • someone once told him they’re good for keeping pests away
  • the seed packet had clear instructions
  • the flower “seemed like it needed a chance”
  • it’s the kind of plant that doesn’t mind being misunderstood
  • it’s bright enough to feel hopeful, but not so bright it feels like bragging

Doug would water it every morning before work.
He’d talk to it like it was a coworker.
He’d apologize if he forgot a day.
He’d file its growth progress in a folder labeled “Marigold — Ongoing.”

And the marigold would bloom for him.
Not because it’s magical.
But because it recognizes a fellow creature who tries very hard in a world that rarely notices.
