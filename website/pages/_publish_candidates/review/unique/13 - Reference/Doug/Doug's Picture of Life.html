1. Order, but not perfection Doug doesn’t dream of a flawless world. He dreams of a world where things mostly make sense, where corners behave, where shapes stay in their lanes. He wants life to feel: • predictable • understandable • label‑able • sortable into folders Not rigid. Just… tidy.
  2. A place for everything Doug loves the idea that every object, every task, every shape has a proper home. His ideal life looks like: • a desk with drawers that don’t stick • a filing cabinet that closes on the first try • a town where people follow the rules even if they’re silly • a world where nothing sharp surprises him
  3. Quiet purpose Doug doesn’t want glory. He doesn’t want recognition. He doesn’t want a parade for “Shape Safety Awareness Week.” He wants: • a job that matters to him • a routine that feels meaningful • a sense that he’s preventing something, even if no one else sees it He wants to feel useful.
  4. Gentle stability Doug’s picture of life is not dramatic. It’s not heroic. It’s not cinematic. It’s: • morning coffee • a stack of forms • a slow afternoon where nothing goes wrong • a walk home where the sunset is pleasantly rectangular
  5. A world where he’s not the punchline Doug doesn’t need admiration. He just wants people to take him seriously once in a while. Not always. Just enough to feel like he’s part of the machinery of the town, not the weird bolt someone forgot to tighten.

In short: Doug wants life to be calm, orderly, slightly silly, and quietly meaningful — a world where he can do his job, file his reports, and go home feeling like he kept the chaos at bay. He doesn’t want perfection. He wants peace.
