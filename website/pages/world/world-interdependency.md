## World Model Foundations

Threshold’s world is constructed as a multi‑layer reactive system. Each layer represents a different class of forces, constraints, and behaviors. The layers are not independent; they interact continuously, forming a dynamic equilibrium that shifts as nodes, chambers, and external pressures evolve.

The world model is designed to ensure that every change has consequences. A node’s movement affects chamber pressure. Chamber pressure affects stability. Stability affects drift. Drift affects collapse. Collapse affects migration. Migration affects the global distribution of nodes, which feeds back into pressure. This cyclical structure is the core of Threshold’s interdependency model.

### Layer Interaction Principles

1. **No layer is isolated.**  
   Every layer influences at least one other layer. Pressure affects stability. Stability affects drift. Drift affects collapse. Collapse affects migration.

2. **Layers propagate change.**  
   A force applied in one layer propagates through others. A pressure spike in one chamber can destabilize adjacent chambers, triggering drift or collapse.

3. **Layers maintain equilibrium until disrupted.**  
   Chambers and nodes tend toward stable configurations. Pressure, drift, and external forces disrupt equilibrium, causing transitions.

4. **Transitions are rule‑driven.**  
   Drift, collapse, and migration follow deterministic rules based on thresholds, force vectors, and stability values.

### Global Forces

Threshold’s world is influenced by several global forces:

- **Ambient Pressure:**  
  A background force that affects all chambers equally. It represents environmental stress, global instability, or systemic tension.

- **Directional Drift:**  
  A slow, persistent force that nudges nodes toward certain chambers or layers. It represents long‑term trends or gravitational pull.

- **Collapse Cascades:**  
  When one chamber collapses, adjacent chambers may experience pressure spikes, triggering chain reactions.

- **Migration Currents:**  
  Large‑scale flows of nodes between chambers, driven by global instability or major events.

### Local Forces

Local forces operate within chambers:

- **Internal Pressure:**  
  Generated by node interactions, chamber boundaries, and environmental conditions.

- **Stability Decay:**  
  Chambers lose stability over time as pressure accumulates.

- **Boundary Stress:**  
  Pressure applied to chamber boundaries can cause cracks, leaks, or collapse.

- **Node Density:**  
  High node density increases pressure and accelerates stability decay.

### Node Influence

Nodes exert force on chambers and layers:

- **Pressure Contribution:**  
  Nodes generate pressure based on their class and behavior.

- **Stability Impact:**  
  Some nodes stabilize chambers; others destabilize them.

- **Drift Vectors:**

- **Collapse Potential:**

### Chamber Influence

Chambers exert force on nodes:

- **Migration Gates:**

- **Collapse Triggers:**
