Purpose Collapse Signals are the early, mid, and late‑stage indicators that a system (role, identity, species‑expression, or signal‑processing structure) is approaching collapse. They are not symptoms. They are structural warnings emitted by the system itself. Collapse Signals allow: • early detection • drift interpretation • collapse prevention • collapse mapping • recovery planning They are the language of structural failure.

  1. Collapse Signal Categories Collapse signals fall into three universal classes: 1.1 Soft Signals (Early Stage) These appear when drift begins to exceed tolerance but the structure is still intact. Characteristics: • subtle • emotional • relational • easily dismissed Examples: • boundary blur • hesitation • emotional fog • reduced initiative • scanning without action • tonal softening or flattening Soft signals indicate incipient drift, not collapse.

1.2 Sharp Signals (Mid Stage) These appear when the system is actively destabilizing. Characteristics: • sudden • directional • patterned • interpretable Examples: • abrupt tone shifts • pattern enforcement • hyper‑vigilance • over‑analysis • rigidity • relational withdrawal Sharp signals indicate active destabilization.

1.3 Hard Signals (Late Stage) These appear when collapse is imminent or already underway. Characteristics: • non‑negotiable • structural • overwhelming • system‑wide Examples: • shutdown • lock • dissociation • emotional inversion • signal overload • identity fracture Hard signals indicate collapse activation.
