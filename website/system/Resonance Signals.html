00 Meta Systems — Signal Cluster / Identity Cluster Resonance Signals are signals that carry identity‑encoded emotional frequency, capable of amplifying, stabilizing, or destabilizing beings, environments, and roles. Unlike ordinary signals, which transmit information, Resonance Signals transmit state — the internal condition of the sender, the environment, or the system itself. A Resonance Signal does not say something. It is something.

Purpose Resonance Signals define: • how emotional states propagate through the world • how identity influences signal behavior • how resonance loops begin • how species and roles attune to one another • how collapse or healing spreads • how environments amplify or dampen meaning They are the emotional physics of Threshold.

Primary Resonance Signal Types

  1. Harmonic Signals Signals that stabilize and align. • clarity • coherence • emotional grounding • identity reinforcement Common in Air, Earth, and Healer‑aligned species.

  2. Amplification Signals Signals that intensify emotional or identity states. • emotional charge • resonance loop acceleration • role overexpression Common in Ember and Ethyl environments.

  3. Dissonant Signals Signals that introduce instability. • semantic conflict • emotional turbulence • drift acceleration Common in Mist and Stone environments.

  4. Inversion Signals Signals that flip meaning or identity polarity. • shadow activation • role inversion • collapse precursors Common in Mist–Ember boundary zones.

  5. Restorative Signals Signals that repair or integrate. • emotional healing • identity realignment • collapse recovery Common in Healer‑aligned species and Earth environments.

Signal Mechanics Resonance Encoding Every Resonance Signal carries: • emotional charge • identity signature • species resonance • environmental curvature • drift susceptibility This is why the same signal behaves differently depending on who receives it.

Propagation Resonance Signals travel through: • emotional fields • environmental layers • species attunement • role alignment • memory and lineage Propagation speed and stability depend on curvature.

Amplification Conditions A Resonance Signal amplifies when: • the receiver shares identity resonance • the environment matches the signal’s element • a resonance loop is already active • emotional charge is high This is how cascades begin.

Dampening Conditions A Resonance Signal weakens when: • the environment is mismatched • the receiver has low sensitivity • drift thresholds are low • identity is stable This is how collapse is prevented.

Environmental Influence Each Layer shapes Resonance Signals differently: • Air: clarity, long‑range propagation • Earth: grounding, dampening • Ember: amplification, volatility • Mist: ambiguity, echoing • Stone: rigidity, fracture risk • Ethyl: symbolic bleed‑through, surreal resonance This is where resonance becomes predictable.

Species Influence Species alter resonance through: • sensory attunement • emotional resonance • drift susceptibility • shadow tendencies This is where the Species–Role Sense Table and Species–Role Expression Table plug in.

Role Influence Roles shape resonance through: • archetypal stance • emotional posture • relational position • drift vectors This is where Role Drift and Role Identity Drift connect.

Downstream Effects Resonance Signals lead to: • resonance loops • identity drift • role drift • collapse signals • threshold events • healing or integration They are the starting point of most systemic motion.
