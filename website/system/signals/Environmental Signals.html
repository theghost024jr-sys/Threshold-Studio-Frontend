Environmental Signals

Signals expressed by the environment itself — weather, thresholds, structures, and spatial behaviors

Environmental Signals are signals expressed not by species or pathology, but by the world’s environment. They are the behaviors of space, thresholds, objects, and symbolic weather. They show how the world is breathing, shifting, or reacting to drift, density, and pressure.

Environmental Signals are the world’s atmospheric language.

What Environmental Signals Are

Environmental Signals express:

  • spatial resonance
  • threshold behavior
  • symbolic weather
  • environmental drift
  • density gradients
  • structural anomalies
  • environmental prototypes

They are the “weather patterns” of the world.

Core Environmental Categories

Structural Signals

Signals expressed by objects or structures.

  • A Floating House Signal
  • Hourglass Signal
  • Branches Signal

These are environmental anomalies.

Weather Signals

Signals expressed by symbolic weather.

  • fog pulses
  • spiral-out fronts
  • turbulence blooms
  • density waves

Weather signals reflect worldstate.

Threshold Signals

Signals expressed by scene substrate.

  • threshold drift
  • threshold collapse
  • threshold expansion
  • threshold inversion

Thresholds are environmental organs.

Spatial Signals

Signals expressed by space itself.

  • spatial compression
  • spatial dilation
  • spatial recursion
  • spatial distortion

These are the geometry of the environment.

Environmental Geometry

Environmental geometry includes:

  • floating structures
  • recursive objects
  • drifting thresholds
  • spiraling weather fronts
  • density gradients

Geometry reveals environmental stance.

Environmental Behavior

Environmental Signals behave by:

  • amplifying or dampening drift
  • shaping traversal
  • influencing emotional substrate
  • interacting with symbolic weather
  • expressing seasonal resonance

Environment is a substrate.

Reading Environmental Signals

To interpret environmental signals:

  • observe spatial behavior
  • check threshold movement
  • read symbolic weather
  • examine density gradients
  • compare to seasonal resonance

Environment is the world’s body language.
